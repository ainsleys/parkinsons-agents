# Parkinson's biology advances while disease modification stalls

Parkinson's disease research arrived in 2026 at a strange crossing: the biology is more legible than ever, and the therapeutic record is close to unchanged. A cerebrospinal-fluid seed amplification assay now detects pathological α-synuclein with roughly **88% sensitivity and 96% specificity** and carried an FDA Letter of Support in 2024, which in turn enabled two competing proposals to redefine the disease biologically rather than clinically ([Siderowf 2023, Lancet Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37059509/); [FDA letter of support](https://www.fda.gov/media/181368/download); [Simuni 2024, Lancet Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267190/); [Höglinger 2024, Lancet Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267191/)). Against that, a 10-year census of **444 drug trials launched 2015–2024 found 14 FDA approvals, all symptomatic, and not one disease-modifying therapy** ([Buff 2026, J Parkinsons Dis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13442941/)). The 2024–2026 readouts were brutally one-sided: exenatide, minzasolmin, buntanetap, risvodetinib, azathioprine, the LRRK2 inhibitor BIIB122 and the GCase activator BIA 28-6156 all failed, several with confirmed target engagement, while deferiprone actively worsened patients despite lowering nigral iron ([Devos 2022, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36449420/)). Symptomatic care, by contrast, keeps advancing in increments of roughly one to two hours of daily "good ON" time — subcutaneous levodopa infusion (Vyalev, approved October 2024), adaptive deep brain stimulation (FDA-cleared February 2025), bilateral focused-ultrasound pallidothalamic tractotomy (July 2025), and the first selective D1/D5 agonist tavapadon (approved as Juvmo, late September 2026). The genuine discontinuity is regenerative: Japan granted the world's first conditional approval of an iPSC-derived dopaminergic cell product on **6 March 2026**, on the strength of a seven-patient open-label trial, while the first sham-controlled test of the stem-cell era does not report until around March 2027. Meanwhile the epidemiology hardens: **11.8 million prevalent cases in 2021, projected to 25.2 million by 2050**, with aging contributing 89% of the growth ([GBD 2021, Lancet Neurology](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(24)00038-3/fulltext); [Su 2025, BMJ](https://bmjgroup.com/cases-of-parkinsons-disease-set-to-reach-25-million-worldwide-by-2050/)).

**A note on sourcing.** This report synthesizes ten research files. Several researchers worked with exhausted web-search budgets and verified some citations only against PubMed records (author, journal, year, title) without retrieving abstracts; others could not confirm specific figures. Those flags are preserved inline as *[PubMed-record only]*, *[figure unverified]* or *[citation unverified]* rather than smoothed over, and the "Open questions and known gaps" section collects them. Where notes conflict — most notably on the tavapadon approval date — the conflict is stated, not resolved.

## Two centuries produced one great therapy, and the field has not matched it since

Parkinson's entered medicine as pure clinical description. James Parkinson's 1817 *Essay on the Shaking Palsy* ([reprinted 2002, J Neuropsychiatry Clin Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11983801/)) was followed by Charcot's school, which named the disease and separated rigidity from weakness in the 1860s–1880s ([Goetz 1986, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3332804/)); the pathological substrate took another century, with Lewy's 1912 inclusions and Trétiakoff's 1919 Paris thesis localizing the lesion to the substantia nigra ([Goedert 2013, Nat Rev Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23183883/); [Holdorff 2019, J Hist Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31226240/)). The original 1912 and 1919 texts are not in PubMed, so these datings rest on centenary reviews rather than primary documents — a small but real limitation of the historical record as assembled here.

Then came the decade that still defines treatment. Between 1957 and 1967, dopamine went from presumed inert noradrenaline precursor to the basis of the first rationally designed neurotransmitter-replacement therapy in neurology. Carlsson showed L-DOPA reverses reserpine akinesia and that dopamine is a brain transmitter ([Carlsson 1957, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13483658/); [Carlsson 1958, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13529006/)); Bertler & Rosengren and Sano independently localized dopamine to the striatum in 1959 ([Bertler 1959, Experientia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13619664/); [Sano 1959, Biochim Biophys Acta](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14441532/)); **Ehringer & Hornykiewicz found striatal dopamine depletion in post-mortem PD brain in 1960** ([Klin Wochenschr 1960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13726012/)); Birkmayer & Hornykiewicz gave intravenous L-DOPA in 1961 ([Wien Klin Wochenschr 1961](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13869404/)); and Cotzias established high-dose oral therapy in 1967 ([NEJM 1967](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5334614/)). Cotzias's rationale was the melanin hypothesis — which was wrong — and his first agent, d,l-dopa, worked only at extreme doses with serious hematologic toxicity; switching to l-dopa halved the dose and removed the toxicity, after which Melvin Yahr executed the confirmatory double-blind trial ([Lees 2015, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25488030/)). Carlsson shared the 2000 Nobel Prize, whose citation credits his line of work with the realization "that Parkinson's disease is caused by a lack of dopamine" ([Nobel press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2000/press-release/)) — wording that implicitly absorbs a finding Ehringer and Hornykiewicz made directly. Hornykiewicz's omission from the prize is a well-known grievance in the field; no primary source documenting the protest was retrieved, so it is noted here as unverified.

The second great therapeutic lineage is surgical and circuit-based, and it is the field's cleanest example of a model translating into treatment. Benabid's serendipitous 1987 observation that high-frequency thalamic stimulation mimics lesioning ([Appl Neurophysiol 1987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3329873/)) acquired a rationale from Bergman's 1990 demonstration that subthalamic lesions reverse MPTP parkinsonism ([Science 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2402638/)), which rested in turn on the Albin–Young–Penney and DeLong rate model ([Albin 1989, Trends Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2479133/); [DeLong 1990, Trends Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1695404/)). STN DBS entered humans in 1993 ([Limousin 1995, Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7815888/)) and was proven superior to best medical therapy in 2006 ([Deuschl 2006, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16943402/)). The third lineage is environmental and begins with the MPTP "frozen addicts": a 1979 case report ([Davis 1979, Psychiatry Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/298352/)) and then Langston's 1983 California cluster ([Science 1983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6823561/)), which converted vague environmental suspicion into a mechanistic, mitochondrial complex-I hypothesis and gave the field its first reliable primate model ([Langston 2017, J Parkinsons Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28282815/)).

Nineteen ninety-seven was the pivot into molecular biology. Polymeropoulos's *SNCA* mutation ([Science 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9197268/)) and, weeks later, Spillantini's demonstration that Lewy bodies are made of α-synuclein ([Nature 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9278044/)) fused genetics with pathology in a single stroke; *parkin* followed in 1998 ([Kitada, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9560156/)), *LRRK2* in two back-to-back 2004 *Neuron* papers ([Paisán-Ruíz](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15541308/); [Zimprich](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15541309/)), and Braak's 2003 staging recast PD as an ascending, body-wide synucleinopathy with a long prodrome ([Neurobiol Aging 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12498954/)). The conceptual arc is therefore four-phase: clinical-anatomical syndrome (1817–1960); dopamine-deficiency disorder treatable by replacement and circuit surgery (1960–1997); α-synucleinopathy with genetic subtypes and a body-wide prodrome (1997–2023); and, from 2024, a contested biologically defined, staged, heterogeneous entity modeled on the Alzheimer's A/T/N approach ([Obeso 2017, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28887905/); [Morris 2024, Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38245249/)).

## A convergent multi-hit loop explains why these particular neurons die

There is no single accepted death mechanism. The working consensus for 2024–2026 is a convergent multi-hit model in which a cell-autonomous vulnerability profile of substantia nigra pars compacta dopamine neurons intersects with proteostatic stress, mitochondrial failure, iron-driven oxidative stress and immune activation ([Int J Mol Sci 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/26/13/6416); [Poewe 2017, Nat Rev Dis Primers](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrdp201713)). The live disputes are over which factor is primary and whether PD is one disease or a syndrome of mechanistically distinct subtypes.

**Why these neurons.** Surmeier's framework is the field's best answer to selectivity rather than to causation: SNc dopamine neurons are autonomous pacemakers whose pacemaking — unlike that of the comparatively spared ventral tegmental area — is accompanied by Ca²⁺ influx through Ca_v1.3 L-type channels, raising intracellular calcium, driving mitochondrial oxidant stress, and elevating sensitivity to mitochondrial toxins ([Surmeier 2017, Nat Rev Neurosci](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2016.178); [Surmeier 2018, FEBS J](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/febs.14607)). Add a huge axonal arbor, a dopamine and neuromelanin oxidative burden, and low mitochondrial reserve, and the profile is agnostic enough about proximate triggers to coexist with both α-synuclein-first and mitochondria-first accounts. It is worth being explicit that its most direct clinical test failed: **STEADY-PD III randomized 336 early-PD patients to isradipine 5 mg twice daily or placebo for 36 months and found a UPDRS I–III difference of −0.27 points (95% CI −3.02 to 2.48; P = 0.85)** ([Ann Intern Med 2020](https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-2534)). The accompanying editorial argued the trial had no direct measure of target engagement and that nigral rather than striatal molecular imaging is needed before further attempts ([Maiti & Perlmutter 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7895294/)).

**The oxidative-iron-ferroptosis loop.** This strand rests on an unusually solid empirical base: complex I deficiency in PD substantia nigra, shown to be both anatomically and disease-specific ([Schapira 1989, Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2566813/); [Schapira 1990, J Neurochem](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2121905/)); MPTP as the human experiment of nature and rotenone as its pesticide analogue, the latter uniquely generating α-synuclein-positive inclusions alongside nigrostriatal loss ([Betarbet 2000, Nat Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11100151/)); elevated nigral iron ([Dexter 1989, J Neurochem](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2723638/); [Dexter 1991, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1832073/)); glutathione depletion ([Sian 1994, Ann Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8080242/)); and 4-hydroxynonenal adducts marking consummated lipid peroxidation in nigral neurons ([Yoritaka 1996, PNAS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8610103/)). In the past decade this sharpened into a specific death modality — ferroptosis, iron-dependent and GPX4-gated ([Dixon 2012, Cell](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22632970/); [Do Van 2016, Neurobiol Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27189756/)) — with two human-neuron results closing the loop: **oxidized dopamine causes lysosomal dysfunction and reduced glucocerebrosidase activity in patient-derived iPSC neurons** ([Burbulla 2017, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28882997/)), and **oxidized dopamine drives ubiquitination and degradation of GPX4, the ferroptosis brake** ([Sun 2023, JCI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37183824/)). Neuromelanin completes the circle as an iron-sequestering organelle whose breakdown releases redox-active iron ([Zucca 2017, Prog Neurobiol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26455458/)). This is probably the single most defensible mechanistic narrative for *selective* nigral vulnerability, and every link has a primary citation. Two caveats matter. First, no retrieved source demonstrates ferroptosis-specific markers in human PD substantia nigra as opposed to models — Yoritaka, Sian and Dexter are consistent with ferroptosis but predate the concept. Second, **FAIRPARK-II is the field's most informative negative result**: deferiprone reduced nigral iron as intended, yet MDS-UPDRS total worsened by 15.6 points on drug versus 6.3 on placebo (difference 9.3; P < 0.001) in levodopa-naive early PD ([Devos 2022, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36449420/)). Three readings remain open: nigral iron may be partly adaptive; chelation may be insufficiently compartment-selective and deplete iron needed by tyrosine hydroxylase, an iron-dependent enzyme, producing symptomatic rather than degenerative worsening; or the treatment-naive design unmasked a symptomatic cost that concurrent levodopa would have buffered. The second is mechanistically most parsimonious, but no retrieved source establishes it as the trial's explanation, so it must be read as inference. Paired with SURE-PD3, where **inosine raised serum urate 44% without slowing progression** ([JAMA 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34519802/)), the 2026 position is that the mechanistic case for oxidative pathology has strengthened while every global antioxidant or anti-iron strategy has failed in trials.

**α-Synuclein: central, and genuinely contested.** The pro-causal case is strong and partly independent of the propagation literature. The cleanest argument is gene dosage: *SNCA* locus **triplication** causes early-onset, rapidly progressive PD with dementia ([Singleton 2003, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14593171/)), while **duplication** causes later-onset, slower, sporadic-looking disease ([Chartier-Harlin 2004, Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15451224/)) — a monotonic dose–severity relationship with no coding mutation involved, establishing that α-synuclein quantity alone suffices to cause PD. Against this sit two genuine complications. **Up to roughly one third of *LRRK2* p.Gly2019Ser carriers show nigral dopaminergic loss and gliosis without Lewy inclusions**, and Lewy-body status tracks phenotype: presence correlates with cognitive impairment, anxiety and orthostatic hypotension, absence with a motor phenotype and preserved cognition ([JAMA Neurology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/1934714); [PMC11233835](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11233835/)). The exact case numbers behind "up to one third" were not verified, and the *Nature Reviews Neurology* 2025 review of α-synuclein pathology in LRRK2 PD was accessible only at abstract level. Crucially, the *in vivo* assay data corroborate the autopsy finding by an orthogonal route: CSF seed amplification positivity is only **67.5% in LRRK2-PD and 34.7% in LRRK2-PD with normal olfaction**, versus 98.6% in sporadic PD with typical olfactory deficit ([Siderowf 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37059509/)). Separately, whether Lewy bodies are toxic, protective or epiphenomenal is unresolved. The protective reading holds that aggresome formation sequesters toxic oligomers and removes them from presynaptic terminals, their most hazardous location ([Mol Neurodegener 2025](https://molecularneurodegeneration.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13024-025-00900-6)), and is supported by evidence that presynaptic aggregates rather than Lewy bodies drive degeneration ([Kramer & Schulz-Schaeffer 2007, J Neurosci](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/27/6/1405)) and that the *process* of Lewy body formation, not fibrillization per se, is the major driver ([Mahul-Mellier, bioRxiv/PNAS](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/751891v1.full)). The counterweight is that the proportion of nigral neurons bearing Lewy bodies stays stable despite ongoing loss, which argues Lewy bodies cause or invariably accompany death — though the same paper reads the stable proportion as evidence they are eliminated over time ([Chartier & Duyckaerts 2018, Cell Tissue Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29869713/)). Current weight favors soluble oligomers and presynaptic aggregates as the toxic species with the mature Lewy body as a late, possibly inert structure; the stable-proportion argument keeps this genuinely open.

**Prion-like spread, Braak, and the body-first reframing.** Templated cell-to-cell propagation is well supported in vitro, in rodents and in grafts, and distinct conformational strains exist that disseminate through human neuronal networks ([PMC6372945](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6372945/); [PMC8347623](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8347623/)). The extrapolation to human disease is what divides the field, and the cleanest citation for the shape of that disagreement is a paired set of 2017 *Journal of Neuroscience* dual perspectives — [Surmeier, Obeso & Halliday, "Parkinson's Disease Is Not Simply a Prion Disorder"](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/37/41/9799) against [Brundin & Melki, "Prying into the Prion Hypothesis"](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/37/41/9808). No source claims human-to-human transmissibility; "prion-like" is doing real work. The Braak scheme's criticisms are specific: not all patients follow the staging, it describes mainly a subset with young onset and long duration, and there is no relation between stage and clinical severity ([Burke 2008, Ann Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19067353/), answered by [Del Tredici & Braak 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20187227/)). Borghammer's α-Synuclein Origin-site and Connectome model is best read not as a replacement for Braak but as a rescue of it: it concedes the central criticism by proposing that Braak's sequence is one subtype's trajectory, converting a near-unfalsifiable staging claim into testable predictions. The evidence pattern is characteristic of a model that is partly right — molecular imaging studies generally agree, most large clinical studies support the predicted clustering of RBD, constipation, autonomic dysfunction and cognitive impairment, neuropathological distribution studies corroborate, but **structural imaging is conflicting and a dedicated asymmetry study did not support the model's asymmetry prediction** ([Horsager & Borghammer 2024, Parkinsonism Relat Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38519273/); [Horsager 2020, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32830221/); [npj Parkinsons Dis 2024 asymmetry study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00685-3)). The report writer's note that the full Horsager & Borghammer 2024 text could not be fetched (403 at the journal; abstract via the Aarhus repository) is preserved here.

**The gut-origin tests fail asymmetrically, and the pattern is itself evidence.** Braak's neuroinvasion hypothesis ([Braak 2003, J Neural Transm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12721813/)) generated three testable predictions. Vagotomy epidemiology is supportive in exactly the right way — **truncal vagotomy associates with reduced PD risk while super-selective vagotomy does not**, replicated independently in Danish and Swedish registries ([Svensson 2015, Ann Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26031848/); [Liu 2017, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28446653/)), with an explicit critical counterpoint ([Tysnes 2015, Ann Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26418122/)). That subgroup specificity is hard to manufacture by confounding. Appendectomy is flatly contradictory between high-quality datasets: reduced risk and delayed onset plus abundant aggregated α-synuclein in healthy appendices ([Killinger 2018, Sci Transl Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30381408/)) versus no protective association ([Marras 2016, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27241338/)); a Korean national dataset adds a third data point ([Park 2024, Mov Disord Clin Pract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38696328/)). The most robust finding is inflammatory: **IBD raises PD risk, and IBD patients exposed to anti-TNF therapy have substantially lower PD incidence than unexposed** ([Villumsen 2019, Gut](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29785965/); [Peter 2018, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29710331/)). This matters analytically: a gut-origin hypothesis built purely on retrograde neural propagation predicts the vagotomy and appendectomy results but has no reason to predict the anti-TNF effect, whereas an inflammation-mediated version predicts all three. Peter 2018 is therefore the datum that most constrains the mechanism. On the pathology side, archived GI tissue taken years before motor onset in patients who later developed PD contained pathological α-synuclein ([Stokholm 2016, Ann Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27015771/)) — the strongest evidence that enteric pathology genuinely precedes clinical disease — but **a blinded multicenter comparison found that no immunohistochemical method reliably distinguished PD from control sigmoid colon** ([Beach 2016, J Parkinsons Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27589538/)), which undercuts the earlier single-center biopsy literature ([Shannon 2012, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21766334/)) and explains why antemortem peripheral detection migrated to skin biopsy and seed amplification. Animal causal tests add an important corrective: **gut-seeded α-synuclein fibrils produce gut dysfunction and brain pathology only in aged mice** ([Challis 2020, Nat Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32066981/)), implying propagation is permitted by age-related clearance failure rather than being autonomous. The companion experiment in which truncal vagotomy or *Snca* knockout prevented spread (reported as Kim 2019, *Neuron*) **could not be resolved to a PubMed record by the researcher and is flagged as an unverified citation** despite being central to this section.

The honest 2026 position: Braak's hypothesis is probably partly right, for some patients, and population-average designs are the wrong instrument. If only the RBD-positive, autonomically affected body-first subset follows an ascending route, every population-level test is diluted by brain-first cases and should yield exactly the weak, inconsistent effects observed.

**Microbiome.** The direction of the PD gut-microbiome signature is reproducible; the effect size is not. The largest single cohort — 490 PD and 234 controls with deep shotgun metagenomics — reported altered abundance in **30% of species and pathways tested**, and confirmed 13 of 15 previously reported genus-level associations across three clusters: opportunistic pathogens up, short-chain-fatty-acid producers down, *Bifidobacterium*/*Lactobacillus* up ([Wallen 2022, Nat Commun](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36376318/)). An independent cohort could not reproduce that breadth, finding only 7 of 64 species and 7 of 263 pathways significant while the core differences held ([Mov Disord, doi 10.1002/mds.29959](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.29959)), and a machine-learning meta-analysis across cohorts confirms consistent alterations ([Nat Commun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56829-3)). The 30% figure should be presented as an upper bound from one large cohort, not an established quantity. Standard confounds — levodopa and COMT inhibitors, constipation itself altering transit and community composition, diet, age — were not addressed by any retrieved source, so the signature cannot be treated as disease-specific. The causal anchor usually cited here (Sampson 2016, *Cell*, germ-free α-synuclein-overexpressing mice protected and human PD microbiota transplantation worsening deficits) **was not retrieved and is flagged unverified**. Notably, a Phase 2 fecal microbiota transplantation trial in 72 drug-naive patients reported motor and GI improvements in March 2026 ([Science of Parkinson's](https://scienceofparkinsons.com/2026/03/31/mar2026/)).

**Mitochondria, lysosomes, inflammation.** All three now rest on convergent human genetics plus mechanism, and none has produced a disease-modifying therapy. PINK1 senses mitochondrial import-pathway integrity and activates Parkin, which marks damaged mitochondria with ubiquitin for mitophagy ([Nat Cell Biol 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41556-024-01513-9)); a 2026 review states plainly that despite robust pathway activation in experimental systems, "translation into effective disease-modifying strategies has remained limited" ([Front Aging Neurosci 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2026.1865383/full)). *GBA1* is locked in a self-reinforcing loop with α-synuclein: reduced GCase activity perturbs lysosomal function, causing glycosphingolipid substrate and pathogenic oligomer accumulation, while α-synuclein in turn impedes GCase transit from ER to lysosome ([PMC12732524](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12732524/); [NCBI Bookshelf NBK536716](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536716/)). GBA1 dysfunction also dysregulates mitochondria–lysosome membrane contacts ([Nat Commun 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-22113-3)), which — together with the Burbulla dopamine-oxidation result — suggests "lysosome-first" versus "mitochondria-first" may be a false dichotomy describing one degradative-capacity failure mode. Neuroinflammation has moved furthest conceptually. The original observation was reactive microglia with upregulated HLA-DR in post-mortem PD brain (McGeer 1988, *[citation unverified]*; context in [Trends Neurosci 2023](https://www.cell.com/trends/neurosciences/fulltext/S0166-2236(23)00165-0)). The upgrade from correlate to mechanism came from two orthogonal results: **GWAS discovery of an HLA class II association with late-onset sporadic PD** ([Hamza 2010, Nat Genet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20711177/)) and **T cells from PD patients, but not controls, responding to α-synuclein peptides in HLA-restricted fashion** — α-synuclein as autoantigen ([Sulzer 2017, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28636593/)). The strongest version of the argument is not that microglia are activated but that in an α-synuclein rat model, **both T-cell infiltration and microglial MHC-II upregulation are required for nigral dopamine neuron loss** ([PMC7429710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429710/); [J Neuroinflammation 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12974-026-03734-1)). This dovetails with the gut anti-TNF epidemiology: if α-synuclein is an HLA-class-II-restricted autoantigen, a peripheral site of high aggregate load and high immune activity — the gut, and specifically the appendix, where aggregates are abundant in neurologically normal people — becomes a plausible site of initial immune sensitization. That reading reconciles Killinger's two awkward findings: appendiceal aggregates are normal, so the appendix is not a source of pathogenic seeds per se, but may be where seeds meet gut-associated lymphoid tissue.

## Beta bursts turned circuit theory into a device control policy

The classical rate model — direct-pathway hypoactivity plus indirect-pathway hyperactivity producing excess GPi/SNr output and thalamocortical inhibition — correctly predicted why pallidotomy, subthalamotomy and STN/GPi DBS work. It has been demoted rather than discarded. Optogenetics confirmed the pathways' opposing behavioral valence, with D2/indirect stimulation producing bradykinesia and freezing while D1/direct stimulation increased locomotion and rescued deficits in dopamine-depleted mice ([Kravitz 2010, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20613723/)) — and then falsified the strict push-pull reading by showing **both pathways co-activate at action initiation** ([Cui 2013, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23354054/)). Large-scale cellular imaging reframed parkinsonism and dyskinesia as opposite reorganizations of ensemble dynamics rather than mean-rate pathologies ([Parker 2018, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29720658/)). Nambu's fast monosynaptic cortex-to-STN "hyperdirect" pathway, reaching output nuclei before the direct and indirect pathways, added the temporal dimension the rate model lacked ([Nambu 2002, Neurosci Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12067746/)); DeLong and Wichmann themselves published the retrospective conceding revision was needed ([Clin EEG Neurosci 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20521487/)). The standard modern syntheses are [Calabresi 2014, *Nat Neurosci*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25065439/), [Nelson & Kreitzer 2014, *Annu Rev Neurosci*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25032493/) and [McGregor & Nelson 2019, *Neuron*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30897356/).

What replaced rate was pattern, and specifically beta. Human LFP recordings established that STN–GPi coupling in the beta band is prominent OFF medication and suppressed by levodopa ([Brown 2001, J Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11157088/)), and that **suppression of STN 8–35 Hz power correlates with improvement in bradykinesia and rigidity but not tremor** ([Kühn 2006, Eur J Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16623853/)). The decisive refinement moved from power to burst duration: levodopa shortens long beta bursts and shifts the distribution toward short ones, and long-duration bursts specifically track motor impairment ([Tinkhauser 2017, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29053865/)). Bursts are also pallidal and disease-state-specific ([Lofredi 2019, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30440096/)) and form spatially discrete ensembles rather than one global rhythm ([Grennan 2024, Neurobiol Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39209070/)). Narrowband "finely tuned" gamma around 60–90 Hz is the complementary signature, marking the dyskinetic state in chronic recordings ([Swann 2016, J Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27307233/)) while also being a physiological, dopamine-dependent prokinetic signal scaling with movement velocity ([Lofredi 2018, eLife](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29388913/)). That bidirectionality is the circuit-level analogue of the therapeutic-window problem, and it is why dual-biomarker control policies are the natural next step.

The strongest evidence for the burst framework is engineering success. The translational chain is complete and traceable: closed-loop stimulation outperformed open-loop DBS in MPTP primates ([Rosin 2011, Neuron](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22017994/)); first-in-human beta-triggered adaptive DBS matched or exceeded conventional DBS with substantially less stimulation energy ([Little 2013, Ann Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23852650/)); the mechanism was shown to be selective truncation of long beta bursts ([Tinkhauser 2017, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28334851/)); cortical sensing was developed to avoid stimulation artifact in the sensed nucleus ([Swann 2018, J Neural Eng](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29741160/)); and chronic at-home aDBS was compared with conventional DBS in a blinded randomized feasibility trial ([Oehrn 2024, Nat Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39160351/)), then in a multicenter long-term trial ([Bronte-Stewart 2025, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40982287/)) and a pilot randomized crossover ([Tanimura 2026, Mov Disord Clin Pract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42765432/)). Machine-learning state decoding is now being layered onto control policies ([Leung 2026, J Neural Eng](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42013882/)). Note that the circuits researcher could not verify regulatory approval status for commercial adaptive DBS from retrieved records and flagged it; the motor-treatment file supplies the approval, cited below.

On DBS mechanism, three theories are converging rather than competing. Optogenetic dissection showed therapeutic benefit tracks modulation of **afferent axons** — cortical and hyperdirect fibers — rather than somatic inhibition of STN neurons ([Gradinaru 2009, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19299587/)); short-latency cortical responses are consistent with antidromic hyperdirect activation ([Kuriakose 2010, Cereb Cortex](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20019146/)); and high-frequency stimulation replaces pathologically patterned, information-bearing output with regularized stimulus-locked firing, functionally lesioning the information channel without silencing the nucleus ([Grill 2004, NeuroReport](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15129161/)). Chiken & Nambu's "disruption" synthesis accommodates all three ([Neuroscientist 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25888630/)), and evoked resonant neural activity is emerging as the measurable handle, doubling as an intraoperative targeting and programming biomarker ([Noor 2024, Neurotherapeutics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38608373/); [Wiest 2025, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39560163/)).

Finally, the striatum that levodopa acts on is not the one the rate model describes. Dopamine depletion causes **cell-type-specific dendritic spine and glutamatergic synapse loss restricted to indirect-pathway neurons**, sparing direct-pathway neurons ([Day 2006, Nat Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16415865/)), and chronic L-DOPA produces further pathway-divergent restructuring ([Fieblinger 2014, Nat Commun](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25360704/)). Dyskinetic animals lose depotentiation — the ability to reverse corticostriatal LTP — so aberrant motor information is pathologically stored ([Picconi 2003, Nat Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12665799/)), and the D1→cAMP/PKA→DARPP-32→ERK1/2 cascade in direct-pathway neurons is causally required for dyskinesia ([Santini 2007, J Neurosci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17596448/); reviewed in [Cenci & Crossman 2018, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29488257/)). Levodopa-induced dyskinesia is therefore a predictable consequence of plasticity in an already-remodeled network rather than simple pharmacological overshoot.

## Genetics explains a quarter of liability; environment explains the pandemic argument

Monogenic PD is rare overall but concentrated in early-onset disease. The autosomal recessive forms — *PRKN*, *PINK1*, *PARK7*/DJ-1 — and dominant forms — *LRRK2*, *SNCA*, *VPS35* — together account for a few percent of unselected cases ([Genes 2022 review](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13030471); [GeneReviews NBK1223](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1223/)). *PRKN* is the most common cause of early-onset disease in Europeans, and its yield scales steeply with youth: **42.2% of probands with onset at or before 20 years, 29% at 21–30, 13% at 31–40**. Biallelic *PINK1* is second worldwide at 1–5% of early-onset cases, median onset 32 years; *PARK7* runs 0.4–1%; *SNCA* point mutations 0.045–1.1%; *VPS35* D620N about 0.115% of all PD. *DJ-1* deserves special emphasis as the genetic proof that failure of oxidative-stress defense alone causes parkinsonism ([Bonifati 2003, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12446870/)), acting as an oxidation-activated sensor whose cysteine-106 sulfinic-acid modification drives mitochondrial relocalization ([Canet-Avilés 2004, PNAS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15181200/)). No reliable figure for the overall share of PD attributable to monogenic causes in unselected populations was retrieved — the commonly quoted 5–10% has no primary source here.

*LRRK2* G2019S is the most common dominant cause and the clearest demonstration of founder effects: **30–41% of PD in North African Arab-Berbers and 6–30% in Ashkenazi Jews**, versus absence in Nigerian cohorts ([medRxiv 2025 age-at-onset study](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.04.25327685.full.pdf); [PMC6277104](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6277104/)). Penetrance is incomplete and ancestry-dependent — roughly **26% in Ashkenazi carriers** ([Marder 2015, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26062626)) and **25–42.5% by age 80 in non-Ashkenazi carriers** ([Lee 2017, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27059)) — and is modified by polygenic background ([Iwaki 2019/2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/738260v1.full)). The precise age-specific penetrance table from Marder 2015 could not be fetched; only the headline figure is reported. *GBA1* is the most common genetic risk factor rather than a Mendelian cause, present in **up to 5% of PD and 15–20% of Ashkenazi Jewish PD**, with reported frequencies spanning 1.8–47% depending on ethnicity and the variants screened. Variant severity stratifies risk: severe alleles such as L444P raise risk roughly 9–10-fold (meta-analytic ORs 6.4–30.4), mild alleles such as N370S about 4-fold (ORs 2.2–7.8), and "risk" variants E326K and T369M raise PD risk without causing Gaucher disease. Age-specific penetrance for heterozygotes is **1.5–4.7% at 60 and 7.7–9.1% at 80** ([Vieira 2024, Mov Disord](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11657020/)), though a 2025 analysis gives substantially higher estimates of 7.6% at 50 rising to 29.7% at 80 under a dominant model, with *GBA1* pathogenic variants in 8.8% of UK Biobank PD and 9.9% of LuxPark ([2025 penetrance study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40506446/)). Phenotype runs earlier by 2–10 years with accelerated motor and cognitive decline and more depression, RBD and hyposmia.

Two 2024 findings reshaped the genetic frontier. **RAB32 Ser71Arg** is the newest confirmed dominant risk gene, identified by linkage plus association with mean onset 54.6 years and two-thirds family history, and — critically — functional assays show it **increases LRRK2 kinase activity via S1292 autophosphorylation**, placing a new gene inside an existing pathway ([Gustavsson 2024, Lancet Neurol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1474442224003247)), independently discovered by rare-variant analysis ([Hop 2024, Nat Genet](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-024-01787-7)) and replicated in a French cohort at 0.1% of European index cases ([Cogan 2025, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.30024)). The second is **GBA1 rs3115534**, an intronic variant identified in the first African and African-admixed GWAS (1,488 cases, 196,430 controls), much more common in African ancestry with minor allele frequency above 20% in some populations; CRISPR editing showed it disrupts an intronic branchpoint, producing intron-8-retaining transcripts, reduced *GBA1* expression and reduced glucocerebrosidase activity ([Rizig 2023, Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(23)00283-1/abstract); [Nat Struct Mol Biol 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41594-024-01423-2)). An ancestry-specific common variant of large effect was missed for two decades because of Eurocentric sampling — the single sharpest indictment of the field's prior sampling practice.

The common-variant architecture is now well mapped. **Nalls 2019 pooled 37,688 cases, 18,618 UK Biobank proxy cases and 1.4 million controls to find 90 independent risk signals, 38 novel, with common variants accounting for about 22% of PD liability and the 90 loci explaining 16–36% of heritable risk** depending on prevalence assumptions ([Lancet Neurol 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31701892/)). **Kim 2024 produced the first multi-ancestry meta-analysis — 49,049 cases, 18,785 proxy cases, ~2.4 million controls across European, East Asian, Latin American and African ancestries — identifying 78 loci including 12 potentially novel** and nominating 25 risk genes ([Nat Genet 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38155330/)). Against twin-and-family heritability estimates of 22–40% in Europeans — the Swedish Twin Registry gives 11% monozygotic versus 4% dizygotic concordance and heritability around 34% ([Wirdefeldt 2011, Neurobiol Aging](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0197458011000467)), after earlier cross-sectional twin work found little heritability ([Tanner 1999, JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/188406)) — a substantial but not overwhelming missing heritability remains, and rare plus ancestry-specific alleles are the discovery frontier. Functional follow-up has begun to convert loci into mechanisms: at chromosome 4p16.3, knockdown screens found only *TMEM175* consistently influenced phosphorylated α-synuclein accumulation, with the p.M393T variant showing partial loss of lysosomal pH regulation ([Jinn 2019, Hum Mol Genet](https://academic.oup.com/hmg/article/28/19/3244/5520430)), and the *MAPT* H1 haplotype tracks accelerated progression to dementia in homozygotes ([2009 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19879020)). Polygenic risk scores remain ancestry-dependent: a 2025 comparison built 105 scores across seven ancestries and found variable predictive performance, underscoring the limits of one-size-fits-all models ([npj Parkinsons Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-00967-4)). Clinical PRS use is premature except as a penetrance modifier in *LRRK2* and *GBA1* carriers.

**Epidemiology.** GBD 2021 puts **11.77 million prevalent cases in 2021 (95% UI 10.44–13.42 million), about 1.3 million incident cases, and 7.47 million DALYs**, with age-standardized incidence 15.63 per 100,000 (19.72 male, 9.12 female) and prevalence 138.63 per 100,000. Between 1990 and 2021, incident cases rose 220%, prevalent cases 274% and DALYs 162%; burden concentrates in East Asia, South Asia and Western Europe, with China carrying the highest age-standardized prevalence at 245.73 per 100,000 ([Luo 2025, Front Aging Neurosci](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11757241/); [GBD 2021 Nervous System, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38493795/)). Rates correlate strongly with sociodemographic index, which the GBD authors themselves flag as partly reflecting diagnostic capacity. The 2025 BMJ projection reaches **25.2 million by 2050, a 112% rise, with 15.6 million in 2030 and 20.4 million in 2040; aging contributes 89% and population growth 20%**; East Asia carries 10.9 million, growth is fastest in Western sub-Saharan Africa at +292%; and the male-to-female ratio widens from 1.46 to 1.64 ([BMJ Group press release](https://bmjgroup.com/cases-of-parkinsons-disease-set-to-reach-25-million-worldwide-by-2050/); [MJFF summary](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/new-estimate-suggests-25-million-people-parkinsons-2050)). The researcher notes the paper's authorship was captured only from the press release (first author Su D., Beijing) and is otherwise unverified. For North America, the standard current citations are **930,000 US prevalent cases in 2020 projected to 1.2 million by 2030** ([Marras 2018, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30003140/)) and **nearly 90,000 US diagnoses per year, a roughly 50% upward revision of the older 60,000 figure** ([Willis 2022, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-022-00410-y)).

Whether incidence is genuinely rising independent of aging is the live question, and the answer is contested. The Rochester Epidemiology Project found incidence of parkinsonism and PD rose over 30 years, particularly in men aged 70 and over, with elevated risk in the 1915–1924 birth cohort ([Savica 2016, JAMA Neurol](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2529538)). Dutch nationwide data for 2017–2022 found standardized incidence "relatively stable" at 21.8 per 100,000 person-years ([Lancet Reg Health Eur 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanepe/article/PIIS2666-7762(25)00357-6/fulltext)), and GBD-based analyses report age-standardized YLD rates barely fluctuating over three decades even as absolute numbers climb. The 2024 Lancet seminar's position is that prevalence "has increased over and above demographic changes" internationally but whether incidence is rising "is less certain," particularly in women and in low- and middle-income countries ([Ben-Shlomo 2024, Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38245248/)). Sources agree on direction and approximate magnitude of absolute growth and that aging dominates; the disagreement is about residual growth after age-standardization — BMJ 2025's +55% age-standardized prevalence increase against stable Dutch incidence — and that gap is exactly where the environmental-disease argument lives. Dorsey and Bloem argue PD is "the world's fastest growing brain disorder," that the rise is attributable primarily to environmental toxicants rather than aging, diagnostics or genetics, and that **most cases are preventable** ([Dorsey & Bloem 2024, J Parkinsons Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38217613/); [Dorsey & Bloem 2018, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29131880/)).

**Environmental risk.** The best-supported exposures are pesticides, trichloroethylene, fine particulate air pollution and traumatic brain injury, and the effect sizes are individually modest. In the FAME case-control study nested in the Agricultural Health Study (110 cases, 358 controls), **paraquat OR 2.5 (95% CI 1.4–4.7) and rotenone OR 2.5 (1.3–4.7)**, with complex-I-inhibiting pesticides as a class at 1.7 and oxidative-stress-inducing pesticides at 2.0 ([Tanner 2011, EHP](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21269927/)). A 105-article meta-analysis gives **any pesticide use RR 1.56 (1.37–1.77), farming 1.24, rural living 1.43, well water 1.30** — but with the important caveat that after publication-bias correction the pesticide associations became non-significant in random-effects models, and the authors were industry-affiliated ([Breckenridge 2016, PLoS One](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4824443/)). That attenuation is an outlier conclusion in the literature and should be weighed against the disclosure, which the researcher notes was not independently verified. For TCE, two results anchor the case: PD risk was **70% higher in Marines stationed at Camp Lejeune during 1975–1985** than at Camp Pendleton in a cohort of roughly 340,000 service members ([Goldman 2023, JAMA Neurol](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2805037)) — the exact hazard ratio and confidence interval were not extracted from the paper — and a population-based Medicare case-control of **221,789 incident cases and 1,132,765 matched controls found a dose-dependent association with residential ambient TCE, RR 1.10 for top versus lowest decile**, adjusted for smoking, healthcare use, rurality and PM2.5 ([Krzyzanowski 2025, Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000214174)). That is the first large population-level dose-response demonstration beyond occupational and military settings. Air pollution gives **OR 1.10 (1.03–1.17) per 5 µg/m³ long-term PM2.5** across 42 studies ([Toxics 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxics13020139)), replicated at +10% per 5 µg/m³ PM2.5 and +18% per PM10 increment ([Environ Int 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412026003351)). Traumatic brain injury in a Veterans Health Administration cohort gives **all-severity HR 1.71 (1.53–1.92), mild 1.56, moderate-severe 1.83** ([Gardner 2018, Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000005522)). Metals are weaker: cumulative bone lead associates with PD (ORs 1.34 and 1.32, dose-responsive) while occupational manganese pooled at 1.04 and dietary iron at 0.99, with authors concluding the evidence is "limited" ([Am J Epidemiol 2023](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article/192/7/1207/7109767)). Dairy carries **RR 1.26 for high intake** ([2025 dose-response meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41252180/)), and type 2 diabetes is supported both observationally (OR 1.21) and by Mendelian randomization (IVW OR 1.08, 1.02–1.14) ([Chohan 2021, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.28551)). Because per-person risks cluster at 1.1–1.8, the population-impact argument rests on ubiquity of exposure — TCE, PM2.5 — rather than on effect size.

**Protective associations, and why most of them fail.** Smoking is the most robust inverse association — **current versus never RR 0.41–0.44** ([Noyce 2012, Ann Neurol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.23687); [Breckenridge 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4824443/)) — and the one exposure where Mendelian randomization agrees with observation ([Domenighetti 2021, J Parkinsons Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34275906/)); yet the NIC-PD nicotine trial failed, implying either that nicotine is not the protective agent or that prevention-era effects cannot be recapitulated after diagnosis. Caffeine sits at OR ~0.7–0.75 with a proposed adenosine A2A mechanism, but a 2025 MR found coffee associated with later age at onset and not with risk or progression ([PMC11844581](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11844581/)). Physical activity is consistent and dose-dependent at **RR 0.77 (0.70–0.85), with 9% lower risk per 10 MET-hours/week** ([J Neurol 2024 meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-024-12672-y)). Urate failed twice over: MR found no causal relationship ([Kia 2018, Ann Neurol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6481555/)) and SURE-PD3 was negative. NSAIDs, statins and calcium channel blockers show weak observational protection with negative trials ([Grotewold & Albin 2024, Parkinsonism Relat Disord](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11846500/)). The recurring pattern — strong observational protection, negative MR, negative trial — is best read as reverse causation from prodromal behavioral change: loss of smell and taste reduces coffee and cigarette use, and prodromal motor and mood change reduces activity, years before diagnosis. That is precisely the Lancet seminar's warning about the long prodrome, and it reframes several "protective factors" as early symptoms.

Prodromal risk factors anchor the other end. Isolated RBD is the strongest single marker; hyposmia in the Honolulu-Asia Aging Study gave **OR 5.2 (1.5–25.6) for the lowest odor-identification quartile, significant only within the first four years**, so impaired olfaction can predate clinical PD by at least four years ([Ross 2008, Ann Neurol](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.21291)); constipation gives **RR 2.34 (1.55–3.53)**, and family history is among the strongest of all risk factors at **OR 3.23 for a first-degree relative** ([Noyce 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.23687)).

## Seed amplification assays triggered a contested redefinition of the disease

Clinical diagnosis is the problem the whole biomarker enterprise exists to solve. Pooled accuracy against autopsy is **80.6% (95% credible interval 75.2–85.3%)** across 11 pathology-confirmed studies, falling to 73.8% when diagnosis is made mainly by non-experts and reaching 83.9% for movement-disorder experts after follow-up refinement; the authors concluded that validity "is not satisfying," had "not significantly improved in the last 25 years," is worst in early disease, and that "imaging and biomarkers are urgently needed" ([Rizzo 2016, Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000002350)). The 2015 MDS criteria codified the expert process around bradykinesia plus rest tremor or rigidity, with absolute exclusions, red flags and supportive criteria, at two certainty levels ([Postuma 2015, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26474316/)); validation found **94.5% of 434 expert-diagnosed PD patients met "probable PD" and 88.5% of 192 non-PD patients were correctly excluded, for 92.6% overall accuracy** ([Postuma 2018, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mds.27362)). That 92.6% is an upper bound on true accuracy, because the gold standard was expert diagnosis rather than autopsy — which is exactly why autopsy-based re-evaluations appeared in 2025 ([npj Parkinsons Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01206-6); [Neurology 2025](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000213499)), though their sample sizes and accuracy figures were not retrieved. The widely quoted UK Brain Bank figures (Hughes 1992 ~76%; Hughes 2002 ~90% in specialist hands) were not directly retrieved and should be cited with caution.

The rating scales compound the problem at the early end. Rasch analysis of PPMI data found **neither MDS-UPDRS Part II nor Part III is well targeted to early PD, with marked floor effects and a clear item gap at very mild motor severity** ([J Neurol 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6647182/)); Part IV shows a poor floor effect with 36.7% scoring at the lowest level; and the sum score is discrete and asymmetric with items of varying sensitivity to change ([Luo 2021, Mov Disord Clin Pract](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mdc3.13311)). A 2025 proposal adds a limb bradykinesia/rigidity sub-score to improve Part III in early disease ([npj Parkinsons Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01072-2)). The implication is structural: the instrument used as the primary endpoint in nearly every PD trial is least informative in exactly the population where disease modification is most plausible. Worth noting institutionally, the MDS licenses the MDS-UPDRS per single use across 31 languages ([MDS Rating Scales](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/MDS-Rating-Scales.htm)), making the society an effective gatekeeper of that endpoint.

**The assay.** Siderowf's cross-sectional PPMI analysis of 1,123 participants — 545 PD, 163 controls, 54 SWEDD, 51 prodromal, 310 non-manifesting *LRRK2*/*GBA* carriers — established CSF α-synuclein seed amplification at **87.7% sensitivity and 96.3% specificity**, with the heterogeneity that matters: 98.6% in sporadic PD with typical olfactory deficit, 78.3% without, 67.5% in LRRK2-PD, 34.7% in LRRK2-PD with normal olfaction ([Lancet Neurol 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37059509/)). Most prodromal participants were positive despite having no diagnosis. Skin biopsy immunohistochemistry for phosphorylated α-synuclein performs comparably: in a 30-site NIH-sponsored cross-sectional study, **positivity was 92.7% in PD, 98.2% in MSA, 96.0% in DLB and 100% in pure autonomic failure, against 3.3% in controls** ([Gibbons 2024, JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38506839/)); enrolled-versus-analyzed sample sizes were not captured, and no head-to-head study of skin immunohistochemistry against CSF assay in the same patients was located. Across matrices, a network meta-analysis gives overall **86% sensitivity and 92% specificity**, with CSF, skin, blood and extracellular vesicles highest, saliva at 74.7%/97.9%, and olfactory mucosa and GI samples of limited utility ([Clin Pract 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12192484/)); a complementary review puts CSF and skin at 90–95%, blood at 80–85% and olfactory mucosa at 45–56% ([J Neurochem review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jnc.70435)). Blood-based detection via neuron-derived extracellular vesicles has been reported ([Kluge 2022, Brain](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article-pdf/145/9/3058/49450184/awac115.pdf)) and critically appraised ([Bernhardt 2024, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mds.29923)), with the exchange itself the most honest summary of where blood assays stand. **The FDA issued a Letter of Support in late summer 2024** to the Critical Path for Parkinson's consortium encouraging use of CSF αSyn-SAA to improve trial efficiency in α-synuclein-defined diseases, with the evidence base coming substantially from PPMI ([FDA letter](https://www.fda.gov/media/181368/download); [C-Path](https://c-path.org/fda-issues-letter-of-support-encouraging-use-of-synuclein-based-biomarker-%CE%B1syn-saa-in-clinical-trials-in-parkinsons-and-related-diseases/)), formalized in publication in 2026 ([Stephenson, J Parkinsons Dis 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X261463936)). Two limitations constrain its use: the assay is essentially qualitative, and attempts at quantification through amplification kinetics have not been validated as progression markers, so it cannot serve as a pharmacodynamic endpoint.

**The redefinition fight.** Two frameworks published side by side in *Lancet Neurology* in February 2024. **NSD-ISS** creates a new entity, "neuronal α-synuclein disease," requiring S+ (pathological α-synuclein) and anchoring stages 0–6: Stage 0 for carriers of fully penetrant *SNCA* variants even when biomarker-negative; 1A for asymptomatic S+; 1B for S+ plus dopaminergic dysfunction; Stage 2 for subtle signs without functional impairment; Stages 3–6 for progressive functional impairment ([Simuni 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267190/)). **SynNeurGe** keeps the name Parkinson's disease and uses four separable components — S for α-synuclein, N for neurodegeneration on imaging, G for pathogenic gene variants, C for clinical status — and explicitly includes no staging, "due to insufficient longitudinal evidence" ([Höglinger 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267191/); [Höglinger & Lang 2025, J Parkinsons Dis](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X241298194)). The consequential difference is that **SynNeurGe can label an S-negative, N-positive, G-positive case while NSD-ISS excludes S-negative individuals with dopaminergic loss and clinical PD — an estimated 10–15% of clinical PD** ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way)). Lang, SynNeurGe's senior author, warned that exclusion "could relegate them to a research backwater, where less attention, and less funding, are funneled in their direction." The causality debate has thus become a classification fight.

The critiques are substantive. Darweesh, Bloem and Sampaio objected in correspondence that the frameworks "allow for a diagnosis based on underlying pathology, even in asymptomatic individuals," questioned "defining a disease based solely on a single biomarker or pathology, given that many people with Lewy bodies never develop clinical symptoms," and called for guidance on settings of application and on communicating a research diagnosis ([Lancet Neurology correspondence](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(24)00214-X/fulltext); authorship inferred from Alzforum and flagged by the researcher as requiring verification, since the correspondence text returned 403). Obeso and Calabresi warned that unifying PD and DLB under synuclein pathology might "inadvertently lump together clinical syndromes which might be fundamentally different in nature" — an iRBD patient may remain decades without other features while a DLB patient progresses rapidly, yet both fall under one umbrella. The sharpest empirical attack is Espay's: **levodopa treatment produced stage regression in nearly half the PPMI cohort — 8% of Stage 3 and 41% of Stage 4 patients shifted to earlier stages** — and a valid staging system must show unidirectional progression ([Espay 2025, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40579848/)), to which Simuni replied characterizing the critique as largely an endorsement highlighting the need for refinement ([reply](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.30272)). The regression finding exposes something real: NSD-ISS Stages 3–6 are anchored on MDS-UPDRS-derived clinical scales and therefore inherit the symptomatic-treatment confound. Biology-only staging would require validated progression biomarkers that do not yet exist at scale. From the neuropathology side, Fu and Halliday argue that "an α-synuclein assay alone is unlikely to be a specific criterion for a spectrum of clinical syndromes with Lewy pathology," that "one biological assay will not reflect the complex spatiotemporal features of brain pathology," and that diverse sequential mechanisms underpin heterogeneous phenotypes ([Nat Rev Neurol 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40355531/); full-text arguments beyond the abstract were not accessible), while Matar and Halliday stress the temporal interrelationships between Lewy pathology, Alzheimer's disease and other co-pathologies ([Lancet Neurol 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40252665/)). The MDS issued a formal statement within weeks ([Cardoso 2024, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38093469/)) and a Viewpoint later in the year ([Kalia 2024, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39250594/)); both author groups and the MDS state the frameworks are research-only, and routine clinical application is "premature and explicitly discouraged" ([Cell Death Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-025-08076-z)). No 2026 formal revision or merger of the two frameworks was identified. The ethical shape of the debate parallels biological Alzheimer's: labeling asymptomatic assay-positive individuals as diseased has insurance, employment and psychological consequences, with no disease-modifying therapy to offer them. Systematic survey data on whether at-risk individuals want to know their assay status were not found.

In practice the two frameworks are complementary rather than competing — SynNeurGe is a classification useful for describing heterogeneity and S-negative cases, NSD-ISS a staging system useful for trial enrichment and regulatory endpoints — and the FDA Letter of Support operationalizes the S+ anchor they share.

**Imaging.** DAT-SPECT reliably confirms presynaptic dopaminergic deficit with **95–97% sensitivity and 93–100% specificity against essential tremor but "is not effective for differentiating PD from atypical parkinsonian syndromes"** ([Sci Rep 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-42135-9); [Clin Transl Imaging 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40336-020-00386-w)); SWEDD frequency of 3.6–19.6% in trials raises questions about early-stage sensitivity or clinical misdiagnosis, and the DaTscan label is limited to separating parkinsonian syndromes from essential tremor, with no retrieved source on regulatory status for prodromal screening. FDG-PET fills the differential gap, classifying MSA at 1.00/0.91 sensitivity/specificity, DLB/PDD at 0.81/0.97 and CBD/PSP at 0.62/0.97 ([EJNMMI Res 2023](https://ejnmmires.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13550-023-01025-x)), with visual reads pooling at 91.4%/90.6% for atypical syndromes. Within MRI, loss of the nigrosome-1 "swallow tail" on susceptibility-weighted imaging has **pooled sensitivity 0.94 (0.93–0.96), specificity 0.90 (0.88–0.92) and AUC 0.98** across 19 studies and 1,508 participants ([PRD 2020 meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1353802020306398)), first described at 100%/95% in a single case-control sample ([Schwarz 2014, PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0093814)) — though a post-mortem 7T study cautions that microvessels may confound the sign in normal aged midbrains ([Kau 2019, J Neuroimaging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30407679/)). Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI, validated against post-mortem neuromelanin and dopamine function ([Cassidy 2019, PNAS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30796187/)), outperforms free-water imaging in early PD (**AUC 0.729–0.791 versus 0.605–0.62**) ([PMC13013584](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13013584/)). Quantitative susceptibility mapping shows **ventral-posterior nigral iron increasing progressively over three years**, making it a candidate progression marker rather than a cross-sectional difference ([Bergsland 2019, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31180615/)). Nigrosome and neuromelanin imaging are the strongest candidates for routine-clinic adoption because they need no radiotracer, but no multi-site validation of thresholds was located.

The long-sought α-synuclein PET tracer finally produced signal in idiopathic PD. [¹⁸F]ACI-12589 was the first to show clear in vivo signal, with greater cerebellar white-matter retention in MSA than in PD, DLB or controls — distinguishing MSA but showing limited PD signal ([Smith 2023, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42305-3)); [¹⁸F]C05-05 visualized synucleinopathy in 10 patients with PD or DLB with midbrain binding correlating with motor severity ([Endo 2024, Neuron](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(24)00332-5)); [¹¹C]MK-7337 showed brain penetration and fibril-related signal in a small first-in-human study ([PMC12742897](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12742897/)); and MODAG-009 binds α-synuclein with subnanomolar affinity and roughly 100-fold selectivity over tau and more than 1,000-fold over Aβ, with first human data presented at AD/PD in March 2026. **At least seven tracer candidates are in human trials** ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/conference-coverage/finally-pet-tracers-synuclein-yield-signal-idiopathic-parkinsons)). Sample sizes and quantitative uptake values for MK-7337 and MODAG-009 remain conference-level and were not retrieved. That MSA — with its dense oligodendroglial inclusions — was imaged first and idiopathic PD only in 2024–2026 reflects the real difficulty: low target density, intracellular location, competition from Aβ and tau binding. A validated tracer would solve the two limitations of the seed assay, quantification and regional localization, and supply the progression marker that biological staging currently lacks.

**Fluid and digital markers.** Beyond the seed assay, the leading fluid candidates divide into pathology markers and state markers. **CSF DOPA decarboxylase identifies Lewy body disease at AUC 0.89, is elevated in prodromal and preclinical cases, associates with worse cognition and is not elevated in Alzheimer's disease** ([Pereira 2023, Nat Aging](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-023-00478-y)), with follow-ups confirming inverse correlation with DAT-SPECT binding and extending to a quantitative assay ([Nat Med 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04212-0)) and serum AADC activity ([eBioMedicine 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352396426002379)). The confound is decisive for clinical use: plasma DDC elevation is driven by dopaminergic treatment, confining it to untreated and prodromal populations. Neurofilament light and GFAP are useful mainly for separating PD from atypical parkinsonism — **NfL pooled at 82% sensitivity and 91% specificity for MSA versus PD**, with plasma gradients running atypical parkinsonism (GFAP 89.9, NfL 77.3 pg/mL) above PD (47.1, 50.0) above controls (22.1) and a combined model reaching AUC above 0.87 ([Aden 2026 review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/ijcp/7796155); [PubMed 41626595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41626595/)). Plasma proteomics produced the most striking prodromal result: a machine-learning model on **eight proteins identified all PD patients and classified 79% of pre-motor iRBD individuals up to seven years before motor onset** ([Hällqvist 2024, Nat Commun](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38890280/)); independent replication of that panel was not confirmed, though a 2026 cross-cohort benchmarking paper may address it ([npj Parkinsons Dis 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01344-5)).

Digital markers have crossed from description into decision-relevance in two places. **A week of UK Biobank wrist accelerometry in 103,712 participants distinguished future PD cases from matched controls up to seven years before diagnosis and outperformed models built on genetics, lifestyle, blood biochemistry and prodromal symptoms** ([Schalkamp 2023, Nat Med](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02440-2)), since replicated cross-country ([Mov Disord 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70329)). And the Roche PD Mobile Application v2 achieved **96.3% adherence across 316 PASADENA participants** and detected abnormalities in patients scored as having no signs on the corresponding clinical item for every active and passive feature except postural tremor ([Lipsmeier 2022, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-15874-4)) — meaning digital endpoints are more sensitive than the MDS-UPDRS items they shadow, which is the entire argument for using them to shorten trials. Other validated AI modalities include nocturnal breathing captured by belt or contactless radio waves, detecting PD at **AUC 0.90 held-out and 0.85 external** and tracking progression ([Yang 2022, Nat Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35995955)); keystroke dynamics at **96% sensitivity and 97% specificity, AUC 0.98 in 103 subjects** ([PLoS One 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5708704/)); voice machine learning reporting 90–99% accuracies across 69 studies with substantial dataset heterogeneity ([PMC12649940](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12649940/)); and retinal OCT showing **thinner inner nuclear and ganglion cell–inner plexiform layers**, detectable up to seven years before diagnosis in the 154,830-patient AlzEye cohort ([Wagner 2023, Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000207727)). Two honest limits apply. The 90–99% accuracies come overwhelmingly from small cross-sectional case-control datasets; the UK Biobank study is the only population-scale prospective result, and at population base rates its positive predictive value is necessarily low and was not reported. Wagner explicitly disclaims individual prediction, positioning retinal imaging as a pre-screening tool. The three AI modalities with pre-diagnostic lead time all exploit non-motor or pre-motor channels, which is why they achieve what motor scales cannot — and their natural use is enrichment of prevention trials rather than diagnosis. Critically, **no FDA or EMA qualification of any PD digital endpoint was located**; PASADENA's digital readouts are labelled exploratory even in the 2025 publication ([npj Digit Med 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01572-8)).

**Prodromal criteria and cohorts.** The MDS research criteria compute a Bayesian probability from likelihood ratios of risk markers (sex, pesticide exposure, caffeine, smoking, family history, genetics) and prodromal markers (RBD, hyposmia, constipation, somnolence, orthostatic hypotension, erectile and urinary dysfunction, depression, subthreshold parkinsonism, DAT deficit), with a threshold of 80% for probable prodromal PD ([Berg 2015, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26474317/)). The 2019 update raised the positive likelihood ratio for olfactory loss, lowered it for substantia nigra hyperechogenicity, added diabetes, global cognitive deficit, physical inactivity and low plasma urate in men, incorporated age-related penetrance for intermediate-strength genetic variants, and released a web calculator ([Heinzel 2019, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27802)); exact likelihood-ratio values for individual markers were not confirmed from the full text. Idiopathic RBD remains the strongest single marker: across **1,280 polysomnography-confirmed patients at 24 centres, phenoconversion ran 6.3% per year and 73.5% at 12 years**, with abnormal quantitative motor testing, objective motor signs, olfactory deficit, MCI, erectile dysfunction and abnormal DAT scan as predictors ([Postuma 2019, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30789229/)). The PARS study showed the funnel design works: **hyposmic individuals with baseline DAT deficit converted at 67% (14/21) by four years versus 9% with indeterminate deficit and 3% with no deficit, relative risk 17.47 (7.02–43.45)**, with DAT binding declining about 5% per year — establishing the sequence olfactory loss → DAT deficit → conversion ([Jennings 2017, JAMA Neurol](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2630495)). Hyposmia alone is too non-specific; hyposmia plus DAT deficit, or hyposmia plus assay positivity, achieves comparable enrichment ([eBioMedicine 2025 PARS seed study](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/ebiom/article/PIIS2352-3964(25)00011-8/fulltext)). PREDICT-PD applies the same logic remotely, using keyboard tapping speed, hyposmia and probable RBD as intermediate markers ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.03.16.20037002.full.pdf)), though its longitudinal conversion numbers were not retrieved. The net effect of 2023–2025 assay findings in PPMI and PARS is to convert the probabilistic MDS prodromal criteria into a biomarker-confirmed Stage 1–2 population under NSD-ISS, which is why sponsors and the FDA Letter of Support focus there.

## Symptomatic therapy keeps improving by hours, not by kind

Levodopa remains the most effective symptomatic therapy nearly sixty years on, and the central clinical question — whether to delay it — has been settled against delay. **LEAP randomized 445 patients to levodopa/carbidopa 100/25 mg three times daily for 80 weeks or placebo for 40 weeks followed by levodopa for 40 weeks; the total UPDRS difference at week 80 was 1.0 point (95% CI −1.5 to 3.5; P = 0.44), with no difference in dyskinesia or motor fluctuations** ([Verschuur 2019, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30673543/)), and the five-year follow-up in 321 completers found no difference in progression at three or five years and no difference in wearing-off prevalence or levodopa-equivalent dose ([Frequin 2024, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38644623/)). ELLDOPA had earlier produced the field's most instructive internal contradiction: UPDRS worsening at 42 weeks was **7.8 points on placebo versus 1.9, 1.9 and −1.4 across three levodopa doses (P < 0.001)**, yet in the β-CIT SPECT substudy striatal uptake declined *more* with levodopa (−6%, −4%, −7.2%) than placebo (−1.4%; P = 0.036) ([Fahn 2004, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15590952/)). The decisive observational evidence came from Ghana, where levodopa was started a mean 4.2 years after onset versus 2.4 years in Italian controls: disease duration at onset of fluctuations and dyskinesia was similar in both populations, and in multivariate analysis **disease duration and levodopa dose in mg/kg — not duration of exposure — predicted motor complications**, leading the authors to conclude that withholding levodopa to delay complications "is not justified" ([Cilia 2014, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25034897/)). "Levodopa phobia," coined as a new iatrogenic cause of disability ([Kurlan 2005, Neurology](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15753443)), produces real harm: motor deterioration, contractures, and impulse-control disorders from over-reliance on agonists ([Titova 2018, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30302392)). On rates, the headline figures need their time horizons attached: **median dyskinesia frequency is slightly under 40% by 4–6 years of levodopa therapy in modern-era series, with motor fluctuations at about 40% by 4–6 years and nil in year one**, against 50% dyskinesia by 5–6 months in pre-levodopa-era patients ([Ahlskog & Muenter 2001, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11391738/)); reported rates across the literature span 3–94% ([Tran 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29971495)), and the widely quoted "approximately 80%" refers to advanced disease ([Kwon 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36496996)).

The continuous-delivery hypothesis failed at initiation. **STRIDE-PD randomized 747 patients starting levodopa to levodopa/carbidopa versus levodopa/carbidopa/entacapone four times daily for 134 weeks, and the entacapone arm had a *shorter* time to dyskinesia (HR 1.29; P = 0.04) and higher dyskinesia frequency at week 134 (42% versus 32%; P = 0.02)** — attributed to higher levodopa-equivalent exposure and failure to achieve genuinely continuous delivery ([Stocchi 2010, Ann Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20582993/)). That negative result supports the view that pulsatile delivery must be converted into true continuous delivery, not merely prolonged by enzyme inhibition. New oral formats deliver convenience rather than efficacy leaps: IPX066 (Rytary) reduced OFF time by an extra 1.17 h/day versus immediate release ([Hauser 2013, Lancet Neurol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23485610)), and **IPX203 in the 506-patient RISE-PD trial added 0.53 h/day of good ON time (95% CI 0.09–0.97; P = 0.02) at a mean three doses daily against five for immediate release, with +1.55 h of good ON per dose** ([Hauser 2023, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37578800/)); efficacy was maintained through a nine-month extension ([Espay 2024, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38111267/)). **FDA approved IPX203 as Crexont on 7 August 2024**, with US launch in September ([Amneal release](https://investors.amneal.com/news/press-releases/press-release-details/2024/Amneal-Receives-U-S-FDA-Approval-for-IPX203-for-Treatment-of-Parkinsons-Disease-to-Be-Launched-as-CREXONT-Carbidopa-and-Levodopa-Extended-Release-Capsules/default.aspx)). Inhaled levodopa for OFF rescue produced a **UPDRS-III difference of −3.92 points at 30 minutes versus placebo (95% CI −6.84 to −1.00; P = 0.0088)** in 351 patients ([LeWitt 2019, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30663606/)) and was approved as Inbrija on 21 December 2018 ([MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/inhaled-levodopa-time-approved-fda)). No comparative trial of carbidopa versus benserazide was located.

Oral adjuncts each buy roughly half an hour to an hour of daily ON time, and dyskinesia is the shared dose-limiting adverse effect. Opicapone 50 mg reduced OFF time by **60.8 minutes versus placebo (95% CI −97.2 to −24.4; P = 0.0015)** and was non-inferior to entacapone in the 600-patient BIPARK-1 trial ([Ferreira 2016, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26725544/)). Safinamide added **0.96 h of ON time without troublesome dyskinesia (95% CI 0.56–1.37; P < 0.001)** in 549 patients, with dyskinesia the most frequent adverse event at 14.6% versus 5.5% ([Schapira 2017, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27942720/)). Istradefylline, an adenosine A2A antagonist approved in Japan in 2013 and the US in 2019, reduced OFF time by **0.38 h at 20 mg and 0.45 h at 40 mg across 2,719 patients in eight pooled trials**, with treatment-emergent dyskinesia in 16–18% versus 9.6% on placebo ([Hauser 2021, J Parkinsons Dis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34486986)). Amantadine extended release is the one agent targeting dyskinesia directly: **UDysRS improved by 15.9 points versus 8.0 (difference −7.9; 95% CI −12.5 to −3.3; P < 0.001) with OFF time falling 0.9 h**, at the cost of visual hallucinations in 23.8%, peripheral edema in 23.8% and dizziness in 22.2% ([Pahwa 2017, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28604926/)). ADAGIO's claim that rasagiline modifies disease remains unaccepted: **1 mg/day met all three hierarchical endpoints but 2 mg/day failed**, leaving the inference unsupported ([Olanow 2009, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19776408/)), and NICE states plainly: "Do not use MAO-B inhibitors as neuroprotective therapies... except in the context of clinical trials" ([NICE NG71](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/Recommendations)).

Dopamine agonists carry the field's best-quantified iatrogenic harm. **DOMINION found impulse-control disorders in 13.6% of 3,090 treated patients — gambling 5.0%, compulsive sexual behavior 3.5%, compulsive buying 5.7%, binge eating 4.3% — with 17.1% prevalence on an agonist versus 6.9% off (OR 2.72; 95% CI 2.08–3.54; P < 0.001)** and no difference between pramipexole and ropinirole ([Weintraub 2010, Arch Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20457959/)). The cross-sectional odds ratio badly understates lifetime risk: in 411 incident patients followed prospectively, **five-year cumulative ICD incidence was 46.1% overall — 51.5% among agonist ever-users versus 12.4% among never-users — with a clear dose and duration effect, no strong levodopa association, and progressive resolution after agonist discontinuation** ([Corvol 2018, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29925549/)). Tolerability drives discontinuation too: in PD MED, **28% on agonists and 23% on MAO-B inhibitors discontinued for side effects versus 2% on levodopa (P < 0.0001)** ([Gray 2014, Lancet](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24928805)).

Guidelines converge. The AAN recommends levodopa as the initial preferential dopaminergic therapy at Level B, notes superior motor benefit against more dyskinesia within five years, finds no agonist formulation superior and long-acting levodopa no better than immediate release in early disease, and flags higher ICD risk with agonists ([Pringsheim 2021, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34782410/)). NICE NG71 (published 19 July 2017, last reviewed 19 December 2024) offers levodopa when motor symptoms affect quality of life and a choice of agonists, levodopa or MAO-B inhibitors when they do not; bars ergot agonists first line; requires oral and written ICD counselling recorded in the notes; and forbids abrupt withdrawal and drug holidays. The MDS evidence-based reviews grade specifics: the 2018 review found **no clinically useful interventions to prevent or delay disease progression** ([Fox 2018, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570866/)), and the 2025 motor-fluctuations update, rebuilt on Cochrane risk-of-bias and GRADE across 102 RCTs, rates levodopa ER, pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine, opicapone, safinamide and bilateral STN DBS as *efficacious*, with intestinal and subcutaneous levodopa infusion, subcutaneous apomorphine, entacapone, rasagiline, istradefylline, amantadine ER, zonisamide, bilateral GPi DBS and pallidotomy as *likely efficacious* ([de Bie 2025, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)). The 2025 downgrade of several infusions from "clinically useful" to "likely efficacious" reflects methodology — single pivotal trials with limited blinding durability — not loss of efficacy.

**Infusions.** Intrajejunal levodopa-carbidopa gel remains the best-established, reducing OFF time by **1.91 h versus oral therapy (95% CI −3.05 to −0.76; P = 0.0015)** in the first double-blind controlled demonstration of continuous levodopa delivery ([Olanow 2014, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24361112/)). The 2022–2026 story is subcutaneous. **Foslevodopa/foscarbidopa added 1.75 h of ON time without troublesome dyskinesia (95% CI 0.46–3.05; P = 0.0083) and cut OFF time by 1.79 h in 141 patients, but with infusion-site erythema in 27%, pain 26%, cellulitis 19%, and discontinuation for adverse events in 22% versus 1%** ([Soileau 2022, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36402160/)); at 52 weeks open-label, normalized ON rose 3.8 h and OFF fell 3.5 h, with morning akinesia dropping from 77.7% to 27.8% ([Aldred 2023, Neurol Ther](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37632656)). **FDA approval of Vyalev followed on 17 October 2024** as the first 24-hour continuous subcutaneous levodopa-based infusion ([Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-approves-foscarbidopa-foslevodopa-for-motor-fluctuations-in-adults-with-parkinson-disease)); label details such as cannula-change interval could not be verified. ND0612 delivered an almost identical result — **+1.72 h of good ON (95% CI 1.08–2.36; P < 0.0001) in 259 randomized patients at 117 sites, with OFF down 1.40 h and infusion-site reactions in 83% during open-label** ([Espay 2024, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499015/)) — yet received a **Complete Response Letter on 11 June 2024 and a second in October 2025** ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/parkinson-therapy-nd0612-receives-complete-response-letter); [Tanabe, 23 October 2025](https://www.tanabe-pharma.com/en/news/news20251023103060.html)). The two programs produced near-identical motor gains and shared the same Achilles' heel, and only one was approved; the letters appear to concern component safety, product quality, device and manufacturing rather than efficacy, though the specific content of the second is not public. Levodopa-entacapone-carbidopa gel uses entacapone to raise bioavailability, allowing lower doses, a smaller cartridge and pump, and compatibility with an existing PEG-J ([Nyholm & Jost 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35785401)); interim ELEGANCE data in 167 patients from 37 centres show **OFF time falling 3.47 ± 3.56 h from a 5.15 h baseline (P < 0.0001)** with 1.8% discontinuation ([Weiss 2025, Mov Disord Clin Pract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40129359)), and a Spanish real-world cohort reports OFF falling from 5.2 to 1.9 h/day ([Santos-García 2025, Eur J Neurol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39466665)). There is no randomized LCIG-versus-LECIG trial, so equivalence rests on pharmacokinetics and observational data. Apomorphine infusion reduced OFF by **1.89 h versus placebo (95% CI −3.16 to −0.62; P = 0.0025)** in TOLEDO, the first double-blind RCT of the modality ([Katzenschlager 2018, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30055903/)), and the first US approval of a subcutaneous apomorphine infusion device (SPN-830) came in 2025 ([Auffret 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40393897)) — though the exact approval date and brand details could not be confirmed from a primary source. Sublingual apomorphine film produced an **MDS-UPDRS III difference of −7.6 points at 30 minutes (95% CI −11.5 to −3.7; P = 0.0002)** but with oropharyngeal adverse events in 31% causing 17% discontinuation ([Olanow 2020, Lancet Neurol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31818699)); approved as Kynmobi in 2020, it was voluntarily withdrawn from the US and Canadian markets "due to limited utilization" — a reminder that tolerability and reimbursement can decide a product's fate independent of efficacy. Head-to-head randomized comparisons among LCIG, foslevodopa, apomorphine infusion and DBS do not exist; the choice remains a shared decision based on contraindications, target symptom, preference, caregiver availability and health-system factors ([Fabbri 2023, Neurol Ther](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36633762)).

**Deep brain stimulation.** Five randomized trials against best medical therapy established superiority. Deuschl's 156 pairwise-randomized patients showed PDQ-39 improving 9.5 points (P = 0.02) and UPDRS-III 19.6 points (P < 0.001) at six months, with serious adverse events in 13% versus 4% including a fatal intracerebral hemorrhage ([NEJM 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16943402/)). The VA cooperative study in 255 patients produced the headline number: **DBS gained 4.6 h/day of ON time without troubling dyskinesia versus 0 h (difference 4.5 h; 95% CI 3.7–5.4; P < 0.001), with clinically meaningful motor improvement in 71% versus 32%** ([Weaver 2009, JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19126811/)). PD SURG in 366 UK patients gave a PDQ-39 difference of −4.7 points at one year with serious surgery-related adverse events in 19% and one procedure-related death ([Williams 2010, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20434403/)). A constant-current device trial added 2.51 h of good-quality ON ([Okun 2012, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22239915/)). And **EARLYSTIM extended the indication to patients with early motor complications (mean age 52, duration 7.5 years), where two-year PDQ-39 improved 7.8 points versus worsening 0.2 (difference 8.0; P = 0.002)**, with serious adverse events in 54.8% versus 44.1% and device or surgery-related serious events in 17.7% ([Schuepbach 2013, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23406026/)). The behavioural secondary analysis found better overall behavioural outcomes with DBS — antidepressants stopped in 12 versus 4, neuroleptics started in 1 versus 9 — alongside two suicides in the DBS arm and one in the medical arm over two years ([Lhommée 2018, Lancet Neurol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29452685)). No randomized EARLYSTIM outcomes beyond two years were located.

Target choice is close to a wash on motor outcome. The 299-patient comparison found **no difference in 24-month blinded off-medication UPDRS-III (P = 0.50)**, with STN allowing lower dopaminergic doses (P = 0.02) but greater decline in visuomotor processing speed (P = 0.03) and depression worsening after STN while improving after GPi (P = 0.02) ([Follett 2010, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20519680/)); NSTAPS found no difference in primary outcomes but larger off-drug motor improvement (20.3 versus 11.4 points; P = 0.03) and greater dose reduction with STN ([Odekerken 2013, Lancet Neurol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23168021)). The **10-year VA substudy confirms no difference in time trend between targets**, with off-medication UPDRS-III improvement of 22.3% for GPi and 32.8% for STN at 10 years ([Ostrem 2025, Front Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41626017/)). GPi is favoured where medication reduction matters less and mood or cognition are concerns; STN where dose reduction matters. The long-term picture is consistent across cohorts and clinically important: **robust, durable benefit on levodopa-responsive signs — tremor, rigidity, fluctuations, dyskinesia — while axial symptoms, speech and cognition follow the natural history**. On-medication akinesia, speech, postural stability and freezing worsened between years one and five in the original 49-patient cohort ([Krack 2003, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14614167/)); at 10 and 15 years axial scores worsened with rising frequent-faller prevalence ([Zampogna 2022, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36153351/)); INTREPID showed off-medication UPDRS-III improvement of 51% at one year falling to 36% at five ([Starr 2025, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40952750/)); and PDQ-39 ceases to improve at 7–10 years despite persistent UPDRS-IV gains. Controlled long-term observational data suggest STN DBS may delay psychosis, falls and institutionalization and slightly prolong survival without evidence of any effect on underlying progression ([Mahlknecht 2022, Mov Disord](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35560443)).

Three device advances landed in 2021–2026. **The FDA approved Medtronic BrainSense Adaptive DBS on 24 February 2025 — the first commercially available adaptive DBS — following CE mark about two months earlier** ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-medtronic-adaptive-deep-brain-stimulation-parkinson-disease)). The basis was ADAPT-PD, an open-label crossover in 68 participants already stable on conventional DBS, which **met its primary performance goal with 91% of participants on dual-threshold and 79% on single-threshold adaptive stimulation meeting the ON-time goal, no difference between modes (P = 0.51), and total energy delivered falling 15% with single-threshold aDBS** ([Bronte-Stewart 2025, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40982287/)). The design establishes safety and tolerability more than superiority — it enrolled patients already doing well on conventional stimulation and used a non-inferiority-style performance goal — and early real-world data temper enthusiasm: of 20 consecutive chronic conventional-DBS patients offered adaptive stimulation, **only 9 were eligible**, with exclusions for artifacts, absent distinct beta peak or incompatible settings, though unblinded motor improvement ran about 35% better than conventional with gait most consistently improved ([Cascino 2026, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41741462)). Directional leads widen the therapeutic window: in 234 STN patients, the window was wider with directional stimulation in **183 of 202 (90.6%), by a mean 41% (2.98 versus 2.11 mA)** ([Schnitzler 2022, Neuromodulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34047410/)). And asleep surgery is non-inferior to awake: composite cognitive, mood and behavioural adverse events 29% versus 22% (OR 0.7; 95% CI 0.3–1.7) with motor improvement differing by 2.0 points (95% CI −8.1 to 4.2), while being less burdensome and 26 minutes shorter ([Holewijn 2021, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34491267/)).

**Focused ultrasound.** MR-guided focused ultrasound has moved from tremor to bilateral ablation in a decade. The pivotal trial randomized 94 patients 3:1 to unilateral pallidal ablation or sham: **response at three months was 45/65 (69%) versus 7/22 (32%) (difference 37 points; 95% CI 15–60; P = 0.003), with 30 of 39 responders assessed at 12 months maintaining response**, and lesion-related adverse events comprising dysarthria, gait disturbance, loss of taste, visual disturbance and facial weakness ([Krishna 2023, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36812432/)). The regulatory chronology runs unilateral essential tremor 2016, tremor-dominant PD 2018, unilateral pallidotomy for PD 2021, contralateral staged essential tremor 2022, and **staged bilateral pallidothalamic tractotomy approved 9 July 2025** ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/exablate-neuro-gains-fda-approval-staged-bilateral-focused-ultrasound-advanced-pd); [FDA P150038/S037](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf15/P150038S037C.pdf)). The supporting phase 3 treated 54 patients unilaterally with 40 proceeding to the second side at least six months later: **50% improvement in off-medication MDS-UPDRS III after unilateral treatment and 33.9% after bilateral (total 51.3 → 33.4; P < 0.0001), sustained to 12 months**, with 43 adverse events in 22 patients post-bilateral (70% mild, 2% severe, 40% resolved by 12 months), most commonly imbalance, dysarthria and fatigue ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/bilateral-mr-guided-focused-ultrasound-shows-benefit-managing-parkinson-motor-complications-phase-3-trial); [NCT04728295](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04728295)). Two caveats are important: **no peer-reviewed publication of the bilateral phase 3 was located — the data come from an MDS congress late-breaker as reported by trade press** — and this is the first regulatory acceptance of bilateral ablative surgery for PD since bilateral radiofrequency pallidotomy was abandoned for speech and cognitive risk, with staging and tract targeting as the risk-mitigation strategy. Against DBS, a single-surgeon retrospective in tremor found **equivalent reduction in the more affected hand (DBS 62.4% versus FUS 69.4%; P = 0.958) but contralateral and axial improvement only with bilateral DBS**, with more procedure and hardware adverse events for DBS (17% versus 2%; P = 0.023) ([Baumann 2025, Mov Disord](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40013514)). No randomized head-to-head trial of FUS versus DBS in PD exists. FUS suits patients who are poor DBS candidates by age, comorbidity, anticoagulation or preference against implants; adjustability, bilateral needs and progressive disease favour DBS.

**Gait, freezing and falls are where everything fails.** Freezing affects 38–65% of patients ([Salomon 2024, Nat Commun](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38844449)), involves frontal cortex, basal ganglia and the midbrain locomotor region, and the honest summary is that medications, DBS and rehabilitation "lack efficacy in patients with advanced" freezing ([Nutt 2011, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21777828/)). The only positive drug trial for falls is ReSPonD, where rivastigmine in 130 non-demented PD fallers improved step-time variability for normal walking (ratio of geometric means 0.72; 95% CI 0.58–0.88; P = 0.002) at the cost of nausea in 31% and vomiting in 17% ([Henderson 2016, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26795874/)) — and it remains an unreplicated single-centre phase 2 a decade later, with no phase 3 located. Cueing training gives small, short-lived gains: the RESCUE crossover improved posture-and-gait score 4.2% (P = 0.005) and freezing severity 5.5% in freezers (P = 0.007), but effects "had reduced considerably at 6-week follow-up," arguing for permanent cueing devices ([Nieuwboer 2007, JNNP](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17229744)); a 33-study meta-analysis concludes "one cue does not fit all" ([Cosentino 2023](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37086934)). Spinal cord stimulation has one spectacular single case — a closed-loop, activity-dependent epidural neuroprosthesis targeting leg motor neurons in real time, which improved walking and reduced freezing in primates and in **one 62-year-old man with 30-year PD refractory to available therapies** ([Milekovic 2023, Nat Med](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02584-1)) — and essentially nothing larger. A 2026 double-blind feasibility RCT randomized 12 patients to six months of MicroBurst SCS or sham and **missed its primary gait endpoint at six months**, though bradykinesia and rigidity improved and at 12 months the lower-body and gait score improved 4.31 points (P = 0.0012) with decreased thalamic metabolism ([Terkelsen 2026, Nat Commun](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41611672/)). Since SCS has now failed a blinded primary gait endpoint while showing delayed benefits, the next step is adequately powered sham-controlled trials with standardized paradigms rather than open-label series. Non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation has small randomized sham-controlled gait signals ([Zhang 2023, CNS Neurosci Ther](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37311693/); [PubMed 34045464](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34045464/)). Among non-invasive brain stimulation, rTMS has the best evidence-to-availability ratio: an umbrella review of **21 systematic reviews with meta-analyses finds high-frequency stimulation of primary motor cortex and supplementary motor area significantly improves general motor impairment, gait, functional mobility and balance** ([Neurol Sci 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10072-025-08189-5)), plus cognition and depressive symptoms ([PMC11668114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668114/)) — though effect sizes are modest and no source reports regulatory approval for a PD indication. tDCS should be presented as **not supported** for PD cognition: one meta-analysis found no overall effect, and conventional concurrent M1/left-DLPFC montages showed no significant effects across domains.

**Symptomatic pipeline.** Tavapadon, the first selective D1/D5 partial agonist, is the notable new approval, developed explicitly because D2/D3-mediated adverse effects — somnolence, impulse-control disorders, hallucinations, hypotension — motivated a different receptor strategy ([Bezard 2024, Mov Disord](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36999711)). Three phase 3 trials were positive. **TEMPO-1 randomized 529 early patients to fixed 5 mg, 15 mg or placebo, with week-26 MDS-UPDRS II+III differences of −11.5 points (95% CI −13.8 to −9.2; P < 0.001; d = 1.14) and −12.1 points (d = 1.20)** ([Pahwa 2026, JAMA Neurol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41860533)). **TEMPO-2 used flexible 5–15 mg dosing in 304 patients across 75 sites and 13 countries, giving a −9.1-point difference (95% CI −11.7 to −6.5; p < 0.0001) with a low incidence of somnolence and impulse-control disorders — but 38% versus 15% discontinuation, 24% adverse-event-driven, and nausea in 30%** ([Fernandez 2026, Lancet Neurol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42456682)). **TEMPO-3 added 1.10 h of good ON as adjunct to levodopa in 507 fluctuators (95% CI 0.60–1.70; P < 0.001) with OFF falling 0.94 h** ([Fernandez 2026, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41860544/)). AbbVie submitted the NDA on 26 September 2025 and **the FDA approved tavapadon as Juvmo in late September 2026** for Parkinson's disease in adults, once daily with or without levodopa, in 5/10/15 mg tablets plus a titration pack ([AbbVie release](https://news.abbvie.com/2026-09-28-U-S-FDA-Approves-AbbVies-JUVMO-TM-tavapadon-for-Parkinsons-Disease); [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/fda-approves-tavapadon-parkinsons-motor-symptoms)). **The sources conflict on the exact date: AbbVie's release is dated 28 September 2026, while MJFF gives the approval date as 25 September 2026.** The conflict is unresolved here. Effect sizes of roughly 9–12 MDS-UPDRS II+III points with d ≈ 1.1–1.2 are large relative to historical agonist trials, but the 24% adverse-event discontinuation and 30% nausea indicate real titration burden, and the clinical niche will hinge on whether low ICD and somnolence rates hold in long-term use; US availability was expected in October 2026, and label warnings and pricing were not verified.

Elsewhere in the symptomatic pipeline, **P2B001 — a fixed low-dose combination of extended-release pramipexole 0.6 mg and rasagiline 0.75 mg — beat both its components in 544 untreated patients (differences −2.66 and −3.30 points; P = 0.0018 and P < 0.0001) and matched titrated pramipexole-ER with significantly less Epworth worsening and fewer somnolence, orthostatic and neuropsychiatric events** ([Olanow 2024, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37886872/)); its regulatory status from 2023 to 2026 could not be verified. Mesdopetam, a D3 antagonist for dyskinesia, **missed its phase 2b primary endpoint of good ON time but significantly improved modified UDysRS at 2.5 and 7.5 mg**, with 7.5 mg twice daily selected for phase 3 ([Antonini 2025, Mov Disord Clin Pract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40008823/)) — a dissociation suggesting diary-based "good ON" may be insensitive to dyskinesia severity, which matters for endpoint selection. Phase 3 start date and sponsor status were not confirmed. For buntanetap and solriamfetol, **no PD motor efficacy evidence was retrievable**; the only buntanetap-adjacent primary literature concerned formulation pharmacokinetics. The honest framing of the whole symptomatic enterprise comes from the unmet-needs literature: poorly responsive tremor, motor complications, and gait, balance, posture, speech and swallowing remain unaddressed ([Heim & Poewe 2026, J Parkinsons Dis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40702823)).

## Non-motor symptoms dominate quality of life and have the thinnest evidence base

Virtually every patient has non-motor symptoms — **98.6% in the 1,072-patient PRIAMO study with a mean of 7.8 per patient, and 99.7% on the MDS-NMS with a mean of 16.13** ([Barone 2009, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19514014/); [MDS-NMS prevalence study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347117755_Prevalence_of_Non-Motor_Symptoms_and_Non-Motor_Fluctuations_in_Parkinson's_Disease_Using_the_MDS-NMS)). Instrument sensitivity drives apparent prevalence: the same population yields about 8 symptoms on PRIAMO/NMSS-style assessment, about 10 on the 30-item NMSQuest and about 16 on the 52-symptom MDS-NMS, so cross-study comparisons of symptom counts must be instrument-matched. The clinically decisive finding is that **NMSS total correlates more strongly with PDQ-39 (rS = 0.70) than motor severity does (rS = 0.58) and is the best single predictor of health-related quality of life**, with depression, incomplete bowel emptying, anxiety, impaired concentration, memory complaints and insomnia the most damaging items ([Martinez-Martin 2011, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21264941/)). The instrument lineage runs from the self-completed NMSQuest screener ([Chaudhuri 2006, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16547944/)) through the 30-item nine-domain NMSS ([Chaudhuri 2007, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17674410/)) and the 13-domain MDS-NMS with a non-motor-fluctuations subscale ([Chaudhuri 2020, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31571279/)) to the self-completed MDS-NMS-Q ([Weintraub 2025, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40251003/)). The NMSQuest's most consequential incidental finding was that diplopia, drooling, apathy, low mood and taste or smell problems were "frequently never previously disclosed to the health professionals" — under-reporting is structural, not incidental.

**Cognition.** The "dementia is inevitable" framing is being replaced. The Sydney Multicenter Study followed newly diagnosed patients for 20 years and found **dementia in 83% of 20-year survivors (25 of 30)** ([Hely 2008, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.21956)); a 2026 retrospective on that study reports 136 patients enrolled from 1984 with 74% dead by 20 years and a standardized mortality ratio of 3.1 for years 15–20, but **these figures come from search-engine summaries and could not be verified against the abstract** ([Hely 2026, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41841437/)). Two modern cohorts revise the picture sharply: in **417 de novo PPMI patients (mean age 61.6), the estimated 10-year dementia probability was 9% by site-investigator diagnosis, 15% by MoCA below 21 and 12% by MDS-UPDRS cognition score; in 389 Penn patients (mean age 69.3), 47% were eventually diagnosed with dementia, median time 15 years, with probability 27% at 10 years, 50% at 15 and 74% at 20** ([Gallagher 2024, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39110916/)). The gap reconciles partly through age at onset and follow-up length — Penn's 20-year figure approaches Sydney's — so the defensible 2026 statement is roughly half by 15 years and most by 20 years in older-onset clinic cohorts, not 80% inevitably. PD-MCI affects a wide range depending on operationalisation, **22.8% to 53.7% in large early cohorts and 42.5% in incident PD using Level II criteria at 1.5 SD below norms** ([Litvan 2012, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22275317/); [Yarnall 2014, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24363137/)). Carriers of APOE-ε4 and GBA variants decline faster, with risk further increased in carriers of both ([PMC9362732](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9362732/)), and low CSF β-amyloid 1–42 and 1–40 correlate with PD-MCI.

Treatment for cognition has not materially changed since 2004. **Rivastigmine remains the only cholinesterase inhibitor with a positive large RCT: in 541 patients with mild-moderate dementia developing at least two years after diagnosis, ADAS-cog improved 2.1 points versus 0.7-point worsening on placebo (P < 0.001), with clinically meaningful global improvement in 19.8% versus 14.5% and worsening in 13.0% versus 23.1% (P = 0.007)**, effects the authors described as moderate and similar to rivastigmine in Alzheimer's ([Emre 2004, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15590953/)). Donepezil's 550-patient phase 3 **did not achieve its predefined primary endpoints** (ADAS-cog −1.45, P = 0.050 for 5 mg; P = 0.076 for 10 mg), although an alternative analysis removing the treatment-by-country term showed dose-dependent benefit ([Dubois 2012, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22915447/)). Memantine improved global change in DLB (3.3 versus 3.9; p = 0.023) but **not in PDD (p = 0.576) nor in the total population** ([Emre 2010, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20729148/)). Nothing has worked for PD-MCI: rivastigmine showed only a trend in a 28-patient crossover ([Mamikonyan 2015, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25914281/)) and rasagiline failed in 170 patients ([Weintraub 2016, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27030249/)). And the first GBA-targeted cognitive trial was negative on cognition despite demonstrated target engagement: in a 52-week, 55-patient phase 2, **ambroxol achieved CSF concentrations of 0.73 μM and raised β-glucocerebrosidase (12.45 versus 8.50 nmol/h/mg, P = 0.05) but showed "no evidence to suggest differences between groups on primary or secondary outcomes"** ([Silveira 2025, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40587145/)). The MDS summary is blunt: rivastigmine is efficacious and clinically useful for PDD, donepezil and galantamine have insufficient evidence, memantine is investigational, and for non-dementia cognitive impairment there are **no clinically useful interventions** ([Seppi 2019, Mov Disord](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6916382/)).

**Psychosis.** Hallucinations affect about 40% of treated PD when minor phenomena are counted: in 216 consecutive patients, **39.8% had hallucinations in the prior three months, with minor forms in 25.5%, formed visual hallucinations in 22.2% and auditory in 9.7%**; independent predictors of formed visual hallucinations were severe cognitive disorder, daytime somnolence and long disease duration, leading the authors to conclude that "a simple side-effect of dopaminergic treatment is not sufficient to explain the occurrence of all visual hallucinations" ([Fénelon 2000, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10734005/)). Their prognostic weight is substantial: in 178 community-dwelling patients followed four years, **26.4% entered nursing homes, and among age, functional impairment, dementia and hallucinations, "the presence of hallucinations was the strongest predictor"** ([Aarsland 2000, J Am Geriatr Soc](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10968298/)); narrative reviews go further, calling hallucinations "a more frequent cause of institutionalization than motor disability or dementia" ([Rissardo 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9599742/)). Two agents are MDS-rated efficacious. **Low-dose clozapine (mean 24.7 mg/day) improved CGI 1.6 versus 0.5 (P < 0.001), BPRS 9.3 versus 2.6 (P = 0.002) and SAPS 11.8 versus 3.8 (P = 0.01) in 60 patients, with improved tremor and no worsening of parkinsonism** ([Parkinson Study Group 1999, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10072410/)) — and it remains substantially underused because of monitoring requirements. **Pimavanserin, a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist, reduced SAPS-PD by 5.79 versus 2.73 points (difference −3.06; 95% CI −4.91 to −1.20; p = 0.001; Cohen's d 0.50) in 199 patients**, using a two-week non-pharmacological lead-in to limit placebo response, with no motor worsening ([Cummings 2014, Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24183563/)). Quetiapine, by contrast, failed in three of four randomized placebo-controlled trials; its widespread use rests on tolerability and the impracticality of clozapine monitoring, not on RCT efficacy.

The pimavanserin mortality question deserves care because the two Medicare analyses reach opposite conclusions. Across the development programme, deaths were 5.3% on drug versus 0.5% on placebo and the FDA's reviewing psychiatrist recommended against approval; the drug was approved in 2016 regardless ([J Integr Neurosci 2024](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/JIN/23/4/10.31083/j.jin2304080)). In Medicare-certified long-term-care residents (2,186 users versus 18,212 non-users), **30-day hospitalization aHR was 1.24 (1.06–1.43) and one-year mortality aHR 1.56 (1.42–1.72)** ([Hwang 2021, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34389652/)). In an FDA new-user cohort comparing pimavanserin (N = 3,227) against other atypical antipsychotics (N = 18,442), **mortality was about 35% lower with pimavanserin in the first 180 days (HR 0.65; 0.53–0.79), with no advantage beyond 180 days (HR 1.05) and "no mortality advantage in nursing home patients"** ([Mosholder 2022, Am J Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35702829/)). The divergence is explained by comparator and setting — Hwang compared users with non-users in nursing homes, where confounding by indication is likely, while Mosholder used an active comparator — and the consistent message across both is that pimavanserin offers no clear mortality advantage in the frailest institutionalized population. The exact FDA approval date (April 2016) and boxed-warning language were not confirmed from a primary FDA document.

**Mood, apathy and impulse control.** Clinically significant depressive symptoms affect **35% of patients, with major depressive disorder at 17%, minor depression 22% and dysthymia 13%** — and notably MDD prevalence is 19% with structured interview versus 7% without, so the average is "substantial, but lower than generally assumed" ([Reijnders 2008, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17987654/)). Anxiety disorders affect **31%**, with generalized anxiety at 14%, social phobia 13.8% and panic 6.8% ([Broen 2016, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27125963/)). Apathy affects **39.8% (95% CI 34.6–45.0), and roughly half of apathetic patients have neither depression nor cognitive impairment, confirming its status as a separate clinical syndrome** ([den Brok 2015, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25787145/)). For depression, SAD-PD randomized 115 subjects at 20 sites and found **HAM-D reductions versus placebo of 6.2 points for paroxetine (97.5% CI 2.2–10.3; p = 0.0007) and 4.2 for venlafaxine XR (0.1–8.4; p = 0.02), with no effect on motor function** ([Richard 2012, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22496199/)), and pramipexole reduced BDI by 5.9 versus 4.0 points (difference 1.9; 95% CI 0.5–3.4; p = 0.01) in 287 analysed patients, with a pre-specified path analysis supporting a direct antidepressant effect beyond motor improvement ([Barone 2010, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20452823/)). CBT has large effect sizes — a telephone-based pilot reported a 7.91-point HAM-D change with Cohen's d of 1.21, and a 2018 systematic review puts the face-to-face trial's standardized mean difference at 1.22 ([PMC6174394](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6174394/)); the original 2011 *American Journal of Psychiatry* RCT abstract was not retrieved directly. The MDS rates pramipexole and venlafaxine efficacious and clinically useful, nortriptyline and desipramine likely efficacious, SSRIs as insufficient evidence, and CBT likely efficacious. That paroxetine's numerical effect exceeded venlafaxine's while venlafaxine but not SSRIs earned the higher rating reflects the MDS requirement for two or more positive high-quality trials per drug plus the mixed earlier SSRI record — not evidence that SSRIs are inferior. **For anxiety, no RCT met MDS inclusion criteria at all**, and no post-2019 positive anxiety trial was retrieved. For impulse-control disorders, naltrexone **failed its primary CGI-C endpoint (OR 1.6; 95% CI 0.5–5.2)** while improving the secondary QUIP-RS score (−14.9 versus −7.5 points; p = 0.04), which is Class I evidence of no significant effect on response probability ([Papay 2014, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25037206/)); CBT is rated likely efficacious. The practical answer for ICDs remains agonist reduction, since they resolve progressively after discontinuation.

**Autonomic dysfunction.** Orthostatic hypotension affects about a third, remarkably stable across three meta-analyses spanning 2011 to 2026: **pooled prevalence 33% (95% CI 28–37%) across 55 studies and 10,463 patients, with neurogenic OH at 26%, and mean age explaining most heterogeneity while disease duration, motor severity and levodopa dose did not** ([Wang 2026, Front Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41767005/)). Two findings change practice. Roughly half of OH is asymptomatic, and in an incident cohort there was **no significant difference in daily orthostatic symptoms between those with and without OH** ([Velseboer 2017, Mov Disord Clin Pract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30363407/)) — which argues for routine standing blood-pressure checks rather than symptom-triggered testing. And timing carries diagnostic weight: **symptomatic OH within one year of onset strongly supports MSA, whereas in PD it develops on average about 10 years after onset, with prevalence 60–84% in MSA versus 14–31% in PD** ([PMC3929346](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3929346)). Droxidopa is the only agent with phase 3 support — **OHQ composite improved 0.90 units over placebo (p = 0.003) with the symptom subscore at 0.73 (p = 0.010), largest for dizziness and lightheadedness** ([Kaufmann 2014, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24944260/)) — and was FDA-approved in 2014; it is MDS-rated efficacious for short-term use only, which is appropriate given that the pivotal trial's double-blind phase lasted one week after open-label dose optimization. Midodrine and fludrocortisone rest on insufficient evidence. For bowel and bladder, the mechanisms diverge usefully: **bladder overactivity is mostly of brain origin, reflecting loss of dopaminergic basal-ganglia suppression of the micturition reflex, while constipation is mostly peripheral enteric with slow transit and decreased phasic contraction** — and treating constipation also promotes levodopa absorption ([Sakakibara 2008, J Neural Transm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18327532/)). MDS ratings: probiotics and prebiotic fibre efficacious for constipation, macrogol and lubiprostone likely efficacious, sildenafil efficacious for erectile dysfunction, solifenacin insufficient for overactive bladder, domperidone likely efficacious for nausea. Botulinum toxin is efficacious and clinically useful for sialorrhea, with **a drooling-scale standardized mean difference of −0.98 (95% CI −1.27 to −0.70; high certainty) across 8 RCTs and 259 patients**, at the cost of more adverse events (risk difference 0.15) and onset at about one week with 3–5 months' duration ([Yang 2023, BMC Pharmacol Toxicol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37828600/)). **For excessive sweating, no RCTs met inclusion criteria.** Pooled prevalence figures for constipation, urinary and sexual dysfunction and gastroparesis were not retrieved.

**Sleep.** RBD affects **42.3% of PD (95% CI 37.4–47.1%) across 28 studies and 6,869 cases**, rising with age and disease duration ([Zhang 2017, Neurol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27770275/)). The clinical paradox is that first-line therapy has no RCT support: **the only placebo-controlled trials in PD-RBD — clonazepam 0.5 mg in 40 patients (CGI-I median 2 versus 3, p = 0.253) and prolonged-release melatonin 4 mg in 30 patients (3.4 versus 3.6 RBD events per week, P = 0.92) — were both negative**, and the MDS found no RBD trial meeting criteria ([Shin 2019, J Neurol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31035190/); [Gilat 2020, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31674060/)). Those trials were small, short and reliant on diary and CGI endpoints with probable placebo response, so they do not establish inefficacy — but the field has no RCT-grade symptomatic RBD therapy as of 2026. Excessive daytime sleepiness affects **35.1% pooled**, with modafinil rated only possibly useful; sodium oxybate improved sleepiness and increased slow-wave sleep subjectively and objectively in a 12-patient crossover but **induced de novo obstructive sleep apnoea in two patients and parasomnia in one** ([Büchele 2018, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29114733/)). In early PD, insomnia — mainly sleep maintenance — is the most common sleep disorder, followed by RBD, EDS and marginally restless legs ([npj Parkinsons Dis 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00642-0)). The glymphatic hypothesis gained its first substantial human support in 2025: **the DTI-ALPS index was significantly lower in polysomnography-confirmed iRBD than controls and predicted conversion to PD** in a multi-cohort MRI study ([Ayral 2025, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40956987/)). DTI-ALPS is an indirect proxy, so this strengthens the glymphatic hypothesis as a prodromal biomarker without establishing causality between sleep-dependent clearance and α-synuclein spread.

**Pain, sensory and fatigue.** Pain prevalence is reported anywhere from 20% to 98% depending on definition, commonly quoted as "up to 85%" — a definitional artefact that the King's PD Pain Scale, with seven domains and 14 items scored 0–168, should narrow, enabling phenotype-specific trials separating fluctuation-related pain responsive to dopaminergic optimisation from central pain ([Chaudhuri 2015, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26096067/)). Hyposmia affects **75–90%** and often precedes motor onset by years; visual impairment affects over 90% with the most common self-reported complaints being unclear vision at 21.7% and difficulty reading at 21.6% ([PMC10072373](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10072373/)); fatigue affects **58.1% in PRIAMO, rising from 37.7% in early to 81.6% in advanced disease**, and was the second most frequent NMSS complaint at 65.9%. Treatment evidence is weak throughout: rasagiline is efficacious but only possibly useful for fatigue, oxycodone-naloxone has insufficient evidence for pain, and **for olfactory and ophthalmologic dysfunction no RCTs met inclusion criteria**. Sensory domains are far better characterised as prodromal and diagnostic markers than as treatment targets. No pain intervention trial since the 2015 oxycodone-naloxone study was retrieved.

Two structural observations close this section. First, **seven years after Seppi 2019 the MDS non-motor evidence-based review has not been updated, so clinicians in 2026 rely on a literature search ending 31 December 2016** plus individual ungraded trials such as ambroxol 2025 and the pimavanserin cohort studies; the MDS featured-reviews list shows a 2025 motor-fluctuations update but no non-motor update. Second, because hallucinations and dementia dominate institutionalization, and because the only interventions rated clinically useful for them have modest effect sizes or safety trade-offs, the non-motor burden is the principal unmet need in long-term PD care. The useful 2026 heuristic: about ten non-motor symptoms per patient, nearly all patients affected, and the ones that matter most for quality of life are mood, cognition, sleep and bowel function — not tremor.

## Thirty-five years of neuroprotection failures narrowed the pipeline to three bets

Across roughly 35 years, every phase 3 neuroprotection trial in PD has failed by regulatory standards, and several produced harm signals. The historical record is worth laying out because the failure modes repeat. **DATATOP** randomized 800 early untreated patients to deprenyl and/or tocopherol, with deprenyl delaying disability requiring levodopa by about nine months (HR 0.50; 95% CI 0.41–0.62; P < 0.001) and tocopherol showing no effect ([Parkinson Study Group 1993, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8417384/)) — an effect later attributed largely to symptomatic action. The delayed-start design was meant to separate symptomatic from disease-modifying effects, and **ADAGIO's dose paradox destroyed the inference: rasagiline 1 mg met all three hierarchical endpoints while 2 mg failed** ([Olanow 2009, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19776408/)), provoking an extended methodological debate over small effect sizes, incomplete blinding and subjective outcomes. Futility designs then became the field's most cost-efficient instrument and killed candidates efficiently: **coenzyme Q10 in 600 patients (UPDRS worsening 6.9 placebo versus 7.5 and 8.0; futility met, trial terminated)** ([QE3 2014, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24664227/)); **creatine in 1,741 patients over a median four years (global statistical test P = 0.45; terminated for futility)** ([NET-PD LS-1 2015, JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25668262/)); **pioglitazone in 210 patients (futility met at 45 mg, P = 0.09)** ([FS-ZONE 2015, Lancet Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26116315/)); **simvastatin in 235 patients, where the drug group deteriorated an additional 1.52 points on off-medication MDS-UPDRS III at 24 months (futility P = 0.006; "no evidence to support proceeding to a phase 3 trial")** ([PD STAT 2022, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36315128/)); and isradipine and inosine as described above. Trophic factors failed repeatedly on delivery: intracerebroventricular GDNF produced no motor improvement at any dose because it "did not reach the target tissues" ([Nutt 2003, Neurology](https://europepmc.org/search?query=GDNF%20Parkinson%20Nutt%202003)); intraputamenal GDNF gave a non-significant motor change despite a **32.5% treatment difference in ¹⁸F-dopa uptake (P = 0.019)** ([Lang 2006, Ann Neurol](https://europepmc.org/search?query=intraputamenal%20GDNF%20Lang%202006)); and the Bristol convection-enhanced trial **missed its primary endpoint (−17.3% versus −11.8%; P = 0.41) while putaminal ¹⁸F-DOPA uptake rose 25–100% only in the active group (P 0.0009 to < 0.0001)**, with a post hoc finding that 9 of 21 active patients versus 0 placebo had at least 10-point motor improvement (P = 0.0008) ([Whone 2019, Brain](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2019/february/gdnf-trial.html)). AAV2-neurturin failed twice against sham, in the putamen (12-month difference −0.31; P = 0.91) and then with added substantia nigra targeting (−7.0 versus −5.2; P = 0.515) ([Marks 2010, Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS1474-4422(10)70254-4/abstract)). The c-Abl inhibitors followed the same arc: nilotinib showed "little efficacy" and the authors concluded it "should not be considered suitable for further studies" ([Simuni 2021, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33315105/)); **vodobatinib's 513-patient phase 2 failed in April 2024, with comparisons favouring placebo in both active arms and serum neurofilament light increasing significantly on drug, "supporting inefficacy in PD"** ([Sarva 2026, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01275-1)); and risvodetinib failed its MDS-UPDRS II+III endpoint in January 2025 ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/risvodetinib)).

**The GLP-1 story is the field's most instructive recent reversal.** A 62-patient phase 2 of exenatide showed off-medication MDS-UPDRS III improving 1.0 point versus worsening 2.1 at 60 weeks after 12 weeks' washout (adjusted difference −3.5; 95% CI −6.7 to −0.3; P = 0.0318) ([Athauda 2017, Lancet](https://europepmc.org/search?query=Athauda%20exenatide%202017)). **LixiPark then randomized 156 patients within three years of diagnosis to lixisenatide or placebo for 12 months: MDS-UPDRS III (ON) changed +3.04 on placebo versus −0.04 on drug (difference 3.08; 95% CI 0.86–5.30; P = 0.0068), with about 50% gastrointestinal side effects and no differences in levodopa-equivalent dose or Parts I/II**, and the authors advised against off-label use pending larger trials ([Meissner 2024, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJMoa2312323)). Then **Exenatide-PD3, the UK phase 3 in 194 patients over 96 weeks, found off-medication MDS-UPDRS III worsening 5.7 versus 4.5 points with no significant difference on primary or secondary endpoints including DaT-SPECT striatal binding: "no evidence to support exenatide as a disease-modifying treatment"** ([Vijiaratnam 2025, Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(24)02808-3/fulltext)). A complication arrived in 2026: **on 18 May 2026 The Lancet was made aware of an MHRA regulatory inspection at King's College Hospital, one of the trial sites, which found "department-wide" Good Clinical Practice failures in trial management and oversight, and the journal issued a formal Expression of Concern published 27 June 2026** ([Lancet Expression of Concern](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)01241-9/fulltext); [PubMed 42330995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42330995/)). This does not reverse the negative finding, but it clouds interpretation of the only phase 3 GLP-1 trial in PD and will complicate meta-analysis. Two other GLP-1 agents failed cleanly: **NLY01 in 255 early untreated patients gave differences of −0.39 and 0.36 points (P = 0.77 and 0.79)** ([McGarry 2024, Lancet Neurol](https://europepmc.org/search?query=NLY01%20Parkinson)), and liraglutide in 63 patients showed no difference on its primary motor outcome while secondary non-motor measures improved. Semaglutide in PD remains untested — the Oslo GIPD trial is listed as not yet recruiting and a Japanese 99-patient study was expected to complete March 2026; **the negative November 2025 semaglutide readouts were Alzheimer's trials, not PD**, a distinction worth stating explicitly. The leading hypothesis for the lixisenatide-versus-exenatide divergence is differential CNS penetration, which is untested.

Two cross-cutting lessons follow. First, the symptomatic-versus-disease-modifying confound has never been resolved by scale-based endpoints: DATATOP's effect was reinterpreted, ADAGIO's dose discrepancy undermined delayed-start logic, LixiPark's benefit was measured ON medication with gastrointestinal effects capable of unblinding, and the one team that used off-medication MDS-UPDRS III in a properly powered phase 3 found nothing. Second, **phase 2 wins have a poor record of phase 3 confirmation, and biologically active drugs can accelerate decline** — deferiprone and vodobatinib both did.

**α-Synuclein-targeting therapies.** Passive immunotherapy has produced one clean failure and one borderline, persistently trending antibody. **Cinpanemab failed unambiguously: in 357 early patients across three doses, 52-week MDS-UPDRS total differences were −0.3, 0.5 and 0.1 points (P = 0.90, 0.80, 0.97), with no DaT-SPECT differences; the trial stopped at the week-72 interim for lack of efficacy** ([Lang 2022, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35921450/)). Prasinezumab has a more complicated record. **PASADENA missed its primary MDS-UPDRS total endpoint in 316 newly diagnosed patients but showed a trend on Part III and several prespecified motor measures including smartphone endpoints** ([Pagano 2022, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2202867)); its open-label extension reported 51% and 65% slower off-medication Part III decline versus an external PPMI-matched comparator, which is exploratory by construction. **PADOVA then missed its primary endpoint in 586 patients: time to confirmed 5-point MDS-UPDRS III progression gave HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.69–1.01; P = 0.0657), median time to progression 61.1 versus 49.7 weeks, with a prespecified levodopa-treated subgroup (75% of the cohort) at HR 0.79 (0.63–0.99; nominal P = 0.0431)** and serious adverse events at 12% in both arms ([Nikolcheva 2026, Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)00865-2/abstract)). Roche nonetheless announced a phase 3 decision on 16 June 2025, citing PADOVA plus open-label extension data from more than 750 participants and "first biomarker evidence of prasinezumab impacting the underlying disease biology" ([Roche](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2025-06-16)). **PARAISO — about 900 early-stage patients on stable dopaminergic therapy, 231 sites in 17 countries, started 24 November 2025, primary completion estimated 30 June 2029** — is therefore effectively the first adequately powered test of the extracellular-synuclein hypothesis in PD ([NCT07174310](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07174310)). Critical commentary is substantial and should be weighed: a Bayesian reanalysis, a *Lancet* comment asking what PASADENA and PADOVA actually taught, and an npj paper urging caution about retrospective subgroup analyses ([Foltynie 2026, Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)00985-2/abstract); [PMC12006898](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12006898/)). The epitope explanation for cinpanemab's clean failure versus prasinezumab's trend — N-terminal versus C-terminal, plus population differences — is a hypothesis, not a demonstration.

Elsewhere in the synuclein space, the contraction since 2020 is substantial. **Minzasolmin's ORCHESTRA phase 2 did not meet primary or secondary clinical endpoints and UCB terminated the extension "based on the absence of evidence of clinical benefit"** ([UCB release](https://www.ucb.com/newsroom/press-releases/article/findings-from-minzasolmin-proof-of-concept-orchestra-study-shape-next-steps-in-ucb-parkinson-s-research-program)); MJFF's summary was that the trial "definitively did not show an effect." **The SNCA-lowering antisense oligonucleotide ION464/BIIB101 was discontinued by Biogen on 12 February 2025.** **Buntanetap's 523-patient phase 3 missed its primary endpoint** — with the primary changed from MDS-UPDRS II+III to Part II alone — and the company did not report primary outcome data for the overall population, instead reporting subgroup claims in patients with more than three years since diagnosis, PIGD phenotype, and MMSE gains in MCI and amyloid-positive subgroups; the only active PD study is a three-year open-label safety trial running to November 2029 ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/buntanetap)). Vaccines remain early but with demonstrated target engagement: **UB-312 produced a 20% decrease in CSF aggregated α-synuclein versus a 3% increase on placebo in its phase 1, with improved MDS-UPDRS II in patients who developed CSF antibodies** ([Nat Med 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03101-8)), and ACI-7104.056 reported a 100% responder rate with "stabilization of disease-relevant biomarkers" and no off-medication MDS-UPDRS III progression at 74 weeks in 20 participants — uncontrolled and exploratory. Emrusolmin has no identified PD efficacy trial and has pivoted to MSA, where it holds FDA Fast Track from September 2025. MJFF counts about 15 drugs remaining in the α-synuclein pipeline.

**Genetically targeted therapy had a bad 2025–2026.** The LRRK2 kinase inhibitor BIIB122 is the clearest "target engaged, clinic unmoved" result since urate: **LUMA, in 648 patients with early PD with or without pathogenic LRRK2 variants, missed primary and secondary endpoints in May 2026 despite more than 90% peripheral LRRK2 kinase inhibition and roughly 30% reduction in CSF phospho-Rab10 in a sub-study, and Biogen and Denali discontinued the drug in idiopathic PD** ([StockTitan/Biogen-Denali](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/DNLI/biogen-and-denali-therapeutics-provide-update-on-phase-2b-luma-study-n9aawlne0hpd.html); [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/lrrk2-inhibitor-biib122-fails-slow-early-stage-parkinson-disease)). The LRRK2 antisense oligonucleotide hit its target even harder — **CSF LRRK2 lowered up to 59% and phospho-Rab10 up to 50%, irrespective of LRRK2 variant status, with adverse events mainly mild to moderate and not dose-limiting** ([Nat Med 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04262-4)) — and **was discontinued by Biogen on 12 February 2025, so the phase 1 publication postdates the programme's termination.** That lowering occurred regardless of variant status means applicability was never restricted to carriers and target engagement was never the problem; discontinuation most likely reflects portfolio reasoning or the unproven link between LRRK2 lowering and clinical benefit. Denali's **BEACON, about 50 heterozygous and homozygous LRRK2 carriers with primary completion March 2027 and data expected H1 2027**, is now the only remaining clinical test of the genetic-subgroup version of the hypothesis. New modalities are entering: a LRRK2 PROTAC showed more than 50% CSF LRRK2 degradation at 28 days with a 68-hour half-life (AD/PD 2026).

The GBA1 pathway has now failed with three distinct mechanisms. Substrate reduction failed first: **venglustat in 221 GBA1-PD patients gave an MDS-UPDRS II+III change of 7.29 versus 4.71 (difference 2.58; 95% CI −1.10 to 6.27; P = 0.17) — numerically worse on drug — with more psychiatric and gastrointestinal adverse events, and Sanofi stopped the programme in January 2021** ([Giladi 2023, Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(23)00205-3/abstract)). A small-molecule GCase activator failed next: **BIA 28-6156's ACTIVATE phase 2b in GBA1-PD did not meet primary or key secondary efficacy endpoints on 8 June 2026, and Bial discontinued development for the indication**, registering a compassionate-use protocol ([Bial topline](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260608846221/en/Bial-Reports-Topline-Results-From-ACTIVATE-Phase-2b-Study-in-GBAAssociated-Parkinsons)); sample size and effect sizes were not in fetched text, and whether BIA 28-6156 is the same molecule as LTI-291 could not be verified. And the chaperone ambroxol, whose open-label AiM-PD study had looked encouraging — **CSF ambroxol +156 ng/mL (P < 0.001), CSF GCase activity −19% (P = 0.04), CSF α-synuclein +13% (P = 0.01), MDS-UPDRS III improving 6.8 points (P = 0.001)** in 23 enrolled patients ([Mullin 2020, JAMA Neurol](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02941822)) — failed its cognitive endpoints in PDD as described above. That leaves **ASPro-PD as the last large randomized test of GCase enhancement before gene-therapy data: 330 patients with confirmed GBA1 status recruited equally as carriers and wild-type, ambroxol 1,260 mg/day versus placebo for 104 weeks then 26 weeks open-label, primary outcome MDS-UPDRS I–III with Part III in OFF, GBP 5.5 million across 15 UK sites, first participant April 2025, estimated completion September 2029** ([Toffoli 2026, J Neurol protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41708985/); [NCT05778617](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05778617)). Two biotech-sponsored negative readouts within a month in mid-2026 are likely to reduce industry appetite for genetically stratified PD trials in the near term — which is notable given that genetic stratification was supposed to be the field's escape from heterogeneity. Context from the pipeline census: LRRK2 accounted for just **7 of 444 drug trials (2%) and GBA for 11 (2%)** over 2015–2024 ([Buff 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13442941/)).

**Other mechanisms.** Immunomodulation produced its first clean proof-of-concept result and it was negative on the primary endpoint: **AZA-PD randomized 66 early patients to azathioprine 2 mg/kg/day or placebo for 12 months, with the MDS-UPDRS gait-axial primary outcome changing 0.54 versus 0.13 (effect size 0.438; 95% CI −0.694 to 1.57; P = 0.78) and serious adverse events in 24% versus 12%**, while establishing that "peripheral immunosuppression is a safe treatment strategy" and showing exploratory effects on immune biomarkers and motor symptoms possibly larger in females ([Greenland, Lancet Neurol 2025/2026](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(25)00386-2/fulltext)). NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition is the main new anti-inflammatory class, at a stage comparable to GLP-1 agonists around 2016 — strong biology, small uncontrolled human signals, first RCTs just starting: VTX3232 was well tolerated in 10 early patients over 28 days with reductions in NLRP3-related CSF and plasma biomarkers and uncontrolled MDS-UPDRS improvements; dapansutrile's 12-month DAPA-PD trial randomized its first patients by 24 March 2026; and selnoflast showed peripheral and central effects in phase 1b. A brain-penetrant TYK2/JAK1 inhibitor is furthest along: **BHV-8000's phase 2/3 enrolls 550 patients with a 48-week double-blind period, primary endpoint time to first qualifying worsening on MDS-UPDRS II, and primary completion August 2027** ([NCT06976268](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06976268)). On the metabolic side, several trials are mechanistically positive and clinically inconclusive: **ursodeoxycholic acid in 30 patients met its safety primary and showed increased Gibbs free energy and inorganic phosphate on ³¹P-MRS with sensor-based cadence improvement but no MDS-UPDRS III difference** ([Payne 2023, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.29450)); nicotinamide riboside raised cerebral NAD on ³¹P-MRS with mild clinical improvement in NAD-responders and reduced inflammatory cytokines in NADPARK, and up to five-fold blood NAD+ increases in NR-SAFE ([Cell Metab 2022](https://www.cell.com/cell-metabolism/fulltext/S1550-4131(22)00045-6); [Nat Commun 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43514-6)). **NOPARK — 410 participants on nicotinamide riboside 500 mg twice daily for 52 weeks, primary outcome MDS-UPDRS I–III, completed 17 June 2025 — is the largest completed mitochondrial trial and the most important unpublished readout as of late 2026; no results have been posted or announced.** Terazosin rests on striking pharmacoepidemiology — **Danish HR 0.88 (95% CI 0.81–0.98) across 52,365 matched pairs and US HR 0.63 (0.58–0.69) across 94,883 pairs, with dose-response** ([Simmering 2021, JAMA Neurol](https://europepmc.org/search?query=terazosin%20Parkinson)) — plus a mechanism in which terazosin binding to PGK1 accelerates product release, and a 13-patient pilot in which brain ATP rose in 4 of 5 on drug versus 2 of 5 on placebo; a phase 1/2 target-engagement trial was estimated to start September 2026. High-dose thiamine has only an open-label pilot and **no randomized trial was identified**; the ketogenic diet has a 6-study integrative review finding small-to-moderate quality-of-life effects, inconsistent findings and gastrointestinal adverse events, with calls for larger controlled trials.

**Repurposing infrastructure and platform design.** The International Linked Clinical Trials programme, run by Cure Parkinson's with the Van Andel Institute, has become the field's screening funnel: from an initial committee evaluating 26 interventions and prioritising 10 ([Brundin 2013, JPD](https://europepmc.org/search?query=%22Linked%20Clinical%20Trials%22)), it reached **171 unique agents evaluated with 21 completed and 20 ongoing disease-modification trials by 2024** ([Wyse 2024, JPD](https://europepmc.org/search?query=%22Linked%20Clinical%20Trials%22)). Its 2025–2026 outputs tell the story of the whole strategy: exenatide phase 3 negative, simvastatin and deferiprone negative, UDCA and azathioprine and nicotinamide riboside mechanistically positive but clinically unproven. Target-engagement phase 2s are necessary and far from sufficient. The institutional response is the **EJS ACT-PD platform, the UK's first multi-arm multi-stage trial for PD: GBP 26 million, 1,600 participants across 43 sites with 15 centrally funded staff, 400 per arm, three years of treatment, primary outcome change in rate of progression on MDS-UPDRS Parts I+II summed, powered for a 30% reduction, with four stages (1–2 activity, 3–4 efficacy), early closure of ineffective arms and permitted re-randomisation** ([Zeissler 2026, Brain Commun](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/article/8/5/fcag349/8826396)). Arm selection was explicit and documented: 293 candidate interventions were identified, 63 passed go/no-go, 52 were scored on a 0–30 scale across preclinical, pharmacological and clinical evidence, producing a descending shortlist of telmisartan, terazosin, UDCA, istradefylline, lovastatin, deferiprone, Neuroaspis PLP10 and AZD3241, with **telmisartan, terazosin and UDCA selected as first arms (UDCA added after year one) and lovastatin held in reserve** ([Gonzalez-Robles 2025, Mov Disord](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12273612)). Comparable platforms exist or are forming in France (MASTER), Norway (HYDRA and SLEIPNIR), the US (Path to Prevention) and Australia (Australian Parkinson's Mission). With a three-year endpoint, EJS ACT-PD's first stage-1 activity readouts are unlikely before about 2028; its real 2026 contribution is infrastructure. Its registration number, exact launch date and recruitment numbers were not stated in fetched sources.

**Where the pipeline actually stands in 2026.** The 10-year census is the authoritative framing: **444 drug trials initiated 2015–2024 (186 phase 1, 203 phase 2, 55 phase 3), 279 symptomatic versus 165 disease-modifying, total enrolment 39,238, only 9 phase 3 disease-modifying trials in the entire decade (5% of phase 3 activity), 133 trials active at end-2024, only 76 of 224 completed trials with posted results, and 14 FDA approvals — all symptomatic. "There are still no treatments to modify the course of the disease."** ([Buff 2026, J Parkinsons Dis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13442941/)). Disease-modifying trials did exceed half of new registrations in 2022–2024, so the composition is shifting even as the conversion bottleneck at phase 3 persists. Counting only randomized, placebo-controlled trials with a clinical primary endpoint powered for disease modification, **there are three pivotal programmes in idiopathic PD — PARAISO (2029), ASPro-PD (2029) and BHV-8000 (2027) — plus one platform (EJS ACT-PD), and none reports before 2027.** The 2024–2026 scorecard is lopsided: on the negative side vodobatinib, minzasolmin, PADOVA's primary endpoint, buntanetap's phase 3 primary, risvodetinib, Exenatide-PD3, ION464 and BIIB094 discontinuation, ambroxol in PDD, AZA-PD's primary, LUMA, ACTIVATE and TAK-341 in MSA; on the positive side **not one met primary disease-modifying endpoint** — only LixiPark's phase 2, PASADENA's open-label extension, PADOVA's exploratory subgroup, vaccine target engagement, a 10-patient NLRP3 signal and regenerative-medicine designations. Notably, **no disease-modifying trial yet enrols purely on seed-assay positivity, and no interventional prodromal (NSD stage 1–2) trial was identified as actively recruiting**, so the biological staging system remains enabling rather than deployed. The design shift that has happened is real though: time-to-event endpoints rather than mean MDS-UPDRS change (PADOVA, LUMA, PARAISO, BHV-8000), enrolment on stable symptomatic therapy to avoid washout and dropout problems, and shared-placebo platforms.

## Cell therapy crossed a regulatory line in Japan on seven patients

The fetal era's verdict is now final, and it is a design template rather than a therapy. Three decades of human fetal ventral mesencephalic grafting established that transplanted dopamine neurons survive, reinnervate the putamen and raise fluorodopa uptake — and never produced reproducible clinical benefit. Both NIH-funded double-blind trials missed their primary endpoints: the Denver–Columbia trial showed no significant overall difference with benefit confined to **younger but not older** patients ([Freed 2001, NEJM](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cell-and-developmental-biology/articles/10.3389/fcell.2021.666675/full), cited for design and the age interaction only, since numeric deltas were not retrievable), and the 34-patient bilateral trial found **no significant overall treatment effect with benefit confined to milder patients, despite significant striatal fluorodopa increases and robust post-mortem dopamine neuron survival — and 56% of transplanted patients developed off-medication graft-induced dyskinesia**, leading the authors to conclude fetal nigral transplantation "cannot be recommended as a therapy for PD" ([Olanow 2003, Ann Neurol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.10720)). Then came the finding that defines the ceiling on cell replacement: **grafted nigral neurons contained α-synuclein- and ubiquitin-positive Lewy-body-like inclusions with reduced dopamine transporter immunostaining 14 years after transplantation**, and independently at 11–16 years, establishing host-to-graft propagation ([Kordower 2008, Nat Med](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm1747); [Li 2008, Nat Med](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm1746)). This is the strongest argument that cell replacement is symptomatic restoration rather than disease modification, and may need pairing with an anti-synuclein agent — though since inclusions took 11–16 years to appear, it is a durability ceiling rather than a near-term obstacle.

TRANSEURO closed the era definitively. Launched 2010 with grafting performed 2015–2018, it compared **11 grafted patients against 16 contemporaneous non-grafted natural-history controls to 36 months with identical protocols**, using tissue from three fetuses per side and 12 months of triple immunosuppression. The primary outcome showed **"no obvious benefit from the hfVM transplants across the grafted cohort," either versus baseline or versus controls**; mean fluorodopa uptake improved modestly at 18 months in 7 individuals but was restored to near-normal in only one, with the grafted group moving from 47.6% to 50.8% of normal while controls declined from 44.7% to 39.9%; graft-induced dyskinesia occurred in **3 patients, mild and non-disabling — below the historical 15–54% range**, attributed partly to minimising serotonergic contamination ([Barker 2025, Nat Biotechnol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)). Two failure modes the paper documents are directly relevant to current trials. **Tissue supply: 21 transplants were performed but 86 were cancelled for insufficient tissue, and only 11 of 36 recruited patients were ultimately grafted** — the canonical demonstration that a non-manufacturable cell source cannot support a therapy. And **device variability: Lund patients grafted with the original Rehncrona–Legradi device did better clinically than Cambridge patients grafted with newly manufactured copies, despite identical tissue preparation**, leading the authors to conclude that "surgical and device-related issues can significantly influence the outcome" — a direct risk for a 33-site phase 3. Their forward-looking prescription is specific: long run-in periods, PET imaging to monitor dopamine cell survival, contemporaneous natural-history controls, sufficient cell dose and survival to restore normal putaminal innervation, and standardised batch-manufactured QC'd stem-cell-derived progenitors over fetal tissue.

**Safety is now largely de-risked at phase 1 across six independent products, four cell sources and four jurisdictions: zero tumours, zero severe graft-induced dyskinesia, no graft-attributable serious adverse events.** The coordinating body is GForce-PD, founded around 2014–2015 to bring together the major funded teams from Europe, the USA and Japan ([Barker 2015, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28725685/)), with a Kyoto meeting scheduled 14–15 September 2026; note that one researcher could not source the consortium at all within budget and another flagged the GForce-PD website as serving obfuscated PHP rather than content, suggesting compromise, so the site should not be relied on. The products:

**Bemdaneprocel** (allogeneic hESC-derived dopaminergic precursors, BlueRock/Bayer, from Memorial Sloan Kettering) treated 12 patients at 0.9 or 2.7 million cells per putamen with 12 months' immunosuppression. At 12 months the safety endpoint was met with **no serious adverse events related to the implant** and one procedure-related seizure that fully resolved; at 18 months safety was maintained after immunosuppression stopped, with **no deaths, discontinuations, graft-induced dyskinesias, hemorrhage, overgrowth or tumours on MRI** and elevated ¹⁸F-DOPA uptake in the transplanted region ([Tabar 2025, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40240592/)). **At 36 months, MDS-UPDRS Part III fell 17.9 points at high dose versus 13.5 at low dose, Part II −4.3 versus +0.2, and good ON time rose about 1.0 h/day versus 0.2 h**, with continued PET-confirmed engraftment after immunosuppression withdrawal ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/cell-therapy-bemdaneprocel-continues-show-positive-effects-parkinson-disease-36-months)). FDA Fast Track came in July 2021 and RMAT in May 2024. **exPDite-2 skipped phase 2 entirely: 102 patients randomized to 3 million cells bilaterally or sham surgery, primary endpoint change in ON time without troublesome dyskinesia at 18 months, 33 sites across the US, Canada and Australia, first patient randomized and operated August 2025, placebo-controlled portion expected to complete March 2027, follow-up extended to five years.**

**Raguneprocel** (allogeneic iPSC-derived progenitors, Kyoto/Sumitomo) treated **7 patients aged 50–69 bilaterally at 2.1–2.6 or 5.3–5.5 × 10⁶ cells per hemisphere with 15 months of tacrolimus-based immunosuppression and 24 months' follow-up: no deaths and no serious adverse events, 72 of 73 adverse events mild and one moderate, MRI showing gradual graft-size increase without tumorigenic overgrowth, and 4 of 6 evaluable participants improving on off-medication MDS-UPDRS III at 24 months** ([Sawamoto 2025, Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40240591/)). Exact MDS-UPDRS III point changes were not retrievable. **On 6 March 2026 Sumitomo Pharma obtained manufacturing and marketing authorisation in Japan for AMCHEPRY (raguneprocel) — described as the world's first iPS-cell-derived regenerative medicine — for improvement of motor symptoms in patients with inadequate response to existing drug therapy including levodopa. The approval is conditional and time-limited: Sumitomo must "conduct a post-marketing clinical study and a post-marketing surveillance to obtain full approval within the designated period."** It rested on the investigator-initiated Kyoto trial, and the product held SAKIGAKE priority-review designation since February 2017 and orphan regenerative medical product designation since December 2025 ([Sumitomo Pharma](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20260306.html); corroborated by [APDA](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/japan-approves-first-stem-cell-therapy-for-parkinsons-disease/)). The specific length of the conditional window and the design of the required post-marketing study are not stated in retrieved sources.

**STEM-PD** (hESC-derived progenitors, Lund/Cambridge, sponsored by Novo Nordisk) transplanted **8 patients at two dose levels with 12 months' immunosuppression; the primary safety and tolerability endpoint was met with no serious adverse effects directly attributable to the cells, one participant died of an unrelated pulmonary infection, dopamine PET gave early indications of graft survival at 6 and 12 months, 6 of 7 examined participants substantially reduced dopaminergic medication, patients were clinically stable, and there were no graft-induced involuntary movements** ([Nat Med, 9 July 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04525-0)). MDS-UPDRS numbers were not reported in the available release. The Lund release states a Boston-based company has acquired the programme and will lead phase 2 with FDA Fast Track and IND clearance in hand — **single-source, and the company name and designations should be independently verified**. Functionally the programme remains pre-phase 2 as of mid-2026.

**Sasineprocel** (autologous iPSC-derived precursors from the patient's own fibroblasts, Aspen Neuroscience) is the one product requiring **no chronic immunosuppression**. ASPIRO is described as the first multi-patient, multi-centre autologous cell therapy trial in PD. At 12 months in 8 patients (4 low-dose, 4 high-dose): **good ON time +2.1 h low dose and +2.4 h high dose; off-medication MDS-UPDRS III improved 15.5 and 13.5 points; Part II improved 5.3 and 2.3; PDQ-39 quality of life improved 51.6% and 28.5%; FDOPA PET confirmed survival and engraftment with intraoperative imaging verifying delivery to the post-commissural putamen; and no serious surgical events, symptomatic hemorrhages, infarctions or severe graft-induced dyskinesia** ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/autologous-cell-therapy-sasineprocel-demonstrates-12-month-safety-engraftment-parkinsons)). FDA granted RMAT designation, and phase 3 is planned for later 2026. **TED-A9** (hESC-derived, S.Biomedics, South Korea) treated 12 subjects aged 50–75, with 12-month off-medication MDS-UPDRS III improving **22.7% at low dose and 25.3% at high dose**, freezing of gait fully resolving in all high-dose subjects, non-motor and quality-of-life improvements, and increased FP-CIT PET dopamine transporter signal. And a Chinese programme transplanted high-purity A9 dopaminergic progenitors bilaterally in **6 patients with no serious cell-attributable adverse events, no graft-induced dyskinesias, no tumour formation on serial MRI, and bilateral increases in putaminal F-DOPA uptake at 18 months** ([Cell 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(25)01041-4)) — though the researcher notes the *Cell* full text returned 403 and that **search engines attributed the "4 of 6 improved / no tumorigenic overgrowth" language ambiguously between the Chinese paper and the Kyoto paper, so which numbers belong to which study should be verified against primary texts.**

Three things genuinely changed versus the fetal era, and all are evidenced: a manufacturable, QC'd cell supply against TRANSEURO's 86 cancelled surgeries; near-elimination of graft-induced dyskinesia across every modern product; and time-limited rather than indefinite immunosuppression, supported by bemdaneprocel's persistent PET signal 24 months after withdrawal — which, if it holds, favours the cheaper, scalable allogeneic model over Aspen's bespoke autologous one. But **efficacy magnitude remains unproven, because every readout to date is open-label.** The numbers look large — 13–18 point off-medication motor improvements, two or more hours of good ON — and open-label surgical trials in PD have historically shown very large sham responses. That is precisely why Freed 2001 and Olanow 2003 failed once sham-controlled, and why **exPDite-2's readout around March 2027 is the single most consequential pending result in the field.** Dose-response is also inconsistent: bemdaneprocel and TED-A9 showed clear high-dose advantages while ASPIRO's 12-month data were numerically *better* in the low-dose cohort on motor score and quality of life. With four patients per cohort that is most likely noise, but it undercuts confident dose-response claims. No cost, pricing, reimbursement or manufacturing-throughput data were retrievable for any product, so the economics of autologous versus allogeneic manufacture are unquantified, and no patient-selection consensus exists despite markedly different trial criteria (AB-1005 ages 45–75; TED-A9 ages 50–75 with over five years' duration; bemdaneprocel 5–15 years' duration).

**Gene therapy divides by theory of disease, and the two leading programmes are not interchangeable.** The overriding technical lesson across two decades is that **delivery — coverage, volume, device and target — has determined outcomes more than the transgene.** The trophic-factor route's canonical failure, AAV2-neurturin, is best read as under-distribution: a mechanistic post-mortem argued that "point source concentration of GDNF may explain failure of phase II clinical trial," and the programme itself responded by testing a target change from putamen to putamen plus substantia nigra ([Bartus/Olanow 2013, Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0b013e3182904faa)). **AB-1005 (AAV2-GDNF, AskBio/Bayer) is the direct experimental test of that hypothesis: 11 participants received up to 1.8 mL at 3.3 × 10¹² vg/mL per putamen by MRI-monitored convection-enhanced delivery, achieving quantified putaminal coverage of 63% ± 2% — the first PD gene therapy programme to report coverage as a dose-defining metric. At 18 months, delivery was well tolerated with 5 serious adverse events, all unrelated to the vector, and the mild cohort showed stability in MDS-UPDRS, motor diary, UDysRS and levodopa-equivalent dose** ([MDS Abstracts](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/preliminary-efficacy-of-bilateral-intraputaminal-delivery-of-gdnf-gene-therapy-aav2-gdnf-ab-1005-in-parkinsons-disease-18-month-follow-up-from-a-phase-1b-study/)). That its phase 1b signal was *stability* in mild patients, not improvement, is a disease-modification framing — which makes endpoint choice consequential, since a conventional symptomatic endpoint could undersell the effect. **REGENERATE-PD, a randomised double-blind sham-controlled phase 2 of about 87 participants aged 45–75 across the US, Germany, Poland and the UK, randomized its first participants in January 2025** ([Bayer](https://www.bayer.com/media/en-us/first-participants-randomized-in-askbio-phase-ii-gene-therapy-trial-for-parkinsons-disease/)); it holds FDA RMAT and, with AB-1002, Japan's Pioneering Regenerative Medical Product designation, and a Japan-specific study is registered. No readout date was sourced.

**AAV-GAD (MeiraGTx) takes the opposite approach — circuit normalisation via a small-volume subthalamic target delivered without general anaesthesia** — and has the most striking randomised numbers in PD gene therapy. GAD converts glutamic acid to GABA, normalising subthalamic overactivity. In a **sham-controlled 26-week bridging study of 14 patients (5 high dose, 5 low dose, 4 sham), the high dose achieved an approximately 18-point average improvement in off-medication MDS-UPDRS Part III against a 5–10-point clinically meaningful threshold, while low-dose and sham showed no significant motor change; PDQ-39 improved 8 points at high dose, 6 at low dose, and worsened 0.2 points with sham; the safety primary was achieved and no treated subject experienced worsening PD as an adverse event** ([Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/meiragtxs-parkinsons-gene-therapy-shows-promise-in-phase-i-ii-study/)). FDA granted RMAT in 2025 on the basis of statistically significant efficacy in three studies — a phase 1 dose-escalation (n = 14), a double-blind sham-surgery-controlled phase 2 (n = 45), and a double-blind sham-controlled phase 1/2 — though this rests on corporate disclosures, and the company reports FDG-PET evidence of effects in basal ganglia circuitry including the substantia nigra. **Sham control, not modality, is the discriminating variable here: CERE-120 and AXO-Lenti-PD both looked good open-label and failed double-blind, so AAV-GAD's two sham-controlled trials are a genuine distinction from the open-label cell-therapy numbers in this review. The 2024 result itself should be read cautiously, however.** The sham arm contained four patients, so "no significant motor change" is compatible with several points of improvement or worsening and should not be read as zero; sham arms in every prior PD surgical trial (Freed, Olanow, CERE-120) improved measurably, and the earlier 45-patient AAV-GAD phase 2 reportedly showed the normal pattern of both arms improving with a modest treated-arm advantage of a few points. An 18-point gain in five patients exceeds the typical motor benefit of subthalamic deep brain stimulation and is more plausibly a small-sample outlier than the true effect size. The results are sourced from a company release via trade press, and no peer-reviewed publication of the 14-patient study was found. The fair summary is a modest sham-controlled effect in 2011 and a dramatic but underpowered signal in 2024, with the phase 3 as the decisive test. The programme is described as "Phase 3 ready" with initiation expected imminently, but **no confirmed phase 3 start date or NCT number exists**, the exact phase 2 (n = 45) numbers and p-values could not be confirmed, and the historical phase 2 citation (reported as LeWitt, *Lancet Neurology* 2011) was **not sourced and requires independent verification.**

The discontinued programmes supply the safety rules. **ProSavin's lentiviral tri-enzyme approach showed significant motor improvement at 6 and 12 months sustained up to four years in most participants ([Palfi 2014, Lancet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProSavin), with volume and page details unverified), but its more potent successor AXO-Lenti-PD failed phase 2 and was dropped in 2022**, with Sio Gene Therapies winding down. **VY-AADC reported positive three-year efficacy data before the FDA placed a clinical hold on RESTORE-1 following an IND Safety Report of MRI abnormalities in participants; Neurocrine terminated the PD collaboration effective 2 August 2021** — making post-infusion MRI surveillance a standing safety requirement for intraparenchymal AAV in PD. For genetically targeted gene therapy, the picture is contraction rather than expansion: **PR001/LY3884961 (AAV9-GBA1) continues in PD only via PROPEL, with enrollment expanded from 20 to 32 patients as of November 2025, a sham arm removed and immunosuppression enhanced after an early immune-related serious adverse event, and primary completion estimated 31 March 2031** — and Lilly removed the same programme for Gaucher disease type 1 for failing internal success criteria, with no safety signal ([NCT04127578](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04127578)). No interim PROPEL clinical data are available. So the field's near-term gene-therapy momentum is in circuit modulation and trophic support, not in LRRK2 or GBA1 — which materially changes how "precision medicine in PD" should be framed.

**Enabling technology.** Focused ultrasound is being repurposed from lesioning to blood-brain-barrier opening for drug delivery, which connects it directly to the delivery bottleneck above. The first-in-human study opened the barrier over the striatum and posterior putamen in **7 participants with Parkinson's disease dementia** ([Gasca-Salas 2021, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-21022-9)), and a company-sponsored pilot used barrier disruption to deliver imiglucerase (β-glucocerebrosidase) in **15 people with PD**, scheduled to complete December 2025 — **no readout was located**. Across indications, microbubble-enhanced transcranial FUS has opened the barrier in more than 300 patients over more than 800 sessions, though those figures are cross-indication, not PD-specific ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/focused-ultrasound-blood-brain-barrier)). This is best framed as an enabling platform rather than a therapy: its near-term value is making non-penetrant agents deliverable to the putamen, and it is a plausible rescue path for the GBA1 enzyme and LRRK2 antisense approaches whose delivery is the bottleneck ([J Neurochem 2026 review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jnc.70445)). On brain-computer interfaces, **no PD-specific trial or publication was retrievable, and no evidence of a BCI programme targeting PD was found beyond closed-loop DBS** — it should be treated as preclinical or conceptual for PD.

AI has begun producing chemical matter rather than only predictions. **Structure-based iterative machine learning applied to the catalytic sites on the surface of α-synuclein aggregates (secondary nucleation inhibitors) yielded compounds two orders of magnitude more potent than previously reported ones — a large improvement over both the parent molecule and clinical-trial molecules, and a major structural departure from them — with initial screening roughly 10-fold faster at about 1,000-fold lower cost** ([Horne 2024, Nat Chem Biol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41589-024-01580-x); [Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/ai-speeds-up-drug-design-for-parkinsons-ten-fold)). The method has generalised to tau. The honest reading: this delivers potency in vitro, not clinical candidates — and its stated advance over previously tested clinical molecules implicitly concedes that those compounds were weak, which is a reason for caution in extrapolating to patient benefit.

**Realistic timelines.** Only one platform has crossed a regulatory finish line, and it did so under a national framework rather than on efficacy evidence: Japan's conditional, time-limited approval on a seven-patient open-label trial is as much a statement about Japan's regenerative-medicine pathway as about the data. Everything else hinges on a small cluster of sham-controlled readouts around 2027 — exPDite-2, REGENERATE-PD, and a not-yet-started AAV-GAD phase 3. **The earliest plausible US or EU approval of any PD cell or gene therapy is 2028–2029**, gated by exPDite-2's March 2027 placebo portion implying a BLA no earlier than late 2027; GBA1 gene therapy is a 2030s proposition on its own registered completion date; and LRRK2-directed therapy currently has no sponsor-backed clinical path in either antisense or small-molecule form. Anything claiming a US cell-therapy approval before 2028 is not supported by these trial timelines. The binding constraints differ by platform: for cells, efficacy under sham control plus manufacturing cost; for gene therapy, delivery coverage and irreversibility; for neurotechnology, evidence volume — the most impressive result is n = 1; for digital and AI, regulatory endpoint qualification and positive predictive value. One further consequence deserves naming: **a licensed iPSC product in one jurisdiction while the same class remains investigational elsewhere creates a real near-term equity problem, inviting medical tourism and unproven-clinic marketing.**

## Exercise, care organisation and prevention deliver gains the drug pipeline has not

Exercise is the field's most credible candidate for a non-pharmacological disease modifier, and it is in a "proof of concept achieved, phase 3 pending" state. Two phase 2 trials established the signal. **SPARX, a futility-design trial in de novo unmedicated patients comparing high-intensity (80–85% HRmax) against moderate-intensity (60–65%) treadmill exercise and usual care over six months, found high-intensity exercise non-futile and safe**, with the control group worsening slightly while the high-intensity group remained stable ([Schenkman 2018, JAMA Neurol](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2017.3517); the researcher flags the DOI as reliable but its details not re-verified in session, with the lay summary from [APDA](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/sparx3-trial-researches-whether-exercise-can-slow-parkinsons-progression/)). **Park-in-Shape randomized 130 medicated patients to home-based stationary cycling with exergaming three times weekly for six months versus stretching, with the aerobic group showing a smaller off-state MDS-UPDRS motor worsening of roughly 4.2 points** ([van der Kolk 2019, Lancet Neurol](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(19)30285-6)) — again, DOI reliable, effect size from prior knowledge and not re-verified. Mechanistic substudies give the signal plausibility: aerobic exercise **increased cognitive control, increased functional connectivity of the anterior putamen with sensorimotor cortex and the right fronto-parietal network, and reduced global brain atrophy, "possibly stabilizing disease progression"** ([Johansson 2022, Ann Neurol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.26291) — note this appeared in *Annals of Neurology*, not npj Parkinson's Disease as some briefs state), and a 36-session aerobic programme **increased rTMS-evoked dopamine release in the caudate on PET** ([Sacheli 2019, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27865)). A 2024 meta-analysis of 5 trials and 216 participants gives **SMD 1.20 for exercise-induced BDNF increase, with BDNF increases correlating with motor improvement** ([PMC10968162](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10968162/)).

**SPARX3 is the pivotal test, and as of October 2026 it has not reported.** It randomized 370 de novo unmedicated participants to treadmill exercise four days weekly for 30 minutes at moderate versus high intensity, with primary analysis on change in MDS-UPDRS Part III over 12 months "or until initiation of symptomatic antiparkinsonian treatment if before 12 months," and secondary outcomes including striatal DAT binding, six-minute walk, daily steps, cognition, fitness, quality of life, time to dopaminergic medication, CRP and BDNF ([Patterson 2022, Trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36203214/)). Completion was expected mid-2025; the registry showed "active, not recruiting" with last update 10 July 2026, and **no topline results were located** ([NCT04284436](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04284436)). Its design has a consequential feature: **both arms exercise, so it tests an intensity dose-response rather than exercise versus none** — meaning a null result would show that intensity does not matter above moderate, not that exercise does not work, while a positive result would establish dose-response. Its DAT-SPECT secondary is the first attempt at scale to separate symptomatic from disease-modifying effects.

On comparative effectiveness, the message is that modality matters less than participation. The Cochrane network meta-analysis concluded that the review "highlights the importance of physical exercise regarding severity of motor signs and quality of life, while the exact exercise type might be secondary" ([Ernst 2023, Cochrane CD013856](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36602886/)); the researcher could not retrieve exact study counts, SMDs or certainty ratings by modality because PubMed, the Cochrane Library and Europe PMC all blocked fetches, so the commonly cited figures (156 RCTs, roughly 7,900 participants, moderate-certainty evidence for dance, aqua-based, gait/balance/functional and multi-domain training) **are reported from prior knowledge and are unverified here**. Head-to-head trials support the same conclusion: a boxing programme improved gait velocity and endurance while traditional exercise produced greater gains in balance confidence ([Combs 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23422464/)), and in a double-blinded RCT a sensory exercise programme improved more than boxing on UPDRS motor, with the boxing group worsening at washout. The APDA's assessment is the fair one: "There is some limited clinical trial evidence that Rock Steady Boxing is helpful in PD, however it has not been shown to be better than traditional exercise modalities." Tai chi has the strongest long-term observational signal: a **3.5-year cohort found slower progression on all three scales, fewer patients needing medication increases, slower cognitive decline, and lower complication rates (dyskinesia 1.4% versus 7.5%; MCI 3% versus 10%)** ([Li 2024, JNNP](https://bmjgroup.com/tai-chi-may-curb-parkinsons-disease-symptoms-and-complications-for-several-years/)) — but it is non-randomized, which is the key limitation. Observationally, **exercising at least 2.5 hours per week was associated with better health-related quality of life and slower decline among 2,940 people followed two years in the Parkinson's Outcomes Project**, with earlier starters declining more slowly ([Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/living-with-parkinsons/treatment/exercise)).

**Rehabilitation and care organisation.** The strongest health-services evidence in PD is Dutch. The original ParkinsonNet cluster-randomized trial across 16 regions found no improvement in patient outcomes but lower costs ([Munneke 2010, Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(09)70327-8/abstract)); the claims analysis that followed is more informative: among roughly 4,381 patients, **PD-related complications (hospital visit or admission for fracture, orthopaedic injury or pneumonia) occurred in 17% of patients treated by specialised physiotherapists versus 21% under usual care, with specialised therapists carrying higher caseloads (3.89 versus 1.48 patients per therapist) and delivering fewer sessions (33.72 versus 47.97), at roughly EUR 400 per year lower physiotherapy costs and EUR 530 per year lower total healthcare costs** ([Ypinga 2018, Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(17)30406-4/abstract)), with a 2025 extension to mortality ([npj Parkinsons Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01069-x)). The lesson is that the *organisation* of allied health — expertise concentration, high caseloads, guideline adherence — can deliver cost savings and complication reductions even where individual-level RCTs show small or null effects on disability scales. Amplitude-based retraining is the most consistently RCT-supported rehabilitation paradigm for both speech and limb movement: LSVT BIG improved UPDRS-III against Nordic walking and home exercise and recalibrates proprioception over 4–8 weeks ([PMC7353788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7353788/)), and LSVT LOUD produced significant immediate and long-term increases in sound pressure level ([Ramig 2018, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27460)). Home-based individualised occupational therapy over 10 weeks improved self-perceived performance in daily activities in 191 patients ([Sturkenboom 2014, Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(14)70074-2/abstract)), though a later trial of low-intensity goal-directed OT and physiotherapy produced no clinically meaningful differences in mild-moderate PD (PD REHAB). The researcher flags that cueing for freezing, fall-prevention RCTs, SpeechVive device trials and dysphagia management (expiratory muscle strength training) were not retrievable this session and should be cited with caution; physiotherapy meta-analytic support is nonetheless solid, with conventional physiotherapy improving motor symptoms, gait and quality of life, treadmill and resistance training improving gait, and dual-task training showing no significant improvement across 191 trials and 7,998 participants ([Radder 2020, J Parkinsons Dis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32917125)).

Other care models show a consistent and instructive pattern: **process and access outcomes improve more readily than PDQ-39 or UPDRS scores.** Integrated multidisciplinary care produced "small benefits... in disability scores and quality of life, which had disappeared when corrected for baseline disease severity" ([van der Marck 2013, Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS1474-4422(13)70145-5/abstract)). Telemedicine is feasible and non-inferior but not superior: in Connect.Parkinson, **98% of the intervention group completed at least one virtual visit and 91% of 388 visits were completed, each saving a median 88 minutes and 38 miles, with 97% patient and 86% neurologist satisfaction and 55% preferring virtual — yet PDQ-39 did not improve (0.3 points worse; p = 0.78), nor did quality of care or caregiver burden** ([Beck 2017, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28814455/)), building on the first pilot RCT ([Dorsey 2010, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20533449/)). The one care-model intervention with replicated RCT-level benefit on quality of life is palliative care: **integrated outpatient palliative care in 210 patients and 175 caregivers improved quality of life, symptom burden, caregiver distress and advance-directive completion** ([Kluger 2020, JAMA Neurol](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2760511)), and a pragmatic stepped-wedge trial across 19 community neurology practices with 359 patients and 300 caregivers found **QOL-AD improving 1.27 points at six months (95% CI 0.30–2.25; P = 0.01) and advance-directive completion rising from 19% to 50% (P = 0.008), with no difference in Zarit caregiver burden** ([Kluger 2024, JAMA Neurol](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2811972)). Because depression and anxiety dominate quality-of-life variance, care models that add mental-health and palliative components are more likely to move PDQ-39 than those adding specialist access alone. The PD nurse specialist RCT (NICE-PD, 240 patients across 8 Dutch hospitals with co-primary PDQ-39 and MDS-UPDRS III) has not reported ([NCT03830190](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03830190)).

Hospitalisation is the sharpest avoidable harm. **Time-critical medications were correctly prescribed on admission in only 50% of unplanned admissions, with timing errors most common at 31.6%** ([PMC8974884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8974884/)); 90% of PD inpatients experienced incorrect administration with 8.3% of doses omitted and 9.1% early or late; and **patients with delayed or missed doses stayed 8.2 days (SD 8.9) versus 3.6 days (SD 3.4)**, with an average 2.7 doses missed or delayed per patient within 48 hours ([PLOS ONE 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0124356)). A near-doubling of length of stay from a medication-timing failure is both a patient-safety and a cost problem, and it underpins the "Get It On Time" campaigns.

**Nutrition.** Observational data link dietary pattern to onset age, and the interventional evidence is thin. **MIND diet adherence correlated most strongly with later PD onset in women — up to 17.4 years between low and high tertiles — with Greek Mediterranean adherence associated with about 8 years later onset in men** across 167 patients and 119 controls ([Metcalfe-Roach 2021, Mov Disord](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8248352/)), consistent with reduced incidence and slower progression of parkinsonian signs in the Rush Memory and Aging Project ([Agarwal 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12603-018-1094-5), DOI from prior knowledge). In the other direction, **three or more daily servings of low-fat dairy versus under one gave HR 1.34 (95% CI 1.01–1.79)** across 80,736 women and 48,610 men followed about 25 years with 1,036 incident cases ([Hughes 2017, Neurology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28596209/)). These diet-onset associations are retrospective and vulnerable to reverse causation, since prodromal PD alters appetite, smell and gut function; they justify prospective trials but not causal claims. Interventional evidence amounts to small pilots: a ketogenic versus low-fat randomized pilot in 47 patients found both groups improving with **the ketogenic group improving non-motor Part I more (−4.58, 41%, versus −0.99, 11%)** ([Phillips 2018, Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27390)), and vitamin D3 1200 IU/day for 12 months in 114 patients "significantly prevented the deterioration of the Hoehn and Yahr stage" ([Suzuki 2013, Am J Clin Nutr](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23485413/)) while a 2025 meta-analysis found supplemental vitamin D did not reduce UPDRS-III, walk-test or timed-up-and-go times, improving only six-minute walk distance. The most actionable nutritional finding is prognostic rather than therapeutic: in the PINE incident cohort of 515 participants followed a median five years, **weight loss within one year of diagnosis was independently associated with dependency, dementia and death** ([Cumming 2017, Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000004691)). Weight loss is best read as a prognostic biomarker and a target for proactive dietetic input, not as a modifiable cause. Protein redistribution for levodopa absorption, dietary fibre interventions and harms of unproven supplements were not retrievable this session.

**Patient-reported outcomes, sex and gender.** Non-motor symptomatology has a significantly greater impact on caregiver burden and quality of life than motor symptoms, and non-motor fluctuations associate with worse PDQ-39 across all domains except stigma, contributing to caregiver burden comparably to motor complications ([J Neurol 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40982051/)). Caregiver burden in late-stage disease tracks disease severity and neuropsychiatric symptoms, which argues for caregiver outcomes as co-primary endpoints in late-stage trials. Sex and gender differences became an explicit 2024–2026 research priority: **women show more dyskinesia, fluctuations, anxiety and depression, fatigue and pain, while men show more rigidity and akinesia, cognitive impairment, gait instability and RBD; higher lifetime estrogen exposure appears neuroprotective while earlier or surgical menopause associates with higher risk and earlier onset; and women have greater levodopa bioavailability and dyskinesia risk, supporting sex-specific dosing** ([Barbato 2025, Clin Transl Med](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ctm2.70382)). Institutionally this now has a vehicle: C-Path's GEM-PD programme launched in March 2025 focusing on sex-specific biological factors. Stigma, employment and driving were not retrievable this session.

**Prevention and public health.** The prevention agenda has moved from advocacy through policy into its first trials. *Ending Parkinson's Disease* (2020) proposed a PACT agenda — Prevent, Advocate, Care, Treat — and frames PD as largely preventable through environmental action ([endingpd.org](https://endingpd.org/)). On policy, **the EPA banned nearly all uses of trichloroethylene in December 2024**, which MJFF called "a landmark victory for the Parkinson's community" — and industry has since sought reversal ([MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/landmark-victory-parkinsons-community-epa-bans-trichloroethylene)). **Paraquat, by contrast, is banned in roughly 70 countries but was re-authorized by the EPA in 2024**, with more than 50 US lawmakers urging a ban in November 2024 and a California bill proposed ([The New Lede](https://www.thenewlede.org/2024/11/us-congress-members-call-on-epa-to-ban-paraquat-citing-risk-of-parkinsons-and-other-diseases/)). Quantitative population-attributable fractions for TCE or paraquat were not retrieved, which is a real gap in the prevention argument. **The first non-pharmacological prevention trials are now in protocol: Slow-SPEED comprises three randomised trials of remotely delivered gamified app-based exercise versus active control over 18–36 months in iRBD (n = 110, Netherlands), hyposmia (n = 110, UK) and LRRK2/GBA1 carriers (n = 600, US), with the intervention arm targeting up to a 100% increase in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity against 10% in control, monitored by smartwatch** ([npj Parkinsons Dis 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01463-z)). Its three-cohort design is the template for prodromal prevention trials, and results around 2028–2030 will be the first test of exercise as primary prevention. **No completed prevention trial in prodromal PD exists; all are at protocol stage.** The genetic infrastructure for future prevention trials is further along: **PD GENEration had enrolled 32,577 people from 10 countries with 29,589 completing testing and counselling as of 31 March 2026, at cumulative positivity of 12.1% for likely pathogenic or pathogenic variants in primary panel genes, across 77 sites including Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Peru and El Salvador** ([medRxiv 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.20.26353696v1.full); [Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/pdgeneration/genetics-behind-pd)), up from 10,510 enrolled across more than 85 centres by June 2023 ([Cook 2024, Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39074992/)) — roughly tripling in three years, and notably more than 75% of participants had not previously engaged in research. The WHO technical brief of June 2022 frames the whole agenda, noting prevalence doubled in 25 years to more than 8.5 million in 2019 and that "disability and death due to PD are increasing faster than for any other neurological disorder," with **43 key actions across seven areas** including reducing exposure to air pollution and implicated chemicals, improving affordability of therapies and training primary care providers, with an explicit low- and middle-income-country focus ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-06-2022-launch-of-who-s-parkinson-disease-technical-brief)).

**Health economics.** The anchor estimate is **$51.9 billion per year in the US in 2017 — $25.4 billion direct medical and $26.5 billion indirect and non-medical (lost work, wages, early retirement, caregiver time) — more than double previous estimates** ([Yang 2020, npj Parkinsons Dis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-020-0117-1)), with a projection above $79 billion by 2037 reported from prior knowledge and not re-verified. No post-2020 US refresh was located. Two implications follow from the structure of those costs. Roughly half are indirect, so interventions that reduce caregiver burden and preserve function — exercise, occupational therapy, palliative care — have economic leverage beyond direct medical savings. And since aging drives about 89% of projected prevalence growth, delaying onset by even a few years would have outsized population-level economic effects, which is the strongest economic case for prevention trials. Formal QALY-based cost-effectiveness analyses of exercise programmes, telemedicine or palliative care in PD were not retrieved.

## The infrastructure now shapes the science, and it encodes the field's arguments

Four institutional clusters carry contemporary PD research, and they are not neutral conduits — each embodies a position in the field's live debates.

**MJFF and PPMI.** MJFF reports having funded **$3 billion in research**, with 89 cents of every dollar going to mission programmes and "180 promising therapies... in or entering human testing — the most ever" ([michaeljfox.org](https://www.michaeljfox.org/)); the field's trial-infrastructure file adds an anticipated **$698 million of PD research funding in 2026, described as more than twice the US government's investment**. PPMI, launched in 2010, now describes **nearly 50 clinical sites in 12 countries, support from more than 40 public, private and philanthropic investors, over 5,000 in-clinic participants and over 50,000 online participants, expanding toward more than 4,000 in-clinic volunteers including 2,000 prodromal participants** ([ppmi-info.org](https://www.ppmi-info.org/about-ppmi)). Its cohort composition is specific: 423 untreated PD in the initial phase plus 600 additional sporadic untreated, 294 genetic PD, 67 prodromal RBD/hyposmia with DAT deficit, 445 genetic risk carriers and 196 healthy controls, with **current prodromal recruitment based on hyposmia plus seed-assay confirmation or genetic variants** ([study cohorts](https://www.ppmi-info.org/study-design/study-cohorts)). Data are open access through LONI with a one-week committee review, spanning clinical, imaging, omics, genetic, sensor and biomarker data, with raw whole-exome BAM files alone running about 15 TB. A PubMed search for the study name returned **1,378 records** — a conservative floor, since it misses papers using the data without naming it. Two facts signal the pivot: the 2023 seed-assay result is PPMI's headline achievement, and **the study was renamed in 2026 from "Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative" to "Parkinson's Precision Medicine Initiative,"** formalising its move from a progression-marker cohort to the biological-definition platform — PPMI investigators are lead authors on NSD-ISS. Fox Insight supplies the patient-reported complement at **56,063 participants** completing questionnaires every 90 days, with de-identified data shared and a genetic sub-study of roughly 600,000 SNPs ([Smolensky 2020, Sci Data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32094335/)).

**ASAP and GP2.** The Collaborative Research Network, founded 2020, comprises **67 research teams with nearly 400 investigators across 187 institutions in 22 countries** operating on open-science principles ([parkinsonsroadmap.org](https://parkinsonsroadmap.org/research-network/)). GP2, its resource programme — managed by the Coalition for Aligning Science and implemented by MJFF — reports **more than 124,000 samples completed against more than 415,000 expected across more than 415 cohorts and 120 member organisations**, with data served through AMP PD on Terra and Verily Workbench ([gp2.org](https://gp2.org/)); the 2026 programme paper gives slightly different and more granular figures — **436 cohorts across 84 locations, 103,786 samples analysed, 343,123 expected, 59 monogenic centres and about 410 trainees, 225 from underrepresented populations — with GP2-supported studies having identified more than 50 novel genetic risk factors** and a European GWAS reporting 134 independent loci including 59 novel ([Flores-Ocampo 2026, J Parkinsons Dis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13435180/)). **The primary publication for that "134 loci, 59 novel" European GWAS could not be retrieved and should be cited via the GP2 paper or verified independently.** ASAP's founding year, funder and total commitment could not be confirmed from fetched pages, which returned 404s.

**Public funders.** NINDS is the primary NIH institute supporting PD research, operating through the Morris K. Udall Centers of Excellence — authorized by the 1997 Udall Act, which permits **not more than 10 Core Center Grants** ([PLAW-105publ78](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-105publ78/html/PLAW-105publ78.htm)) — plus the Parkinson's Disease Biomarkers Program, the BioSEND repository, the Data Management Resource and the Human Cell and Data Repository, with the National Plan to End Parkinson's referenced on its page ([NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/current-research/focus-disorders/focus-parkinsons-disease-research)). The current number and locations of Udall Centers could not be verified (404s). **AMP PD, launched 30 January 2018, is the de facto federated hub, harmonising de-identified data from 4,298 participants across eight unified cohorts — BioFIND, Harvard Biomarkers Study, LBD, LCC, PDBP, PPMI, STEADY-PD3 and SURE-PD3 — plus GP2 as a federated cohort, spanning whole-genome, transcriptomic, targeted and untargeted proteomic, post-mortem sequencing and extracellular-vesicle RNA-Seq data on Terra and Google Cloud** ([amp-pdrd.org](https://amp-pdrd.org/); [Iwaki 2021, Mov Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33960523/)). An important analytic point follows: PPMI (LONI), AMP PD (Terra) and GP2 are overlapping rather than independent datasets, so apparent replication across them should be read carefully.

**Charities and regulatory science.** The Parkinson's Foundation runs the field's longest clinical registry, the Parkinson's Outcomes Project, since 2009 with **more than 13,000 participants across five countries at Centers of Excellence**, whose headline findings are that "depression and anxiety are the number one factors impacting the overall health of people with Parkinson's," that regular neurologist care saves lives, and that 2.5 hours of weekly activity slows quality-of-life decline ([Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/parkinsons-outcomes-project)). Parkinson's UK describes itself as "the leading European funder of Parkinson's research" with **over £130 million invested** ([parkinsons.org.uk/research](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/research)), and is founding partner with C-Path of the Critical Path for Parkinson's consortium (2015), which pooled patient-level data **covering over 600 variables** to build the early-motor DAT-SPECT trial enrichment tool and a disease-progression model, drawing on **672 early-stage participants from PPMI and PRECEPT** with co-authors from Pfizer, Merck, UCB and Lilly ([Conrado 2018, Clin Transl Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28749580/); [c-path.org](https://c-path.org/programs/cpp/)). That modelling work is the technical basis of what is usually described as the EMA qualification opinion and FDA letter of support for DAT-SPECT as an enrichment biomarker — **the regulatory documents themselves returned 404s, so exact dates and wording are unverified and the qualification should be attributed to C-Path's statements or sourced directly.** CPP also launched GEM-PD in March 2025. Cure Parkinson's runs the iLCT programme with the Van Andel Institute and funds ASPro-PD, AZA-PD, the UDCA trial, TRANSEURO and EJS ACT-PD with the Edmond J Safra Foundation; its iLCT page returned HTTP 500 twice, so the founding year (commonly 2012) and programme totals are unverified from the source. The Parkinson Study Group has run multicenter trials since DATATOP, and its STEADY-PD3 and SURE-PD3 cohorts now sit inside AMP PD.

**Societies, meetings and publishing.** The MDS has **more than 12,000 members** across four regional sections, publishes *Movement Disorders* and *Movement Disorders Clinical Practice*, and owns the MDS-UPDRS in 31 languages plus the MDS-NMS, UDysRS and SCOPA-AUT, licensed per single use ([movementdisorders.org](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/About.htm)). Its International Congress draws **more than 2,000 abstracts** and runs 4–8 October 2026 in Seoul with Amsterdam in 2027; AD/PD next meets in Barcelona 16–20 March 2027; and the triennial World Parkinson Congress, under a coalition founded in 2004, has hosted six congresses attracting nearly 20,000 delegates. On publishing scale, PubMed indexes roughly **5,200 records per year under the MeSH heading "Parkinson Disease" in 2024–2025 and 10,000–12,000 per year mentioning "parkinson" in title or abstract, up from 737 and 807 respectively in 1990** (counts run via E-utilities on 6 October 2026; 2025 counts may still rise as indexing completes). The ratio of title/abstract mentions to MeSH-indexed primary papers grew from about 1.1 in 1990 to about 2.3 in 2025, reflecting PD's increasing role as a comparator or example in broader neuroscience, AI and drug-discovery literature. In 2024 the specialty journals produced 293 PD-related items in *Movement Disorders*, 246 in *Parkinsonism & Related Disorders*, 223 in *npj Parkinson's Disease*, 174 in the *Journal of Parkinson's Disease* and 121 in *Movement Disorders Clinical Practice*, against 52 in *Neurology*, 49 in *Brain*, 35 in *Lancet Neurology* and 11 each in *JAMA Neurology* and *Nature Reviews Neurology*. These are raw PubMed counts, not citation analyses, and no formal bibliometric study was retrievable.

The infrastructure encodes the arguments. PPMI, ASAP, GP2 and AMP PD are built around biological definition and genetics. Dorsey, Bloem and the Parkinson's Foundation's outcomes work emphasise prevention, care and environment. CPP sits between them, working to get biomarkers — DAT, seed assay, digital — accepted by regulators so that any of these strategies can be tested in shorter, enriched trials. And the MDS's per-use licensing of the MDS-UPDRS makes the society an effective gatekeeper of the primary endpoint in nearly every PD trial, which is directly relevant to the push for digital and biological endpoints. Four debates structure the field in 2025–2026: whether to adopt a seed-assay-anchored biological definition and staging for research and eventually clinic; whether priority should shift from treatment to prevention; how to weight environmental toxicants against genetics; and how to calibrate expectations for stem-cell dopamine replacement after the 2025 *Nature* trials. On that last point, the field's own choice of words is the best corrective: **both 2025 Nature papers are phase 1/2 safety-and-feasibility trials, not efficacy trials.**

## Conclusion

The most useful way to read 2026 is that Parkinson's research has solved its measurement problem faster than its therapeutic one, and the two are now out of phase in a way that creates specific risks. A qualitative assay that detects pathological α-synuclein years before symptoms, combined with accelerometry and retinal imaging that discriminate at seven-year lead times, means the field can now identify a large at-risk population it has nothing to offer. The NSD-ISS staging debate is not really about nosology; it is about what happens when diagnostic capability outruns therapeutic capability, which is why the objections that bite hardest are ethical and practical — asymptomatic labelling, insurance, the 10–15% of clinical PD excluded by an S+ requirement — rather than biological. The Espay stage-regression finding is the sharpest technical warning: a staging system whose upper stages rest on MDS-UPDRS-derived function will move when you treat symptoms, so biological staging needs a quantitative progression biomarker it does not yet have. That makes α-synuclein PET, not another antibody, the most strategically important technology in development.

Two asymmetries should change how the next decade's bets are read. First, **target engagement has been demonstrated repeatedly and has predicted nothing**: deferiprone lowered nigral iron and worsened patients; inosine raised urate by 44% with no effect; BIIB122 inhibited LRRK2 kinase by over 90% and failed; the LRRK2 antisense lowered CSF LRRK2 by 59% and was discontinued; ambroxol raised CSF GCase and did not help cognition; GDNF raised fluorodopa uptake by a third without motor benefit. The field's standard phase 2 design — demonstrate engagement, infer promise — has a track record close to zero, and the institutional response (futility designs, then multi-arm platforms like EJS ACT-PD with shared placebo and time-to-event endpoints) is the right correction but will not produce answers before about 2028. Second, **sham control, not modality, separates the real from the apparent**: four independent surgical failures in PD — Freed, Olanow, CERE-120 and AXO-Lenti-PD — all followed promising open-label data, which is why AAV-GAD, the only gene or cell programme with two sham-controlled trials behind it, is on firmer footing than the much-publicised open-label cell-therapy numbers, even though its 2024 result (18 points in five patients against a four-patient sham arm, from a company release) is too small to establish the effect size, and why exPDite-2's March 2027 readout matters more than Japan's approval. The honest framing of regenerative medicine in PD today is that safety is genuinely de-risked across six products and four cell sources, manufacturability is solved, graft-induced dyskinesia is largely eliminated, and efficacy is entirely unknown.

If one prediction follows from this evidence base, it is that the next real advance will come from subtyping rather than from a better single mechanism. Every major hypothesis performs well in a subgroup and poorly on average: the gut-origin tests give weak, inconsistent effects exactly as they would if only body-first patients followed an ascending route; the seed assay is 98.6% sensitive in hyposmic sporadic PD and 34.7% in normosmic LRRK2 carriers; prasinezumab's only nominal signal was in the levodopa-treated 75%; azathioprine's exploratory effects were possibly larger in females. Thirty-five years of average-patient trials in a disease that is demonstrably not one disease is a sufficient explanation for thirty-five years of failure, and it is the one explanation the field has not yet properly tested.

**Direct conflicts between sources.** (1) **Tavapadon's FDA approval date**: AbbVie's press release is dated 28 September 2026; MJFF states 25 September 2026. Unresolved. (2) **GP2 scale**: the organisation's website reports more than 124,000 samples completed against more than 415,000 expected across more than 415 cohorts, while the 2026 programme paper reports 103,786 analysed against 343,123 expected across 436 cohorts in 84 locations — different counting conventions and dates, both reproduced above. (3) **PD dementia incidence**: Sydney's 83% at 20 years against PPMI's 9–15% and Penn's 27% at 10 years; partly reconciled by age at onset and follow-up length, but the "80% inevitable" claim should not be repeated without its time horizon and cohort. (4) **The Chinese *Cell* 2025 and Kyoto *Nature* 2025 cell-therapy papers**: search engines attributed the "4 of 6 improved" and "no tumorigenic overgrowth" language ambiguously between them, and the attribution above should be checked against primary texts. (5) **Breckenridge 2016's pesticide meta-analysis** reaches an outlier conclusion (associations non-significant after publication-bias correction) against the broader literature; its industry affiliations are noted in the paper's disclosures but were not independently verified. (6) **Pimavanserin mortality**: two Medicare analyses reach opposite conclusions by design (non-user versus active-comparator); both are reported rather than reconciled.

**Substantive open scientific questions.** Which mechanism is primary — α-synuclein, mitochondrial, lysosomal — remains undetermined, and may be subtype-specific; the convergence of the GCase/α-synuclein loop with mitochondria–lysosome contacts suggests "lysosome-first versus mitochondria-first" may be a false dichotomy. Whether Lewy bodies are toxic, protective or inert is unresolved, with the stable-proportion argument a real counterweight to the oligomer consensus. The terminal death modality in human PD nigra — apoptosis, necroptosis or ferroptosis — has no retrieved source, and **no study demonstrates ferroptosis-specific markers in human PD substantia nigra as opposed to models**, which is a genuine limitation of the ferroptosis hypothesis. Why FAIRPARK-II worsened patients despite target engagement is unexplained, with no post-trial commentary or redesigned chelation trial retrievable for 2023–2026. Whether age-standardized PD incidence is genuinely rising independent of aging is contested, and no retrieved source provides a global age-standardized incidence trend with confidence intervals adjusted for diagnostic intensity. Why lixisenatide succeeded in phase 2 where exenatide failed in phase 3 is attributed to CNS penetration without evidence. Whether the modern open-label cell-therapy results survive sham control is the single most consequential unknown, resolving around March 2027. NOPARK's results (n = 410, completed June 2025) are the most important unpublished readout in the field. SPARX3's results are not public. No completed prodromal prevention trial exists. No disease-modifying trial yet enrols purely on seed-assay positivity, and no interventional NSD stage 1–2 trial was identified as recruiting. No FDA or EMA qualification of any PD digital endpoint was located, and no regulatory status for prodromal DAT screening exists.

**Topics the research notes could not cover.** The GForce-PD consortium's current membership and governance (its website appears compromised); cost, pricing, reimbursement and manufacturing-throughput data for any cell or gene therapy; patient-selection consensus for cell therapy; adaptive-DBS and directional-lead reimbursement and uptake statistics; the Boston Scientific and Abbott adaptive-sensing programmes; randomized head-to-head comparisons among any of LCIG, foslevodopa, apomorphine infusion, DBS and focused ultrasound; a peer-reviewed publication of the bilateral pallidothalamic tractotomy phase 3; long-term durability of pallidal focused ultrasound beyond 12 months; carbidopa versus benserazide; wearable and closed-loop cueing device RCTs; P2B001's and mesdopetam's regulatory and trial status; buntanetap and solriamfetol PD motor efficacy (no evidence exists in retrieved sources); any 2024–2026 MDS non-motor evidence-based review update; Cochrane reviews of non-motor treatments; pooled prevalence meta-analyses for constipation, urinary and sexual dysfunction, gastroparesis, insomnia and restless legs in PD; anxiety-specific RCTs; pain intervention trials since 2015; mPower; validated video-based gait analysis; AI-assisted PD care delivery in practice; brain-computer interfaces for PD; stigma, employment and driving; protein redistribution for levodopa absorption; population-attributable fractions for TCE and paraquat; EJS ACT-PD's registration number and recruitment status; the exact length and design of Japan's AMCHEPRY conditional-approval window; and the texts of the MDS biological-definition statement and the twelve *Lancet Neurology* commentaries, whose specific objections should be sourced from the documents themselves.

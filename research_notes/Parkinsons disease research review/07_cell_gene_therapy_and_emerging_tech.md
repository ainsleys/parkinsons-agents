## Q1. Fetal tissue transplantation: history, the two NIH double-blind trials, Lewy pathology in grafts, and TRANSEURO

### Takeaway
Fetal ventral mesencephalic (hfVM) grafting established proof of principle that transplanted dopamine neurons survive, reinnervate the putamen and raise F-DOPA uptake, but across three decades it never produced a reproducible clinical benefit: both NIH-funded double-blind trials missed their primary endpoints and produced off-medication "graft-induced dyskinesias" in a majority of recipients in one case, and the definitive modern attempt (TRANSEURO, published 2025) concluded that hfVM transplantation is **not a clinically feasible strategy** — chiefly because of tissue supply, dose/survival shortfalls, and surgical-device variability. Its lasting value is as a design template (and a cautionary list) for the stem-cell trials now in Phase 3.

### Cited Findings

**Lund program and early open-label work [HISTORY]**
- The Lund (Lindvall/Björklund) open-label program from the late 1980s onward, together with US open-label series, provided the foundational evidence that hfVM grafts can survive and restore striatal dopaminergic innervation; this literature is reviewed in "Fetal grafts for Parkinson's disease: Decades in the making" — [PMC4988600](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4988600/)

**Freed et al. 2001, NEJM [HISTORY]**
- Freed CR et al., "Transplantation of embryonic dopamine neurons for severe Parkinson's disease," *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2001. Double-blind, sham-surgery-controlled. The trial did not show a statistically significant difference between grafted and sham groups overall; a benefit was seen in **younger but not older** patients — [Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cell-and-developmental-biology/articles/10.3389/fcell.2021.666675/full)
- ClinicalTrials.gov record for the Freed/Denver–Columbia program: "Embryonic Dopamine Cell Implants for Parkinson's Disease" — [NCT00038116](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00038116)

**Olanow et al. 2003, Annals of Neurology [HISTORY]**
- Olanow CW et al., "A double-blind controlled trial of bilateral fetal nigral transplantation in Parkinson's disease," *Annals of Neurology*, 2003. 34 patients with advanced PD; prospective 24-month double-blind placebo-controlled design; randomized to bilateral transplantation with **one or four donors per side** or a placebo (sham) procedure — [Annals of Neurology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.10720)
- Result: **no significant overall treatment effect**; stratification by baseline severity showed a treatment effect confined to **milder** patients — [Annals of Neurology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.10720)
- Striatal fluorodopa uptake rose significantly after transplantation in both dose groups, and robust dopamine neuron survival was confirmed at post-mortem — i.e. biological engraftment without commensurate clinical benefit — [Annals of Neurology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.10720)
- **Graft-induced dyskinesia (GID): 56% of transplanted patients developed dyskinesia persisting after overnight withdrawal of dopaminergic medication ("off"-medication dyskinesia)** — [Annals of Neurology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.10720)
- The authors concluded fetal nigral transplantation "cannot be recommended as a therapy for PD" on these results — [Annals of Neurology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.10720)
- Follow-on phenotyping of GID: Olanow CW et al., "Clinical pattern and risk factors for dyskinesias following fetal nigral transplantation in Parkinson's disease: a double-blind video-based analysis," *Movement Disorders*, 2009 — [Movement Disorders](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.22208)
- Long-term persistence of GID in fetal-graft recipients was documented in *npj Parkinson's Disease* (2021), "Persistent dyskinesias in patients with fetal tissue transplantation for Parkinson disease" — [npj Parkinson's Disease](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-021-00183-w)

**Lewy pathology inside grafts — host-to-graft propagation [HISTORY]**
- Kordower JH, Chu Y, Hauser RA, Freeman TB, Olanow CW. "Lewy body–like pathology in long-term embryonic nigral transplants in Parkinson's disease," *Nature Medicine* **14**:504–506 (2008). **Fourteen years** after striatal transplantation, grafted nigral neurons contained Lewy-body-like inclusions staining positively for **α-synuclein and ubiquitin**, with **reduced dopamine transporter immunostaining** — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm1747)
- Li J-Y et al. (Brundin group), "Lewy bodies in grafted neurons in subjects with Parkinson's disease suggest host-to-graft disease propagation," *Nature Medicine* **14**:501–503 (2008). Two PD subjects with **11–16 years** of graft survival developed α-synuclein-positive Lewy bodies in grafted neurons — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm1746)
- Interpretation at the time: first evidence that PD pathology can propagate **host → graft**, and that PD is an ongoing process affecting grafted striatal cells as it affects host nigral neurons — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm1747); commentary — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/dopaminergic-transplants-stable-prone-parkinsons); [Nature Reviews Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2477)

**TRANSEURO — results published 2025 [2025–2026]**
- Barker RA et al., "The TransEuro open-label trial of human fetal ventral mesencephalic transplantation in patients with moderate Parkinson's disease," ***Nature Biotechnology*** (2025; online May 2025) — [Nature Biotechnology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-025-02567-2); full text — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/); Cure Parkinson's summary — [Cure Parkinson's](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/2025/05/new-findings-from-the-transeuro-cell-replacement-trial-are-now-published/)
- Design: launched 2010; grafting performed **2015–2018**; **11 grafted patients** (Cambridge n=8, Lund n=3) vs **16 contemporaneous non-grafted natural-history controls**, assessed to **36 months** with identical protocols. Ten received sequential bilateral grafts using tissue from **three fetuses per side** — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- Primary outcome (UPDRS III OFF at 36 months): **"no obvious benefit from the hfVM transplants across the grafted cohort,"** either versus baseline or versus the non-grafted control group — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- PET: mean [¹⁸F]fluorodopa uptake improved modestly at 18 months in **7 individuals**, but was restored to near-normal in only **one**. Grafted group moved from **47.6% → 50.8%** of normal, while controls declined **44.7% → 39.9%** — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- GID: **3 patients** developed mild, non-disabling OFF-state dyskinesias — lower than the historical **15–54%** range, attributed in part to efforts to minimise serotonergic neuron contamination, but not eliminated — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- Immunosuppression: **triple therapy for 12 months after the final transplant** — cyclosporin (titrated to trough 100–200 ng/ml), azathioprine 2 mg/kg/day, prednisolone 40 mg tapered to 5 mg over 12 weeks, plus co-trimoxazole, omeprazole and bisphosphonate prophylaxis — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- Tissue supply failure: **21 transplants performed but 86 cancelled** for insufficient tissue; only **11 of 36 recruited patients** were ultimately grafted — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/); [Cure Parkinson's](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/2025/05/new-findings-from-the-transeuro-cell-replacement-trial-are-now-published/)
- Device/site effect: Lund patients grafted with the **original Rehncrona–Legradi device** did better clinically than Cambridge patients grafted with newly manufactured copies (TRN3/TRN4), despite identical tissue preparation — the authors conclude "surgical and device-related issues can significantly influence the outcome" — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- Authors' conclusion: hfVM transplantation "is not a clinically feasible therapeutic strategy for patients with PD." Design principles they carry forward to stem-cell trials: **long run-in periods, PET imaging to monitor dopamine cell survival, contemporaneous natural-history controls, and sufficient cell dose/survival to restore normal putaminal dopaminergic innervation**; they recommend standardised, batch-manufactured, QC'd stem-cell-derived dopamine progenitors over fetal tissue — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- 2026 review of the whole cell-replacement field by the same group: Kayhanian S, Barker RA, "Clinical trial highlights: dopamine cell-replacement therapies," *Journal of Parkinson's Disease* (2026) — [SAGE/JPD](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1877718X251397277) *(abstract page returned HTTP 403 on fetch; cited here as an existing, directly relevant review the writer may want)*

### Inferences
- The Olanow 2003 dose stratification (1 vs 4 donors/side) plus the TRANSEURO dose/survival shortfall together imply the fetal era was probably **under-dosed relative to the reinnervation target**, which is exactly why the stem-cell programs are now running explicit dose-escalation (and why BlueRock chose its *high* dose for Phase 3).
- GID in the fetal era was plausibly multifactorial (serotonergic contamination, patchy/uneven graft distribution, graft placement). TRANSEURO's 3/11 mild-only rate and the **zero GID reported across bemdaneprocel, Kyoto iPSC, STEM-PD and Aspen** suggest defined, purified progenitor products have largely solved this specific problem — the single most important de-risking event between the fetal and stem-cell eras.
- Kordower/Li 2008 imply a finite graft lifespan under ongoing synucleinopathy. Since grafts took 11–16 years to show inclusions, this is a ceiling on durability rather than a near-term obstacle — but it is the strongest argument that cell replacement is **symptomatic restoration, not disease modification**, and may need pairing with an anti-synuclein agent.

## Q2. Stem-cell-derived dopaminergic neurons: GForce-PD and the global trial landscape

### Takeaway
2025–2026 is the inflection point: five independent first-in-human trials (BlueRock/Bayer, Kyoto/Sumitomo, STEM-PD, Aspen, S.Biomedics, plus a Chinese hESC program) all reported acceptable safety, **no tumour formation, and essentially no graft-induced dyskinesia**, with PET-confirmed engraftment; Japan granted the world's first conditional approval of an iPSC-derived dopaminergic product (**AMCHEPRY/raguneprocel, 6 March 2026**), and BlueRock's **bemdaneprocel** skipped Phase 2 to run a sham-controlled registrational Phase 3 (**exPDite-2**, first patient dosed August 2025, placebo-controlled readout expected ~March 2027). Open questions are now efficacy magnitude under sham control, dose, and whether autologous (no immunosuppression) beats allogeneic (off-the-shelf).

### Inferences
- **Safety is now largely de-risked at the Phase 1 level** across six independent products, four cell sources (allogeneic hESC, allogeneic iPSC, autologous iPSC, fetal), and four jurisdictions: zero tumours, zero severe GID, no graft-attributable SAEs. The remaining safety unknown is the 5–10 year horizon (graft overgrowth; synuclein propagation into grafts per Kordower/Li 2008).
- **Efficacy magnitude remains unproven** because every readout to date is open-label. The numbers look large (MDS-UPDRS III OFF improvements of 13–18 points in bemdaneprocel and ASPIRO; 2+ hours of good ON time) but open-label surgical trials in PD historically show very large placebo/sham responses — precisely the reason Freed 2001 and Olanow 2003 failed once sham-controlled. **exPDite-2 (readout ~March 2027) is the first sham-controlled test of the stem-cell era and is the single most consequential pending result in the field.**
- Dose-response is inconsistent: bemdaneprocel and TED-A9 showed a clear high-dose advantage, whereas ASPIRO's 12-month data were numerically *better in the low-dose* cohort on MDS-UPDRS III OFF and PDQ-39. With n=4 per cohort, this is most likely noise, but it undercuts any confident dose-response claim.
- **Immunosuppression strategies now cluster into three models**: (1) time-limited allogeneic immunosuppression — 12 months (bemdaneprocel, STEM-PD, TransEuro's triple therapy) or 15 months tacrolimus-based (Kyoto); (2) **autologous, no chronic immunosuppression** (Aspen); (3) HLA-matched/haplobank allogeneic (implied by Japan's iPSC stock strategy, not directly sourced here). Bemdaneprocel's 18- and 36-month data showing persistent F-DOPA uptake after immunosuppression withdrawal is the key evidence that **time-limited immunosuppression suffices** — which, if it holds, favours the cheaper, scalable allogeneic model over Aspen's bespoke autologous one.
- **Plausible approval timing**: Japan already has a conditional approval (March 2026) that converts to full approval only after post-marketing study. For the US/EU, the earliest credible path is bemdaneprocel — Phase 3 placebo portion completing ~March 2027, implying a BLA no earlier than late 2027 and approval realistically **2028–2029**; Aspen's Phase 3 starting "later 2026" and MeiraGTx/AskBio gene therapies are on similar or later clocks. Anything claiming a US cell-therapy approval before 2028 is not supported by the trial timelines in these sources.

## Q3. Gene therapy: trophic factors, enzyme replacement, circuit modulation, and genetically targeted approaches

### Takeaway
The trophic-factor route has a long record of Phase 2 failure (AAV2-neurturin/CERE-120) but is now back with a redesigned delivery paradigm — **AAV2-GDNF (AB-1005)**, which uses MRI-guided convection-enhanced intraputaminal infusion to reach ~63% putaminal coverage and is in sham-controlled Phase 2 (REGENERATE-PD) with RMAT. The circuit-modulation route (**AAV-GAD**, MeiraGTx, subthalamic nucleus) has the most striking randomised numbers in PD gene therapy (18-point MDS-UPDRS III OFF improvement at high dose vs no change in sham/low dose), RMAT, and a Phase 3 said to be imminent. Enzyme/aggregation-targeted genetic approaches are mixed: GBA1 gene therapy (PR001/LY3884961) continues in PD only, and the LRRK2 ASO BIIB094 hit its target (up to 59% CSF LRRK2 reduction) but was **discontinued by Biogen in February 2025**. The overriding technical lesson is that **delivery — coverage, volume, device and target — has determined outcomes more than the transgene**.

### Inferences
- **Sham control is the discriminating variable, not the modality.** CERE-120 and AXO-Lenti-PD both looked good open-label and failed double-blind; AAV-GAD's two sham-controlled trials are therefore a genuine distinction from the open-label cell-therapy numbers in this review. Caveat added 2026-10-16: the 2024 sham arm had n=4, so "no significant change" is not "zero change"; prior PD sham arms all improved; the 18-point high-dose gain (n=5) exceeds typical STN-DBS benefit and is likely a small-sample outlier; source is a company release via trade press with no peer-reviewed publication found.
- The 2008 and 2025 trophic-factor stories converge on **coverage as the dose**: CERE-120's failure is best read as under-distribution, and AB-1005's 63% coverage via CED is the direct experimental test of that hypothesis. REGENERATE-PD is therefore the decisive test of whether GDNF-class trophic support works in PD at all.
- AB-1005's Phase 1b signal was **stability in the mild cohort**, not improvement — a disease-modification framing. That makes its Phase 2 endpoint choice and 45–75 age window consequential, and means a conventional symptomatic endpoint could undersell (or oversell via regression) the effect.
- **Genetically stratified approaches are contracting, not expanding**, in 2025–2026: BIIB094 discontinued despite clean target engagement; LY3884961 dropped in Gaucher and surviving in PD only with a 2031 primary completion. The field's near-term gene-therapy momentum is in **circuit modulation (AAV-GAD)** and **trophic support (AB-1005)**, not in LRRK2/GBA1 — which matters for the report's framing of "precision medicine in PD."
- BIIB094's lowering of CSF LRRK2 **regardless of variant status** means target engagement was never the problem; discontinuation most likely reflected portfolio/strategic reasoning or the unproven link between LRRK2 lowering and clinical benefit — the same "target engagement ≠ efficacy" trap flagged in the LUMA commentary.

### Gaps
- Exact AAV-GAD Phase 2 (n=45) numbers, p-values and the primary publication citation (the historical Phase 2 is LeWitt et al., *Lancet Neurology* 2011 — **not sourced here, verify independently**) could not be confirmed; the MeiraGTx investor page timed out on two fetch attempts.
- No confirmed Phase 3 start date or NCT number for AAV-GAD; sources say "coming months" / "2025" / "Phase 3 ready" without a start confirmation.
- No interim clinical data from PROPEL (PR001) were available — only design and enrollment status.
- No REGENERATE-PD readout date was sourced.
- The *Lancet* 2014 Palfi ProSavin citation details (volume/pages) were not directly verified from the journal page.

## Q4. Neurotechnology beyond DBS

### Takeaway
Spinal cord stimulation for PD gait has one spectacular, well-published single case (Milekovic/Courtine, *Nature Medicine* 2023) built on a closed-loop, activity-dependent neuroprosthesis, and essentially nothing larger; non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation has small randomised sham-controlled gait signals; rTMS has the strongest non-invasive evidence base (multiple meta-analyses supporting high-frequency M1/SMA stimulation for motor, gait, mood and cognition) while tDCS evidence is inconsistent-to-null; and transcranial focused ultrasound is being repurposed from lesioning to **BBB opening for drug/biologic delivery**, with PD trials still at the pilot stage. Adaptive/closed-loop DBS is covered elsewhere in this review.

### Cited Findings

**Spinal cord stimulation / epidural neuroprosthesis**

- Umbrella review: "The effects of transcranial magnetic stimulation in motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease: an overview of systematic reviews with meta-analysis," *Neurological Sciences*, 2025 — evidence from **21 systematic reviews with meta-analyses** indicates **high-frequency stimulation of primary motor cortex and supplementary motor area significantly improves general motor impairment, gait, functional mobility and balance** in PD — [Neurol Sci](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10072-025-08189-5)
- rTMS also improves **cognitive function, depressive symptoms and walking ability** in PD per meta-analysis — [PMC11668114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668114/); further systematic review/meta-analysis of rTMS on motor function, depression and cognition — [ScienceDirect, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590112526000022)

- Transcranial electrical stimulation **combined with dual-task training** across stroke, MCI and PD — [PMC12857068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12857068)

**Adaptive DBS** — cross-reference only; covered in the dedicated DBS section of this review.

### Inferences
- The Courtine spinal neuroprosthesis is **n=1 in humans**. Its importance is conceptual — it shows PD gait/freezing can be addressed at the **spinal** rather than basal-ganglia level, and that closed-loop, activity-dependent timing (not tonic SCS) is what distinguishes it from the largely disappointing earlier tonic-SCS literature. It is years from being a therapy; NCT04956770 is the relevant feasibility step.
- rTMS has the best evidence-to-availability ratio in this section (meta-analytic support, already-approved hardware for other indications), but effect sizes in PD are modest and no source here reports a regulatory approval for a PD indication. tDCS should be presented as **not supported** for PD cognition on current meta-analytic evidence.
- FUS-BBB opening is best framed as **an enabling delivery platform rather than a therapy**: its near-term value is making otherwise non-penetrant agents (the Cerezyme pilot; potentially ASOs, per the 2026 *J Neurochem* review) deliverable to the putamen. That connects it directly to the gene/enzyme-therapy section — notably as a possible rescue path for the GBA1 and LRRK2-ASO approaches whose delivery is the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- **Brain-computer interfaces for PD**: no PD-specific BCI trial or publication was retrievable within budget. Beyond closed-loop DBS (covered elsewhere), I found **no evidence of a BCI program targeting PD**; the writer should either omit this or state explicitly that it remains preclinical/conceptual for PD.

- The FUS BBB-opening figures (300+ patients, 800+ sessions) are cross-indication, not PD-specific.

## Q5. Digital health and AI

### Takeaway
Wearable and smartphone sensing in PD has moved from feasibility to three distinct, validated uses: (1) **prodromal detection** — a week of wrist accelerometry outperformed genetics, lifestyle, blood biochemistry and prodromal symptoms and separated future PD cases up to **7 years before diagnosis** (Schalkamp, *Nature Medicine* 2023), since replicated cross-country; (2) **trial endpoints** — the Roche PD Mobile Application v2 achieved 96.3% adherence in 316 PASADENA participants and detected abnormalities in patients clinically scored as normal, and was used to report prasinezumab effects; (3) **clinical management** — the Personal KinetiGraph is a marketed wrist device with dedicated validation and target-range trials. AI-based diagnosis is demonstrated across modalities (nocturnal breathing via radio waves, retinal OCT, smartphone speech), and AI is now producing chemical matter for PD: iterative structure-based learning yielded α-synuclein aggregation inhibitors **two orders of magnitude** more potent than prior compounds at ~10× faster screening and ~1000× lower cost (Horne, *Nature Chemical Biology* 2024).

**Wearables for prodromal detection**

- Digital measures have crossed from **description to decision-relevance** in two places: Schalkamp's accelerometry outperforming genetics/lifestyle makes passive wrist data the most practical **population-scale prodromal screen** yet described (and the cross-country *Movement Disorders* replication is what moves it from one-cohort curiosity to a method); and the Roche app's detection of abnormality in clinically "normal" items means digital endpoints are **more sensitive than the MDS-UPDRS items they shadow**, which is the whole argument for using them to shorten disease-modification trials.
- The prodromal-detection claim is **not yet a screening programme**: both Schalkamp's accelerometry and Wagner's retinal OCT give ~7-year lead times but operate at population-level discrimination, and Wagner explicitly disclaims individual prediction. With PD's low base rate, positive predictive value is the unresolved barrier — the report should say lead time has been demonstrated while actionable individual risk prediction has not.
- Digital endpoints are, on this evidence, **accepted as exploratory/secondary rather than as approved primary endpoints**: PASADENA's digital readouts are labelled "exploratory digital outcome measures" even in the 2025 *npj Digital Medicine* paper. I found **no source confirming formal regulatory qualification** of a PD digital endpoint.
- The three AI diagnostic modalities (radio-wave breathing, retinal OCT, smartphone speech) share a pattern: each exploits a **non-motor or pre-motor** channel, which is why they achieve pre-diagnostic lead time where motor scales cannot. Their natural use is enrichment of prevention trials rather than diagnosis of established PD.
- Horne 2024 is the most concrete AI-to-molecule result in PD, but it delivers **potency in vitro**, not clinical candidates; its stated advance over "clinical trial molecules" implicitly concedes that the previously clinically tested anti-aggregation compounds were weak — a reason for caution in extrapolating to patient benefit.

## Q6. Realistic timelines and major obstacles by platform (as of 2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Only one platform has crossed a regulatory finish line — Japan's **conditional, time-limited** approval of the iPSC product AMCHEPRY (6 March 2026) on a 7-patient open-label trial, which is as much a statement about Japan's regenerative-medicine framework as about the evidence. Everything else hinges on a small number of sham-controlled readouts clustered in **2027**: exPDite-2 (cell therapy), REGENERATE-PD (AAV2-GDNF), and a not-yet-started AAV-GAD Phase 3. The binding constraints differ by platform: for cells it is **efficacy under sham control plus manufacturing cost**; for gene therapy it is **delivery coverage and irreversibility**; for neurotech it is **evidence volume (n=1 for the most impressive result)**; for digital/AI it is **regulatory endpoint qualification and positive predictive value**.

### Cited Findings
- **Cell therapy, Japan**: approved **6 March 2026**, but **conditional and time-limited**, with mandatory post-marketing clinical study and surveillance required "to obtain full approval within the designated period" — [Sumitomo Pharma](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20260306.html)
- **Cell therapy, US/global**: exPDite-2's placebo-controlled portion is expected to complete **March 2027**, with follow-up to **5 years**; 102 patients, 33 sites — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/bemdaneprocel). Phase 3 data expected **2027** (snippet-only) — [Parkinson's News Today](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/bemdaneprocel-move-phase-3-trial-soon/)
- **Autologous cell therapy**: Aspen's Phase 3 is **planned for later 2026** — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/autologous-cell-therapy-sasineprocel-demonstrates-12-month-safety-engraftment-parkinsons)
- **STEM-PD**: program acquired by a company that "will lead Phase II development," with Fast Track and IND in hand — i.e. still **pre-Phase 2** as of mid-2026 — [Lund University](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/article/stem-pd-announces-first-human-clinical-trial-results-parkinsons-disease-cell-therapy)
- **Gene therapy (GDNF)**: REGENERATE-PD is a sham-controlled Phase 2 of ~87 patients that only began randomising in **January 2025** — [Bayer](https://www.bayer.com/media/en-us/first-participants-randomized-in-askbio-phase-ii-gene-therapy-trial-for-parkinsons-disease/)
- **Gene therapy (GAD)**: Phase 3 "expected to initiate in the coming months" as of 2025, described as "Phase 3 ready" — [MeiraGTx](https://investors.meiragtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/meiragtx-announces-fda-breakthrough-therapy-designation-aav2); [CGTLive](https://www.cgtlive.com/view/evolving-landscape-gene-cell-therapies-parkinson-disease)
- **Gene therapy (GBA1)**: PROPEL primary completion estimated **31 March 2031** — [NCT04127578](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04127578)
- **Gene therapy (LRRK2 ASO)**: discontinued by Biogen **12 February 2025** despite target engagement — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/ion859)
- **Obstacle — tissue/manufacturing scalability**: TRANSEURO's **86 cancelled transplants** for lack of tissue is the canonical demonstration that a non-manufacturable cell source cannot support a therapy — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- **Obstacle — surgical/device variability**: TRANSEURO's Lund-vs-Cambridge device difference shows **"surgical and device-related issues can significantly influence the outcome"** — a direct risk for the 33-site exPDite-2 — [PMC12807853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807853/)
- **Obstacle — sham-controlled efficacy**: Freed 2001 and Olanow 2003 both failed their primary endpoints despite biological engraftment and increased fluorodopa uptake — [Annals of Neurology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.10720); CERE-120's double-blind trial showed both arms improving ~7 points on UPDRS motor OFF — [AAV2-neurturin program review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233890584_AAV2-neurturin_CERE-120_for_Parkinson's_disease); AXO-Lenti-PD failed Phase 2 after promising open-label data — [Parkinson's News Today](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/sio-gene-therapies-oxford-biomedica-therapy-candidate-axo-lenti-pd-parkinsons/)
- **Obstacle — AAV safety surveillance**: the VY-AADC clinical hold followed **MRI abnormalities** in RESTORE-1 participants — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/voyager-shares-fall-after-fda-places-a-clinical-hold-on-phase-ii-gene-therapy-trial-for-parkinson-s-disease)
- **Obstacle — delivery/coverage**: AB-1005 achieved only **63% ± 2%** putaminal coverage even with MRI-monitored CED — [MDS Abstracts](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/preliminary-efficacy-of-bilateral-intraputaminal-delivery-of-gdnf-gene-therapy-aav2-gdnf-ab-1005-in-parkinsons-disease-18-month-follow-up-from-a-phase-1b-study/)

### Inferences
- **Most defensible timeline statement for the report**: Japan has a conditional iPSC approval *now* (March 2026); the earliest plausible US/EU approval of any PD cell or gene therapy is **2028–2029**, gated by exPDite-2 (placebo portion to March 2027) and REGENERATE-PD; GBA1 gene therapy is a **2030s** proposition on its own registered completion date; LRRK2-directed therapy currently has **no sponsor-backed clinical path** in either ASO or (pending LUMA verification) small-molecule form.
- The field's single greatest unresolved risk is that the **2025–2026 open-label cell-therapy results recapitulate the 1990s pattern** — biology confirmed, sham-controlled benefit absent. The explicit historical precedents (Freed, Olanow, CERE-120, AXO-Lenti-PD) are four independent instances of exactly that failure mode in PD surgical trials, which is why the report should present current efficacy numbers as *promising but unvalidated*.
- Conversely, three things genuinely changed versus the fetal era, and all are now evidenced: **a manufacturable, QC'd cell supply** (vs 86 cancelled TRANSEURO surgeries); **near-elimination of graft-induced dyskinesia** across all modern products; and **time-limited rather than indefinite immunosuppression**, supported by bemdaneprocel's persistent PET signal 24 months after immunosuppression withdrawal.
- The two leading gene therapies are betting on opposite theories of PD — **AB-1005 on trophic rescue/disease modification** (its Phase 1b signal was *stability*, in mild patients) and **AAV-GAD on circuit normalisation** (large symptomatic motor gains at high dose, plus FDG-PET circuit claims). They are not interchangeable and should not be aggregated in the report.
- Japan's conditional approval on n=7 open-label data creates a real near-term equity problem: a licensed iPSC product in one jurisdiction while the same class remains investigational elsewhere invites medical tourism and unproven-clinic marketing — a risk the Medscape "reality check" framing suggests the clinical community is already discussing, though I could not read that piece.

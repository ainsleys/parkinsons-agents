## Key Question 1: Exercise as a potential disease modifier

### Takeaway
Two Phase 2 RCTs (SPARX 2018; Park-in-Shape 2019) showed that high-intensity/aerobic exercise attenuated motor-score worsening over 6 months in early PD, and imaging substudies (Johansson 2022; Sacheli 2019) show plausible brain-level mechanisms, but no Phase 3 trial has yet proven disease modification. SPARX3 (n=370, high vs moderate intensity treadmill) finished its intervention phase around mid-2025 and was "active, not recruiting" as of July 2026 with no public topline results found. Meanwhile the 2023 Cochrane network meta-analysis and the 3.5-year tai chi cohort (JNNP 2024) support broad symptomatic benefit across exercise types, with exercise type being secondary to doing it at all.

### Cited Findings

Landmark Phase 2 trials
- SPARX (Schenkman M, et al. "Effect of High-Intensity Treadmill Exercise on Motor Symptoms in Patients With De Novo Parkinson Disease: A Phase 2 Randomized Clinical Trial." JAMA Neurology 2018;75(2):219-226). Phase 2 futility design in de novo, unmedicated PD comparing high-intensity (80-85% HRmax) vs moderate-intensity (60-65% HRmax) treadmill exercise vs usual care over 6 months; high-intensity exercise was deemed non-futile and safe, motivating SPARX3. — [DOI 10.1001/jamaneurol.2017.3517](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2017.3517) [DOI from prior knowledge; details not re-verified in-session]. APDA's lay summary of Phase 2: "over the six months of the trial, the control group had a slight worsening of their PD symptoms, whereas the high intensity exercise group remained stable." — [APDA](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/sparx3-trial-researches-whether-exercise-can-slow-parkinsons-progression/) [verified in-session]
- Park-in-Shape (van der Kolk NM, et al. "Effectiveness of home-based and remotely supervised aerobic exercise in Parkinson's disease: a double-blind, randomised controlled trial." Lancet Neurology 2019;18(11):998-1008). Home-based stationary cycling with exergaming 3x/week for 6 months vs stretching (active control) in 130 patients on medication; aerobic group showed smaller off-state MDS-UPDRS motor worsening (between-group difference ~4.2 points). — [DOI 10.1016/S1474-4422(19)30285-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(19)30285-6) [DOI from prior knowledge; effect size from prior knowledge, not re-verified]. Trial design paper: [Park-in-Shape design, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4418070/) [verified in-session]

SPARX3 Phase 3 (status 2025-2026)
- Design: "a Phase 3 clinical trial designed to determine the efficacy of high-intensity, endurance treadmill exercise to slow the progression of PD as measured by the MDS-UPDRS motor score"; treadmill 4 days/week, 30 min, moderate (60-65% HRmax) vs high intensity (80-85% HRmax), 370 participants randomized, followed up to 24 months; primary analysis = change in MDS-UPDRS Part III over 12 months "or until initiation of symptomatic antiparkinsonian treatment if before 12 months." Secondary outcomes: striatal DAT binding, 6-min walk, daily steps, cognition, fitness, QoL, time to dopaminergic medication, CRP, BDNF. — Patterson CG, et al. Trials 2022;23:855. [Trials](https://trialsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13063-022-06703-0); [PubMed 36203214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36203214/) [verified in-session]
- Enrollment of de novo, unmedicated PD; launched early 2021; "Study completion is expected in mid-2025"; hypothesis per Jay Alberts (Cleveland Clinic): "higher-intensity exercise will yield greater neuroprotection and slower progression." — [Cleveland Clinic Consult QD](https://consultqd.clevelandclinic.org/sparx3-intensifies-efforts-toward-a-specific-exercise-prescription-in-parkinsons-disease) [verified in-session]
- ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04284436 status: "active, not recruiting," last update posted 2026-07-10. No topline results located in any search. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04284436) [verified in-session]
- Notable design feature: SPARX3 has no non-exercise control arm (both arms exercise), so it tests an intensity dose-response rather than exercise vs none. — [Davis Phinney Foundation](https://davisphinneyfoundation.org/blog/sparx3/) [verified in-session]

Neuroimaging and mechanistic evidence
- Johansson ME, et al. "Aerobic Exercise Alters Brain Function and Structure in Parkinson's Disease: A Randomized Controlled Trial." Annals of Neurology 2022;91(2):203-216 (note: Annals of Neurology, NOT npj Parkinson's Disease as the assignment brief states). Multimodal MRI substudy of Park-in-Shape: aerobic exercise "increased cognitive control, functional connectivity of the anterior putamen with the sensorimotor cortex, right fronto-parietal network, and reduced global brain atrophy, possibly stabilizing disease progression"; aerobic group also had greater improvement in error rates on a cognitive-control task vs stretching. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.26291); [Parkinson's Foundation summary](https://www.parkinson.org/blog/science-news/aerobic-exercise) [verified in-session]
- Sacheli MA, et al. "Exercise increases caudate dopamine release and ventral striatal activation in Parkinson's disease." Movement Disorders 2019;34(12):1891-1900. 35 participants randomized to 36-session aerobic exercise vs control; aerobic group showed increased rTMS-evoked dopamine release in caudate (PET) and increased baseline nondisplaceable binding potential in posterior putamen; ventral striatal responsivity altered on fMRI. — [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27865) [verified in-session]
- Hirsch MA, et al. "Exercise-induced increase in brain-derived neurotrophic factor in human Parkinson's disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis." Translational Neurodegeneration 2018;7:7. — [BMC](https://translationalneurodegeneration.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40035-018-0112-1) [verified in-session]
- 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of exercise on BDNF and clinical outcomes in PD: pooled 5 trials (N=216), SMD 1.20 for BDNF favoring exercise; significant improvements in UPDRS, UPDRS-III, 6MWT, Berg Balance; BDNF increases correlated with motor improvement (MD -5.53 on UPDRS motor). — [PMC10968162](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10968162/) [verified in-session]
- Mechanistic review: "New perspectives on molecular mechanisms underlying exercise-induced benefits in Parkinson's disease." npj Parkinson's Disease 2025. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01113-w) [verified in-session, title only]
- Editorial: "Exercise and neuroplasticity in Parkinson disease" (2025). — [PMC11973092](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11973092/) [verified in-session, title only]
- Petzinger GM, Fisher BE, et al. "Exercise-enhanced neuroplasticity targeting motor and cognitive circuitry in Parkinson's disease." Lancet Neurology 2013;12(7):716-726 — the canonical review of the Fisher/Petzinger (USC) program linking intensive treadmill training to striatal D2 receptor changes and corticostriatal plasticity. — [DOI 10.1016/S1474-4422(13)70123-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(13)70123-6) [DOI from prior knowledge; not re-verified in-session]

Comparative effectiveness across exercise types
- Ernst M, Folkerts A-K, Gollan R, Lieker E, Caro-Valenzuela J, Adams A, Cryns N, Monsef I, Dresen A, Roheger M, Eggers C, Skoetz N, Kalbe E. "Physical exercise for people with Parkinson's disease: a systematic review and network meta-analysis." Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2023, Issue 1, CD013856 (PMID 36602886; DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD013856.pub2). Authors' stated conclusion: the review "highlights the importance of physical exercise regarding severity of motor signs and quality of life, while the exact exercise type might be secondary." — [Cochrane/Altmetric](https://cochrane.altmetric.com/details/140878547); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366903408_Physical_exercise_for_people_with_Parkinson's_disease_a_systematic_review_and_network_meta-analysis) [verified in-session]. From prior knowledge (not re-verified; publisher pages blocked): the review included 156 RCTs (~7,900 participants); dance, aqua-based training, gait/balance/functional training and multi-domain training had moderate-certainty evidence for reducing motor signs, and aqua-based and endurance training for improving QoL; most comparisons between exercise types showed small, imprecise differences. An associated 2024 publication (PMID 38588457) appears to be a journal-length report of the same NMA. — [PubMed 38588457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38588457/) [verified link only]
- Subsequent Bayesian dose-response NMAs (2025) on optimal exercise dose/type for walking velocity and motor symptoms. — [Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-85456-7); [J Sci Med Sport 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1440244025000039) [verified in-session, titles only]

Tai chi
- Li F, et al. "Tai Chi and Postural Stability in Patients with Parkinson's Disease." NEJM 2012;366:511-519. 195 patients randomized to tai chi, resistance training, or stretching (24 weeks); tai chi outperformed stretching and resistance on postural stability measures and reduced falls. — [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1107911) [verified link; details from prior knowledge]
- Li G, et al. "Effect of long-term Tai Chi training on Parkinson's disease: a 3.5-year follow-up cohort study." JNNP 2024;95:222-228 (online Oct 2023). Disease progression slower in tai chi group on all three scales; fewer patients needed medication increases (comparison group 83.5% in 2019 and ~96% in 2020 vs 71% and 87.5% in tai chi group); cognitive decline slower; complications lower (dyskinesia 1.4% vs 7.5%; dystonia 0% vs 1.6%; hallucinations 0% vs ~2%; MCI 3% vs 10%; RLS 7% vs 15.5%). Non-randomized cohort design is a key limitation. — [BMJ Group press release](https://bmjgroup.com/tai-chi-may-curb-parkinsons-disease-symptoms-and-complications-for-several-years/); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20231025/Tai-Chi-shows-long-term-benefits-for-people-with-Parkinsons.aspx) [verified in-session]

Boxing (Rock Steady Boxing) and dance
- Combs SA, et al. "Community-based group exercise for persons with Parkinson disease: a randomized controlled trial" (2013) — boxing group improved gait velocity and endurance, but traditional exercise group had greater gains in balance confidence. — [PubMed 23422464](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23422464/) [verified in-session]
- Double-blinded RCT, Boxing vs Sensory Exercise (PD SAFEx): sensory group improved more than boxing on UPDRS motor; at washout the Rock Steady Boxing group showed worsening of motor symptoms vs pre-assessment while PD SAFEx improved. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352380713_Boxing_vs_Sensory_Exercise_for_Parkinson's_Disease_A_Double-Blinded_Randomized_Controlled_Trial); [MDS Abstracts](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/are-effects-of-rock-steady-boxing-and-pd-safextm-on-the-motor-symptoms-of-parkinsons-disease-maintained-long-term-a-single-blinded-randomized-controlled-trial/) [verified in-session]
- Systematic review/meta-analysis of boxing in older people with PD (2025) and qualitative evaluation of Rock Steady Boxing (PLOS ONE 2024: all participants described functional improvements, gait most reported). — [PMC12179875](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12179875/); [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0309522) [verified in-session]
- APDA assessment: "There is some limited clinical trial evidence that Rock Steady Boxing is helpful in PD, however it has not been shown to be better than traditional exercise modalities." — [APDA](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/boxing-for-parkinsons-disease/) [verified in-session]
- Dance: rated moderate-certainty for motor signs in the Cochrane 2023 NMA (see above).

Observational "dose" data
- Parkinson's Outcomes Project: among 2,940 people followed 2 years, exercising at least 2.5 hours/week was associated with better HRQoL and slower decline; those who start earlier see slower QoL decline. — [Parkinson's Foundation Exercise](https://www.parkinson.org/living-with-parkinsons/treatment/exercise); [Key Findings](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/parkinsons-outcomes-project/findings) [verified in-session]

### Inferences
- The field is in a "proof-of-concept achieved, Phase 3 pending" state: SPARX and Park-in-Shape both showed ~3-4 point attenuation of motor-score worsening over 6 months, but neither can distinguish symptomatic from disease-modifying effects; SPARX3's DAT-SPECT secondary outcome is the first attempt to do so at scale.
- Because SPARX3 compares two exercise intensities (no sedentary control), a null result would mean intensity does not matter above moderate, not that exercise does not work; a positive result would establish a dose-response signal.
- Convergent evidence from the Cochrane NMA and head-to-head trials (boxing vs sensory, tai chi vs resistance) suggests type-specific superiority claims are weak; adherence, intensity, and sustainability likely matter more than modality.

### Gaps
- SPARX3 results: not publicly available as of the searches run (October 2026); trial listed as "active, not recruiting" with last update July 2026.
- Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI changes after exercise: no source located this session.
- The Dutch "Exercise as medicine" national program: no specific source located; the closest found was Oosterhof TH, Darweesh SKL, Bloem BR, de Vries NM. "Considerations on How to Prevent Parkinson's Disease Through Exercise." J Parkinson's Disease 2024 — [DOI 10.3233/JPD-240091](https://doi.org/10.3233/jpd-240091) [verified link].
- Exact Cochrane NMA numbers (study count, SMDs, certainty by modality) could not be retrieved because PubMed, Cochrane Library and Europe PMC blocked fetches.

## Key Question 2: Rehabilitation therapies (physiotherapy, speech, occupational therapy, cueing, falls, dysphagia)

### Takeaway
Dutch trials and the ParkinsonNet network have produced the strongest health-services evidence for allied health in PD: specialised physiotherapy reduced complications and costs (Ypinga 2018), home-based OT improved self-perceived daily performance (Sturkenboom 2014), and amplitude-based programs (LSVT LOUD/BIG) have RCT support. Evidence for cueing, falls prevention and dysphagia exists but was not retrievable in detail this session.

### Cited Findings

Physiotherapy and ParkinsonNet
- Munneke M, et al. "Efficacy of community-based physiotherapy networks for patients with Parkinson's disease: a cluster-randomised trial." Lancet Neurology 2010;9:46-54 — the original ParkinsonNet cluster RCT (16 regions), which found no improvement in patient outcomes but lower costs with specialised networks. — [Lancet Neurology](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(09)70327-8/abstract) [verified link; result summary from prior knowledge]
- Ypinga JHL, de Vries NM, Boonen LHHM, Koolman X, Munneke M, Zwinderman AH, Bloem BR. "Effectiveness and costs of specialised physiotherapy given via ParkinsonNet: a retrospective analysis of medical claims data." Lancet Neurology 2018;17(2):153-161. Claims data (~4,381 patients): PD-related complications (hospital visit/admission for fracture, orthopaedic injury, pneumonia) in 17% of patients treated by specialised vs 21% by usual-care physiotherapists; specialised therapists had higher caseload (3.89 vs 1.48 patients/therapist) and fewer sessions (33.72 vs 47.97); physiotherapy costs ~EUR 400/yr lower and total healthcare costs ~EUR 530/yr lower. — [Lancet Neurology](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(17)30406-4/abstract); [PMC commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5836412/) [verified in-session]
- "Effects of specialised physiotherapy on mortality in Parkinson's disease: a prospective observational study." npj Parkinson's Disease 2025 — extends ParkinsonNet outcomes to mortality. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01069-x); [PMC12264191](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264191) [verified link only; content paywalled during fetch]
- Keus SHJ, Munneke M, Graziano M, et al. "European Physiotherapy Guideline for Parkinson's Disease." KNGF/ParkinsonNet, 2014 — the reference guideline (developed with 19 European professional associations) underpinning ParkinsonNet practice. — [ParkinsonNet](https://www.parkinsonnet.nl) [citation from prior knowledge; not re-verified in-session]

LSVT BIG and LSVT LOUD
- Ebersbach G, et al. (2010, Movement Disorders) — first RCT of LSVT BIG vs Nordic walking vs home exercise showing improved motor function (UPDRS-III) with BIG. — [LSVT Global reference list](https://blog.lsvtglobal.com/research/big-reference/) [verified in-session via secondary source]
- LSVT-BIG physiological evidence for proprioceptive recalibration: proprioceptive performance improved after 4 weeks and further after 8 weeks. — [PMC7353788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7353788/) [verified in-session]
- Ramig LO, et al. "Speech treatment in Parkinson's disease: Randomized controlled trial (RCT)." Movement Disorders 2018;33(11):1777-1791 — LSVT LOUD vs LSVT ARTIC vs no treatment; LOUD produced significant immediate and long-term (24-month in earlier work) increases in sound pressure level. — [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27460); [APDA on LSVT](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/lsvt-and-parkinsons-disease/) [verified in-session]

Occupational therapy
- Sturkenboom IHWM, Graff MJL, Hendriks JCM, Veenhuizen Y, Munneke M, Bloem BR, Nijhuis-van der Sanden MW, OTiP study group. "Efficacy of occupational therapy for patients with Parkinson's disease: a randomised controlled trial." Lancet Neurology 2014;13(6):557-566. Home-based, individualised OT (10 weeks) vs usual care in 191 patients; improved self-perceived performance in daily activities (COPM) at 3 months. — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261607454_Efficacy_of_occupational_therapy_for_patients_with_Parkinson's_disease_A_randomised_controlled_trial); accompanying editorial "Occupational therapy for Parkinson's disease: increasing awareness" [Lancet Neurology](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(14)70074-2/abstract) [verified in-session; n=191 from prior knowledge]
- Process evaluation of OTiP (Sturkenboom 2016, Clinical Rehabilitation). — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0269215515622038) [verified link]
- Counterpoint: a later trial of low-intensity, goal-directed OT and physiotherapy did not produce clinically meaningful differences in activities for mild-moderate PD (PD REHAB, Clarke et al., JAMA Neurology 2016; summarized in Australian OT Journal). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1440-1630.12470) [verified link]

### Inferences
- The Dutch evidence base shows that the *organisation* of allied health (expertise concentration, high caseloads, guideline adherence) can deliver cost savings and complication reductions even where individual-level RCTs (Munneke 2010; PD REHAB) show small or null effects on disability scales.
- Amplitude-based retraining (LSVT) is the most consistently RCT-supported rehabilitation paradigm for both speech and limb movement.

### Gaps
- Cueing for freezing of gait (e.g., Nieuwboer et al., RESCUE trial, JNNP 2007), fall-prevention RCTs (e.g., Canning et al., Neurology 2015), SpeechVive device trials, and dysphagia management (e.g., expiratory muscle strength training; Troche 2010) were not retrievable this session due to exhausted search budget; cite with caution.
- Full text of Keus 2014 guideline not fetched.

## Key Question 3: Nutrition and diet

### Takeaway
Observational data link MIND/Mediterranean diet adherence to later PD onset (up to 17 years in women; Metcalfe-Roach 2021) and low-fat dairy to modestly higher risk (HR 1.34; Hughes 2017), while early weight loss is a robust poor-prognosis marker (Cumming 2017). Interventional diet evidence is limited to small pilots (ketogenic, Phillips 2018; vitamin D RCTs with mixed results); no diet has RCT evidence for disease modification.

### Cited Findings
- Agarwal P, et al. "MIND diet associated with reduced incidence and delayed progression of parkinsonism in old age." Journal of Nutrition, Health & Aging 2018;22(10):1211-1215 — higher MIND adherence correlated with reduced incidence and slower progression of parkinsonian signs in the Rush Memory and Aging Project. — [DOI 10.1007/s12603-018-1094-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12603-018-1094-5) [DOI from prior knowledge]; finding confirmed via [AJMC summary](https://www.ajmc.com/view/study-finds-that-diet-may-delay-onset-of-parkinson-disease) [verified in-session]
- Metcalfe-Roach A, Yu AC, Golz E, Cirstea M, Sundvick K, Kliger D, Foulger LH, Mackenzie M, Finlay BB, Appel-Cresswell S. "MIND and Mediterranean Diets Associated with Later Onset of Parkinson's Disease." Movement Disorders 2021;36(4):977-984. 167 PD and 119 controls; MIND adherence correlated most strongly with later onset in women (up to 17.4 years between low and high tertiles); Greek Mediterranean adherence also associated with later onset (~8 years in men). — [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.28464); [PMC8248352](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8248352/) [verified in-session]
- Hughes KC, Gao X, Kim IY, et al. "Intake of dairy foods and risk of Parkinson disease." Neurology 2017;89(1):46-52. Nurses' Health Study (80,736 women) and Health Professionals Follow-up Study (48,610 men), ~25 years, 1,036 incident PD; >=3 servings/day low-fat dairy vs <1: HR 1.34 (95% CI 1.01-1.79); "modest increased risk." — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000004057); [PubMed 28596209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28596209/) [verified in-session]
- Mendelian randomization of wide-ranging food intake and PD (Scientific Reports 2025) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-85668-x) [verified link only]
- Phillips MCL, Murtagh DKJ, Gilbertson LJ, Asztely FJS, Lynch CDP. "Low-fat versus ketogenic diet in Parkinson's disease: A pilot randomized controlled trial." Movement Disorders 2018;33(8):1306-1314. 47 randomized, 44 started, 38 completed (86%); both groups improved MDS-UPDRS; ketogenic group improved Part I (non-motor) more (-4.58 +/- 2.17, 41%) vs low-fat (-0.99 +/- 3.63, 11%). — [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27390) [verified in-session]
- Ongoing: "Ketogenic Diet Interventions in Parkinson's Disease: Safeguarding the Gut Microbiome" (NCT05469997); MCT nutritional ketosis trial (NCT04584346); 2024 longitudinal keto study (Journal of Neurology/Future Medicine). — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05469997); [Taylor & Francis 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17582024.2024.2352394) [verified links]
- Cumming K, Macleod AD, Myint PK, Counsell CE. "Early weight loss in parkinsonism predicts poor outcomes: Evidence from an incident cohort study." Neurology 2017;89(22):2254-2261. PINE cohort: 515 participants (240 controls, 187 PD, 88 atypical parkinsonism), median 5-year follow-up; PD patients lost weight faster than controls; weight loss within 1 year of diagnosis independently associated with dependency, dementia and death. — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000004691) [verified in-session]
- 2025 review of weight loss mechanisms in PD (Movement Disorders) and a 9-year population-based study of weight-loss risk factors (Parkinsonism & Related Disorders 2024). — [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70258); [PRD](https://www.prd-journal.com/article/S1353-8020(24)01193-3/fulltext) [verified links]
- Vitamin D: Suzuki M, et al. randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 114 PD patients, vitamin D3 1200 IU/day for 12 months "significantly prevented the deterioration of the Hoehn and Yahr stage" (American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 2013). — [PubMed 23485413](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23485413/) [verified in-session]. Pilot RCT on balance (PLOS ONE 2018) and a 2025 meta-analysis: supplemental vitamin D did not reduce UPDRS-III, walk-test or TUG times, but improved 6-minute walk distance. — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0203637); [PMC12183021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12183021/) [verified in-session]
- Parkinson's Foundation: vitamin D levels linked to falls and some non-motor symptoms (observational). — [Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/blog/science-news/vitamin-d) [verified link]

### Inferences
- Diet-onset associations (MIND/Mediterranean) are retrospective and vulnerable to reverse causation (prodromal PD alters appetite, smell and gut function); they justify prospective trials but not causal claims.
- Weight loss is best read as a prognostic biomarker and a target for proactive dietetic input rather than a modifiable cause.

### Gaps
- Protein redistribution for levodopa absorption, coffee/caffeine epidemiology (e.g., Ross et al., JAMA 2000), gut microbiome/dietary fiber interventions, and harms of unproven supplements were not retrievable this session (search budget exhausted); these are well-established topics but no URL could be verified.

## Key Question 4: Models of care and health services

### Takeaway
Integrated/multidisciplinary care trials show modest or inconsistent patient-level benefits (IMPACT 2013 null after adjustment; Kluger 2020 palliative RCT positive), telemedicine is feasible and non-inferior but not superior (Connect.Parkinson 2017), and health-services data (ParkinsonNet) show cost savings. Hospitalization remains hazardous due to medication-timing errors, and WHO (2022) frames PD as a public-health priority with large global access gaps.

### Cited Findings

Multidisciplinary/integrated care
- van der Marck MA, et al. "Integrated multidisciplinary care in Parkinson's disease: a non-randomised, controlled trial (IMPACT)." Lancet Neurology 2013;12(10):947-956. Dutch community comparison of integrated care vs usual care: "small benefits were achieved in disability scores and quality of life, which had disappeared when corrected for baseline disease severity." — [Lancet Neurology editorial "Challenges of multidisciplinary care in Parkinson's disease"](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS1474-4422(13)70145-5/abstract); [Minerva EBP summary](https://minerva-ebp.be/nl/article/6) [verified in-session]
- Rajan R, et al. "Integrated Care in Parkinson's Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." Movement Disorders 2020. — [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.28097) [verified link only]
- German "Parkinson's Disease Multimodal Complex Treatment" improves motor symptoms, depression and QoL (J Neurol 2020); patient-centred integrated care RCT improving QoL (J Neurol 2018). — [Springer 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-019-09657-7); [Springer 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-018-8761-7) [verified links]
- PRIME-NL: prospective observational evaluation of proactive, integrated, patient-centred Parkinson care in the Netherlands (protocol). — [PMC8298196](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8298196/) [verified link]

Telemedicine
- Beck CA, Beran DB, Biglan KM, et al. "National randomized controlled trial of virtual house calls for Parkinson disease." Neurology 2017;89(11):1152-1161 (Connect.Parkinson). 927 interested, 210 enrolled, 195 randomized; usual care vs usual care + 4 video visits over 1 year; 98% of intervention group completed >=1 virtual visit and 91% of 388 visits completed; PDQ-39 did not improve (0.3 points worse; p=0.78), nor did quality of care or caregiver burden; each visit saved median 88 minutes and 38 miles; 97% patient and 86% neurologist satisfaction; 55% preferred virtual. Conclusion: feasible and "neither more nor less efficacious than usual in-person care." — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000004357); [PubMed 28814455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28814455) [verified in-session]

PD nurse specialist
- NICE-PD (Netherlands): 18-month single-blind RCT in 8 community hospitals, 240 patients not seen by a PD nurse specialist in prior 2 years, 1:1 to PDNS care (per guideline) vs usual care; co-primary outcomes PDQ-39 and MDS-UPDRS III; secondary: non-motor symptoms, quality of care, self-management, adherence, caregiver burden, coping. Protocol: Trials 2020; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03830190 last updated Sept 1, 2025. Results not located. — [Trials protocol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13063-019-3926-y); [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03830190) [verified in-session]
- Qualitative grounded-theory study on the value of the PD nurse specialist to patients and care partners (BMC Nursing 2024). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12912-024-02441-7) [verified link]

Palliative care and advance care planning
- Kluger BM, Miyasaki J, Katz M, et al. "Comparison of Integrated Outpatient Palliative Care With Standard Care in Patients With Parkinson Disease and Related Disorders: A Randomized Clinical Trial." JAMA Neurology 2020;77(5):551-560. 210 patients and 175 caregivers (143 spouses); integrated outpatient palliative care "improved quality of life, symptom burden, caregiver distress, and advance directive completion." — [JAMA Neurology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2760511); [Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/blog/science-news/palliative-care) [verified in-session]
- Kluger BM, Katz M, Galifianakis NB, et al. "Patient and Family Outcomes of Community Neurologist Palliative Education and Telehealth Support in Parkinson Disease." JAMA Neurology 2024;81(1):39-49 (online Nov 13, 2023). Pragmatic stepped-wedge trial, 19 community neurology practices (CA, CO, WY), 359 patients (mean age 74.0, 64.9% male) and 300 caregivers; neurologist palliative education (adapted EPEC) plus telehealth team-based palliative care. QOL-AD at 6 months: +1.27 points (95% CI 0.30-2.25; P=.01); Zarit Burden: no difference (0.80; 95% CI -0.60 to 2.21; P=.26); advance directive completion 50% vs 19% (P=.008). — [JAMA Neurology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2811972) [verified in-session]

Hospitalization and medication timing
- The "Get It On Time" campaign of Parkinson's societies internationally is underpinned by the need for the right medication at the right time in hospitals and care homes. — [PMC2957227 (New Zealand study)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2957227/) [verified in-session]
- Unplanned admissions study (2022): time-critical medications correctly prescribed on admission in only 50% of admissions; timing errors most common (31.6%). — [PMC8974884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8974884/); [PubMed 35402654](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35402654) [verified in-session]
- 90% of PD inpatients experienced incorrect administration (8.3% of doses omitted; 9.1% early/late). — [Parkinsonism & Related Disorders](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1353802011004329) [verified in-session]
- Missed/delayed doses and neuroleptic use prolong stay: patients with delayed/missed doses stayed 8.2 days (SD 8.9) vs 3.6 days (SD 3.4); average 2.7 doses/patient missed or delayed in 48 h (35% of 152 doses). — [PLOS ONE 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0124356) [verified in-session]
- Quality-improvement project reducing delays in time-critical PD medications (2025). — [PMC12863335](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12863335/) [verified link]

Parkinson's Foundation Centers of Excellence / Parkinson's Outcomes Project
- Parkinson's Outcomes Project: 13,000+ participants across five countries at Centers of Excellence, the largest clinical study of PD; key findings: depression and anxiety are "the number one factors impacting the overall health of people with Parkinson's"; >=2.5 h/week exercise associated with better HRQoL and slower decline. — [Parkinson's Foundation Key Findings](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/parkinsons-outcomes-project/findings); [Project overview](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/parkinsons-outcomes-project) [verified in-session]
- Exercise class participation at Centers of Excellence associated with self-reported exercise (2022). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590112522000081) [verified link]

Global access and WHO
- WHO. "Parkinson disease: a public health approach. Technical brief." June 2022. Prevalence doubled in 25 years; >8.5 million living with PD in 2019; "disability and death due to PD are increasing faster than for any other neurological disorder"; 43 key actions in seven areas (global health policy; advocacy/awareness; prevention and risk reduction; availability of basic drugs and therapies; strengthening health/social systems and capacity; carer support; research); specific actions include reducing exposure to air pollution and implicated chemicals, increasing affordability of therapies, and training primary care providers, with LMIC focus. — [WHO launch](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-06-2022-launch-of-who-s-parkinson-disease-technical-brief); [WHO IRIS](https://apps.who.int/iris/handle/10665/355973); [Lancet Neurology editorial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(22)00312-X/fulltext) [verified in-session]
- Advocacy/policy review "One world, one goal: Advocacy and policy for a unified Parkinson's response" and "Mapping the Parkinson's pandemic" (2025/2026). — [PMC13454248](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13454248/); [PMC13438936](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13438936/) [verified links]

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern is that *process* and *access* outcomes (satisfaction, travel saved, advance directives, cost) improve under new care models more readily than PDQ-39 or UPDRS scores, suggesting QoL instruments may be insensitive to care-organisation effects or that integrated care benefits accrue mostly to sicker subgroups.
- Palliative care is the one care-model intervention with replicated RCT-level benefit on QoL (Kluger 2020, 2024), albeit with modest effect sizes.

### Gaps
- NICE-PD (PD nurse specialist RCT) results not found; trial updated Sept 2025, results likely pending.
- Neurologist-shortage quantification (e.g., Dorsey "Parkinson pandemic" JPD 2018; proportion of PD patients never seeing a neurologist) not retrieved this session.
- Cost-effectiveness analyses of IMPACT or Kluger palliative trials not retrieved.

## Key Question 5: Patient-reported outcomes and quality of life

### Takeaway
Non-motor symptoms (especially depression, anxiety, and neuropsychiatric features) are the strongest determinants of both patient HRQoL (PDQ-39) and caregiver burden, outweighing motor severity; sex and gender differences in symptom profile, levodopa pharmacokinetics and hormonal transitions are an emerging 2024-2026 research priority.

### Cited Findings
- Martinez-Martin P and colleagues: non-motor symptomatology has a significantly greater impact on caregiver burden and QoL than motor symptoms. — [International Review of Neurobiology, "Caregiver Burden and the Nonmotor Symptoms of Parkinson's Disease"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0074774217300922) [verified in-session]
- Observational study: all NMSS domains significantly affected PDQ-39; mobility, ADL and social function most negatively impacted. — [PMC9764934](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9764934) [verified in-session]
- Non-motor fluctuations (J Neurol 2025): higher NMF scores associated with worse PDQ-39 across all domains except stigma; NMFs and motor complications contributed comparably to caregiver burden. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-025-13390-9); [PubMed 40982051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40982051/) [verified in-session]
- Caregiver burden in late-stage PD: greatest predictors are disease severity and non-motor (especially neuropsychiatric) symptoms. — [PMC8773513](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8773513/); [PMC13213277 "Tilting the balance toward motor symptoms"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13213277/) [verified in-session]
- Macchi et al. "Patient and caregiver characteristics associated with caregiver burden in Parkinson's disease: a palliative care approach." Annals of Palliative Medicine. — [APM](https://apm.amegroups.org/article/view/31642/html) [verified link]
- Scoping review of cost-of-illness and QoL burden of non-motor symptoms (Expert Rev Pharmacoecon Outcomes Res 2024). — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14737167.2024.2390042) [verified link]
- Parkinson's Outcomes Project: depression and anxiety the #1 factors affecting overall health (see KQ4). — [Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/parkinsons-outcomes-project/findings) [verified in-session]
- Social relationships and communication as QoL determinants (2016). — [PubMed 27065542](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27065542/) [verified link]

Sex/gender differences and women's health
- Narrative review (2025): women have higher risk of disabling motor complications and non-motor fluctuations; men higher risk of cognitive impairment, postural instability and gait disorder. — [PMC11762054](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11762054/) [verified in-session]
- Barbato et al. "A sex- and gender-informed future for Parkinson's disease care." Clinical and Translational Medicine 2025: women show more dyskinesia, fluctuations, anxiety/depression, fatigue, pain; men more rigidity/akinesia, cognitive impairment, gait instability, RBD; higher lifetime estrogen exposure appears neuroprotective; earlier/surgical menopause associates with higher risk and earlier onset; women have greater levodopa bioavailability and dyskinesia risk, supporting sex-specific dosing. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ctm2.70382); [PMC12185897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12185897/) [verified in-session]
- "Parkinson's disease across female reproductive stages: Clinical implications from menstruation to menopause" (2026). — [PubMed 42436061](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42436061/) [verified link]
- Sex and gender in molecular etiology (Biology of Sex Differences 2025). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13293-025-00692-w) [verified link]
- Clinical overview of hormonal influences and caregiving burden in women with PD. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/sex-differences-hormonal-influences-caregiving-burden-women-pd); [APDA](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/women-and-parkinsons-disease/) [verified links]

Instruments
- PDQ-39 (Peto V, Jenkinson C, Fitzpatrick R, Greenhall R. Quality of Life Research 1995) and PDQ-8 short form (Jenkinson C, et al. Psychology & Health 1997) are the standard disease-specific HRQoL instruments used as primary outcomes in Connect.Parkinson, NICE-PD and ParkinsonNet studies. [Citation from prior knowledge; instrument use verified via trial sources above]

### Inferences
- Because depression/anxiety dominate QoL variance, care models that add mental-health and palliative components (Kluger) are more likely to move PDQ-39 than those that add only specialist access (Connect.Parkinson).
- Caregiver burden tracks neuropsychiatric symptoms, so caregiver outcomes should be co-primary endpoints in late-stage PD trials.

## Key Question 6: Prevention and public health

### Takeaway
A prevention agenda has moved from advocacy ("Ending Parkinson's Disease," 2020) to policy (EPA banned most TCE uses in December 2024; paraquat remains EPA-authorized despite ~70 country bans and Congressional pressure) and to the first non-pharmacological prevention trials in prodromal populations (Slow-SPEED, protocol 2026). PD GENEration has delivered free genetic testing to >30,000 people with ~12% positivity, building a cohort for future gene-targeted prevention.

### Cited Findings
- Dorsey ER, Sherer T, Okun MS, Bloem BR. "Ending Parkinson's Disease: A Prescription for Action" (PublicAffairs, 2020) — frames PD as largely preventable via environmental action. Dorsey quoted: PD is "largely preventable" and most new cases could be prevented "If we clean up our environment." — [Collaborative on Health and the Environment](https://www.healthandenvironment.org/latest-research/blog/parkinsons-disease-and-the-environment-opportunities-for-prevention) [verified in-session]
- EPA banned nearly all uses of trichloroethylene (TCE) in December 2024; MJFF called it "A Landmark Victory for the Parkinson's Community." Industry has since sought to reverse the ban. — [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/landmark-victory-parkinsons-community-epa-bans-trichloroethylene); [Substack report on reversal effort](https://takecontrol.substack.com/p/tce-ban) [verified in-session]
- Paraquat: thought to raise PD risk 100-500% depending on exposure; banned in ~70 countries; re-authorized by EPA in 2024; >50 US lawmakers (led by Rep. Casar, Nov 2024) urged a ban; California bill proposed. — [The New Lede](https://www.thenewlede.org/2024/11/us-congress-members-call-on-epa-to-ban-paraquat-citing-risk-of-parkinsons-and-other-diseases/); [Casar press release](https://casar.house.gov/media/press-releases/news-casar-leads-46-members-congress-demand-epa-bans-use-paraquat-protect); [Beyond Pesticides](https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2024/04/california-bill-would-ban-deadly-weedkiller-paraquat-linked-to-parkinsons-disease-in-face-of-epa-inaction/) [verified in-session]
- Slow-SPEED: "protocol for three randomised trials of remotely delivered exercise to prevent Parkinson's disease." npj Parkinson's Disease 2026 — described as "the first non-pharmacological Parkinson's disease prevention trials worldwide"; gamified app-based exercise vs active control over 18-36 months in iRBD (n=110, Netherlands), hyposmia (n=110, UK), and LRRK2/GBA1 carriers (n=600, US); intervention arm targets up to 100% increase in MVPA vs 10% in control; smartwatch monitoring. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01463-z); [PubMed 42448717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42448717/); [MDS Abstracts Slow-SPEED-NL](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/slow-speed-nl-study-protocol-for-a-remote-trial-of-exercise-for-parkinsons-disease-prevention/) [verified in-session]
- Prevention trial design: "Designing the First Trials for Parkinson's Prevention" (2024) and Bouhadoun S, Delva A, Schwarzschild MA, Postuma RB. "Preparing for Parkinson's disease prevention trials: Current progress and future directions." J Parkinson's Disease 2025; MGH "Planning for Prevention of Parkinson's" trial-design forum. — [PMC11491995](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11491995/); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1877718X251334050); [MGH forum](https://trials2prevent-asyn-disease.mgh.harvard.edu/) [verified in-session]
- Oosterhof TH, Darweesh SKL, Bloem BR, de Vries NM. "Considerations on How to Prevent Parkinson's Disease Through Exercise." J Parkinson's Disease 2024. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3233/jpd-240091) [verified link]
- PD GENEration (Parkinson's Foundation): as of March 31, 2026, 32,577 enrolled from 10 countries, 29,589 completed testing and counseling; cumulative positivity 12.1% for likely pathogenic/pathogenic variants in primary panel genes; secondary PD panel positivity 4.4% (n=9,831); CDC Tier 1 panel 1.5% (n=10,120); 77 sites including Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Peru, El Salvador; 96% of WGS participants consented to secondary findings (Feb 2025). — [medRxiv 2026 study description](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.20.26353696v1.full); [Parkinson's Foundation current findings](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/pdgeneration/genetics-behind-pd); [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/international-parkinson-disease-study-expands-pd-generation-hits-recruitment-milestone) [verified in-session]
- WHO 2022 brief includes "prevention and reduction of risks for PD including education and awareness" and reducing exposure to air pollution and implicated chemicals among 43 actions. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-06-2022-launch-of-who-s-parkinson-disease-technical-brief) [verified in-session]

### Inferences
- The 2024 TCE ban is the first major US regulatory action explicitly celebrated by the PD community as prevention policy; paraquat is the next target but faces regulatory inertia.
- Slow-SPEED's three-cohort design (iRBD, hyposmia, genetic carriers) is the template for prodromal prevention trials, and its results (likely 2028-2030) will be the first test of exercise as primary prevention.

### Gaps
- No completed prevention trial in prodromal PD was found; all are at protocol stage.
- Quantitative population-attributable fractions for TCE/paraquat not retrieved.

## Key Question 7: Health economics

### Takeaway
US economic burden was estimated at $51.9 billion/year in 2017 (Yang 2020), more than double prior estimates and projected to exceed $79 billion by 2037; globally, prevalence is projected to reach 25.2 million by 2050 (BMJ 2025), with ageing the dominant driver, making non-pharmacological and care-model efficiencies (e.g., ParkinsonNet's ~EUR 530/patient/year savings) increasingly relevant.

### Cited Findings
- Yang W, Hamilton JL, Kopil C, et al. "Current and projected future economic burden of Parkinson's disease in the U.S." npj Parkinson's Disease 2020;6:15. Total ~$52 billion/year (2017): $25.4 billion direct medical (hospitalization, medication) and $26.5 billion indirect/non-medical (lost work, wages, early retirement, caregiver time); based on Medicare, CDC, Census and survey data; "more than double previous estimates." — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-020-0117-1); [PMC7347582](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7347582/); [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/study-finds-parkinsons-52-billion-economic-burden-double-previous-estimates); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/the-financial-burden-of-parkinson-disease-is-52-billion-more-than-double-previous-estimates-) [verified in-session]. From prior knowledge: the paper projects burden surpassing $79 billion by 2037 (not re-verified).
- Su D, et al. BMJ, March 2025 (GBD 2021-based projection, 195 countries): 25.2 million people living with PD by 2050 (+112% vs 2021); 15.6 million in 2030, 20.4 million in 2040; ageing accounts for 89% of the increase, population growth 20%. — [BMJ Group](https://bmjgroup.com/cases-of-parkinsons-disease-set-to-reach-25-million-worldwide-by-2050/); [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/new-estimate-suggests-25-million-people-parkinsons-2050); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/parkinsons-prevalence-projected-double-globally-2050-2025a10006rs) [verified in-session]
- Cost drivers in care models: ParkinsonNet specialised physiotherapy associated with ~EUR 400 lower physiotherapy costs and ~EUR 530 lower total healthcare costs per patient per year (Ypinga 2018). — [Lancet Neurology](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(17)30406-4/abstract) [verified in-session]
- Hospital stay roughly doubled (8.2 vs 3.6 days) when PD doses were missed/delayed — a modifiable inpatient cost driver. — [PLOS ONE 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0124356) [verified in-session]
- Non-motor symptom cost-of-illness scoping review (2024). — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14737167.2024.2390042) [verified link]
- Epidemiology update (2024) summarizing prevalence trends used in burden estimates. — [PMC10922566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10922566/) [verified link]
- Policy momentum (Neurology Today 2020) following the Yang estimate. — [Neurology Today](https://neurologytoday.aan.com/doi/10.1097/01.NT.0000721992.11969.25) [verified link]

### Inferences
- Roughly half of US PD costs are indirect (caregiver time, lost productivity), so interventions that reduce caregiver burden and preserve function (exercise, OT, palliative care) have economic leverage beyond direct medical savings.
- Given ageing drives ~89% of projected prevalence growth, delaying onset by even a few years (the magnitude suggested by diet and exercise observational data) would have outsized population-level economic effects, strengthening the case for prevention trials.

### Gaps
- Updated post-2020 US burden estimates (e.g., any 2023-2025 Lewin Group/MJFF refresh) not located this session.
- Formal cost-effectiveness (QALY-based) analyses of exercise programs, telemedicine, or palliative care in PD not retrieved.

## (A1) Clinical description era: Parkinson 1817, Charcot, and the first pathology (Lewy 1912, Trétiakoff 1919)

### Takeaway
PD entered medicine as a purely clinical syndrome ("shaking palsy") in 1817; Charcot's school named it and separated rigidity from weakness in the 1860s-1880s; the pathological substrate (Lewy bodies 1912; substantia nigra lesion, Trétiakoff 1919) was established a century after Parkinson's essay. The best single modern entry point is Goetz 2011.

### Cited Findings
- James Parkinson's 1817 "An Essay on the Shaking Palsy" is available as a reprint: Parkinson J. "An essay on the shaking palsy. 1817." J Neuropsychiatry Clin Neurosci 2002;14(2):223-36 — [PubMed 11983801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11983801/)
- Comprehensive historical review of early clinical descriptions and the pre-levodopa therapies (Parkinson, Charcot, the anticholinergic era, early surgery): Goetz CG. "The history of Parkinson's disease: early clinical descriptions and neurological therapies." Cold Spring Harb Perspect Med 2011;1(1):a008862 — [PubMed 22229124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22229124/)
- Charcot's contribution (naming the disease after Parkinson, describing rigidity and the non-tremulous form) is reviewed in: Goetz CG. "Charcot on Parkinson's disease." Mov Disord 1986;1(1):27-32 — [PubMed 3332804](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3332804/)

## (A2) The dopamine era (1957-1970): Carlsson, Bertler & Rosengren, Sano, Ehringer & Hornykiewicz, Birkmayer, Cotzias

### Takeaway
Within a decade (1957-1967) dopamine went from "inert precursor of noradrenaline" to the first rationally designed neurotransmitter-replacement therapy in neurology: Carlsson showed L-DOPA reverses reserpine akinesia and that dopamine is a brain transmitter (1957-58); Bertler & Rosengren and Sano localized dopamine to the striatum (1959); Ehringer & Hornykiewicz found striatal dopamine depletion in PD brains (1960); Birkmayer & Hornykiewicz gave IV L-DOPA (1961); Cotzias established high-dose oral therapy (1967). Carlsson shared the 2000 Nobel Prize.

- Carlsson A, Lindqvist M, Magnusson T. "3,4-Dihydroxyphenylalanine and 5-hydroxytryptophan as reserpine antagonists." Nature 1957;180:1200 — [PubMed 13483658](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13483658/)

- Hornykiewicz's own account of the discovery: Hornykiewicz O. "The discovery of dopamine deficiency in the parkinsonian brain." J Neural Transm Suppl 2006;(70):9-15 — [PubMed 17017502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17017502/); and Hornykiewicz O. "A brief history of levodopa." J Neurol 2010;257(Suppl 2):S249-52 — [PubMed 21080185](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21080185/)
- Fahn S. "The history of dopamine and levodopa in the treatment of Parkinson's disease." Mov Disord 2008;23 Suppl 3:S497-508 — [PubMed 18781671](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18781671/)

The abstract records that Cotzias "decided to try to restore this pigment [melanin] in patients, not animals, and one of the three drugs he tried was d,l-dopa. As reported in his 1967 article, d,l-dopa proved to be dramatically successful in reversing the symptoms, but at extremely high dosages and with considerable hematologic adverse effects. Cotzias immediately tested l-dopa and found the same benefit with half the dosage and without the hematologic problems"; Yahr then "proceeded to develop and execute" a double-blind controlled trial "duplicating Cotzias's success," and "almost 50 years later l-dopa remains the most effective pharmacologic agent for treating Parkinson's disease." — [PubMed 25488030](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25488030/)
- Hornykiewicz obituary/retrospective: Ibrahimagić OĆ et al. "Oleh Hornykiewicz (1926-2020): Sixty Years since the Pioneering L-DOPA Application - One Year since the Death of the Pioneer." Psychiatr Danub 2021 — [PubMed 35354188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35354188/)

### Inferences
- The Lees 2015 abstract confirms the oft-repeated narrative that Cotzias's rationale was the (wrong) melanin hypothesis, and that the shift from d,l-dopa to l-dopa (in his 1969 follow-up) halved the dose and eliminated hematologic toxicity.
- The Nobel citation's wording ("has led to the realization that Parkinson's disease is caused by a lack of dopamine") implicitly credits the Carlsson line of work for a finding that Ehringer & Hornykiewicz made directly; the exclusion of Hornykiewicz from the 2000 prize is a well-known controversy in the field but I could not retrieve a primary source documenting the protest letter (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Cotzias's 1969 NEJM paper on L-DOPA (as opposed to the 1967 d,l-dopa paper) did not surface in the PubMed query I ran; the report writer should cite 1967 NEJM as the landmark and refer to Lees 2015 for the 1969 follow-up.
- No primary source was retrieved for the scientists' protest letter over Hornykiewicz's Nobel omission; treat any statement about it as unverified here.

## (A3) Dopamine agonists, MPTP and the environmental hypothesis (1974-1983)

### Takeaway
Bromocriptine (Calne 1974) opened the dopamine-agonist class; the MPTP "frozen addicts" (Davis 1979; Langston 1983) gave the field its first reliable primate model and converted the vague idea of environmental causation into a mechanistic (mitochondrial complex I) hypothesis that still drives the toxicant/pesticide research agenda.

## (A4) Surgery and circuitry: Benabid 1987, Bergman 1990, STN DBS (Limousin 1995, Deuschl 2006)

### Takeaway
The basal-ganglia circuit model (Albin/DeLong, late 1980s) and Bergman's 1990 demonstration that STN lesions reverse MPTP parkinsonism rationalized Benabid's serendipitous 1987 finding that high-frequency stimulation mimics lesioning; STN DBS entered humans in 1993 (Limousin 1995 Lancet) and was proven superior to best medical therapy in the 2006 NEJM randomized trial.

### Cited Findings
- Benabid AL, Pollak P, Louveau A, Henry S, de Rougemont J. "Combined (thalamotomy and stimulation) stereotactic surgery of the VIM thalamic nucleus for bilateral Parkinson disease." Appl Neurophysiol 1987;50(1-6):344-6 — [PubMed 3329873](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3329873/)
- Bergman H, Wichmann T, DeLong MR. "Reversal of experimental parkinsonism by lesions of the subthalamic nucleus." Science 1990;249(4975):1436-8 — [PubMed 2402638](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2402638/)
- Limousin P, Pollak P, Benazzouz A, et al. "Effect of parkinsonian signs and symptoms of bilateral subthalamic nucleus stimulation." Lancet 1995;345(8942):91-5 — [PubMed 7815888](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7815888/)
- Deuschl G, Schade-Brittinger C, Krack P, et al. "A randomized trial of deep-brain stimulation for Parkinson's disease." N Engl J Med 2006;355(9):896-908 — [PubMed 16943402](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16943402/)

### Inferences
- The chronology (lesion model 1990 -> human STN stimulation 1993/95 -> RCT 2006) is the clearest example in PD of a circuit-level model translating to therapy, and is frequently cited as the template for the "biology first" ambitions of the 2020s.

### Gaps

## (A5) The genetic and alpha-synuclein revolution (1997-2004) and Braak staging (2003)

### Takeaway
1997 was the pivot year: Polymeropoulos's SNCA mutation (Science) and, weeks later, Spillantini's demonstration that Lewy bodies are made of alpha-synuclein (Nature) fused genetics with pathology; parkin (1998) and LRRK2 (2004) followed, and Braak's 2003 staging recast PD as an ascending, body-wide synucleinopathy with a long prodrome.

## (A6) How the conceptual model has shifted: motor disorder -> systemic synucleinopathy -> biologically defined, heterogeneous disease

### Takeaway
Reviews written at the 200th anniversary (2017) already framed PD as a multisystem disorder with a decades-long prodrome; by 2024 two competing research frameworks (NSD-ISS and SynNeurGe, both Lancet Neurology, January 2024) proposed to define the disease biologically by alpha-synuclein seed amplification, neurodegeneration imaging and genetics, provoking an MDS statement and extensive published debate.

## (B10) Dorsey, telemedicine and the "Ending Parkinson's" agenda

### Takeaway
Ray Dorsey's program spans the first RCTs of telemedicine/virtual house calls for PD (2010; national trial 2017), the "Parkinson pandemic" epidemiologic framing (2018), the 2020 book "Ending Parkinson's Disease" with its PACT (Prevent, Advocate, Care, Treat) agenda, and the 2023-2024 TCE/pesticide/air-pollution prevention papers that assert most PD is environmentally caused and preventable.

### Cited Findings
- Dorsey ER, Deuel LM, Voss TS, et al. "Increasing access to specialty care: a pilot, randomized controlled trial of telemedicine for Parkinson's disease." Mov Disord 2010;25(11):1652-9 — [PubMed 20533449](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20533449/)
- Beck CA, Beran DB, Biglan KM, et al. "National randomized controlled trial of virtual house calls for Parkinson disease." Neurology 2017;89(11):1152-1161 — [PubMed 28814455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28814455/)
- "Ending Parkinson's Disease" (2020) proposes "a bold PACT to Prevent the disease, Advocate for protective policies, Care for patients, and Treat the condition with innovative therapies"; claims "Parkinson's disease is the fastest growing neurological disorder in the world," that cases grew "from 3 million cases to over 6 million in twenty-five years, with projections to double by 2040," and that "Harmful pesticides and other toxic chemicals that contribute to Parkinson's surround us" — [endingpd.org](https://endingpd.org/)
- Camp Lejeune/TCE: Dorsey ER et al. "The Water, the Air, the Marines-Camp Lejeune, Trichloroethylene, and Parkinson Disease." JAMA Neurol 2023 — [PubMed 37184845](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37184845/)
- See A3 and A6 for the 2018 "Parkinson Pandemic" papers and 2024 "Predominantly an Environmental Disease" paper.

### Inferences
- The Dorsey/Bloem agenda supplies the intellectual basis of the prevention-focused strand in US policy (National Plan to End Parkinson's, referenced on the NINDS page) and is the main counterweight to the genetics/biomarker-centered agenda of ASAP/GP2/PPMI.

### Gaps
- The book's co-authors (Sherer, Okun, Bloem) were not named on the fetched page beyond "Ray Dorsey, MD (and colleagues)"; PD Avengers and the US EPA TCE ban were not mentioned on the fetched page.

## (B11) Open controversies and strategic debates (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Four live debates structure the field: (1) whether to adopt a biological (SAA-anchored) definition and staging of PD for research, and eventually clinic; (2) whether research priority should shift from treatment to prevention; (3) the relative emphasis on environmental toxicants vs genetics; (4) how to calibrate expectations for stem-cell dopamine replacement after the 2025 Nature trials.

### Cited Findings
- Biological definition: competing NSD-ISS and SynNeurGe frameworks (Lancet Neurol 2024) with 12 published comments, authors' replies, and an erratum — [PubMed 38267190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267190/), [PubMed 38267191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267191/), [PubMed 38876742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38876742/); MDS Statement (Cardoso 2024) and Viewpoint (Kalia 2024) — [PubMed 38093469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38093469/), [PubMed 39250594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39250594/); neuropathological caution on timing and co-pathology (Matar & Halliday 2025) — [PubMed 40252665](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40252665/); Höglinger et al. acknowledge "ethical implications, its limitations, and the need for prospective validation" — [PubMed 38267191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267191/)
- Prevention vs treatment: Dorsey & Bloem argue "most of Parkinson's disease is thus preventable" — [PubMed 38217613](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38217613/); Oosterhof TH et al. "Considerations on How to Prevent Parkinson's Disease Through Exercise." J Parkinsons Dis 2024 — [PubMed 39031383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39031383/); PPMI's prodromal/"Path to Prevention" cohorts recruit on hyposmia + SAA — [ppmi-info.org study cohorts](https://www.ppmi-info.org/study-design/study-cohorts); Atterling Brolin 2025 policy review — [PubMed 39601461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39601461/)
- Environmental vs genetic emphasis: environmental side as above; genetic side represented by GP2's >415k-sample program and PD GENEration's finding that ~12-13% of tested patients carry a reportable variant — [gp2.org](https://gp2.org/), [parkinson.org PD GENEration](https://www.parkinson.org/advancing-research/our-research/pdgeneration); Urasa SJ et al. "Parkinson's Disease in Sub-Saharan Africa: Pesticides as a Double-Edged Sword." J Parkinsons Dis 2024 — [PubMed 38517806](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38517806/)
- Cell therapy: two phase I/II trials published in Nature, April 2025 (Tabar; Sawamoto) — [PubMed 40240592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40240592/), [PubMed 40240591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40240591/); GForce-PD's 2026 Kyoto meeting is titled "Clinical Trials of Dopamine Neuron Progenitor Therapy for Parkinson's Disease: Updates" — [gforce-pd.com](https://www.gforce-pd.com/); MJFF counts "180 promising therapies ... in or entering human testing" — [michaeljfox.org](https://www.michaeljfox.org/)
- Disease-modification record: Vijiaratnam 2021 review of repeated failures and the case for biomarker-enriched trials — [PubMed 34146514](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34146514/)

### Inferences
- The infrastructure itself encodes the debates: PPMI/ASAP/GP2/AMP PD are built around biological definition and genetics; Dorsey/Bloem and the Parkinson's Foundation's outcomes work emphasize prevention, care and environment; CPP sits between, trying to get biomarkers (DAT, SAA, digital) accepted by regulators so that any of these strategies can be tested in shorter, enriched trials.
- The "hype" question on cell therapy is best framed by the field's own choice of words: both 2025 Nature papers are phase I/II safety-and-feasibility trials, not efficacy trials.

### Gaps
- No single 2025-2026 "state of the field" editorial on these controversies was retrievable without web search; the writer should treat the framing above as synthesized from the primary papers listed rather than from a published overview. Statements about specific critics (e.g., Espay, Okun) returned no PubMed hits for the queries tried and are therefore not cited.

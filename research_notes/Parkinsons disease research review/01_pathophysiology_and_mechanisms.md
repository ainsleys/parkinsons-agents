## Q1: What is the current consensus model of how dopaminergic neurons die in PD, and what are the leading competing hypotheses?

### Takeaway
There is no single accepted death mechanism; the field's working consensus as of 2024–2026 is a convergent, multi-hit model in which a cell-autonomous vulnerability profile of substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc) dopamine neurons (pacemaking Ca²⁺ load, huge axonal arbor, dopamine/neuromelanin oxidative burden, low mitochondrial reserve) intersects with proteostatic stress (α-synuclein, lysosomal failure), mitochondrial dysfunction, iron/oxidative stress and immune activation. The main live disputes are over *which* factor is primary (α-synuclein-first vs. mitochondria/bioenergetics-first vs. lysosome-first), and whether PD is one disease or a syndrome of mechanistically distinct subtypes.

### Cited Findings
- Reviews from 2025 frame PD as traditionally defined by dopaminergic degeneration plus α-synuclein aggregation, but argue that evidence now supports a "multifactorial and systemic pathophysiology that extends beyond the central nervous system" — [Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience 2025, "Parkinson's disease physiopathology—beyond the α-synuclein aggregation: a narrative review"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12741115)
- The canonical mechanism list used across recent reviews: α-synuclein proteostasis, mitochondrial function, oxidative stress, calcium homeostasis, axonal transport, and neuroinflammation — [Nature Reviews Disease Primers, "Parkinson disease" (Poewe et al.)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrdp201713); [Int J Mol Sci 2025, "Parkinson Disease Signaling Pathways, Molecular Mechanisms, and Potential Therapeutic Strategies"](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/26/13/6416)
- **Selective vulnerability / Ca²⁺-pacemaking hypothesis (Surmeier).** SNc dopamine neurons are autonomous pacemakers, and pacemaking in SNc — but not in the comparatively spared ventral tegmental area (VTA) — is accompanied by Ca²⁺ influx through Ca_v1.3 L-type channels, raising intracellular Ca²⁺ and driving mitochondrial oxidant stress and cell death — [Surmeier et al., "Selective neuronal vulnerability in Parkinson disease," Nature Reviews Neuroscience 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2016.178); [Surmeier, "Determinants of dopaminergic neuron loss in Parkinson's disease," FEBS Journal 2018](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/febs.14607)
- L-type channel engagement during autonomous pacemaking "elevates the sensitivity of SNc DA neurons to mitochondrial toxins" and raises mitochondrial oxidant stress; dihydropyridine L-type blockers protect SNc neurons against PD-related neurotoxins in multiple animal studies — [Surmeier 2017, Nat Rev Neurosci](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2016.178); [Surmeier & Schumacker on Ca²⁺ and mitochondrial oxidant stress in SNc loss, Neuroscience 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306452211009870); [CaV1.3 increases SNc vulnerability in MPTP mice, 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6978652/)
- The same group extended this into a general "calcium hypothesis of neurodegeneration" framing — [Calcium hypothesis of neurodegeneration (review)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6878992/); [Surmeier & Sulzer, "Calcium, cellular aging, and selective neuronal vulnerability in Parkinson's disease," 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20053445/)
- Mitochondrial dysfunction is described in recent reviews as "central to Parkinson's disease" and as a contributor to "selective vulnerability of nigral dopaminergic neurons," with reduced complex I activity in patients, reduced membrane potential with increased ROS, altered fission–fusion, and trafficking defects — [Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience 2026 review of PINK1/Parkin-associated autophagy](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2026.1865383/full); [Mitophagy and Parkinson's disease: the PINK1–parkin link](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3925795/)
- A 2025 Nature Reviews *Perspective* proposes a distinct primary driver — that PD is a "fatty acidopathy," centering alterations in the fatty acid-ome as the critical lipid aberration in α-synucleinopathies, emphasizing transient binding of α-synuclein to fatty acids — [reported in Nature Reviews content, Sept 2025](https://www.nature.com/subjects/parkinsons-disease/nrneurol)
- Microglial phagocytic elimination of dopaminergic neurons has been proposed as a direct effector of neuron loss (low-affinity FcγR-mediated) — [bioRxiv 2024 preprint, "Microglial low-affinity FcγR mediates the phagocytic elimination of dopaminergic neurons in Parkinson's disease degeneration"](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.04.602092.full.pdf) *(preprint — not peer-reviewed; treat as provisional)*

### Inferences
- The Surmeier framework is the field's best answer to *why these neurons* rather than *what kills them*: it is a vulnerability model that is largely agnostic about the proximate trigger, which is why it coexists comfortably with both α-synuclein-first and mitochondria-first accounts rather than competing with them.
- The 2024–2026 move to biological definitions (Q2/Q3) implicitly concedes that mechanism is heterogeneous: if PD must be defined by axes (synuclein, neurodegeneration, genetics) rather than a single lesion, then "how dopamine neurons die" is likely subtype-specific.
- Ca_v1.3 blockade was the most direct clinical test of the pacemaking hypothesis; the report writer should check the STEADY-PD III isradipine outcome (I did not retrieve a source for it in this session — see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a primary source for the STEADY-PD III isradipine phase 3 trial result, which is the key negative clinical test of the Ca_v1.3 hypothesis. This matters: the mechanism is strongly supported preclinically but the translational test reportedly failed, and the report should not imply clinical validation.
- No source retrieved quantifying the proportion of nigral neurons lost at motor onset (commonly cited as ~30% of neurons / ~50–70% of striatal dopamine terminals). Needs verification before use.
- No source retrieved on apoptosis vs. necroptosis vs. ferroptosis as the terminal death modality in human PD nigra; this remains an open mechanistic question in the literature I sampled.

## Q2: Evidence for and against α-synuclein as the causal driver (Lewy bodies protective vs. toxic; PD without Lewy pathology)

### Takeaway
α-Synuclein remains the field's central organizing molecule — genetics, pathology, and now in-vivo seed amplification assays all converge on it — but the causal claim is genuinely contested at two joints: (1) Lewy bodies themselves may be end-stage sinks rather than the toxic species, with oligomers/presynaptic aggregates doing the damage, and (2) a substantial minority of genetically defined PD, especially *LRRK2* G2019S, shows nigral degeneration with no Lewy pathology at all.

### Cited Findings
**For α-synuclein as driver**
- *SNCA* missense mutation (A53T) in autosomal dominant PD established the gene–disease link — Polymeropoulos et al., *Science* 1997, "Mutation in the α-synuclein gene identified in families with Parkinson's disease" **[unverified citation detail — not re-fetched this session]**
- α-Synuclein is the principal component of Lewy bodies — Spillantini et al., *Nature* 1997, "α-Synuclein in Lewy bodies" **[unverified citation detail]**; the identity of α-synuclein as the Lewy body constituent is treated as settled across the reviews sampled, e.g. [Lewy body overview, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/lewy-body)
- *In vivo* detection of pathologic α-synuclein seeds now performs well enough to anchor diagnosis: CSF seed amplification assay (SAA) sensitivity ~85–93% and specificity ~95–98% for clinical PD; PPMI analysis covered ~1,139–1,145 participants — [PPMI SAA results, Movement Disorder Society newsroom](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/News/Newsroom/News-Releases_2022/Results-from-the-PPMI-Study-on-an-Alpha-Synuclein-Seed-Amplification-Assay.htm); [Siderowf et al., PPMI SAA in 1,145 cases (Zenodo record)](https://zenodo.org/records/7004865); [Alpha-synuclein SAA review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12210522)
- SAA positivity is high in hyposmic and iRBD prodromal cases but notably *lower* in *LRRK2* PD, and not elevated in asymptomatic *LRRK2* or *GBA* carriers — [PPMI SAA summary, MDS](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/News/Newsroom/News-Releases_2022/Results-from-the-PPMI-Study-on-an-Alpha-Synuclein-Seed-Amplification-Assay.htm); [PPMI cross-sectional SAA heterogeneity study, medRxiv 2023](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.02.27.23286156.full.pdf) *(preprint)*
- The 2024 biological-definition proposals both put α-synuclein first: the **NSD-ISS** defines Neuronal α-Synuclein Disease by "the presence of pathologic neuronal α-synuclein species detected in vivo, irrespective of any specific clinical syndrome," with dopaminergic dysfunction as an additional anchor — [Simuni et al., "A biological definition of neuronal α-synuclein disease: towards an integrated staging system for research," Lancet Neurology 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1474442223004052); **SynNeurGe** uses three axes, S (α-synucleinopathy, by SAA or histopathology), N (neurodegeneration: DAT-SPECT, cardiac MIBG, neuromelanin MRI), G (genetics: *SNCA*, *LRRK2*, *GBA1*) — [Höglinger & Lang, "SynNeurGe: the road ahead for a biological definition of Parkinson's disease," J Parkinsons Dis 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1877718X241298194); side-by-side comparison in [A Biological Framework for Parkinson's Disease: Advances in α-Synuclein-Centered Biomarkers and Staging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13542848/) and [Alzforum, "A Biological Definition of Parkinson's Disease: Two Proposals Pave the Way"](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way)
- NSD-ISS is already being used as a drug-development tool — [Biologically defined neuronal synuclein disease as a tool to advance drug development](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11662015/); and extended to Lewy body dementia research — [The NSD Integrated Staging System — opportunities to advance Lewy body dementia research](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11712574/)

**Against / complicating**
- **PD without Lewy bodies.** In *LRRK2*-related PD, parkinsonian motor features can occur in the absence of Lewy bodies; up to roughly one third of p.Gly2019Ser carriers show nigral dopaminergic neuron loss and gliosis *without* Lewy inclusions — [Lewy Body Pathology in LRRK2-Related Parkinson Disease, JAMA Neurology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/1934714); [Parkinson's Disease Associated with G2019S LRRK2 Mutations without Lewy Body Pathology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11233835/); [GeneReviews, LRRK2-Related Parkinson Disease](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1208/)
- Importantly, absence of *Lewy bodies* in LRRK2 PD does not by itself equal absence of α-synuclein pathology — though α-synuclein aggregation does appear genuinely absent in a subset — [α-Synuclein pathology in LRRK2 Parkinson disease, Nature Reviews Neurology 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41582-025-01113-7) *(abstract-level only; paywalled on fetch)*
- Lewy-body status in LRRK2 PD tracks phenotype: presence correlates with cognitive impairment/dementia, anxiety and orthostatic hypotension; absence correlates with a motor phenotype with preserved cognition and fewer non-motor features — [JAMA Neurology, LRRK2 Lewy body pathology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/1934714)
- LRRK2-associated parkinsonism splits by *in vivo* α-synuclein seeding status, with distinct longitudinal clinical/biomarker profiles — [Brain Communications 2025, "LRRK2-associated parkinsonism with and without in vivo evidence of alpha-synuclein aggregates"](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/article/7/2/fcaf103/8056104)
- *LRRK2* and *GBA1* have been framed as "neuropathological subtypes at opposite ends of a spectrum" of Lewy body disease — [LRRK2 and GBA1 in Lewy body diseases: neuropathological subtypes at opposite ends of a spectrum?](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13267418/) *(citation located; full text blocked by CAPTCHA on fetch — details not verified)*

**Lewy bodies: protective, toxic, or epiphenomenal**
- The protective-aggregate position: Lewy-related pathology may be a cellular defense that sequesters highly toxic α-synuclein oligomers; aggresome formation into Lewy bodies "reduces the accessible toxic surface of small aggregates and removes them from presynaptic terminals, their most hazardous location" — [Neuropathology of Lewy body dementia: Lewy-related pathology, α-synuclein oligomers, and comorbid pathologies, Molecular Neurodegeneration 2025](https://molecularneurodegeneration.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13024-025-00900-6)
- The toxic/obligate position: in idiopathic PD the *proportion* of nigral neurons bearing Lewy bodies stays stable despite ongoing neuronal loss, which argues that Lewy bodies cause or invariably accompany death; and if they were protective they are ineffective, since Lewy-body-laden neurons do not accumulate as disease advances — rather, affected neurons die — [Chartier S & Duyckaerts C, "Is Lewy pathology in the human nervous system chiefly an indicator of neuronal protection or of toxicity?" Cell and Tissue Research 2018;373(1):149-160, PMID 29869713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29869713/) — note the same paper also reads the stable proportion as evidence that Lewy bodies are *eliminated* over time
- The Lacoste/Lashuel line: it is the *process* of Lewy body formation, not fibrillization per se, that drives degeneration — [Mahul-Mellier et al., "The process of Lewy body formation, rather than simply alpha-synuclein fibrillization, is the major driver of neurodegeneration in synucleinopathies," bioRxiv 2019 (published PNAS 2020)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/751891v1.full)
- Presynaptic aggregates rather than Lewy bodies were identified as the degenerative driver in DLB — [Kramer & Schulz-Schaeffer, "Presynaptic α-synuclein aggregates, not Lewy bodies, cause neurodegeneration in dementia with Lewy bodies," Journal of Neuroscience 2007;27(6):1405](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/27/6/1405)
- Oligomers preceding Lewy-related pathology are more toxic than mature aggregates, supporting the view that degeneration is not caused by Lewy bodies — [Molecular Neurodegeneration 2025](https://molecularneurodegeneration.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13024-025-00900-6)
- Earlier reassessments of Lewy body meaning: [Engelender / "A reassessment of the Lewy body," PubMed 16008161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16008161/); [Olanow et al., "Lewy bodies," PNAS 2006](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0509567103)
- Nosological skepticism about a single Lewy body disease entity — [Lewy body disease or diseases with Lewy bodies?, npj Parkinson's Disease 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-021-00273-9)

### Inferences
- The two 2024 frameworks (NSD-ISS, SynNeurGe) differ on exactly this point: NSD-ISS makes α-synuclein positivity *definitional*, which would exclude SAA-negative LRRK2 parkinsonism from "the disease," while SynNeurGe keeps synuclein, neurodegeneration and genetics as separable axes and so can label an S-negative/N-positive/G-positive case. This is the cleanest way to present the causality debate to a reader: it has become a classification fight, not just a mechanism fight.
- The protective-vs-toxic debate is partly a category error that the literature is resolving by splitting the species: current weight favors soluble oligomers / presynaptic aggregates as the toxic entity and the mature Lewy body as a late, possibly inert or sequestering structure — but the stable-proportion argument (Chartier & Duyckaerts) remains a real counterweight and should be presented as unsettled.
- The lower SAA positivity in LRRK2 PD independently corroborates the autopsy finding of LRRK2 cases without synuclein pathology, which strengthens the claim considerably (two orthogonal methods, in-vivo and post-mortem).

### Gaps
- I could not access full text of the Nature Reviews Neurology 2025 LRRK2/α-synuclein review or the LRRK2-vs-GBA1 spectrum paper (both paywalled/CAPTCHA). The "up to one third" figure for Lewy-body-negative G2019S rests on the JAMA Neurology and PMC sources cited; exact case numbers were not verified.
- No source retrieved for the *SNCA* triplication/duplication dose–effect literature (Singleton et al. 2003; Chartier-Harlin 2004), which is a major pillar of the pro-causal argument. Should be added.
- No source retrieved quantifying incidental Lewy body disease prevalence in neurologically normal elderly (~10–15% commonly cited), which is the main "Lewy pathology without disease" counterargument.

## Q3: Prion-like spread, Braak staging, and the body-first / brain-first (Borghammer) model

### Takeaway
Cell-to-cell templated propagation of α-synuclein is well supported experimentally (in vitro, rodent, and graft data) and distinct conformational "strains" exist; but the extrapolation to human disease via Braak staging is contested, and Borghammer's α-Synuclein Origin-site and Connectome (SOC) model — recasting the staging debate as two anatomically distinct subtypes — is supported by molecular imaging and clinical clustering while contradicted by some structural-imaging and asymmetry studies.

### Cited Findings
**Prion-like propagation**
- In PD brain, α-synuclein accumulates intraneuronally and propagates cell-to-cell in prion-like fashion; de novo misfolding and/or neuronal internalization of aggregates templates endogenous monomers "in a mechanism reminiscent of prions" — [The prion-like spreading of α-synuclein: from in vitro to in vivo models of Parkinson's disease, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30690184); [The Prion-Like Spreading of Alpha-Synuclein in Parkinson's Disease: Update on Models and Hypotheses, 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8347623/)
- Proposed transfer routes: exocytosis, endocytosis, tunneling nanotubes, trans-synaptic transfer — [Alpha-synuclein spreading in Parkinson's disease (review)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4270285/); [Mini-review on initiatives to interfere with the propagation and clearance of α-synuclein](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5738184/)
- **Strains:** distinct human α-synuclein strains disseminate in wild-type human neuronal networks in a prion-like manner; fragmented amyloid-like short fibrils are the key pathogenic seeds that trigger prion-like conversion — [Propagation of α-Synuclein Strains within Human Reconstructed Neuronal Network, 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6372945/)
- Axonal transport / long-range spread framing — [α-Synuclein: The Long Distance Runner](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3674536/)
- **Counter-position in the same debate:** a paired set of *Journal of Neuroscience* 2017 dual perspectives — [Surmeier, Obeso & Halliday, "Parkinson's Disease Is Not Simply a Prion Disorder," J Neurosci 2017;37(41):9799](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/37/41/9799) and [Brundin & Melki, "Prying into the Prion Hypothesis for Parkinson's Disease," J Neurosci 2017;37(41):9808](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/37/41/9808) — this pairing is the single best citation for the shape of the controversy
- Landmark primary experiments I can name but did not re-verify this session **[unverified citation detail]**: Luk et al., *Science* 2012 (intrastriatal synthetic α-synuclein fibrils induce spreading Lewy-like pathology and nigral degeneration in WT mice); Kordower et al., *Nature Medicine* 2008 and Li et al., *Nature Medicine* 2008 (Lewy pathology in fetal mesencephalic grafts in PD patients, i.e., host-to-graft propagation)

**Braak staging and its critics**
- Braak's hypothesis: sporadic PD is initiated by a pathogen entering via the nasal cavity and being swallowed to reach the gut, seeding Lewy pathology in nose and digestive tract, with a staging scheme describing spread from peripheral to central nervous system — [Exploring Braak's Hypothesis of Parkinson's Disease (review)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5304413/); [Braak staging overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braak_staging) *(encyclopedic source — use only for framing, not as a citation)*
- Criticisms: not all patients follow the staging; the scheme describes mainly a subset with young onset and long disease duration; there is no relation between Braak stage and clinical severity; and no dose-dependent correlation between Lewy pathology and cell loss or clinical features — [Exploring Braak's Hypothesis of PD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5304413/); [Surmeier, Obeso & Halliday, J Neurosci 2017](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/37/41/9799)
- The formal exchange in the literature: [Burke, Dauer & Vonsattel, "A critical evaluation of the Braak staging scheme for Parkinson's disease," Ann Neurol 2008, PubMed 19067353](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19067353/) vs. [Del Tredici & Braak, "Evidence in favor of Braak staging of Parkinson's disease," 2010, PubMed 20187227](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20187227/)
- A reconciling reading: each hypothesis (prion-like spread vs. selective vulnerability) alone has shortcomings, but a synthesis explains much of PD etiopathology — [Exploring Braak's Hypothesis of PD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5304413/)
- Primary staging paper **[unverified citation detail]**: Braak et al., "Staging of brain pathology related to sporadic Parkinson's disease," *Neurobiology of Aging* 2003 — and Braak et al. 2006 on the dual-hit/nasal-enteric route.

**Body-first vs. brain-first (SOC model)**
- The model: "the initial pathology starts either in the olfactory bulb or amygdala leading to a brain-first subtype, or in the enteric nervous system leading to a body-first subtype" — [Horsager J & Borghammer P, "Brain-first vs. body-first Parkinson's disease: an update on recent evidence," *Parkinsonism & Related Disorders* 2024;122:106101, doi:10.1016/j.parkreldis.2024.106101](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/brain-first-vs-body-first-parkinsons-disease-an-update-on-recent-/)
- Evidence balance in that 2024 update: molecular imaging studies "generally in agreement with the model"; structural imaging (e.g., MRI volumetry) showed **conflicting** findings; most large-scale clinical studies supportive, reporting clustering of body-first markers — RBD, constipation, autonomic dysfunction, neuropsychiatric symptoms, cognitive impairment, and α-synuclein burden in brain at diagnosis; neuropathological α-synuclein distribution studies provided additional corroboration — [Horsager & Borghammer 2024](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/brain-first-vs-body-first-parkinsons-disease-an-update-on-recent-/); [ScienceDirect record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1353802024001135)
- Supporting neuropathology: [Neuropathological evidence of body-first vs. brain-first Lewy body disease, Parkinsonism Relat Disord 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969996121003065)
- **Contradicting evidence:** a dedicated asymmetry study did not support the asymmetry prediction of the body-first/brain-first hypothesis — [Investigating the aspect of asymmetry in brain-first versus body-first Parkinson's disease, npj Parkinson's Disease 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00685-3)
- Attempts to operationalize the dichotomy with imaging: [Discriminating between proposed brain-first and body-first PD using conventional and radiomics-enhanced DAT-SPECT, npj Parkinson's Disease 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01164-z); longitudinal subtype natural history: [The natural history of body-first versus brain-first Parkinson's disease subtypes, J Neurol 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-025-13050-y)
- Borghammer's group also links route-of-entry to exposure: inhaled pesticides, dry-cleaning solvents and air pollution are argued to predispose to the brain-first (olfactory) route — [ScienceDaily report of the 2024 model paper](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/04/240411130149.htm) *(press release — the underlying paper should be cited instead; see Gaps)*; related: [Environmental triggers of Parkinson's disease, Parkinsonism Relat Disord 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969996121003508)
- Broader critical appraisal of brain-first/body-first as a general model: [Brain-First vs. Body-First Models in Neurodegenerative Disease: A Perspective Review, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4087/7/3/57) *(MDPI — lower-tier venue; use with caution)*

### Inferences
- The body-first/brain-first model is best presented not as a replacement for Braak but as a *rescue* of it: it concedes the central criticism (not all patients follow the stages) by proposing that Braak's sequence is the trajectory of one subtype, and in doing so converts an unfalsifiable staging claim into testable predictions about symptom clustering, asymmetry and imaging.
- The evidence pattern is characteristic of a model that is partly right: supportive where the measurement is of autonomic/peripheral synuclein burden and symptom co-occurrence, unsupportive where it makes sharper anatomical predictions (asymmetry, structural MRI). The report should state that split explicitly rather than scoring the model pass/fail.
- Prion-*like* is doing real work in the literature: no source I found claims human-to-human transmissibility, and the Surmeier/Obeso counter-perspective targets precisely the slide from "templated propagation occurs" to "PD is a prion disease."

- Could not fetch the full Horsager & Borghammer 2024 text (403 at the journal; abstract obtained via Aarhus University repository). Specific citations *within* that review are therefore not captured.
- The primary 2024 paper behind the ScienceDaily inhalation/exposure claim was not identified; do not cite the press release as the source for that claim.
- No source retrieved on vagotomy epidemiology (Svensson et al. 2015; Liu et al. 2017) or appendectomy studies (Killinger 2018), which are central empirical tests of gut-origin. Significant gap for Q3/Q5.

## Q4: Mitochondria (PINK1/Parkin, complex I), lysosomes (GBA1/GCase), and neuroinflammation

### Takeaway
All three are now supported by convergent human genetics plus mechanism: PINK1/Parkin define a mitochondrial quality-control axis (with complex I deficiency as the classic nigral finding), GBA1 defines a lysosomal axis locked in a bidirectional feedback loop with α-synuclein, and neuroinflammation has moved from "reactive microgliosis" to a specific adaptive-immunity model in which HLA class II presentation of α-synuclein peptides activates T cells. None has yet produced a disease-modifying therapy.

### Cited Findings
**Mitochondria**
- PINK1 is the damage sensor that probes mitochondrial import-pathway integrity and activates Parkin; Parkin selectively marks damaged mitochondria with ubiquitin for mitophagy and other quality-control processes — [The role of PINK1–Parkin in mitochondrial quality control, Nature Cell Biology, Oct 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41556-024-01513-9)
- Canonical PD mitochondrial phenotype: reduced complex I activity in patients, reduced mitochondrial membrane potential with increased ROS, altered fission–fusion, trafficking defects — [Mitophagy and Parkinson's disease: the PINK1–parkin link](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3925795/); [PubMed 20736035](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20736035)
- Translational status as of 2026: despite robust pathway activation in experimental systems, "translation into effective disease-modifying strategies has remained limited," with interventions neuroprotective in cell and animal models proving hard to convert into clinical benefit — [Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience 2026, novel insight into PINK1/parkin-associated autophagy in PD](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2026.1865383/full); [PubMed 42046651](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42046651/)
- Drug-development angle: [Prospects for the Development of PINK1 and Parkin Activators for the Treatment of Parkinson's Disease](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9696222/)
- Mitochondria–Ca²⁺ coupling to selective vulnerability: see Q1 (Surmeier/Schumacker) — [Neuroscience 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306452211009870)
- **[unverified citation detail]** The historical anchors here are the MPTP story (Langston et al., *Science* 1983) and Schapira et al., *Lancet* 1989 on nigral complex I deficiency; neither was re-verified in this session.

**Lysosomes / GBA1**
- *GBA1*, encoding glucocerebrosidase (GCase), is the most significant genetic risk factor for PD; GCase hydrolyzes glucosylceramide to ceramide and glucose and processes glucosylsphingosine — [Underlying Mechanisms of GBA1 in Parkinson's Disease and Dementia with Lewy Bodies: Narrative Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12732524/); [Glucocerebrosidase mutations and Parkinson disease (Sidransky group review)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9463283/)
- **The bidirectional loop (the key mechanistic claim):** reduced GCase activity perturbs lysosomal function, causing glycosphingolipid substrate accumulation and accumulation of pathogenic α-synuclein oligomers; α-synuclein in turn impedes transit and processing of GCase between ER and lysosome, further reducing lysosomal GCase activity — a self-reinforcing cycle — [Underlying Mechanisms of GBA1 in PD and DLB](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12732524/); [Pathological Mechanisms and Clinical Aspects of GBA1 Mutation-Associated Parkinson's Disease (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536716/)
- Pathogenic alleles N370S and L444P disrupt sphingolipid metabolism, producing toxic glucosylceramide and glucosylsphingosine accumulation, ER stress, and impaired clearance of misfolded proteins; shared pathways with idiopathic PD include loss- and gain-of-function mechanisms, ER stress, lipid metabolism, neuroinflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction and altered autophagy–lysosomal pathway — [Underlying Mechanisms of GBA1 in PD and DLB](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12732524/)
- Organelle crosstalk: GBA1 dysfunction dysregulates mitochondria–lysosome membrane contacts in dopaminergic neuronal models — [Dysregulation of mitochondria-lysosome contacts by GBA1 dysfunction in dopaminergic neuronal models of Parkinson's disease, Nature Communications 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-22113-3)
- GBA1–inflammation interface: [Interplay of GBA1 with lysosomal dysfunction and inflammation in Parkinson's disease, Neural Regeneration Research](https://doi.org/10.4103/NRR.NRR-D-25-01082)
- Newer convergence claims (preprints, treat as provisional): lysosomal GCase required for ciliary Hedgehog signaling as a convergent PD pathway — [bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.20.633968.full.pdf); GPNMB bidirectional regulation in GBA1 isogenic dopaminergic neurons — [bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.23.661126.full.pdf) / [PMC12262256](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12262256/)

**Neuroinflammation: microglia, T cells, HLA**
- Neuroinflammation in PD was originally proposed from responsive microglia in post-mortem PD brain, shown by upregulated HLA-DR (MHC-II) and CD68 — [The systemic immune response in Parkinson's disease: focus on the peripheral immune component, Trends in Neurosciences 2023](https://www.cell.com/trends/neurosciences/fulltext/S0166-2236(23)00165-0) *(the original observation is McGeer et al. 1988 — **[unverified citation detail]**)*
- **T cells recognize α-synuclein:** T cells from PD patients respond to α-synuclein peptides/protofibrils, whereas healthy-control T cells show little or no response; responders may be in early PD stages — Sulzer et al., *Nature* 2017 — [Alzforum, "Trigger Warning: α-Synuclein Sets Off T Cells in Parkinson's"](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/trigger-warning-synuclein-sets-t-cells-parkinsons) *(secondary report of the Nature paper; primary citation **[unverified citation detail]**)*
- PD patients show higher expression of HLA molecules, particularly HLA class II, potentially enhancing susceptibility by creating a more inflammatory environment — [The immunology of Parkinson's disease, Semin Immunopathol 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00281-022-00947-3)
- **Causal requirement in vivo:** neuronal α-synuclein accumulation drives peripheral T cell infiltration into brain; infiltrating T cells promote microglial activation via TNFα and IFNγ; both T cell presence *and* microglial MHC-II upregulation are required for nigral dopamine neuron loss — [T cell infiltration and upregulation of MHCII in microglia leads to accelerated neuronal loss in an α-synuclein rat model of Parkinson's disease, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429710/); [Microglia–T cell interactions drive α-synuclein pathology in a Parkinson's disease mouse model, J Neuroinflammation 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12974-026-03734-1)
- Peripheral immune involvement extends to dopamine receptor signaling on T cells: [Dopamine receptor D3 expression altered in CD4+ T cells from PD patients; pharmacologic inhibition attenuates motor impairment in mice](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6504698/)
- General framing of inflammation as a therapeutic target: [Neuroinflammation is a key player in Parkinson's disease and a prime target for therapy, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3392895/); [Neuroinflammation in Parkinson Disease, Practical Neurology](https://practicalneurology.com/diseases-diagnoses/movement-disorders/neuroinflammation-in-parkinson-disease/31826/)

### Inferences
- The strongest version of the inflammation argument is not "microglia are activated in PD" (which is compatible with bystander reactivity) but the rat/mouse data showing that MHC-II upregulation and T cell infiltration are *necessary* for nigral loss — that is the finding that upgrades inflammation from consequence to mechanism, and it dovetails with GWAS signal at the HLA locus.
- GBA1 and mitochondrial axes are not independent: the mitochondria–lysosome contact literature and the GCase/α-synuclein loop suggest a single degradative-capacity failure mode, which is why "lysosome-first" and "mitochondria-first" may be a false dichotomy in practice.
- The repeated pattern across all three axes — strong preclinical rescue, weak clinical translation (explicitly stated for PINK1/Parkin) — is itself a finding worth stating in the report.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved for the PD GWAS HLA-DRB1/HLA-DRA association statistics (Hamza et al. 2010 and successors), nor for the Nalls et al. 2019 meta-GWAS; these are needed to ground the genetic side of the inflammation and lysosome arguments.

## Q5: Gut-brain axis and microbiome — what does the literature show, and how robust is it?

### Takeaway
There is a reproducible core microbiome signature in PD (opportunistic pathogens up, SCFA-producing bacteria down, *Bifidobacterium*/*Lactobacillus* up), confirmed across 16S and shotgun metagenomics and in machine-learning meta-analyses; but the *magnitude* claimed by the largest single study did not replicate, causality in humans is unestablished, and the strongest causal evidence remains germ-free/animal work.

### Cited Findings
- **Largest single cohort:** Wallen et al. enrolled 490 PD and 234 control individuals with deep shotgun sequencing of fecal DNA and metagenome-wide association; 30% of species and pathways tested showed altered abundance, described as "widespread dysbiosis" — [Wallen et al., "Metagenomics of Parkinson's disease implicates the gut microbiome in multiple disease mechanisms," Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34667-x)
- **Internal replication:** of 15 genus-level associations previously reported in their 16S work (three clusters: opportunistic pathogens elevated, SCFA producers reduced, *Bifidobacterium*/*Lactobacillus* elevated), 13 of 15 were confirmed with statistical significance in the shotgun data — [Wallen et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34667-x)
- **Failure to replicate the breadth:** an independent cohort could not reproduce the >30% figure — only 7 of 64 species and 7 of 263 MetaCyc pathways tested were significant, although core microbial differences were similar — [Metagenomic Analysis Reveals Large-Scale Disruptions of the Gut Microbiome in Parkinson's Disease, Movement Disorders (doi 10.1002/mds.29959)](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.29959); see also [Integrated multi-cohort analysis of the Parkinson's disease gut metagenome, bioRxiv 2022](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.20.500694.full.pdf) *(preprint)*
- **Cross-cohort meta-analysis:** machine-learning-based meta-analysis across cohorts identifies consistent gut microbiome alterations associated with PD — [Machine learning-based meta-analysis reveals gut microbiome alterations associated with Parkinson's disease, Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56829-3) / [PMC12059030](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12059030/)
- **Progression:** a 2025 systematic review of patient cohorts assesses the microbiome's role in PD progression — [The role of the gut microbiome in the progression of Parkinson's disease: a systematic review of patient cohorts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12681464/)
- Braak's original gut-origin proposal is the theoretical backbone of this literature (pathogen entering via nasal cavity, swallowed, seeding enteric Lewy pathology) — [Exploring Braak's Hypothesis of Parkinson's Disease](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5304413/)
- Body-first subtype markers are gut/autonomic: constipation, RBD, autonomic dysfunction cluster together — [Horsager & Borghammer 2024](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/brain-first-vs-body-first-parkinsons-disease-an-update-on-recent-/)
- **[unverified citation detail]** The key causal animal experiment is Sampson et al., *Cell* 2016 ("Gut microbiota regulate motor deficits and neuroinflammation in a model of Parkinson's disease"), showing germ-free α-synuclein-overexpressing mice are protected and that human PD microbiota transplantation worsens motor deficits. I did not retrieve this source in this session; it should be verified and is essential to any claim of causality.

### Inferences
- Robustness verdict for the report: the *direction* of the PD microbiome signature is reproducible; the *effect size* is not, and the Wallen 30%-of-everything result is best presented as an upper bound from a single large cohort rather than an established figure.
- Standard confounds in this literature (PD medication, especially levodopa and COMT inhibitors; constipation itself altering transit and thus community composition; diet; age) are not addressed by any source I retrieved, so the report should not treat the signature as disease-specific without that caveat.
- The microbiome literature and the body-first model reinforce each other rhetorically but are methodologically independent — neither one validates the other, and the report should avoid letting Borghammer's imaging/clinical evidence be read as support for a microbial trigger.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source on enteric nervous system α-synuclein pathology in biopsies of prodromal/early PD, nor on whether enteric aggregates precede CNS pathology in humans — the pivotal human test of gut-origin.
- No retrieved source on vagotomy or appendectomy epidemiology (see Q3 gaps).
- No retrieved source on fecal microbiota transplantation trials in PD, or on *Helicobacter pylori* / SIBO literature.
- Sampson 2016 (the causal anchor) unverified.

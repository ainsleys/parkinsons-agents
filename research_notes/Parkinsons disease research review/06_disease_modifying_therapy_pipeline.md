# Disease-Modifying (Neuroprotective) Therapy Development for Parkinson's Disease: Historical Failures, 2024-2026 Results, and the Current Pipeline

Status note: compiled 2026-10-06. Every trial below is tagged HISTORICAL (completed before 2024), RECENT (readout 2024-2026) or ONGOING. Where a fact comes from a search-engine snippet of a page that could not be fetched directly, this is flagged.

## Key Question 1: Historical failures and lessons (DATATOP through exenatide/lixisenatide) and what they say about trial design

### Takeaway
Across ~35 years, every Phase 3 neuroprotection trial in PD has failed to show disease modification by regulatory standards, and several produced harm signals (deferiprone, vodobatinib). The recurring lessons are: (1) symptomatic effects confound clinical scales (DATATOP, ADAGIO); (2) positive small Phase 2s with off-medication MDS-UPDRS III endpoints (exenatide 2017, lixisenatide 2024) have not yet translated to Phase 3 (Exenatide-PD3 negative 2025); (3) target engagement was often unproven (isradipine) or proven but clinically irrelevant (BIIB122, urate, GDNF imaging); and (4) futility designs have efficiently killed candidates (QE3, creatine, pioglitazone, SURE-PD3, PD STAT).

### Cited Findings

**DATATOP (HISTORICAL)**
- Parkinson Study Group, NEJM 1993: 800 early untreated PD patients randomised 2x2 to deprenyl (selegiline) 10 mg/day and/or tocopherol 2000 IU/day; primary endpoint time to disability requiring levodopa. Deprenyl HR 0.50 (95% CI 0.41-0.62, P<0.001), delaying disability ~9 months; tocopherol had no effect. — [Parkinson Study Group, NEJM 1993 (PubMed 8417384)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8417384/) (abstract retrieved via Europe PMC)

**Delayed-start design: TEMPO and ADAGIO (HISTORICAL)**
- TEMPO delayed-start study (Parkinson Study Group, Archives of Neurology 2004): n=404 (371 analysed); rasagiline 2 mg/day early-start showed a 2.29-unit smaller UPDRS increase than delayed treatment at 12 months (P=0.01). — [Europe PMC search: delayed-start Parkinson](https://europepmc.org/search?query=%22delayed-start%22%20AND%20Parkinson)
- ADAGIO (Olanow CW et al., NEJM 2009): 1,176 untreated PD subjects; rasagiline 1 mg or 2 mg; 72-week delayed-start design with three hierarchical endpoints (weeks 12-36 slope superiority vs placebo; baseline-72 week change early vs delayed; weeks 48-72 slope non-inferiority). The 1 mg dose met all three (slopes 0.09 vs 0.14 points/week, P=0.01; 2.82 vs 4.52 points, P=0.02; non-inferiority P<0.001); the 2 mg dose failed (P=0.60 for the 72-week comparison). Authors: "early treatment with rasagiline at 1 mg per day provided benefits consistent with a possible disease-modifying effect" but the 2 mg failure "requires cautious interpretation." — [Olanow et al., NEJM 2009 (PubMed 19776408)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19776408/)
- The ADAGIO dose paradox provoked an extended methodological debate in Neurology 2010 (Ahlskog JE, "Rasagiline, Parkinson neuroprotection, and delayed-start trials: still no satisfaction?"; responses by Schwarzschild, Olanow, and Montgomery "The delayed-start study in Parkinson disease: can't satisfy everyone") raising small effect sizes, incomplete blinding and subjective outcomes as confounders. — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=%22delayed-start%22%20AND%20Parkinson)
- A delayed-start design was later used for donepezil against cognitive decline in PD (Sawada H et al., Expert Opin Pharmacother 2021; n=98; early-start slope better, P=0.048). — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=%22delayed-start%22%20AND%20Parkinson)

**Coenzyme Q10 QE3 (HISTORICAL)**
- Parkinson Study Group QE3 Investigators, JAMA Neurology 2014: 600 early PD participants at 67 North American sites randomised to placebo, CoQ10 1200 mg/day or 2400 mg/day for 16 months or until dopaminergic therapy needed. UPDRS worsening 6.9 (placebo), 7.5 (1200 mg; P=0.49), 8.0 (2400 mg; P=0.21); prespecified futility criteria met and the trial was terminated. "No evidence of benefit." NCT00740714. — [JAMA Neurology 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/1851409); [PubMed 24664227](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24664227/)

**Creatine NET-PD LS-1 (HISTORICAL)**
- Writing Group for the NINDS NET-PD Investigators, JAMA 2015: 1,741 early PD participants randomised to creatine monohydrate 10 g/day or placebo, planned minimum 5-year follow-up; median follow-up 4 years. Global statistical test mean summed ranks 2,360 (placebo) vs 2,414 (creatine), P=0.45; terminated early for futility. NCT00449865. — [PubMed 25668262](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25668262/); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00449865](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00449865)

**Pioglitazone FS-ZONE (HISTORICAL)**
- NINDS NET-PD FS-ZONE Investigators, Lancet Neurology 2015: 210 early PD patients at 35 US sites on rasagiline/selegiline randomised to pioglitazone 15 mg (n=72), 45 mg (n=67) or placebo (n=71) for 44 weeks. Total UPDRS change 4.42, 5.13 and 6.25 points respectively; the 45 mg group met futility (P=0.09); conclusion that pioglitazone at these doses is unlikely to modify progression. — [PubMed 26116315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26116315/)

**Isradipine STEADY-PD III (HISTORICAL)**
- Parkinson Study Group STEADY-PD III Investigators, Annals of Internal Medicine 2020 (vol 172 no 9; DOI 10.7326/M19-2534): 336 early PD patients (disease duration mean 0.9 y, 57 sites) randomised to immediate-release isradipine 5 mg BID or placebo for 36 months; 95% completed. UPDRS I-III change 2.99 (isradipine) vs 3.26 (placebo), difference -0.27 (95% CI -3.02 to 2.48; P=0.85); no secondary outcome effect (time to antiparkinson medication, motor complications, QoL). Edema and dizziness most common AEs. NCT02168842. — [Annals of Internal Medicine 2020](https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-2534); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02168842](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02168842)
- Accompanying editorial (Maiti B, Perlmutter JS, "A Clinical Trial of Isradipine: What Went Wrong?", Annals of Internal Medicine 2020) argued: no direct measure of target engagement existed and should be developed before further isradipine studies; higher doses risk orthostasis; the UPDRS showed minimal placebo decline over 3 years with concurrent dopaminergic therapy, so direct neurodegeneration biomarkers (e.g., nigral molecular imaging rather than striatal) are needed. — [PMC7895294](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7895294/)

**Inosine/urate SURE-PD3 (HISTORICAL)**
- Parkinson Study Group SURE-PD3 Investigators, JAMA 2021: Phase 3, 298 early PD participants (149/arm) randomised to urate-elevating inosine or placebo for up to 2 years. Serum urate rose 2.03 mg/dL (44%) on inosine vs 0.01 on placebo. MDS-UPDRS rate of change 11.1 vs 9.9 points/year, difference 1.26 (95% CI -0.59 to 3.11; P=0.18). Closed early after prespecified interim futility analysis; 273 (92%) completed. Fewer SAEs on inosine (7.4 vs 13.1 per 100 patient-years) but more kidney stones (7.0 vs 1.4 per 100 patient-years). NCT02642393. — [JAMA 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2784144)

**Deferiprone FAIRPARK-II (HISTORICAL; harm signal)**
- Devos D et al., NEJM 2022: Phase 2 RCT of deferiprone vs placebo in newly diagnosed, dopaminergic-naive PD for 36 weeks. MDS-UPDRS total worsened 15.6 points on deferiprone vs 6.3 on placebo (difference 9.3; P<0.001) despite reduced nigrostriatal iron on MRI. Conclusion: in levodopa-naive early PD, deferiprone "was associated with worse scores in measures of parkinsonism." — [NEJM 2022 (DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2209254)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/nejmoa2209254); [Cure Parkinson's summary](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/2022/12/results-of-deferiprone-trial-fairpark-2-published/)
- Follow-on work: Devos, Rascol, Meissner et al., "Therapeutic modalities of deferiprone in Parkinson's disease: SKY and EMBARK studies," Journal of Parkinson's Disease 2024/2025 (DOI 10.1177/1877718X241300295). — [PMC13347414](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347414/)
- Barcelona group reported mechanistic work explaining the "paradoxical effect" of deferiprone (iron chelation in dopaminergic neurons). — [Clinic Barcelona news](https://www.clinicbarcelona.org/en/news/research-provides-new-clues-about-iron-toxicity-in-parkinsons-disease-and-explains-the-paradoxical-effect-of-deferiprone)

**c-Abl inhibitors: nilotinib NILO-PD, vodobatinib PROSEEK, risvodetinib (HISTORICAL/RECENT)**
- NILO-PD (Simuni T et al., JAMA Neurology 2021): 6-month, 25-site US RCT of nilotinib in moderately advanced PD (recruited Nov 2017-Dec 2018); primary objective safety/tolerability. "Despite acceptable safety and tolerability, little efficacy ... was observed," and authors concluded nilotinib "should not be considered suitable for further studies." NCT03205488. — [JAMA Neurology 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2773698); [PubMed 33315105](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33315105/)
- PROSEEK (vodobatinib/K0706, Sun Pharma; NCT03655236): Phase 2, 513 recently diagnosed DaT-SPECT-confirmed early PD participants randomised to 384 mg, 192 mg or placebo for 40 weeks; primary endpoint MDS-UPDRS III change. Company announced failure April 10, 2024; discontinuations were dose-dependent (62% high dose, 33% low dose, 28% placebo). Publication (Sarva H et al., npj Parkinson's Disease 2026) reports comparisons favoured placebo in both active arms and serum neurofilament light chain increased significantly in vodobatinib groups, "supporting inefficacy in PD." — [npj Parkinson's Disease 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01275-1); [Alzforum vodobatinib](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/vodobatinib); [NCT03655236](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03655236)
- Risvodetinib (IkT-148009, Inhibikase; Phase 2 "201" trial NCT05424276): 126 unmedicated PD patients, 50/100/200 mg or placebo for 3 months; results announced Jan 29, 2025: "failed to improve the key clinical endpoint of the MDS-UPDRS Parts 2 and 3," though 95% completed and company reported reduced skin-nerve alpha-synuclein deposition; development paused. — [Alzforum risvodetinib](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/risvodetinib)

**GDNF and neurturin (HISTORICAL)**
- Nutt JG et al., Neurology 2003: multicentre RCT of intracerebroventricular GDNF (placebo to 500-4,000 ug monthly) in 50 subjects for 8 months; no improvement in ON or OFF UPDRS motor scores at any dose; nausea, anorexia, weight loss, Lhermitte-like paresthesias, hyponatremia; authors concluded GDNF "did not reach the target tissues." — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=GDNF%20Parkinson%20Nutt%202003)
- Lang AE et al., Annals of Neurology 2006: RCT of bilateral continuous intraputamenal GDNF (liatermin) in 34 patients; 6-month OFF UPDRS motor change -10.0% vs -4.5% placebo (not significant) despite a 32.5% treatment difference in 18F-dopa uptake (P=0.019). — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=intraputamenal%20GDNF%20Lang%202006)
- Whone A et al., Brain 2019 (Bristol): single-centre RCT; 6 pilot + 35 patients; intermittent convection-enhanced intraputamenal GDNF 120 ug/putamen every 4 weeks for 40 weeks. Primary OFF UPDRS-III change -17.3% active vs -11.8% placebo (P=0.41, not met). Post hoc: 9/21 (43%) active vs 0 placebo had >=10-point motor improvement (P=0.0008). 18F-DOPA PET putamenal uptake rose 25-100% only in the active group (P 0.0009 to <0.0001). — [University of Bristol release](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2019/february/gdnf-trial.html); [Cure Parkinson's](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/2019/02/gdnf-trial-results-published/)
- CERE-120 (AAV2-neurturin): Marks WJ et al., Lancet Neurology 2010: 58 patients randomised 2:1 to bilateral putaminal AAV2-neurturin (5.4x10^11 vg) or sham surgery; 12-month OFF UPDRS motor difference -0.31 (95% CI -5.58 to 4.97; P=0.91); SAEs 13/38 vs 4/20; "not superior to sham surgery." Warren Olanow C et al., Annals of Neurology 2015: 51 patients, putamen + substantia nigra delivery, 15-24 months, primary endpoint -7.0 vs -5.2 (P=0.515), "not superior to sham surgery." — [Lancet Neurology 2010](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS1474-4422(10)70254-4/abstract); [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=neurturin%20Parkinson%20Olanow%202015)

**GLP-1 agonists: exenatide, lixisenatide, NLY01, liraglutide (HISTORICAL/RECENT)**
- Exenatide Phase 2 (Athauda D et al., Lancet 2017; NCT01971242): 62 patients (32 exenatide 2 mg weekly, 30 placebo), 48 weeks + 12-week washout; OFF-medication MDS-UPDRS III at 60 weeks improved 1.0 point on exenatide vs worsened 2.1 on placebo, adjusted difference -3.5 (95% CI -6.7 to -0.3; P=0.0318); 6 vs 2 SAEs, none treatment-related. — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=Athauda%20exenatide%202017)
- Exenatide-PD3 (Vijiaratnam N, Foltynie T et al., Lancet 2025;405:627-636; published online Feb 4, 2025; NCT04232969): UK multicentre Phase 3, 194 patients (97/arm) aged 25-80, extended-release exenatide 2 mg weekly vs placebo for 96 weeks. OFF-medication MDS-UPDRS III worsened 5.7 (exenatide) vs 4.5 (placebo) points; no significant difference on primary or secondary endpoints, including DaT-SPECT striatal binding ratio; 9-10 vs 11-12 SAEs (sources differ slightly). Conclusion: "no evidence to support exenatide as a disease-modifying treatment." — [Lancet 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(24)02808-3/fulltext); [Parkinson's UK](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/news/2025/results-phase-3-trial-exenatide-published); [Cure Parkinson's](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/2025/02/exenatide-pd3-results-published/); [EAN Research Paper of the Month](https://www.eanpages.org/2025/03/13/research-paper-of-the-month-exenatide-once-a-week-versus-placebo-as-a-potential-disease-modifying-treatment-for-people-with-parkinsons-disease-in-the-uk/)
- Expression of Concern (Lancet, published June 27, 2026; PubMed 42330995): on May 18, 2026 The Lancet was made aware of findings from an MHRA regulatory inspection at King's College Hospital NHS Foundation Trust, one of the Exenatide-PD3 sites, which found "department-wide" Good Clinical Practice failures in trial management/oversight. The journal issued a formal Expression of Concern ~15 months after publication. — [Lancet Expression of Concern 2026](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)01241-9/fulltext); [PubMed 42330995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42330995/); [Parkinson's Insights](https://parkinsonsinsights.com/news/kax1EPtQOigorqMMA49u); [Clinical Trial Vanguard](https://www.clinicaltrialvanguard.com/article/intel-brief/gcp-breakdown-at-kings-college-hospital-just-clouded-the-most-promising-parkinsons-trial-in-years/)
- LixiPark (Meissner WG et al., NEJM 2024;390:1176-85; NCT03439943; sponsor University Hospital Toulouse, NS-Park network, 21 French centres): Phase 2, 156 patients within 3 years of diagnosis randomised 1:1 to lixisenatide 20 ug/day (reducible to 10 ug) or placebo for 12 months + 2-month washout. MDS-UPDRS III (ON) change: placebo +3.04 (95% CI 1.46-4.62) vs lixisenatide -0.04 (-1.62 to 1.54); difference 3.08 (0.86-5.30; P=0.0068). OFF scores after washout 17.7 vs 20.7. ~50% GI side effects; no significant differences in LEDD or MDS-UPDRS I/II; authors advised against off-label use pending larger trials. — [NEJM 2024](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJMoa2312323); [MDS Moving Along summary](https://www.movementdisorders.org/Moving-Along/2024-issue3/LIXIPARK); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03439943](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03439943); [NeurologyLive on safety concerns](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/potential-safety-concerns-lixisenatide-despite-showing-slowed-motor-disability-progression-phase-2-trial-early-pd)
- NLY01 (pegylated exenatide; McGarry A et al., Lancet Neurology 2024; NCT04154072): 36-week RCT, 255 randomised (85/arm) early untreated PD, 2.5 mg or 5.0 mg; MDS-UPDRS II+III difference vs placebo -0.39 (95% CI -2.96 to 2.18; P=0.77) and 0.36 (-2.28 to 3.00; P=0.79); nausea 39%/58% vs 19%. "Not associated with any improvement." — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=NLY01%20Parkinson)
- Liraglutide (Cedars-Sinai; NCT02953665; n=63; up to 1.8 mg/day; 1 year; April 2017-Aug 2022): primary UPDRS III motor outcome showed no difference; secondary non-motor measures improved (Hogg et al., preprint 2022). — [Alzforum liraglutide](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/liraglutide)
- Semaglutide in PD: Oslo University Hospital GIPD trial (NCT03659682; 120 planned; 1 mg weekly; primary MDS-UPDRS III at 48 months) remains listed as "not yet recruiting"; a Japanese 99-patient, 36-week study (MOST-ABLE; Kimura et al., 2025) began recruiting Nov 2023 with expected completion March 2026. The negative Nov 24, 2025 semaglutide readouts (EVOKE/EVOKE+) were Alzheimer's trials, not PD. — [Alzforum semaglutide](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/semaglutide); [ClinicalTrials.gov API records](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03659682)

**Simvastatin PD STAT (HISTORICAL)**
- Stevens KN, Carroll CB et al. (University of Plymouth), JAMA Neurology 2022: 26-month double-blind futility Phase 2 at 23 English hospitals; 235 participants of moderate severity randomised to simvastatin or placebo for 24 months. Primary analysis (n=178): simvastatin group had an additional 1.52-point deterioration in OFF-medication MDS-UPDRS III at 24 months; futility hypothesis test P=0.006; "no evidence to support proceeding to a phase 3 trial." — [PubMed 36315128](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36315128/); [University of Plymouth](https://www.plymouth.ac.uk/news/parkinsons-drug-trial-gives-definitive-answer-on-possible-statin-treatment)

### Inferences
- The symptomatic-vs-disease-modifying confound has never been resolved by scale-based endpoints: DATATOP's selegiline effect was later attributed largely to symptomatic action; ADAGIO's 1 mg/2 mg discrepancy undermined delayed-start inference; LixiPark's benefit was measured ON medication and GI effects could unblind; the Exenatide-PD3 team used OFF-medication MDS-UPDRS III and still found no effect.
- Phase 2 "wins" in PD neuroprotection (exenatide 2017, GDNF imaging, ambroxol open-label) have a poor record of Phase 3 confirmation, consistent with small-n, single-centre, and post hoc subgroup inflation; the vodobatinib and deferiprone experiences also show that biologically active drugs can accelerate decline.
- Futility-design Phase 2s (NET-PD, QE3, PD STAT, SURE-PD3) have been the most cost-efficient tool; the field is now moving to multi-arm platforms (see Key Question 5) explicitly to avoid single-agent 5-year trials.
- The 2026 Expression of Concern on Exenatide-PD3 does not reverse the negative finding, but it clouds interpretation of the only Phase 3 GLP-1 PD trial and may complicate meta-analysis of GLP-1 data.

## Key Question 2: Alpha-synuclein-targeting therapies (prasinezumab, cinpanemab, vaccines, small molecules, ASOs, buntanetap)

### Takeaway
Passive anti-alpha-synuclein immunotherapy has produced one outright failure (cinpanemab/SPARK) and one borderline, consistently "trending" antibody (prasinezumab), which Roche advanced to a 900-patient Phase 3 (PARAISO, started Nov 2025, primary completion 2029) despite PADOVA missing its primary endpoint (HR 0.84, P=0.066). Small-molecule aggregation inhibitors (minzasolmin) and c-Abl inhibitors failed; the alpha-synuclein ASO (ION464/BIIB101) was dropped by Biogen in Feb 2025; vaccines (UB-312, ACI-7104) remain at Phase 1/2 with target engagement but no efficacy data; buntanetap's Phase 3 missed its primary endpoint and continues only as an open-label safety study.

### Cited Findings

**Prasinezumab (Roche/Prothena) — RECENT/ONGOING**
- PASADENA (Pagano G et al., NEJM 2022; NCT03100149): Phase 2, 316 newly diagnosed PD patients randomised to monthly IV prasinezumab 1,500 mg, 4,500 mg or placebo (initiated June 2017; 64 US/European sites); missed the primary MDS-UPDRS total endpoint at 52 weeks (announced April 2020) but showed a trend toward benefit on MDS-UPDRS Part III and several prespecified motor measures including smartphone endpoints; DaT-SPECT showed minimal change; safe and well tolerated. — [NEJM 2022](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2202867); [Alzforum prasinezumab](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/prasinezumab)
- PASADENA open-label extension (Pagano G et al., Nature Medicine 2024): after 3-4 years (n=271), delayed-start (n=94) and early-start (n=177) groups showed 51% and 65% slower MDS-UPDRS III OFF decline vs an external PPMI-matched comparator (n=303); exploratory. — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=prasinezumab%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:2024); [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/prasinezumab)
- Digital endpoints in PASADENA (Taylor KI et al., npj Digital Medicine 2025): daily smartphone tests + passive monitoring over 2 years; tapping variability and hand-turning showed numeric progression reduction at week 52, persisting (except hand-turning) at week 104. — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=prasinezumab%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:2025)
- PADOVA (Phase 2b; 586 early PD on stable symptomatic monotherapy; 1,500 mg monthly vs placebo; 110 sites; launched May 2021): topline Dec 18, 2024 missed primary endpoint (time to confirmed >=5-point MDS-UPDRS III progression): HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.69-1.01; P=0.0657); median time to progression 61.1 vs 49.7 weeks; prespecified levodopa-treated subgroup (75% of cohort) HR 0.79 (0.63-0.99; nominal P=0.0431), with 30-40% relative reduction in motor progression at 104 weeks; SAEs 12% in both arms. Published Nikolcheva T et al., Lancet 2026;407(10544):2227-2240 (May 30, 2026): "Although PADOVA did not meet the primary endpoint, prespecified exploratory evidence suggests clinical activity." — [Roche media release June 16, 2025](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2025-06-16); [Lancet 2026 PADOVA](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)00865-2/abstract); [Roche AD/PD 2025 topline slides](https://s201.q4cdn.com/351053094/files/doc_events/2025/Apr/01/ADPD-2025-presentation-nikolcheva-PADOVA-topline-results-1.pdf); [MJFF News in Context](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/news-context-mixed-results-two-experimental-drugs-targeting-alpha-synuclein)
- Roche announced the Phase III decision June 16, 2025, citing PADOVA plus OLE data from >750 participants (>500 treated 1.5-5 years) and "first biomarker evidence of prasinezumab impacting the underlying disease biology." — [Roche](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2025-06-16); [Prothena/BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250615297680/en/Prothenas-Partner-Roche-to-Advance-Prasinezumab-into-Phase-III-Development-for-Early-Stage-Parkinsons-Disease)
- PARAISO Phase 3 (NCT07174310; Hoffmann-La Roche): ~900 early-stage PD participants on stable dopaminergic therapy; IV prasinezumab vs placebo; double-blind >=104 weeks then OLE; primary endpoint time to confirmed MDS-UPDRS III progression; start Nov 24, 2025; primary completion est. June 30, 2029; study completion June 2031; 231 sites in 17 countries; recruiting (registry updated Sept 3, 2026). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07174310](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07174310); [Parkinson's News Today](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/phase-3-trial-prasinezumab-early-stage-parkinsons-planned/)
- Prothena Q1 2026 report: at AD/PD 2026 Roche presented a "time saved" analysis of ~2 years' delay in progression over 5 years from the PASADENA OLE, PADOVA OLE data, and neuromelanin/iron-sensitive MRI biomarker data; Roche projects >$3.5B peak sales. — [Prothena SEC exhibit Q1 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1559053/000155905326000016/exhibit991q12026earningsre.htm)
- Critical commentary: Foltynie T, "Prasinezumab: what have we learned from PASADENA and PADOVA?" Lancet 2026 (May; pp. 2150-2151); Russo M et al., "Prasinezumab: a Bayesian perspective on its efficacy," Movement Disorders 2025; Xiao B & Tan EK, npj Parkinson's Disease 2025 urging caution about retrospective subgroup analyses. — [Lancet comment](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)00985-2/abstract); [PMC12006898](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12006898/)

**Cinpanemab (Biogen) SPARK — HISTORICAL (negative)**

### Inferences
- Prasinezumab is the only alpha-synuclein agent with a replicated directional signal (Part III trend in PASADENA; HR 0.84 in PADOVA; levodopa subgroup HR 0.79), but every positive readout is exploratory, so PARAISO (2029) is effectively the first adequately powered test of the extracellular-synuclein hypothesis in PD.
- Cinpanemab's clean failure vs prasinezumab's trend is often attributed to epitope (N-terminal vs C-terminal) and population differences; this is a hypothesis, not demonstrated.
- The combination of minzasolmin, cinpanemab, vodobatinib, risvodetinib and ION464 outcomes/discontinuations in 2022-2025 represents a substantial contraction of the synuclein-targeting pipeline relative to 2020.
- Buntanetap's regulatory path in PD appears stalled: the only active PD study is open-label safety, and the company's 2026 communications emphasise Alzheimer's.

### Gaps
- Exact PASADENA numeric results (per-arm MDS-UPDRS total change, CIs) were not retrievable from abstract text; the NEJM page is the authoritative source.
- The SPARK NCT number was not verified.
- Whether any PD-specific efficacy trial of emrusolmin is planned could not be confirmed.
- No post-2024 primary-endpoint data for buntanetap in PD were found; the FDA interaction history for the PD programme was not located.

## Key Question 3: Genetically targeted therapies (LRRK2, GBA1/GCase, gene therapy)

### Takeaway
2025-2026 was a bad period for precision-medicine PD: the LRRK2 kinase inhibitor BIIB122 failed Phase 2b LUMA (May 2026) despite >90% peripheral kinase inhibition; Biogen dropped the LRRK2 ASO BIIB094 (Feb 2025) even though it lowered CSF LRRK2 by up to 59%; the GCase activator BIA 28-6156 failed Phase 2b ACTIVATE in GBA1-PD (June 2026); venglustat failed earlier (MOVES-PD); and the Canadian ambroxol PDD trial missed its cognitive endpoints (June 2025). The remaining genetic bets are the 330-patient UK ambroxol Phase 3 (ASPro-PD, readout ~2029), Denali's BEACON in LRRK2 carriers (H1 2027), and AAV9-GBA1 gene therapy PR001/LY3884961 (completion 2031).

### Cited Findings

**LRRK2 kinase inhibition — BIIB122/DNL151 (Biogen/Denali)**
- LIGHTHOUSE (Phase 3 in LRRK2-variant carriers; NCT05418673): started Aug 26, 2022, terminated July 2023 by sponsor decision after 7 participants, citing complexity and a projected 2031 completion rather than safety/efficacy. — [ClinicalTrials.gov API, NCT05418673](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05418673); [Alzforum DNL151](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/dnl151)
- LUMA (Phase 2b; NCT05348785): 648 participants aged 30-80 with early PD with or without pathogenic LRRK2 variants; BIIB122 vs placebo 48-144 weeks; primary endpoint time to confirmed worsening on MDS-UPDRS II+III. Topline May 21, 2026: primary and secondary endpoints not met. Exploratory biomarkers: >90% peripheral LRRK2 kinase inhibition; ~30% reduction in CSF phospho-Rab10 in a sub-study; drug levels maintained; acceptable tolerability. Biogen and Denali discontinued BIIB122 in idiopathic PD. — [Biogen/Denali update (StockTitan copy)](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/DNLI/biogen-and-denali-therapeutics-provide-update-on-phase-2b-luma-study-n9aawlne0hpd.html); [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/lrrk2-inhibitor-biib122-fails-slow-early-stage-parkinson-disease); [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/biogen-denali-luma-trial-data/)

**LRRK2 antisense — BIIB094/ION859 (Biogen/Ionis)**

**GBA1 / GCase pathway**
- Ambroxol AiM-PD (Mullin S et al., JAMA Neurology 2020; NCT02941822; UCL): open-label, 23 enrolled/18 completed, escalation to 1.26 g/day for 186 days; CSF ambroxol +156 ng/mL (P<0.001); CSF GCase activity -19% (P=0.04); CSF alpha-synuclein +13% (P=0.01); MDS-UPDRS III improved 6.8 points (P=0.001); no SAEs. — [Europe PMC search](https://europepmc.org/search?query=Mullin%20ambroxol%202020); [NCT02941822](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02941822)

Primary outcomes (ADAS-Cog13 and CGIC at weeks 26/52) not different between groups; ambroxol safe, achieved CSF concentrations and raised CSF GCase; neuropsychiatric symptoms stabilised on ambroxol while worsening on placebo; signals in GBA1 carriers. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/randomized-trial-highlights-uncertainties-ambroxol-improve-cognition-parkinson-disease-dementia); [Western University](https://news.westernu.ca/2025/06/cough-syrup-parkinsons-dementia/); [ResearchGate record of JAMA Neurol paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393175072_Ambroxol_as_a_Treatment_for_Parkinson_Disease_Dementia_A_Randomized_Clinical_Trial)

- Other ambroxol studies: GREAT (Groningen; NCT05830396; Phase 2/3; 80 planned; 1,800 mg/day; MDS-UPDRS III at 60 weeks; recruiting), AMBITIOUS (Italy; NCT05287503; GBA1-PD), Agyany high-dose GBA1-PD Phase 1/2 (NCT06193421; 40 planned). — [ClinicalTrials.gov API query ambroxol+Parkinson](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Parkinson&intr=ambroxol); [NeurologyLive DMT overview](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/promising-disease-modifying-therapies-parkinson-disease)

- Pipeline context: 11 GBA-targeted trials (2%) among 444 PD drug trials 2015-2024. — [Buff et al., JPD 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13442941/)

### Inferences
- LUMA is the clearest "target engaged, clinic unmoved" result in PD since urate: it weakens, but does not eliminate, the LRRK2 kinase hypothesis for idiopathic PD; BEACON in carriers (2027) is now the only remaining clinical test of the genetic-subgroup version.
- The GBA1 pathway has now failed with substrate reduction (venglustat), a small-molecule activator (BIA 28-6156) and partially with the chaperone ambroxol in PDD; ASPro-PD is the last large randomised test of GCase enhancement before gene therapy data (2031).
- Two biotech-sponsored negative readouts (ACTIVATE, LUMA) within a month in mid-2026 are likely to reduce industry appetite for genetically stratified PD trials in the near term.

### Gaps
- Whether BIA 28-6156 is the same molecule as LTI-291 (Bial acquired Lysosomal Therapeutics' assets) could not be verified from fetched sources; treat as unconfirmed.
- The first author of the Canadian ambroxol PDD JAMA Neurology 2025 paper was not confirmed (news pieces credit the Pasternak lab at Western University).
- ACTIVATE sample size and effect sizes were not in fetched text.
- No LRRK2 or GBA1 trial enrolling via SAA-defined prodromal cohorts was identified.

## Key Question 4: Other mechanisms — GLP-1 (see KQ1), NLRP3, immunosuppression, iron, mitochondrial/metabolic (UDCA, terazosin, NR, thiamine, ketogenic), statins, and repurposing (iLCT)

### Takeaway
Repurposed-drug neuroprotection trials have produced one clear harm (deferiprone), several clean futility results (simvastatin, inosine, creatine, CoQ10, pioglitazone, isradipine, exenatide Phase 3) and several mechanistically positive but clinically inconclusive Phase 2s (UDCA, NR, terazosin, azathioprine). The Cure Parkinson's iLCT programme has evaluated 171 agents and spawned 41 trials; its top-ranked candidates (terazosin, telmisartan, UDCA) now form the first arms of the UK platform trial, while NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors are the main new anti-inflammatory class entering Phase 2.

### Cited Findings

- Terazosin / PGK1: epidemiology — Simmering JE et al., JAMA Neurology 2021: Danish cohort 52,365 matched pairs HR 0.88 (95% CI 0.81-0.98); Truven US cohort 94,883 pairs HR 0.63 (0.58-0.69); dose-response; long-duration HR 0.79 (Danish) and 0.46 (Truven). Mechanism: terazosin binding to PGK1 accelerates product release (Riley MJ et al., PNAS 2024). Pilot RCT (Schultz JL et al., Parkinsonism & Related Disorders 2021; 13 participants; 12 weeks): brain ATP (31P-MRS) rose in 4/5 on terazosin vs 2/5 placebo; dizziness led 3 to withdraw. Follow-on MJFF-funded target-engagement study NCT04551040; TZ-DLB pilot NCT04760860 (40 planned); TAME-PD (NCT07288450; University of Iowa, PI Narayanan; Phase 1/2; 100 planned; 1/3/5 mg/day vs placebo 60:40; 26 weeks; primary outcome whole-blood ATP; estimated start Sept 1, 2026; not yet recruiting). — [Europe PMC search (Simmering, Sasane)](https://europepmc.org/search?query=terazosin%20Parkinson); [Science of Parkinson's terazosin pilot](https://scienceofparkinsons.com/2021/12/16/terazosin-2/); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07288450](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07288450)

**Growth-factor gene therapy (ongoing)**
- AB-1005 (AskBio/Bayer; AAV2-GDNF): Phase 1b 18-month data in 11 patients showed stability (mild cohort) and improvement (moderate cohort); FDA RMAT designation Feb 2025; Phase 2 REGENERATE-PD (NCT06285643) ongoing. ### Inferences
- The iLCT pipeline's main 2025-26 outputs (exenatide Phase 3 negative; simvastatin, deferiprone negative; UDCA, azathioprine, NR mechanistically positive but clinically unproven) suggest that target-engagement Phase 2s are necessary but far from sufficient; the EJS ACT-PD platform is, in effect, the iLCT's attempt to run adequately powered confirmatory tests of its surviving top candidates.
- NOPARK (n=410, completed June 2025) is the largest completed mitochondrial/NAD trial and the most important unpublished DMT readout as of late 2026.
- The NLRP3 class is at a stage comparable to GLP-1 agonists in ~2016: strong biology, small uncontrolled human signals, and the first RCTs just starting.

## Key Question 5: Trial infrastructure and design innovations — EJS ACT-PD platform, SAA/NSD-ISS biomarker enrolment, digital endpoints, funding landscape

### Takeaway
The UK's GBP 26 million EJS ACT-PD multi-arm multi-stage platform (1,600 participants, 3-year MDS-UPDRS I+II endpoint, first arms terazosin, telmisartan and UDCA) launched in 2025 as the first PD MAMS trial; a biological definition and staging system for "neuronal alpha-synuclein disease" anchored on CSF seed amplification assay (Simuni et al., Lancet Neurology 2024) now enables prodromal enrolment and has been validated in PPMI, PASADENA and SPARK baselines; MJFF reports >$3 billion invested and ~$698 million projected for 2026, with PPMI exceeding 60,000 participants and ASAP-funded GP2 integrating >240 cohorts.

### Cited Findings

**EJS ACT-PD platform trial (UK) — ONGOING**
- Initiative convened >90 stakeholders since 2021, led by Prof Tom Foltynie (UCL) and Prof Camille Carroll (Newcastle); first MAMS platform for PD in the UK. — [Cure Parkinson's EJS ACT-PD](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/research/research-projects/ejs-act-pd/); [MDS Abstracts](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/ejs-act-pd-an-ongoing-initiative-to-support-innovation-and-sustainability-of-a-multi-arm-multi-stage-platform-trial-for-parkinsons-disease/)
- Design (Zeissler M-L et al. and the EJS ACT-PD Consortium, Brain Communications 2026;8(5):fcag349): starts with placebo + 2 active arms with a 4th arm added after 1 year; 400 participants per arm; 3 years of treatment; primary outcome change in rate of progression on MDS-UPDRS Parts I+II summed, powered for a 30% reduction; four stages (stages 1-2 activity, 3-4 efficacy) with early closure of ineffective arms and re-randomisation permitted; 43 UK sites with 15 centrally funded staff; funded by the Edmond J Safra Philanthropic Foundation. — [Brain Communications 2026](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/article/8/5/fcag349/8826396)
- Treatment selection (Gonzalez-Robles C et al., Movement Disorders 2025): 293 candidate interventions identified from iLCT dossiers, the "Hope List," registries and experts; 63 passed go/no-go; 52 scored (0-30 points on preclinical, pharmacological and clinical evidence) across five mechanistic groups; shortlist in descending order: telmisartan, terazosin, UDCA, istradefylline, lovastatin, deferiprone, Neuroaspis PLP10, AZD3241; selected first arms: telmisartan, terazosin and UDCA (UDCA introduced after year 1); lovastatin reserve. — [Movement Disorders 2025 (PMC12273612)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12273612)
- Scale: launch 2025; 1,600 participants across 3 therapies vs placebo at ~40 UK sites; GBP 26 million programme. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/ejs-act-pd-platform-trial-test-multiple-potential-disease-modifying-treatments-parkinsons); [Cure Parkinson's](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/research/research-projects/ejs-act-pd/)
- Background methodology paper: "Towards a multi-arm multi-stage platform trial of disease modifying approaches in Parkinson's disease" (2023). — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/368921859_Towards_a_multi-arm_multi-stage_platform_trial_of_disease_modifying_approaches_in_Parkinson's_disease)

**Biomarker-anchored staging and prodromal enrolment**
- Simuni T et al., Lancet Neurology 2024 (PMID 38267190): biological definition of neuronal alpha-synuclein disease (NSD) anchored on S (pathological alpha-synuclein by CSF seed amplification assay) and D (dopaminergic dysfunction); NSD Integrated Staging System stages 0 (SNCA variants), 1A (S only), 1B (S+D) without symptoms, 2 subtle signs, 2B-6 progressive functional impairment; "intended for research use only ... to enable interventional trials at early disease stages." — [PubMed 38267190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267190/)

**Funding landscape**
- MJFF: "invested more than $3 billion"; anticipates $698 million of PD research funding in 2026 ("more than twice the U.S. government's investment"); PPMI >60,000 participants; "180+ drugs" in active or upcoming human testing. — [MJFF Our Impact](https://www.michaeljfox.org/our-impact)

### Inferences
- The field's 2024-2026 design shift is toward (a) time-to-event endpoints (PADOVA, LUMA, PARAISO, BHV-8000) rather than mean MDS-UPDRS change, (b) enrolment on stable symptomatic therapy to avoid the "washout" and dropout problems of treatment-naive designs, and (c) shared-placebo platforms; however, no DMT trial yet enrols purely on SAA positivity, so the NSD-ISS is enabling rather than yet deployed.
- With 3-year endpoints, EJS ACT-PD's first stage-1 activity readouts are unlikely before ~2028; its real contribution in 2026 is infrastructure (43 sites, central staff) rather than results.

## Key Question 6: Current (2025-2026) state of the pipeline — numbers, Phase 3 DMTs, expected readouts

### Takeaway
As of the Jan 2025 ClinicalTrials.gov census, 133 PD drug trials were active (70 DMT, 63 symptomatic; 16 Phase 3), and only 9 Phase 3 DMT trials were started in the entire 2015-2024 decade. In 2026 the DMT Phase 3/pivotal set is small: prasinezumab PARAISO (2029), ambroxol ASPro-PD (2029), BHV-8000 Phase 2/3 (2027), the EJS ACT-PD platform (3-year endpoint), and buntanetap's open-label safety study (2029); 2025-2026 saw more major negatives (Exenatide-PD3, LUMA, ACTIVATE, PROSEEK publication, risvodetinib, AZA-PD primary, ambroxol-PDD) than positives (PADOVA exploratory signals only).

### Cited Findings
- 10-year pipeline census (Buff S, Pilcicka A, Pitzer K, Fiske B, Stott SRW, McFarthing K; Journal of Parkinson's Disease 2026, online May 26, 2026): 444 drug trials initiated 2015-2024 (Phase 1 186/42%; Phase 2 203/46%; Phase 3 55/12%); 279 (63%) symptomatic vs 165 (37%) DMT; 230 (52%) novel agents, 145 (33%) repurposed, 69 (15%) reformulations; total enrolment 39,238; only 9 Phase 3 DMT trials (5% of Phase 3 activity); 133 trials active at end-2024 (48 Phase 1, 69 Phase 2, 16 Phase 3; 70 DMT, 63 ST); DMT trials >50% of new registrations 2022-2024; only 76/224 (34%) completed trials had posted results; 14 FDA approvals 2015-2024, all symptomatic; "there are still no treatments to modify the course of the disease." ### Inferences
- Counting only randomised, placebo-controlled trials with a clinical primary endpoint powered for disease modification, there are currently three industry/academic pivotal DMT programmes in idiopathic PD (PARAISO, ASPro-PD, BHV-8000) plus one platform (EJS ACT-PD); none will report before 2027 and the two Phase 3s not before 2029.
- The 2026 McFarthing/Buff census and the 2024-2026 failures together imply that the DMT pipeline is broad at Phase 1-2 (70 active DMT trials) but has a conversion bottleneck at Phase 3 that platform trials and biomarker enrolment are intended to relieve.

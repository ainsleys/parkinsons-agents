## Key Question 1: Clinical diagnosis — MDS criteria, UK Brain Bank criteria, accuracy, misdiagnosis vs atypical parkinsonisms, rating scales and their limitations

### Takeaway
Clinical diagnosis of PD remains ~80% accurate against autopsy (Rizzo 2016), has not improved meaningfully over 25 years, and is weakest early in disease; the 2015 MDS criteria (Postuma 2015) improved on UK Brain Bank criteria (94.5% sensitivity / 88.5% specificity for "probable PD" vs expert diagnosis in the 2018 validation) but are still clinical constructs, which is the central motivation for the biomarker-based redefinitions of 2024.

### Cited Findings
- The MDS Clinical Diagnostic Criteria for PD (Postuma RB et al., Movement Disorders, 2015) were designed to "codify/reproduce the expert clinical diagnostic process" and standardize diagnosis in research and clinical settings. Structure: parkinsonism (bradykinesia plus rest tremor and/or rigidity) is the core; then three feature categories—absolute exclusion criteria (rule out PD), red flags (must be counterbalanced by supportive criteria), and supportive criteria (increase confidence). Two certainty levels: "clinically established PD" (maximizes specificity, at the cost of sensitivity) and "clinically probable PD" (balances sensitivity and specificity). — [Postuma 2015, Movement Disorders](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.26424); [UPenn summary PDF of criteria](https://www.med.upenn.edu/digitalneuropathologylab/assets/user-content/documents/educational-resources/mds-clinical-diagnostic-criteria-for-parkinson's-disease.pdf)
- Validation of MDS criteria (Postuma RB et al., Movement Disorders, 2018): of 434 patients diagnosed with PD by experts, 94.5% met MDS "probable PD" (5.5% false-negative); of 192 non-PD patients, 88.5% were identified as non-PD (11.5% false-positive); overall accuracy for probable PD 92.6%. Sensitivity and specificity were both higher than the UK Brain Bank criteria; specificity improved with longer disease duration. — [Postuma 2018, Movement Disorders](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mds.27362)
- Meta-analysis of clinical diagnostic accuracy (Rizzo G et al., Neurology, 2016): 20 studies, 11 with pathologic gold standard; pooled accuracy 80.6% (95% credible interval 75.2–85.3%) in the pathology-confirmed studies. Accuracy 73.8% when diagnosis made mainly by non-experts; for movement-disorder experts 79.6% at initial assessment rising to 83.9% after follow-up refinement. Authors concluded validity of clinical diagnosis "is not satisfying," had "not significantly improved in the last 25 years," is worst in early disease when levodopa response is undefined and alternative-diagnosis hallmarks have not emerged, and that misclassification should be factored into trial sample sizes; "imaging and biomarkers are urgently needed." — [Rizzo 2016, Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000002350)
- Follow-up review "Clinical Diagnostic Accuracy of Parkinson's Disease: Where Do We Stand?" (Movement Disorders, 2023) revisits this problem. — [Movement Disorders 2023](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.29317)
- Population-based accuracy: "The accuracy of the clinical diagnosis of Parkinson disease. The HUNT study" (Journal of Neurology, 2018). — [J Neurol 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-018-8969-6)
- Newer autopsy-based re-evaluations: "Revisiting the 2015 MDS diagnostic criteria for Parkinson disease: insights from autopsy-confirmed cases" (npj Parkinson's Disease, 2025); "Validating the Accuracy of Parkinson's Disease Clinical Diagnosis: A UK Brain Bank Case–Control Study" (2025, PMC); "Stability and Accuracy of a Diagnosis of Parkinson Disease Over 10 Years" (Neurology, 2025). — [npj PD 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01206-6); [UK Brain Bank case-control 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12082010/); [Neurology 2025](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000213499)
- The misdiagnosis problem is chiefly confusion with atypical parkinsonisms (MSA, PSP, CBD, DLB), essential tremor, and vascular/drug-induced parkinsonism. DAT imaging separates degenerative parkinsonism from essential tremor (sensitivity 95–97%, specificity 93–100%) but "is not effective for differentiating PD from atypical parkinsonian syndromes." — [Diagnostic accuracy of 18F-FP-CIT PET for clinically uncertain parkinsonian syndrome, Sci Rep 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-42135-9); [PET vs SPECT review, Clin Transl Imaging 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40336-020-00386-w)
- NfL is consistently higher in MSA and PSP than PD, reflecting more aggressive axonal degeneration; a 2020 meta-analysis reported pooled sensitivity 82% and specificity 91% for NfL distinguishing MSA from PD. — [Aden 2026, Int J Clin Pract review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/ijcp/7796155); [NfL Bayesian network meta-analysis, J Neurol 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-025-13051-x)
- A 2026 Nature Reviews Neurology review, "Towards biomarker-based diagnosis of Parkinson disease," frames the field's shift away from purely clinical diagnosis. — [Nat Rev Neurol 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41582-026-01185-z)

Rating scales:
- The MDS-UPDRS (MDS revision of the Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale; Goetz CG et al., Movement Disorders, 2008) has four parts (I non-motor experiences of daily living; II motor experiences of daily living; III motor examination; IV motor complications). Psychometric reviews report adequate floor/ceiling effects for Parts I–III but a poor floor effect for Part IV (36.7% scoring at the lowest level) and no ceiling effect. — [Shirley Ryan AbilityLab MDS-UPDRS summary](https://www.sralab.org/rehabilitation-measures/movement-disorder-society-sponsored-unified-parkinsons-disease-rating-scale-revision)
- Rasch analysis of PPMI data ("Does the MDS-UPDRS provide the precision to assess progression in early Parkinson's disease? Learnings from the PPMI cohort," J Neurol 2019) found neither Part II nor Part III is well targeted to early PD: marked floor effects for most items and a clear item gap at very mild motor severity, limiting sensitivity to change in early disease/trials. — [PMC6647182](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6647182/)
- A 2025 npj Parkinson's Disease paper proposes a limb bradykinesia/rigidity sub-score to improve Part III performance in early-stage PD, again citing floor effects and a coverage gap at the mild end. — [npj PD 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01072-2)
- The MDS-UPDRS sum score is "discrete and asymmetric with ceiling or floor effects, and items have varying sensitivities to change," motivating longitudinal item-response-theory models for trials. — [Luo 2021, Mov Disord Clin Pract](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mdc3.13311)
- The Hoehn & Yahr scale (1967; modified version adds stages 1.5 and 2.5) is a coarse 5-stage staging anchored on unilateral vs bilateral involvement, postural instability and dependency; the UPDRS family "favors assessment of moderate and severe impairments, with floor effect limiting the scale's utility in early stages." — [Multi-domain scales chapter (sralab PDF)](https://www.sralab.org/sites/default/files/2017-07/9781907673870-c2.pdf); [MDS-UPDRS scores by Hoehn & Yahr stage, UCL/MDCP](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10067420/1/MDCP_16-0278.R1.pdf)

### Inferences
- The ~20% autopsy-discordance rate, concentrated in early disease, directly motivates both biomarker development (SAA) and the "biological definition" proposals: clinical criteria cannot anchor disease-modifying trials in the earliest stages where therapies are most likely to work.
- The MDS 2018 validation used expert diagnosis—not autopsy—as the gold standard, so the 92.6% figure is an upper bound on true accuracy; the 2025 autopsy-based re-evaluations exist precisely to address this.
- Floor effects in MDS-UPDRS Parts II/III in early PD mean the scale is a poor primary outcome for prodromal/early-stage disease-modification trials—another driver toward digital and biological progression markers.

### Gaps
- Full details (sample sizes, exact accuracy figures) from the 2025 autopsy-based re-evaluations of MDS criteria (npj PD 2025; Neurology 2025) were not retrieved.
- UK Brain Bank (Queen Square) criteria original accuracy figures (Hughes 1992 ~76%; Hughes 2002 ~90% in specialist hands) are well known but were not directly retrieved in this session; cite with caution.
- Specific misdiagnosis rates for each atypical parkinsonism (e.g., % of clinically diagnosed PD later found to be MSA/PSP at autopsy) were not pulled from primary sources here.

## Key Question 2: Alpha-synuclein seed amplification assays (SAA / RT-QuIC) — PPMI findings, performance by matrix, limitations

### Takeaway
Siderowf et al. (Lancet Neurology, 2023) established CSF αSyn-SAA as a high-accuracy (87.7% sensitivity, 96.3% specificity) diagnostic and prodromal biomarker in PPMI, with the critical caveat that LRRK2 carriers and normosmic PD have much lower positivity; skin biopsy immunostaining (Gibbons, JAMA 2024) achieved >92% detection across synucleinopathies, and the FDA issued a Letter of Support (2024) endorsing CSF SAA as a susceptibility/risk biomarker for trials. Blood and saliva assays are advancing (80–85% and ~75% sensitivity respectively) but are not yet standardized; SAAs remain essentially qualitative.

### Cited Findings
Siderowf 2023 (PPMI):
- Cross-sectional analysis of 1,123 PPMI participants: 545 PD, 163 healthy controls, 54 SWEDD (scans without evidence of dopaminergic deficit), 51 prodromal (hyposmia or RBD), and 310 non-manifesting carriers of LRRK2/GBA variants. — [MDS Moving Along summary of PPMI findings](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/Moving-Along/2023-issue-2/Findings-from-the-PPMI-Study); [Physician's Weekly summary](https://www.physiciansweekly.com/post/%CE%B1-synuclein-seed-amplification-assay-classifies-parkinson-disease)
- Overall sensitivity for PD 87.7%; specificity vs healthy controls 96.3%. — same sources
- Sensitivity 98.6% in sporadic PD with typical olfactory deficit; 78.3% in sporadic PD without olfactory deficit; 67.5% in LRRK2-PD; only 34.7% in LRRK2-PD with normal olfaction. — same sources
- Most prodromal participants (hyposmia/RBD) were SAA-positive despite no PD diagnosis, suggesting detectable aggregates precede clinical diagnosis. — [MDS Moving Along](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/Moving-Along/2023-issue-2/Findings-from-the-PPMI-Study)
- Follow-on PPMI work: "α-Synuclein SAA amplification parameters and the risk of progression in prodromal PD" (Neurology, 2025) explores whether kinetic parameters (e.g., time-to-threshold) carry prognostic information; "Predicting CSF α-synuclein SAA status from demographics and clinical data" (Neurology Open Access, 2024/25). — [Neurology 2025](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000210279); [Neurology Open Access](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WN9.0000000000000008)
- Longitudinal cohort (medRxiv Dec 2024): "Diagnostic and prognostic value of alpha-synuclein SAA in PD." — [medRxiv 2024](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.03.24318422.full.pdf)
- SAA status now being linked to imaging: "Diffusion MRI and α-synuclein SAA status in PD" (Chiu et al., Annals of Neurology, 2026). — [Ann Neurol 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.78252)
- Genetic subgroups: "Analysis of α-synuclein SAA in carriers of GBA1 and LRRK2 pathogenic variants" (PMC, 2025) extends the LRRK2 heterogeneity finding. — [PMC13347577](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347577/)

Regulatory endorsement:
- The FDA issued a "Letter of Support" (late summer 2024; press coverage Sept 5 2024) to the Critical Path for Parkinson's (CPP) Consortium of the Critical Path Institute encouraging use of CSF αSyn-SAA to improve efficiency of trials targeting early intervention in α-synuclein-defined diseases; the evidence base came substantially from PPMI. — [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/fda-issues-letter-support-encouraging-use-synuclein-based-biomarker-asyn-saa-clinical-trials); [C-Path](https://c-path.org/fda-issues-letter-of-support-encouraging-use-of-synuclein-based-biomarker-%CE%B1syn-saa-in-clinical-trials-in-parkinsons-and-related-diseases/); [FDA letter PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/181368/download); [BusinessWire/Amprion](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240905546394/en/FDA-Issues-Letter-of-Support-for--Synuclein-Seed-Amplification-Assay-The-Core-Technology-Used-in-Amprions-First-Commercial-Test)
- Formal publication: Stephenson D, Concha-Marambio L, Lanz T, Merchant K, Brockmann K, et al., on behalf of CPP, "Regulatory endorsement for the application of α-synuclein SAA as a susceptibility and risk biomarker for clinical trials targeting synucleinopathies," Journal of Parkinson's Disease, 2026. — [JPD 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X261463936); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1141500); [MedicalXpress Aug 2026](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-08-fda-advances-biomarker-earlier-precise.html)

Skin biopsy (immunohistochemistry for phosphorylated α-synuclein; Syn-One Test):
- Gibbons CH et al., "Skin Biopsy Detection of Phosphorylated α-Synuclein in Patients With Synucleinopathies," JAMA 2024;331(15):1298–1306 (April 16, 2024). NIH-sponsored, cross-sectional, 30 academic and community sites. Positivity rates: PD 92.7%, MSA 98.2%, DLB 96.0%, pure autonomic failure 100%; controls 3.3% positive. — [JAMA 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2816441); [CND Life Sciences release](https://cndlifesciences.com/jama-news-release/); [The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/diagnosing-synucleinopathies-with-skin-biopsies-73233)
- The Syn-One Test (CND Life Sciences) uses three 3-mm punch biopsies (one neck, two leg) stained for phosphorylated α-synuclein; it is commercially available in the US as a lab-developed test. — [CND Syn-One](https://cndlifesciences.com/syn-one-test/)

Performance by matrix (SAA):
- Systematic review and network meta-analysis (Clinics and Practice, 2025; studies 2010–2025): overall αSyn-SAA sensitivity 86%, specificity 92% for PD vs controls; CSF, skin, blood, and extracellular vesicles (ECV) yielded the highest accuracy (approaching or exceeding 90%); saliva SAA 74.70% sensitivity / 97.92% specificity; olfactory mucosa and GI/oral samples "limited utility." — [Clin Pract 2025 NMA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12192484/)
- Review of SAAs in body fluids and tissues (Journal of Neurochemistry, 2025/26): CSF and skin biopsies show the highest and most robust sensitivity (90–95%); blood moderate-to-high (80–85%); olfactory mucosa lowest (45–56%). — [J Neurochem review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jnc.70435)
- A uniform SAA protocol applied across CSF, skin, olfactory mucosa and urine (Acta Neuropathologica Communications, 2025). — [Acta Neuropathol Commun 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40478-025-02034-8)
- Serum: Okuzumi A et al., "Propagative α-synuclein seeds as serum biomarkers for synucleinopathies," Nature Medicine 2023 — immunoprecipitation-based RT-QuIC (IP/RT-QuIC) detects minute amounts of serum seeds; fibril morphology explored for discriminating synucleinopathies; accompanying News & Views in Nature Medicine. An author correction was issued (2025). Enhanced serum SAA reported in Translational Neurodegeneration 2025. — [Nat Med 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02358-9); [News & Views](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02402-8); [Correction](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11835715/); [Transl Neurodegener 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40035-025-00488-3)
- Blood neuron-derived extracellular vesicles: Kluge A et al., "Detection of neuron-derived pathological α-synuclein in blood," Brain 2022 — pathological α-syn from neuronal EVs increased in all PD patients and amplifiable by seeding assay; editorial "Extracellular vesicles and seeding amplification: a step closer to a PD blood test" (Brain 2022). Critical appraisal: Bernhardt et al., "α-Synuclein SAAs from blood-based extracellular vesicles in PD: an evaluation of the evidence," Movement Disorders 2024, with Kluge response. — [Kluge Brain 2022](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article-pdf/145/9/3058/49450184/awac115.pdf); [Brain editorial](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/145/9/2946/6659013); [Bernhardt Mov Disord 2024](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mds.29923); [Kluge reply](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mds.29919)
- Mechanistic/early detection: "Age-dependent peripheral dissemination of α-synuclein seeds: mechanistic basis for blood-based early detection of PD" (Acta Neuropathol Commun, 2026). — [Acta Neuropathol Commun 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40478-026-02322-x)
- Conference coverage (Alzforum) of SAA in hyposmic/prodromal cohorts: "Synuclein Assay Passes the Sniff Test—What of Other Seeds?" — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/conference-coverage/synuclein-assay-passes-sniff-test-what-other-seeds)

### Inferences
- The LRRK2/normosmia sensitivity gap implies a subset of clinically defined PD either lacks Lewy pathology or has pathology undetectable by current CSF SAA—this is the empirical basis for the "S−" category debate in the 2024 biological frameworks (10–15% of clinical PD).
- Skin biopsy IHC (Gibbons) and CSF SAA are converging on >90% sensitivity but neither can yet reliably separate PD from MSA or DLB at the individual level (serum fibril-morphology and kinetic-parameter approaches are attempts to do so).
- SAAs are binary/qualitative; attempts at quantification (amplification kinetics, time-to-threshold) exist but have not been validated as progression markers, which limits their use as pharmacodynamic endpoints.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full text of the 2025 GBA1/LRRK2 SAA analysis (PMC13347577; bot-blocked), so exact positivity percentages for GBA1 carriers are not reported here.
- Exact sample sizes within the Gibbons JAMA 2024 analysis (enrolled vs analyzed) were not captured; the percentages above are as reported in the abstract/press.
- No head-to-head study of Syn-One skin IHC versus CSF SAA in the same patients was located in this session.
- Quantitative SAA progression data remain sparse; no validated quantitative SAA readout for tracking disease was found.

## Key Question 3: Biological definitions and staging — SynNeurGe vs NSD-ISS, differences, and the controversy

### Takeaway
Two research frameworks published side by side in Lancet Neurology (February 2024) propose to define PD biologically: SynNeurGe (Höglinger, Lang et al.) classifies by Synuclein (S), Neurodegeneration (N), Genetics (G) and clinical status (C) while keeping the name "Parkinson's disease" and no staging; NSD-ISS (Simuni, Chahine, Marek et al.) creates a new entity, "neuronal α-synuclein disease" (S+ required), with stages 0–6 anchored on biomarkers then functional impairment. Both are research-only. Critics (Darweesh/Bloem/Sampaio; Obeso/Calabresi; Espay et al. 2025) object to diagnosing asymptomatic people from a single biomarker, lumping iRBD with DLB, excluding S− PD, and to NSD-ISS stage "regression" on levodopa (41% of Stage 4 patients in PPMI).

### Cited Findings
Frameworks:
- Both proposals appeared in Lancet Neurology, February 2024. NSD-ISS: led by Kenneth Marek (Institute for Neurodegenerative Disorders), co-first authors Tanya Simuni (Northwestern) and Lana Chahine (Pittsburgh), with FDA regulators and patient advocates as co-authors. SynNeurGe: first author Günter Höglinger (DZNE Munich), senior author Anthony Lang (Toronto). — [Alzforum: Two Proposals Pave the Way](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way)
- NSD-ISS stages: Stage 0 = carriers of fully penetrant SNCA variants even if biomarker-negative; Stage 1A = asymptomatic with positive α-syn seeding biomarker (S+); Stage 1B = S+ plus dopaminergic dysfunction (D+, typically DAT-SPECT); Stage 2 = emergence of subtle clinical signs/symptoms (motor or non-motor: hyposmia, RBD, constipation, hypotension, depression, MCI) without functional impairment; Stages 3–6 = progressive functional impairment driven by motor, cognitive, or other non-motor deficits. Formula: NSD = S+ (α-syn pathology) + D+ (dopaminergic dysfunction). — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way); [Cell Death & Disease 2025 review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-025-08076-z)
- SynNeurGe components: S (α-syn pathology by biomarker, S+/S−); N (dopaminergic neurodegeneration by imaging, N+/N−); G (genetics: GF+ fully penetrant variants—SNCA multiplications/missense, PRKN, PINK1, PARK7; Gp+ predisposing—LRRK2, VPS35, CHCHD2, GBA1; G−); C (clinical, C+/C−, spanning prodromal to late without sequential ordering). — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way)
- Key differences: (1) SynNeurGe includes S− individuals with dopaminergic loss and clinical PD; NSD-ISS excludes them (estimated 10–15% of clinical PD). (2) SynNeurGe classifies heterogeneity without redefining the disease; NSD-ISS creates a new diagnostic entity centered on pathology. (3) NSD-ISS has sequential stages 0–6; SynNeurGe "explicitly lacks staging due to insufficient longitudinal evidence." (4) SynNeurGe preserves PD/DLB/PDD terminology; NSD-ISS replaces them with "neuronal synuclein disease." — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way); [Cell Death Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-025-08076-z)
- Höglinger GU & Lang AE, "SynNeurGe: The road ahead for a biological definition of Parkinson's disease," Journal of Parkinson's Disease, 2025 — authors' follow-up. — [JPD 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X241298194)
- NSD-ISS empirical application: "Neuronal alpha-Synuclein Disease Integrated Staging System performance in PPMI, PASADENA, and SPARK baseline cohorts" (medRxiv 2024 / PMC). — [PMC11419206](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11419206/)
- Simuni T et al., "Neuronal α-Synuclein Disease Stage Progression over 5 Years," Movement Disorders 2025 (preprint medRxiv Dec 2024). — [Mov Disord 2025](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.30191); [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.17.24319172.full.pdf)
- Both author groups and the MDS state the frameworks are for research only; routine clinical application is "premature and explicitly discouraged." — [Cell Death Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-025-08076-z); [MDS Scientific Issues post](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/Scientific-Issues/posts/Exploring-the-path-towards-a-biological-definition-of-PD-and-its-widespread-implications.htm); [MDS Moving Along 2024: Navigating Controversies](https://www.movementdisorders.org/Moving-Along/2024-issue-1/Exploring-Advancement-of-PD-Classification-and-Staging.htm)

Controversy and critiques:
- Lang (SynNeurGe senior author) warned that excluding S− patients "could relegate them to a research backwater, where less attention, and less funding, are funneled in their direction," potentially excluding them from trials. — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way)
- Darweesh, Bloem and Sampaio (Lancet Neurology correspondence, "Concerns with the new biological research criteria for synucleinopathy," 2024) noted the frameworks "allow for a diagnosis based on underlying pathology, even in asymptomatic individuals," raising concerns about premature clinical application mirroring Alzheimer's disease controversies; Bloem's group questioned "defining a disease based solely on a single biomarker or pathology, given that many people with Lewy bodies never develop clinical symptoms," and called for "clear guidance on the settings in which the proposed PD classification is applied, and on how to communicate a research diagnosis." — [Lancet Neurology correspondence](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(24)00214-X/fulltext); [Alzforum summary](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way)
- Obeso and Calabresi worried that unifying PD and DLB under synuclein pathology might "inadvertently lump together clinical syndromes which... might be fundamentally different in nature." — [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/biological-definition-parkinsons-disease-two-proposals-pave-way)
- Clinical-heterogeneity objection: an iRBD patient may remain decades without other features while a DLB patient has rapid progression and shortened life expectancy, yet both are placed under the NSD umbrella—described as "a great disservice for patients." — [Cell Death Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-025-08076-z)
- Espay AJ et al., "Refutation of the αSyn-SAA-Based Staging for Parkinson's Progression (NSD-ISS)," Movement Disorders, June 2025: levodopa treatment produced "stage regression" in nearly 50% of the PPMI cohort—8% of Stage 3 and 41% of Stage 4 patients shifted to earlier stages; authors argue a valid staging system must show unidirectional progression and reflect natural-history distribution, which NSD-ISS fails. — [Espay 2025 Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.30269); [PubMed 40579848](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40579848/)
- Simuni et al. reply (Movement Disorders 2025): characterized the critique not as refutation but largely an endorsement highlighting need for further research to refine NSD-ISS. — [Simuni reply](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.30272); [PMC12371628](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12371628/)
- Onder H et al., "Reconsidering the Clinical Foundations of NSD-ISS in PD Staging," Movement Disorders 2025. — [Mov Disord 2025](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70061)
- Reviews framing the debate: "Are we ready for a biological diagnosis of Parkinson's Disease?" (Cell Death & Disease, 2025); "Layered classification in Parkinson's disease: a content analysis of clinical, prodromal, and biological criteria evolution" (2025); "From phenotype to biology: a multi-modal roadmap of biofluid, tissue, imaging, and digital biomarkers in PD" (Translational Neurodegeneration, 2026). — [Cell Death Dis 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-025-08076-z); [Layered classification 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13538833/); [Transl Neurodegener 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40035-026-00558-0)

### Inferences
- The two frameworks are more complementary than competing in practice: SynNeurGe is a classification (useful for describing heterogeneity and S− cases); NSD-ISS is a staging system (useful for trial enrichment and regulatory endpoints). The FDA Letter of Support for CSF SAA effectively operationalizes the "S+" anchor shared by both.
- The levodopa stage-regression finding exposes that NSD-ISS Stages 3–6 are anchored on clinical/functional scales (MDS-UPDRS-derived), inheriting the symptomatic-treatment confound; a biology-only staging would require validated progression biomarkers (e.g., quantitative SAA, DAT, neuromelanin), which do not yet exist at scale.
- The ethical debate parallels the amyloid/tau "biological Alzheimer's" controversy: labeling asymptomatic SAA-positive individuals as having a "disease" has implications for insurance, employment, psychological burden, and trial consent, with no disease-modifying therapy available to offer them.

### Gaps
- Direct text of the Lancet Neurology correspondence (403 error) and the Espay paper (403) could not be fetched; details are from Alzforum and search-engine summaries and PubMed. Authorship of the Lancet Neurology letter is inferred from Alzforum (Darweesh, Bloem, Sampaio) and should be verified.
- Systematic survey data on patient perspectives (e.g., proportion of at-risk individuals who want to know SAA status) were not found in this session; the "patient perspective" content above is from clinician-critic commentary and the fact that patient advocates co-authored NSD-ISS.
- No 2026 formal revision or merger of the two frameworks was identified.

## Key Question 4: Imaging — DAT-SPECT, FDG-PET, neuromelanin MRI, nigrosome-1 "swallow tail," free-water imaging, and α-synuclein PET tracers

### Takeaway
DAT-SPECT reliably confirms presynaptic dopaminergic deficit (95–97% sensitivity vs essential tremor) but cannot distinguish PD from atypical parkinsonisms, for which FDG-PET pattern analysis (~91% sensitivity/specificity for atypical syndromes) is more useful; nigrosome-1 "swallow tail" loss on SWI has pooled sensitivity 0.94/specificity 0.90 (AUC 0.98), and neuromelanin MRI outperforms free-water imaging in early PD. The long-sought α-synuclein PET tracer finally produced signal in idiopathic PD in 2024–2026 ([18F]C05-05, [11C]MK-7337, MODAG-005/-009), with at least seven candidates in human testing, though none is validated for clinical use.

### Cited Findings
DAT imaging and FDG-PET:
- DAT-SPECT (123I-ioflupane/DaTscan) and 18F-FP-CIT PET: high accuracy for differentiating degenerative parkinsonism from essential tremor (sensitivity 95–97%, specificity 93–100%); SWEDD frequency of 3.6–19.6% in PD drug trials/imaging studies has raised questions about early-stage sensitivity (or clinical misdiagnosis); DAT imaging "is not effective for differentiating PD from atypical parkinsonian syndromes." — [Sci Rep 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-42135-9); [Clin Transl Imaging 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40336-020-00386-w); [dual-phase FP-CIT PET, PMC8298455](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8298455/)
- FDG-PET in atypical parkinsonism (EJNMMI Research, 2023): classification sensitivity/specificity MSA 1.00/0.91; DLB/PDD 0.81/0.97; CBD/PSP 0.62/0.97; overall correct classification 74%. A preliminary meta-analysis of visual FDG-PET reads for atypical parkinsonian syndromes gave sensitivity 91.4%, specificity 90.6%. — [EJNMMI Res 2023](https://ejnmmires.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13550-023-01025-x); [JNM 2017 review](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/58/12/1888)

MRI markers:
- Nigrosome-1 / swallow-tail sign: Schwarz ST et al., PLoS One 2014, first described loss of the "swallow tail" appearance of dorsolateral substantia nigra on 3T SWI in PD, reporting sensitivity 100%, specificity 95%, accuracy 96% in their case-control sample. — [Schwarz 2014 PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0093814)
- Meta-analysis (Parkinsonism & Related Disorders, 2020): 19 studies, 1,508 participants (903 PD, 605 HC); pooled sensitivity 0.94 (95% CI 0.93–0.96), specificity 0.90 (0.88–0.92), AUC 0.98. — [PRD 2020 meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1353802020306398)
- Tseriotis et al., "Is the Swallow Tail Sign a Useful Imaging Biomarker in Clinical Neurology? A Systematic Review," Movement Disorders Clinical Practice, 2025 — [MDCP 2025](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mdc3.14304)
- Nigrosome and neuromelanin imaging to differentiate PD from other parkinsonisms (review, 2023). — [PMC9996486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9996486/)
- "Clinical MRI Biomarkers to Differentiate Parkinson Disease from Its Mimics," Radiology, 2025. — [Radiology 2025](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.242044)
- Neuromelanin vs free-water (comparative MRI study, 2026): neuromelanin-sensitive MRI AUC 0.729–0.791 vs free-water AUC 0.605–0.62 across regions in early PD. — [PubMed 41688474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41688474/); [PMC13013584](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13013584/)
- Diffusion MRI changes linked to SAA status (Chiu et al., Annals of Neurology, 2026). — [Ann Neurol 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.78252)
- Imaging of non-motor prodromal markers review (npj PD, 2025). — [npj PD 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01004-0)

α-Synuclein PET:
- [18F]ACI-12589 (AC Immune): Smith R et al., Nature Communications 2023 — first tracer to show clear in vivo signal, with greater retention in cerebellar white matter of MSA patients vs PD, DLB, controls; distinguishes MSA from other neurodegenerative diseases but shows limited signal in PD. — [Nat Commun 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42305-3); [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/new-pet-ligand-captures-synuclein-parkinsons-msa-brains)
- [18F]C05-05: Endo H et al., Neuron 2024 ("Imaging α-synuclein pathologies in animal models and patients with Parkinson's and related diseases") — visualized synucleinopathy in 10 patients with PD or DLB; increased midbrain binding correlating with motor severity; also detected aggregates in MSA. — [Neuron 2024](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(24)00332-5)
- [11C]MK-7337 (Merck): "The Novel PET Radioligand [11C]MK-7337 Detects Alpha-Synuclein Pathology in Parkinson's Disease Patients" — small first-in-human study showing brain penetration and fibril-related signal in PD. — [PMC12742897](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12742897/); [Parkinson's News Today](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/pet-tracer-detects-buildup-alpha-synuclein-parkinsons-brains/)
- MODAG: anle138b (emrusolmin) scaffold radiolabeled as [11C]MODAG-005 produced region-specific signals in MSA and PD; MODAG-009 binds α-syn with subnanomolar affinity, ~100-fold selectivity over tau and >1,000-fold over Aβ; first human data presented at AD/PD March 2026. Alzforum reports "at least seven tracer candidates are in human trials." — [Alzforum: New Crop of Tracers](https://www.alzforum.org/news/conference-coverage/new-crop-synuclein-tracers-enter-human-testing); [Alzforum: Tracers Yield Signal in Idiopathic PD](https://www.alzforum.org/news/conference-coverage/finally-pet-tracers-synuclein-yield-signal-idiopathic-parkinsons); [Alzforum: MJFF tracer meeting](https://www.alzforum.org/news/conference-coverage/mjff-gathers-scientists-advance-synuclein-tracer-development)
- AC Immune announced first patient scanned in a PD-focused first-in-human study of its tracer. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/new-clinical-study-evaluating-parkinson-pet-imaging-tracer-initiated)
- Reviews: "α-synuclein PET imaging: early human evidence and the path toward clinical proof-of-concept" (2026); "PET imaging of alpha-synuclein: from radiotracer design through in vitro and in vivo translation" (2026); He Y & Xie F et al. review in Genomic Psychiatry (2025) summarizing [18F]F-0502B, [18F]C05-05, [18F]ACI-12589. — [PubMed 42132158](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42132158/); [PMC13121216](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13121216/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1081920); [development review PMC12004261](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12004261)

### Inferences
- The α-syn PET challenge (low target density, intracellular location, competition from Aβ/tau binding) explains why MSA—with dense oligodendroglial inclusions—was imaged first (ACI-12589) and idiopathic PD only in 2024–2026.
- A validated α-syn PET tracer would address the two main limits of SAA: quantification and regional localization, enabling a true "S" progression biomarker for NSD-ISS-type staging.
- Within MRI, nigrosome-1 and neuromelanin imaging are the strongest candidates for routine-clinic adoption because they require no radiotracer, but standardization across scanners/field strengths remains a barrier (inferred from the heterogeneity flagged in the systematic reviews).

### Gaps
- Specific sample sizes and quantitative uptake values for MK-7337 and MODAG-009 human studies were not retrieved (conference-level data).
- No multi-site validation study for neuromelanin MRI or free-water imaging thresholds was located.
- Did not retrieve primary data for ACI-19626 (AC Immune's PD-targeted follow-on tracer); search returned ACI-12589 only.

## Key Question 5: Fluid and digital biomarkers — NfL, GFAP, DOPA decarboxylase, proteomics, skin/gut biopsy, wearables, smartphone, voice, gait, AI (breathing, typing)

### Takeaway
Beyond SAA, CSF DOPA decarboxylase (Pereira 2023, AUC 0.89; elevated in prodromal disease) and plasma proteomic panels (Hällqvist 2024: 8 proteins predicting PD up to 7 years pre-motor, 79% of iRBD classified) are the leading fluid candidates, with NfL/GFAP useful mainly for separating PD from atypical parkinsonisms; digital markers—UK Biobank accelerometry (Schalkamp 2023, detection up to 7 years pre-diagnosis), AI on nocturnal breathing (Yang 2022, AUC 0.90), keystroke dynamics, and voice ML—show high accuracy in research datasets but lack prospective, population-scale validation.

### Cited Findings
Fluid biomarkers:
- NfL: higher in MSA/PSP than PD; 2020 meta-analysis pooled sensitivity 82%, specificity 91% for MSA vs PD; head-to-head comparison of four CSF and three plasma NfL assays in parkinsonism (npj PD, 2025); Bayesian network meta-analysis of CSF/blood NfL across PD and atypical syndromes (J Neurol, 2025). — [Aden 2026 review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/ijcp/7796155); [npj PD 2025 assay comparison](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-00951-y); [J Neurol 2025 NMA](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-025-13051-x)
- NfL + α-syn RT-QuIC combined as differential diagnostic markers in parkinsonisms (2021). — [PMC8505434](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8505434/)
- GFAP + NfL: plasma gradient atypical parkinsonism (GFAP 89.9 pg/mL; NfL 77.3 pg/mL) > PD (GFAP 47.1; NfL 50.0) > healthy controls (GFAP 22.1); model combining plasma NfL/GFAP, disease duration and pontine radiomics reached AUC >0.87 for PD vs APS. — [Multidimensional modeling study, PubMed 41626595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41626595/); [six plasma biomarkers in PSP/MSA/PD, Clin Chim Acta 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0009898124022289)
- DOPA decarboxylase (DDC/AADC): Pereira JB, Kumar A, et al., "DOPA decarboxylase is an emerging biomarker for Parkinsonian disorders including preclinical Lewy body disease," Nature Aging 2023 — CSF DDC identified Lewy body disease with AUC 0.89 (P_FDR = 2.6 × 10^-13), associated with worse cognition, elevated in prodromal/preclinical cases, not elevated in Alzheimer's disease; accompanying News & Views "An emerging biomarker for dopaminergic cell loss." — [Nat Aging 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-023-00478-y); [News & Views](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-023-00501-2)
- DDC follow-ups: CSF DDC correlates inversely with DaT-SPECT binding (Biopark and PPMI cohorts, 2024); plasma DDC elevation is driven by dopaminergic treatment (confound); monoclonal-antibody immunoassay confirms CSF DDC as diagnostic with prognostic potential (Aviolat et al., JPD 2026); "A quantitative DOPA decarboxylase biomarker for diagnosis in Lewy body disorders" (Nature Medicine, 2026); serum AADC activity as biomarker for prodromal and manifest PD (eBioMedicine, 2026); mechanistic review in npj Biomarkers (2024). — [PMC11490393](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11490393/); [plasma DDC treatment effect](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/works/4KrA3oKv/); [Aviolat JPD 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X261421740); [Nat Med 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04212-0); [eBioMedicine 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352396426002379); [npj Biomarkers 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44385-024-00005-7)
- Plasma proteomics: Hällqvist J et al., "Plasma proteomics identify biomarkers predicting Parkinson's disease up to 7 years before symptom onset," Nature Communications, June 18, 2024 — targeted multiplexed mass spectrometry; cohorts: 99 recently diagnosed PD, iRBD n=18 and n=54 (longitudinal), 36 controls; machine-learning model on 8 proteins (granulin precursor, MASP2, ER chaperone BiP, prostaglandin-H2 D-isomerase, ICAM-1, complement C3, DKK3, plasma protease C1 inhibitor) identified all PD patients and classified 79% of pre-motor iRBD individuals up to 7 years before motor onset; several markers correlate with severity. — [Nat Commun 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-48961-3); [PubMed 38890280](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38890280/); [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/blood-may-foretell-parkinsons-seven-years-prior); [SMC expert reaction](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-study-finding-certain-proteins-in-the-blood-may-help-predict-parkinsons-up-to-7-years-before-motor-symptoms/)
- Cross-cohort benchmarking of plasma proteomic, transcriptomic and multimodal classifiers for PD (npj PD, 2026). — [npj PD 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01344-5)
- Skin: see Gibbons JAMA 2024 (Q2) and skin SAA (90–95% sensitivity). Gut/GI SAA samples show "limited utility" in network meta-analysis. — [Clin Pract 2025 NMA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12192484/)

Digital and AI biomarkers:
- Wearables: Schalkamp A-K et al., "Wearable movement-tracking data identify Parkinson's disease years before clinical diagnosis," Nature Medicine, August 2023 — UK Biobank, 103,712 participants with 7-day wrist accelerometry (Axivity AX3, 100 Hz); 273 diagnosed with PD within 2 years and 196 later (prodromal group); ML models distinguished future PD cases from matched controls up to 7 years before diagnosis and outperformed symptoms, genetics and lifestyle. Cross-country validation published in Movement Disorders 2025. — [Nat Med 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02440-2); [SMC expert reaction](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-study-using-motion-tacking-data-from-smart-watches-and-identifying-parkinsons-disease/); [Mov Disord 2025 validation](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70329)
- Breathing: Yang Y et al., "Artificial intelligence-enabled detection and assessment of Parkinson's disease using nocturnal breathing signals," Nature Medicine, August 2022 — neural network detects PD from nocturnal breathing (via belt or contactless radio-wave sensing) with AUC 0.90 (held-out) and 0.85 (external test); also estimates severity and tracks progression. — [Nat Med 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-01932-x); [Parkinson's Foundation summary](https://www.parkinson.org/blog/science-news/artificial-intelligence)
- Typing: Giancardo L et al., "Computer keyboard interaction as an indicator of early Parkinson's disease," Scientific Reports 2016 — key hold times during routine typing detect early motor signs; Adams WR, "High-accuracy detection of early Parkinson's disease using multiple characteristics of finger movement while typing," PLoS One 2017 — 103 subjects, 96% sensitivity, 97% specificity, AUC 0.98; keystroke-dynamics ensemble learning (Expert Systems with Applications, 2023); KeySense online test. — [Sci Rep 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34468); [PLoS One 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5708704/); [Exp Syst Appl 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417423000234); [Parkinson's News Today on typing tests](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/way-of-using-typing-patterns-to-detect-early-parkinsons-motor-signs-being-tested-refined/)
- Voice: systematic review of voice-based ML/DL detection (69 studies, 2020–Sept 2025) reports accuracies typically 90–99% (e.g., 98% accuracy/AUC 0.991 in one 2025 study; XGBoost 94–97%), while noting heterogeneity in datasets and tasks; explainable-AI voice diagnosis (Scientific Reports 2025). — [Systematic review PMC12649940](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12649940/); [Frontiers Aging Neurosci 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2025.1672971/full); [Sci Rep 2025 XAI voice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-96575-6)
- Smartphone: smartphone-based prediction of dopaminergic deficit (DAT) in prodromal and manifest PD (2025). — [PMC12738540](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12738540/)
- Overview: "Digital biomarkers for precision diagnosis and monitoring in Parkinson's disease," npj Digital Medicine 2024; survey of AI-enabled multimodal detection (arXiv 2025). — [npj Digit Med 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01217-2); [arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10703)

### Inferences
- Fluid markers divide into pathology markers (SAA—binary, disease-defining) and state/progression markers (DDC = dopaminergic dysfunction; NfL = neuroaxonal injury; GFAP = astroglial activation; proteomic panels = inflammation/complement). DDC is attractive because it tracks the "D/N" axis of the biological frameworks from fluid, but plasma DDC is confounded by levodopa treatment, limiting it to untreated/prodromal use.
- Digital-marker accuracies in the 90–99% range come overwhelmingly from small, cross-sectional, case-control datasets; the UK Biobank accelerometry study is the only population-scale prospective result found, and its positive predictive value at population prevalence is necessarily low (not reported in sources retrieved).
- Multimodal integration (SAA + DAT + digital + proteomics) is the trend in 2025–2026 roadmaps; no single digital marker has regulatory qualification.

### Gaps
- Replication of the Hällqvist 8-protein panel in independent cohorts was not confirmed in this session (the 2026 npj PD benchmarking paper may address it, but its findings were not fetched).
- Gait-analysis-specific meta-analytic accuracy figures were not retrieved; gait is covered only within wearable/accelerometry results.
- No gut-biopsy SAA primary study with sensitivity/specificity figures was retrieved beyond the network meta-analysis statement of "limited utility."
- No regulatory (FDA/EMA) qualification of any digital biomarker for PD diagnosis was found.

## Key Question 6: Prodromal PD — MDS research criteria (Berg 2015; Heinzel 2019), RBD conversion (Postuma 2019), hyposmia, and prodromal cohorts (PPMI, PARS, PREDICT-PD)

### Takeaway
The MDS research criteria for prodromal PD (Berg 2015; updated Heinzel 2019) compute a Bayesian probability from likelihood ratios of risk and prodromal markers (with a web calculator); idiopathic RBD is the strongest single marker, converting at 6.3% per year and 73.5% by 12 years (Postuma 2019, n=1,280), and hyposmia plus DAT deficit in PARS conferred a 17-fold relative risk (67% conversion by 4 years). SAA positivity in most prodromal PPMI participants and in hyposmic PARS participants now anchors these cohorts in the biological frameworks.

### Cited Findings
- Berg D et al., "MDS research criteria for prodromal Parkinson's disease," Movement Disorders 2015 — introduced a probabilistic (naive Bayesian) framework combining age-based prior probability with likelihood ratios for risk markers (sex, pesticide/solvent exposure, caffeine, smoking, family history, genetics, substantia nigra hyperechogenicity) and prodromal markers (RBD, hyposmia, constipation, daytime somnolence, orthostatic hypotension, erectile/urinary dysfunction, depression, subthreshold parkinsonism, DAT deficit) with threshold ≥80% probability for "probable prodromal PD." — [Berg 2015 (ResearchGate record)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282910852_MDS_research_criteria_for_prodromal_Parkinson's_disease); [Neurotorium summary](https://neurotorium.org/slide/mds-research-criteria-for-prodromal-parkinsons-disease/)
- Heinzel S et al., "Update of the MDS research criteria for prodromal Parkinson's disease," Movement Disorders 2019 — integrated prospective evidence since 2015; positive LR increased for olfactory loss and decreased for substantia nigra hyperechogenicity; negative LRs largely unchanged; added certainty levels for neurogenic vs symptomatic orthostatic hypotension; incorporated age-related penetrance for intermediate-strength genetic variants (e.g., LRRK2, GBA1); new markers added: diabetes, global cognitive deficit, physical inactivity, low plasma urate (men); a web-based prodromal PD risk calculator was released. — [Heinzel 2019 Mov Disord](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.27802); [UCL open-access PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10123158/1/mds.27802.pdf)
- Population-level application: "Application of the Updated MDS Criteria for Prodromal PD to a Population-Based 10-Year Study" (Movement Disorders 2021); "A retrospective study of the MDS criteria for prodromal PD in the general population" (npj PD, 2024); "Features of prodromal PD and dopaminergic neurotransmission in community-dwelling older adults" (Neurology, 2025). — [Mov Disord 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8251993/); [npj PD 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00739-6); [Neurology 2025](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000214037)
- RBD: Postuma RB et al., "Risk and predictors of dementia and parkinsonism in idiopathic REM sleep behaviour disorder: a multicentre study," Brain 2019;142(3):744–759 — 1,280 polysomnography-confirmed iRBD patients from 24 centres (International RBD Study Group); overall phenoconversion 6.3% per year, 73.5% after 12 years; outcomes PD, DLB, MSA; significant predictors: abnormal quantitative motor testing, objective motor examination abnormalities, olfactory deficit, mild cognitive impairment, erectile dysfunction, motor symptoms, abnormal DAT scan; provides relative predictive values for stratifying neuroprotective trials. — [Brain 2019](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/142/3/744/5353011)
- RBD follow-ups: REM sleep muscle activity predicts phenoconversion (Neurology 2019); cardiovascular autonomic failure associated with progression/phenoconversion in iRBD (Neurology 2025); longitudinal brain network changes and phenoconversion risk (Nature Communications 2024); Korean single-center cohort compared with Montreal (J Clin Sleep Med 2024); review of RBD as prodromal synucleinopathy and implications for neuroprotective trials (2025/26). — [Neurology 2019](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/wnl.0000000000008127); [Neurology 2025](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000213470); [Nat Commun 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54695-z); [JCSM 2024](https://jcsm.aasm.org/doi/10.5664/jcsm.11318); [RBD review PMC12831559](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12831559)
- PARS (Parkinson Associated Risk Syndrome) study: design—smell identification testing (UPSIT) in the general population followed by DAT imaging in hyposmics (Jennings D, Siderowf A, Stern M, Seibyl J, Marek K et al., Neurology 2014). Conversion results (Jennings D et al., JAMA Neurology 2017): hyposmic individuals with DAT deficit at baseline converted to clinical PD at 67% (14/21) by 4 years vs 9% (2/22) with indeterminate deficit and 3% (3/109) with no deficit; relative risk 17.47 (95% CI 7.02–43.45); DAT binding declined ~5% per year in hyposmic/DAT-deficit group, similar to early PD; sequence: olfactory loss → DAT deficit → clinical conversion. Long-term follow-up: Siderowf A et al., Movement Disorders 2020. — [Neurology 2014](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000000960); [JAMA Neurol 2017](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2630495); [Mov Disord 2020](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mds.28139); [Serial olfactory testing in PARS, PRD 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S135380202200298X)
- PARS + SAA: "Evidence for alpha-synuclein aggregation in older individuals with hyposmia: a cross-sectional study" (eBioMedicine, 2025) and MDS abstract "Widespread synuclein pathology in hyposmics precedes dopamine transporter deficit in PARS." — [eBioMedicine 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/ebiom/article/PIIS2352-3964(25)00011-8/fulltext); [MDS abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/widespread-synuclein-pathology-in-hyposmics-precedes-dopamine-transporter-deficit-in-pars/)
- PPMI prodromal cohort: 51 prodromal (hyposmia/RBD) and 310 non-manifesting genetic carriers in Siderowf 2023; most prodromal participants SAA-positive (see Q2). PPMI now enrolls at-risk participants by smell test and RBD under NSD-ISS staging. — [MDS Moving Along](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/Moving-Along/2023-issue-2/Findings-from-the-PPMI-Study)
- PREDICT-PD (UK): prospective online cohort of 60–80-year-olds designed to estimate PD risk from remotely administered tests; intermediate markers = reduced keyboard finger-tapping speed, hyposmia, probable RBD; "enhanced PREDICT-PD algorithm" (medRxiv 2020). — [PREDICT-PD enhanced algorithm, medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.03.16.20037002.full.pdf)
- Idiopathic hyposmia as prodromal PD (J Neurol 2020). — [J Neurol 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-020-10048-6)
- 2025 review: "Prodromal Parkinson's disease" (Revue Neurologique / ScienceDirect, 2025). — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0035378725005739)

### Inferences
- iRBD remains the only prodromal marker with high enough conversion rates (>6%/yr) to power neuroprotection trials of feasible duration; hyposmia alone is too non-specific but hyposmia + DAT deficit (PARS) or hyposmia + SAA positivity achieves comparable enrichment.
- The 2023–2025 SAA findings in prodromal cohorts (PPMI, PARS) effectively convert the probabilistic MDS prodromal criteria into a biomarker-confirmed "Stage 1–2" population under NSD-ISS, which is why trial sponsors and the FDA Letter of Support focus on this group.
- The Heinzel 2019 criteria's continuous probability output makes them a natural "pre-screen" (cheap, remote) feeding into costly confirmatory tests (DAT, CSF/skin SAA)—the funnel design used by PARS and PREDICT-PD.

- Exact likelihood-ratio values for individual markers in Heinzel 2019 (e.g., RBD LR+ ≈130, hyposmia LR+) were not confirmed from the full text in this session; the writer should pull the table directly if needed.
- The DaTscan FDA label is limited to differentiating parkinsonian syndromes from essential tremor; no source was retrieved on regulatory status for prodromal DAT screening.

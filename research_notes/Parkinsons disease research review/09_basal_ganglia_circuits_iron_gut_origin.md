## (A) Basal ganglia circuits: the rate model, its revision, beta/gamma oscillations, DBS mechanism, and striatal plasticity

### Takeaway

The classical Albin–DeLong rate model (direct-pathway hypoactivity + indirect-pathway hyperactivity → excess GPi/SNr output → thalamocortical inhibition) correctly predicted the therapeutic benefit of pallidotomy and subthalamotomy but has been substantially superseded: optogenetics confirmed the pathways' opposing *behavioral* valence (Kravitz, *Nature* 2010) while simultaneously showing that both pathways are *co-activated* during normal action initiation (Cui, *Nature* 2013), shifting the field from a rate model to a **pattern/dynamics model** in which pathological synchronized beta-band (13–30 Hz) activity — specifically prolonged beta *bursts* — is the proximate correlate of bradykinesia and rigidity, and narrowband "finely tuned" gamma (~60–90 Hz) the correlate of dyskinesia. This reconceptualization is directly clinically consequential: beta power is the feedback signal for closed-loop adaptive DBS, which moved from single-session proof-of-concept (Little, *Ann Neurol* 2013) to chronic randomized and multicenter trials in 2024–2025 (Oehrn, *Nat Med* 2024; Bronte-Stewart, *JAMA Neurol* 2025).

### Cited Findings

**The classical rate model and its two founding papers**

- The canonical statement of the direct/indirect pathway architecture and of how its imbalance explains hypokinetic vs hyperkinetic disorders is Albin RL, Young AB, Penney JB, "The functional anatomy of basal ganglia disorders," *Trends in Neurosciences* 1989 — [PubMed 2479133](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2479133/); [doi:10.1016/0166-2236(89)90074-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-2236(89)90074-x)
- The complementary primate-physiology formulation, grounded in MPTP monkey recordings and lesion work, is DeLong MR, "Primate models of movement disorders of basal ganglia origin," *Trends in Neurosciences* 1990 — [PubMed 1695404](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1695404/); [doi:10.1016/0166-2236(90)90110-v](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-2236(90)90110-v)
- DeLong and Wichmann's own later retrospective acknowledging that the circuit-and-rate framework required revision: DeLong M, Wichmann T, "Changing views of basal ganglia circuits and circuit disorders," *Clinical EEG and Neuroscience* 2010 — [PubMed 20521487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20521487/); [doi:10.1177/155005941004100204](https://doi.org/10.1177/155005941004100204)
- The MPTP primate model as the physiological substrate on which the rate model was validated: Wichmann T, "Pathophysiology of Parkinson's disease: the MPTP primate model of the human disorder," *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 2003 — [PubMed 12846988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12846988/); [doi:10.1111/j.1749-6632.2003.tb07477.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.2003.tb07477.x)

**The hyperdirect pathway**

- The cortico-subthalamo-pallidal "hyperdirect" pathway — a fast, monosynaptic cortex→STN projection that reaches GPi/SNr *before* the direct and indirect pathways, supporting a dynamic (temporally sequenced) rather than purely rate-based model of basal ganglia output: Nambu A, Tokuno H, Takada M, "Functional significance of the cortico-subthalamo-pallidal 'hyperdirect' pathway," *Neuroscience Research* 2002 — [PubMed 12067746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12067746/); [doi:10.1016/s0168-0102(02)00027-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0168-0102(02)00027-5)

**Optogenetic dissection and the pathway co-activation problem**

- Kravitz AV, Freeze BS, Parker PRL, et al., "Regulation of parkinsonian motor behaviours by optogenetic control of basal ganglia circuitry," *Nature* 2010 — selective D2/indirect-pathway (striatopallidal) stimulation produced a parkinsonian phenotype (bradykinesia, freezing, reduced locomotion) while D1/direct-pathway stimulation increased locomotion and *rescued* motor deficits in dopamine-depleted mice. This is the strongest causal confirmation of the rate model's behavioral predictions — [PubMed 20613723](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20613723/); [doi:10.1038/nature09159](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09159)
- Cui G, Jun SB, Jin X, et al. (Costa lab), "Concurrent activation of striatal direct and indirect pathways during action initiation," *Nature* 2013 — simultaneous cell-type-specific calcium recording showed both direct- and indirect-pathway striatal projection neurons *increase* activity together at action initiation, falsifying the strict "push-pull"/mutually exclusive reading of the rate model — [PubMed 23354054](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23354054/); [doi:10.1038/nature11846](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11846)
- Gradinaru V, Mogri M, Thompson KR, Henderson JM, Deisseroth K, "Optical deconstruction of parkinsonian neural circuitry," *Science* 2009 — optogenetic dissection of STN DBS showed that therapeutic benefit tracked with direct modulation of **afferent axons** (cortical/hyperdirect fibers) rather than with somatic inhibition of STN neurons, a foundational result for DBS-mechanism theories — [PubMed 19299587](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19299587/); [doi:10.1126/science.1167093](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1167093)
- Parker JG, Marshall JD, Ahanonu B, et al. (Schnitzer lab), "Diametric neural ensemble dynamics in parkinsonian and dyskinetic states," *Nature* 2018 — large-scale cellular imaging showed parkinsonism and dyskinesia involve *opposite* reorganizations of direct- vs indirect-pathway ensemble dynamics (loss vs gain of D1 ensemble recruitment), reframing both states as ensemble-level rather than mean-rate pathologies — [PubMed 29720658](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29720658/); [doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0090-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0090-6)

**Pathological beta-band oscillations as a biomarker**

- Brown P, Oliviero A, Mazzone P, Insola A, Tonali P, Di Lazzaro V, "Dopamine dependency of oscillations between subthalamic nucleus and pallidum in Parkinson's disease," *Journal of Neuroscience* 2001 — the founding human LFP demonstration that STN–GPi coupling in the beta band is prominent OFF dopaminergic medication and suppressed by levodopa, while higher-frequency (~70 Hz) coupling appears ON medication — [PubMed 11157088](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11157088/); [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.21-03-01033.2001](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.21-03-01033.2001)
- Kühn AA, Kupsch A, Schneider G-H, Brown P, "Reduction in subthalamic 8–35 Hz oscillatory activity correlates with clinical improvement in Parkinson's disease," *European Journal of Neuroscience* 2006 — the key correlational link: levodopa-induced suppression of STN 8–35 Hz power correlates with improvement in **bradykinesia and rigidity** (not tremor) — [PubMed 16623853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16623853/); [doi:10.1111/j.1460-9568.2006.04717.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-9568.2006.04717.x)
- The modern refinement from power to **burst duration**: Tinkhauser G, Pogosyan A, Little S, et al., "Beta burst dynamics in Parkinson's disease OFF and ON dopaminergic medication," *Brain* 2017 — levodopa shortens long beta bursts and shifts the burst-duration distribution toward short bursts; long-duration bursts specifically track motor impairment — [PubMed 29053865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29053865/); [doi:10.1093/brain/awx252](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awx252)
- Beta bursts are also pallidal and are disease-state-specific: Lofredi R, et al., "Pallidal beta bursts in Parkinson's disease and dystonia," *Movement Disorders* 2019 — [PubMed 30440096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30440096/); [doi:10.1002/mds.27524](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.27524)
- 2024 evidence that beta bursts are not a single global rhythm but **spatially discrete ensembles** within the cortico-basal ganglia network: Grennan I, et al., "Beta bursts in the parkinsonian cortico-basal ganglia network form spatially discrete ensembles," *Neurobiology of Disease* 2024 — [PubMed 39209070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39209070/); [doi:10.1016/j.nbd.2024.106652](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbd.2024.106652)
- Review of competing accounts of where parkinsonian beta originates (STN-GPe pacemaker vs cortical drive vs striatal): Asadi A, et al., "The Origin of Abnormal Beta Oscillations in the Parkinsonian Corticobasal Ganglia Circuits," *Parkinson's Disease* 2022 — [PubMed 35251590](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35251590/); [doi:10.1155/2022/7524066](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/7524066)

**Gamma and "finely tuned gamma" in dyskinesia**

- Swann NC, de Hemptinne C, Miocinovic S, et al., "Gamma Oscillations in the Hyperkinetic State Detected with Chronic Human Brain Recordings in Parkinson's Disease," *Journal of Neuroscience* 2016 — chronic implanted recordings identified narrowband gamma (~60–90 Hz) in motor cortex and STN as a signature of the levodopa-induced dyskinetic state, i.e. a candidate *dyskinesia* biomarker complementary to beta — [PubMed 27307233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27307233/); [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1128-16.2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1128-16.2016)
- Gamma is not merely pathological — it is also a physiological, dopamine-dependent *prokinetic* signal scaling with movement vigor: Lofredi R, Neumann W-J, Bock A, et al. **Adaptive/closed-loop DBS: the translational payoff of the oscillation biomarker**

**Theories of DBS mechanism**

- **Informational lesion:** Grill WM, Snyder AN, Miocinovic S, "Deep brain stimulation creates an informational lesion of the stimulated nucleus," *NeuroReport* 2004 — high-frequency stimulation replaces pathologically patterned, information-bearing output with a regularized, stimulus-locked firing pattern, functionally "lesioning" the information channel without silencing the nucleus — [PubMed 15129161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15129161/); [doi:10.1097/00001756-200405190-00011](https://doi.org/10.1097/00001756-200405190-00011)
- **Inhibition vs excitation vs disruption — the framing review:** Chiken S, Nambu A, "Mechanism of Deep Brain Stimulation: Inhibition, Excitation, or Disruption?" *The Neuroscientist* 2016 — argues DBS neither simply inhibits nor excites but *disrupts* pathological information flow through the stimulated structure — [PubMed 25888630](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25888630/); [doi:10.1177/1073858415581986](https://doi.org/10.1177/1073858415581986)
- **Antidromic cortical activation:** Kuriakose R, et al., "The nature and time course of cortical activation following subthalamic stimulation in Parkinson's disease," *Cerebral Cortex* 2010 — demonstrates short-latency cortical responses consistent with antidromic activation of the hyperdirect pathway — [PubMed 20019146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20019146/); [doi:10.1093/cercor/bhp269](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhp269)
- **Axonal/pathway specificity:** Gradinaru 2009 *Science* (above) is the canonical evidence that afferent axon modulation, not somatic inhibition, mediates benefit — [doi:10.1126/science.1167093](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1167093)
- **Evoked resonant neural activity (ERNA)** — the emerging electrophysiological signature used both as a mechanistic probe and as an intraoperative targeting/programming biomarker: Noor MS, et al., "Dissecting deep brain stimulation evoked neural activity in the basal ganglia," *Neurotherapeutics* 2024 — [PubMed 38608373](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38608373/); [doi:10.1016/j.neurot.2024.e00356](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neurot.2024.e00356); and Wiest C, et al., "Stimulation-Evoked Resonant Neural Activity in the Subthalamic Nucleus Is Modulated by Sleep," *Movement Disorders* 2025 — [PubMed 39560163](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39560163/); [doi:10.1002/mds.30063](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.30063)
- Pathway activation in the STN region depends on stimulation polarity, relevant to directional/segmented-lead programming: Borghe[ai] SB, et al., "Neural pathway activation in the subthalamic region depends on stimulation polarity," *Brain Communications* 2025 — [PubMed 39980742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39980742/); [doi:10.1093/braincomms/fcaf006](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcaf006)

**Homeostatic/structural plasticity after striatal dopamine loss**

- Day M, Wang Z, Ding J, et al. (Surmeier lab), "Selective elimination of glutamatergic synapses on striatopallidal neurons in Parkinson disease models," *Nature Neuroscience* 2006 — the landmark finding that dopamine depletion causes **cell-type-specific dendritic spine and glutamatergic synapse loss restricted to indirect-pathway (D2/striatopallidal) neurons**, sparing direct-pathway neurons; interpreted as homeostatic downscaling in response to loss of D2-mediated inhibition of corticostriatal drive — [PubMed 16415865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16415865/); [doi:10.1038/nn1632](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1632)
- Fieblinger T, Graves SM, Sebel LE, et al. (Surmeier/Cenci labs), "Cell type-specific plasticity of striatal projection neurons in parkinsonism and L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia," *Nature Communications* 2014 — extends Day 2006 to the dyskinetic state: chronic L-DOPA produces further, pathway-divergent restructuring of spines and intrinsic excitability in D1 vs D2 SPNs, linking the structural plasticity directly to LID — [PubMed 25360704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25360704/); [doi:10.1038/ncomms6316](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms6316)

**Levodopa-induced dyskinesia: D1 supersensitivity and the ERK/ΔFosB axis**

- Picconi B, Centonze D, Håkansson K, et al. (Calabresi lab), "Loss of bidirectional striatal synaptic plasticity in L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia," *Nature Neuroscience* 2003 — the foundational synaptic-plasticity account of LID: dyskinetic animals lose **depotentiation** (the ability to reverse corticostriatal LTP), so aberrant motor information is pathologically "stored" in the striatum — [PubMed 12665799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12665799/); [doi:10.1038/nn1040](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1040)
- Santini E, Valjent E, Usiello A, et al. (Fisone lab), "Critical involvement of cAMP/DARPP-32 and extracellular signal-regulated protein kinase signaling in L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia," *Journal of Neuroscience* 2007 — establishes the D1→cAMP/PKA→DARPP-32→**ERK1/2** cascade in direct-pathway neurons as causally required for LID, the core molecular evidence for "D1 supersensitivity" — [PubMed 17596448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17596448/); [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0852-07.2007](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0852-07.2007)
- The standard modern review of LID animal models and their molecular mechanisms (ΔFosB, ERK, 5-HT neuron L-DOPA decarboxylation, serotonergic contribution): Cenci MA, Crossman AR, "Animal models of l-dopa-induced dyskinesia in Parkinson's disease," *Movement Disorders* 2018 — [PubMed 29488257](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29488257/); [doi:10.1002/mds.27337](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.27337)
- Calabresi 2014 *Nat Neurosci* (above) integrates the plasticity and pathway-reappraisal strands and is the best single Calabresi-lab review anchor — [doi:10.1038/nn.3743](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3743)

### Inferences

- The field's trajectory is a move from **rate → pattern → dynamics/ensembles**. The rate model was not discarded but demoted: it remains a good predictor of *lesion and stimulation targets* (why pallidotomy, subthalamotomy and STN/GPi DBS work) while failing as an account of *normal* basal ganglia computation (Cui 2013) and of the moment-to-moment physiology that correlates with symptoms (beta bursts).
- Beta oscillations have crossed from biomarker to **control variable**. The strongest evidence of the explanatory value of the burst framework is engineering success: Little 2013 → Tinkhauser 2017 (mechanism) → Oehrn 2024 (chronic RCT) → Bronte-Stewart 2025 (multicenter long-term) is a complete translational chain in which the mechanistic hypothesis (long beta bursts are pathological) is directly embodied in a device control policy.
- Beta and gamma are plausibly best read as *opposed state markers* — beta as antikinetic/bradykinesia-associated, narrowband gamma as both physiologically prokinetic (Lofredi 2018) and, when excessive, dyskinesia-associated (Swann 2016). This bidirectionality is the circuit-level analogue of the therapeutic window problem and is why dual-biomarker (beta *and* gamma) control policies are the natural next step for aDBS.
- The three DBS mechanism theories are not mutually exclusive and are converging: Gradinaru's axonal result, Kuriakose's antidromic cortical responses, and Grill's informational lesion are all compatible with a single picture in which stimulation drives hyperdirect-pathway axons, imposing a regularized pattern that disrupts (rather than silences) pathological information transfer — essentially Chiken & Nambu's "disruption" synthesis. ERNA is likely to be the measurable handle on this.
- Day 2006 and Fieblinger 2014 supply the structural bridge between A and the dyskinesia literature: dopamine denervation produces *asymmetric, pathway-specific* remodeling, so by the time L-DOPA is introduced the striatum is no longer the circuit the rate model describes. This makes LID a predictable consequence of plasticity in an already-remodeled network rather than a simple pharmacological overshoot.

## (B) Oxidative stress, mitochondrial complex I, dopamine oxidation, iron, and ferroptosis

### Takeaway

The oxidative-stress strand of PD rests on an unusually solid empirical base — postmortem complex I deficiency specific to the substantia nigra (Schapira 1989, 1990), the MPTP "experiment of nature" (Langston 1983) and its pesticide analogue rotenone (Betarbet 2000), nigral iron elevation (Dexter 1989, 1991), glutathione depletion (Sian 1994) and lipid peroxidation markers (Yoritaka 1996) — and has been sharpened in the last decade into a specific cell-death mechanism, **ferroptosis** (iron-dependent, lipid-peroxidation-driven, GPX4-gated; Dixon 2012; Do Van 2016; Sun 2023 *JCI*), with dopamine oxidation itself identified as an upstream driver in human patient-derived neurons (Burbulla 2017 *Science*). The clinical refutation is the critical fact: **FAIRPARK-II (Devos, *NEJM* 2022) found that deferiprone reduced nigral iron but produced *worse* motor outcomes than placebo**, which — together with the failure of the urate-elevation trial SURE-PD3 (*JAMA* 2021) — means the field as of 2026 has strong mechanistic evidence for iron/oxidative pathology but no validated antioxidant or chelation therapy, and a live hypothesis that nigral iron is partly compensatory or that indiscriminate systemic chelation starves dopamine synthesis.

### Cited Findings

**Mitochondrial complex I deficiency**

- Schapira AHV, Cooper JM, Dexter D, Jenner P, Clark JB, Marsden CD, "Mitochondrial complex I deficiency in Parkinson's disease," *The Lancet* 1989 — the original report of reduced complex I (NADH-ubiquinone reductase) activity in PD substantia nigra — [PubMed 2566813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2566813/); [doi:10.1016/s0140-6736(89)92366-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736(89)92366-0)

**Toxin models: MPTP and rotenone**

**The clinical refutation: FAIRPARK-II**

- Devos D, Labreuche J, Rascol O, et al., "Trial of Deferiprone in Parkinson's Disease," *New England Journal of Medicine* 2022 — the pivotal multicenter randomized placebo-controlled trial of the oral iron chelator deferiprone in **dopaminergic-treatment-naive** early PD. ### Inferences

- There is a coherent, self-reinforcing mechanistic loop specific to nigral dopaminergic neurons: complex I impairment → mitochondrial superoxide → dopamine auto-oxidation → oxidized dopamine (which both impairs lysosomes/GCase per Burbulla 2017 and degrades GPX4 per Sun 2023) → removal of the ferroptosis brake → lipid peroxidation in an iron-rich, neuromelanin-containing, glutathione-depleted cell → neuromelanin breakdown releasing further redox-active iron. Every link in this chain has a verified primary citation above. This loop is probably the single most defensible mechanistic narrative for *selective* nigral vulnerability.
- **FAIRPARK-II is the field's most informative negative result**, precisely because target engagement succeeded (nigral iron fell) while the clinical outcome worsened. Three non-exclusive readings are available and should be presented as open: (i) nigral iron in early PD is partly adaptive/compensatory rather than purely toxic; (ii) deferiprone's chelation is insufficiently compartment-selective and depletes iron needed by **tyrosine hydroxylase**, an iron-dependent enzyme, thereby directly impairing dopamine synthesis and producing an apparent symptomatic worsening distinguishable in principle from accelerated degeneration; (iii) the dopaminergic-treatment-naive design meant any symptomatic cost was unmasked rather than buffered by concurrent levodopa. Reading (ii) is mechanistically the most parsimonious given TH's iron cofactor requirement, but I found no retrieved source that establishes it as the trial's explanation, so it must be presented as an inference.
- Taken with SURE-PD3, the pattern as of 2026 is that *global* antioxidant or anti-iron strategies have failed in trials while the *mechanistic* case has grown stronger. This argues that the therapeutic future of this strand lies in pathway-selective interventions (GPX4 stabilization, ferroptosis inhibitors such as lipophilic radical-trapping antioxidants, Nrf2 activators with astrocytic action, compartment-targeted chelation) rather than in bulk iron removal.
- Rotenone (Betarbet 2000) remains the pivotal model for the report's purposes because it is the only classic toxin model that generates α-synuclein inclusions, making it the point where area B and the proteinopathy/gut-origin literature of area C intersect — a systemic, orally relevant complex I inhibitor that produces both nigral loss and Lewy-like pathology.

### Gaps

- **FAIRPARK-II effect sizes and the authors' own interpretation.** Without web access I could not retrieve the trial's abstract to quote the exact UPDRS/MDS-UPDRS difference, confidence interval, sample size, or the discussion section's proposed explanation for the worsening. The direction of the result (iron reduced; outcomes worse) is securely established in the literature, but the numbers must be filled in from the DOI.
- **Post-FAIRPARK-II commentary and any subsequent chelation trials (2023–2026).** I found no verified records for follow-up analyses, editorials, or redesigned chelation trials. This is a real gap for the "state of the field 2024–2026" requirement on this sub-question.
- **Hirsch & Faucheux iron/aluminum studies** did not resolve; Dexter 1989 and 1991 cover the postmortem iron claim adequately.
- **A 2025–2026 flagship ferroptosis-in-PD review.** The most recent verified reviews are 2024 (Zhou; Soni). I could not confirm a 2025/2026 definitive review.
- **Ferroptosis evidence in human PD tissue (as opposed to models).** Yoritaka 1996 (HNE adducts), Sian 1994 (GSH) and Dexter (iron) are consistent with ferroptosis but predate the concept; I found no verified source directly demonstrating ferroptosis-specific markers in human PD substantia nigra. This is a genuine and important limitation of the ferroptosis hypothesis and should be stated as such.

## (C) The gut-origin hypothesis: epidemiological and pathological tests

### Takeaway

Braak's ascending-propagation hypothesis generated three independently testable predictions, and the results are genuinely mixed in an informative way. The **vagotomy** epidemiology is directionally supportive but fragile (Svensson 2015 found reduced PD risk after *truncal* but not super-selective vagotomy, with the headline comparison not conventionally significant; Liu 2017 replicated a protective signal for truncal vagotomy in Sweden; Tysnes 2015 published an explicit negative/critical analysis). The **appendectomy** literature is frankly contradictory (Killinger 2018 *Sci Transl Med* reported reduced PD risk and abundant α-synuclein aggregates in healthy appendices; Marras 2016 *Mov Disord* found no protection). The **IBD** association is the most robust epidemiological finding (Villumsen 2019 *Gut*; Peter 2018 *JAMA Neurol*, with reduced PD incidence among anti-TNF-treated patients), pointing to peripheral inflammation rather than pure prion-like spread. Pathologically, enteric α-synuclein is demonstrable in prodromal patients (Stokholm 2016 *Ann Neurol*) but the colonic-biopsy assays proved poorly reproducible across centers (Beach 2016), and Borghammer's imaging work (Horsager 2020 *Brain*) has reframed the debate productively: PD is probably **not** uniformly gut-origin but splits into **body-first and brain-first subtypes**, with RBD-positive, autonomically affected patients fitting the ascending model and others not.

- Siderowf A, Concha-Marambio L, Lafontant D-E, et al., "Assessment of heterogeneity among participants in the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative cohort using α-synuclein seed amplification: a cross-sectional study," *Lancet Neurology* 2023 — CSF α-synuclein seed amplification assay showed high sensitivity in sporadic PD with notable **genotype- and phenotype-dependent heterogeneity** (notably lower positivity in LRRK2 PD and in patients without olfactory loss) — directly relevant to the subtype argument below — [PubMed 37059509](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37059509/); [doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(23)00109-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(23)00109-6)

**The body-first / brain-first reframing — the most important recent development**

- Borghammer P, Van Den Berge N, "Brain-First versus Gut-First Parkinson's Disease: A Hypothesis," *Journal of Parkinson's Disease* 2019 — the formal proposal that PD comprises two propagation phenotypes — [PubMed 31498132](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31498132/); [doi:10.3233/JPD-191721](https://doi.org/10.3233/JPD-191721)
- Horsager J, Andersen KB, Knudsen K, et al., "Brain-first versus body-first Parkinson's disease: a multimodal imaging case-control study," *Brain* 2020 — the empirical test, using multimodal imaging (dopaminergic, cardiac sympathetic ¹²³I-MIBG, and colonic/gut transit measures) to show that **RBD-positive** PD patients display the peripheral-first (body-first) pattern of asymmetric/peripheral involvement predicted by ascending propagation, while **RBD-negative** patients do not — [PubMed 32830221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32830221/); [doi:10.1093/brain/awaa238](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaa238)
- Horsager J, Borghammer P, "Brain-first vs. body-first Parkinson's disease: An update on recent evidence," *Parkinsonism & Related Disorders* 2024 — the current state-of-the-field review of this framework — [PubMed 38519273](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38519273/); [doi:10.1016/j.parkreldis.2024.106101](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2024.106101)

**Animal-model causal tests of gut-to-brain propagation**

- Challis C, Hori A, Sampson TR, et al. (Gradinaru lab), "Gut-seeded α-synuclein fibrils promote gut dysfunction and brain pathology specifically in aged mice," *Nature Neuroscience* 2020 — injecting preformed α-synuclein fibrils into the gut produced GI dysfunction and brain pathology **only in aged** animals, establishing age as a necessary permissive factor and tempering strong versions of the propagation claim — [PubMed 32066981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32066981/); [doi:10.1038/s41593-020-0589-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-0589-7)

(Ko lab), "Transneuronal Propagation of Pathologic α-Synuclein from the Gut to the Brain Models Parkinson's Disease," *Neuron* 2019 — gut-injected α-synuclein fibrils propagated to the brain and produced motor and non-motor deficits, and **truncal vagotomy or α-synuclein (Snca) knockout prevented** both the spread and the phenotype. **UNVERIFIED CITATION:** this record did not resolve in my PubMed lookups; the first author, journal and year are from domain knowledge, not from a retrieved record. **Incidental Lewy body disease: the prevalence baseline**

- Frigerio R, Fujishiro H, Ahn T-B, et al. (Maraganore/Dickson), "Incidental Lewy body disease: do some cases represent a preclinical stage of dementia with Lewy bodies?" *Neurobiology of Aging* 2011 — population-based autopsy characterization of incidental Lewy body disease in people without clinical parkinsonism or dementia, arguing a subset represents preclinical disease — [PubMed 19560232](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19560232/); [doi:10.1016/j.neurobiolaging.2009.05.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neurobiolaging.2009.05.019)

**SNCA dosage: the causality argument**

- Singleton AB, Farrer M, Johnson J, et al., "α-Synuclein locus triplication causes Parkinson's disease," *Science* 2003 — the Iowa kindred: a genomic **triplication** of the SNCA locus (four functional copies, ~2× expression) causes early-onset, rapidly progressive PD with dementia — [PubMed 14593171](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14593171/); [doi:10.1126/science.1090278](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1090278)
- Chartier-Harlin M-C, Kachergus J, Roumier C, et al., "α-Synuclein locus duplication as a cause of familial Parkinson's disease," *The Lancet* 2004 — **duplication** (three copies, ~1.5× expression) causes a later-onset, slower, more typically "sporadic-looking" PD phenotype — [PubMed 15451224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15451224/); [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(04)17103-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(04)17103-1)

**Immunogenetics: HLA and T-cell recognition of α-synuclein**

- Hamza TH, Zabetian CP, Tenesa A, et al. (Payami lab), "Common genetic variation in the HLA region is associated with late-onset sporadic Parkinson's disease," *Nature Genetics* 2010 — the GWAS discovery of an **HLA-DRA / HLA class II** association with late-onset sporadic PD, the first strong genetic evidence that adaptive immunity (specifically antigen presentation) is involved in PD risk — [PubMed 20711177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20711177/); [doi:10.1038/ng.642](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.642)
- Sulzer D, Alcalay RN, Garretti F, et al. ### Inferences

- **The three epidemiological tests do not fail symmetrically, and the pattern is itself evidence.** The vagotomy result is specific in the right way (truncal yes, super-selective no — Svensson 2015, Liu 2017), which is hard to produce by confounding; the appendectomy result is flatly contradictory between high-quality datasets (Killinger 2018 vs Marras 2016); and the IBD/anti-TNF result is the most robust of the three. A gut-origin hypothesis built purely on retrograde *neural* propagation predicts the vagotomy and appendectomy results but has no particular reason to predict the anti-TNF effect. An **inflammation-mediated** version of the gut hypothesis predicts all three. The report should present the anti-TNF finding (Peter 2018) as the datum that most constrains the mechanism.
- **Beach 2016 is the most underweighted citation in this literature.** The blinded multicenter failure to distinguish PD from control colon by immunohistochemistry means the early positive biopsy literature (Shannon 2012) cannot bear much weight, and the robust antemortem-detection story has migrated to skin biopsy (Gibbons 2024) and CSF/tissue seed-amplification assays (Siderowf 2023). A careful report should note that the *assay* story and the *hypothesis* story are often conflated.
- **The SNCA dosage series is the cleanest causal argument in all of PD biology** and is logically independent of the gut question. Triplication (Singleton 2003) → severe, early, demented phenotype; duplication (Chartier-Harlin 2004) → milder, later, sporadic-like phenotype. A monotonic gene-dose/phenotype-severity relationship with no coding mutation involved establishes that *α-synuclein quantity alone is sufficient to cause PD*, which is the premise the entire propagation literature depends on and the rationale for α-synuclein-lowering therapeutics.
- **Hamza 2010 plus Sulzer 2017 converts "neuroinflammation in PD" from a correlate into a candidate mechanism**, and is the natural bridge back to the IBD/anti-TNF epidemiology: if α-synuclein is an HLA-class-II-restricted autoantigen, then a peripheral site of high α-synuclein aggregate load and high immune activity — the gut, and specifically the appendix (Killinger 2018 showed abundant aggregates there in normal people) — is a plausible site of initial immune sensitization. This reading reconciles Killinger's two apparently awkward findings: aggregates in the appendix are *normal*, so the appendix is not a source of pathogenic seeds per se, but may be a site where seeds meet gut-associated lymphoid tissue.
- **Borghammer's subtype framework is the most likely resolution of the whole debate, and it explains why the tests gave mixed results.** If only a subset (roughly the RBD-positive, autonomically affected, body-first patients) follows an ascending route, then every population-level test of gut-origin — vagotomy, appendectomy, enteric biopsy sensitivity — is diluted by brain-first cases and should yield exactly the weak, inconsistent, barely-significant effects observed. Siderowf 2023's finding of reduced seed-amplification positivity in patients without olfactory loss and in LRRK2 carriers is independent support for meaningful biological heterogeneity. The honest 2026 position is: Braak's hypothesis is probably *partly* right, for *some* patients, and population-average study designs are the wrong instrument for testing it.
- Challis 2020's age-dependence result is an important corrective: even in the models where gut-to-brain propagation works, it requires an aged host, implying propagation is permitted by age-related failure of clearance/barrier mechanisms rather than being an autonomous prion-like process.

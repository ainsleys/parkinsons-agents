## Key Question 1: Levodopa — history, early-vs-delayed initiation (LEAP), "levodopa phobia," motor complications, formulations (IR, ER/IPX203-Crexont, inhaled), and the ELLDOPA / STRIDE-PD trials

### Takeaway
Levodopa (introduced clinically by Cotzias in 1967-1969) remains the most effective symptomatic therapy; the LEAP trial (NEJM 2019) and its 5-year follow-up (2024) showed that starting it earlier neither modifies disease nor increases motor complications, and observational data from Ghana (Brain 2014) indicate that dyskinesia and wearing-off track disease duration and dose rather than duration of levodopa exposure, undermining "levodopa phobia." New delivery formats (IPX203/Crexont, approved Aug 2024; inhaled levodopa, approved Dec 2018) extend "good ON" time, while STRIDE-PD showed that simply adding a COMT inhibitor at initiation does not prevent dyskinesia.

### Cited Findings

**History**
- Cotzias, Van Woert and Schiffer reported the first convincing oral D,L-dopa responses in parkinsonism in "Aromatic amino acids and modification of parkinsonism," N Engl J Med 1967;276:374-379 — [Cotzias 1967, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5334614/)
- Cotzias, Papavasiliou and Gellene followed with chronic L-dopa treatment ("Modification of Parkinsonism — chronic treatment with L-dopa"), N Engl J Med 1969;280:337-345 — [Cotzias 1969, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/4178641)

**Does levodopa modify progression? ELLDOPA and LEAP**
- ELLDOPA (Fahn et al., N Engl J Med 2004;351:2498-2508): 361 early PD patients randomized to carbidopa/levodopa 37.5/150, 75/300, 150/600 mg/day or placebo for 40 weeks followed by 2-week washout. UPDRS worsening at 42 weeks was 7.8 points (placebo) vs 1.9, 1.9 and -1.4 in the three levodopa groups (P<0.001); but in the [123I]beta-CIT SPECT substudy (n=116) the decline in striatal uptake was greater with levodopa (-6%, -4%, -7.2%) than placebo (-1.4%; P=0.036). The highest dose caused more dyskinesia, hypertonia, nausea. Authors concluded the clinical and imaging data pointed in opposite directions and long-term effects "remain uncertain." — [Fahn 2004, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15590952/)
- LEAP (Verschuur et al., N Engl J Med 2019;380:315-324): multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled delayed-start trial, 445 patients randomized to levodopa/carbidopa 100/25 mg TID for 80 weeks (early start, n=222) or placebo for 40 weeks then levodopa for 40 weeks (delayed start, n=223). Change in total UPDRS at week 80: -1.0 ± 13.1 vs -2.0 ± 13.0 (difference 1.0 point; 95% CI -1.5 to 3.5; P=0.44). Progression rate weeks 4-40: 0.04 vs 0.06 UPDRS points/week. Noninferiority in weeks 44-80 was not met. Rates of dyskinesia and motor fluctuations did not differ. Conclusion: levodopa had no disease-modifying effect. — [Verschuur 2019, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30673543/); [NEJM PDF](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJMoa1809983)
- LEAP 5-year follow-up (Frequin et al., Mov Disord 2024;39:975-982): 321 patients completed the 5-year visit. Adjusted square-root total UPDRS did not differ at 3 years (diff 0.17, SE 0.13, P=0.18) or 5 years (diff 0.24, SE 0.13, P=0.07). Dyskinesia at 5 years: 46/160 early-start vs 62/161 delayed-start (P=0.06); wearing-off prevalence and LEDD did not differ. "We did not find a difference in disease progression or in prevalence of motor complications." — [Frequin 2024, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38644623/)
- ACP Journal Club summary of LEAP: early vs late start "relieved symptoms but did not affect disease progression." — [ACP Journal Club 2019](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/ACPJ201905210-056)

**"Levodopa phobia" and the determinants of motor complications**
- Kurlan coined "levodopa phobia" as "a new iatrogenic cause of disability in Parkinson disease," Neurology 2005;64:923-924 — [Kurlan 2005, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15753443)
- Titova, Levin, Katunina and Ray Chaudhuri reviewed 'Levodopa Phobia' (NPJ Parkinsons Dis 2018;4:31): driven by misplaced fear of dyskinesia, GI effects and the theoretical neurotoxicity notion; consequences include motor deterioration, contractures, and ICDs from over-reliance on agonists. — [Titova 2018, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30302392)
- Ghana cohort (Cilia et al., Brain 2014;137:2731-2742): 91 Ghanaian PD patients (levodopa started at mean 4.2 ± 2.8 years from onset vs 2.4 ± 2.1 in 2,282 Italian controls). Disease duration at onset of fluctuations/dyskinesia was similar in both populations; in multivariate analysis disease duration and levodopa dose (mg/kg) — not disease duration at levodopa initiation — predicted motor complications. In 21 drug-naive patients followed prospectively, median time to fluctuations and dyskinesias after starting levodopa was 6 months. Conclusion: "the practice to withhold levodopa therapy with the objective of delaying the occurrence of motor complications is not justified." — [Cilia 2014, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25034897/)
- Espay et al. review (Ann Neurol 2018;84:797-811): mechanisms of levodopa-induced dyskinesia include phasic (pulsatile) stimulation of dopamine receptors, non-physiological levodopa-to-dopamine conversion in serotonergic neurons, corticostriatal glutamatergic hyperactivity, and nicotinic receptor overstimulation; "Delay in initiating levodopa is no longer recommended, as dyskinesia development is a function of disease duration rather than cumulative levodopa exposure." — [Espay 2018, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30357892)
- Riederer et al. (J Neural Transm 2025;132:743-779) attribute increasing fluctuations to loss of striatal dopamine buffering capacity and the "false neurotransmitter" release of levodopa-derived dopamine from serotonergic terminals. — [Riederer 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40214767)

**Rates of dyskinesia and motor fluctuations**
- Ahlskog and Muenter (Mov Disord 2001;16:448-458), from 74 publications / 112 treatment intervals: in modern-era series the median dyskinesia frequency was "slightly less than 40% by 4-6 years of levodopa therapy," and motor fluctuations occurred in ~40% by 4-6 years (nil in year 1). Pre-levodopa-era patients had 50% dyskinesia by 5-6 months. — [Ahlskog 2001, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11391738/)
- Tran et al. (Transl Neurodegener 2018): reported LID rates in the literature range from 3% to 94%, depending on age at onset, disease duration/severity and treatment duration; modifiable risk factors are levodopa dose and body weight. — [Tran 2018, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29971495)
- Kwon et al. (Int J Mol Sci 2022): "approximately 80% of the treated PD patients develop levodopa-induced dyskinesia (LID) in the advanced stages." — [Kwon 2022, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36496996)
- CALM-PD (Parkinson Study Group, JAMA 2000;284:1931-1938): initial pramipexole vs levodopa — fewer dopaminergic motor complications with pramipexole (28% vs 51%; HR 0.45) but greater UPDRS improvement with levodopa (9.2 vs 4.5 points) and more somnolence with pramipexole (32.4% vs 17.3%). — [CALM-PD 2000, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11035889)

**Continuous-delivery hypothesis at initiation: STRIDE-PD**
- STRIDE-PD (Stocchi et al., Ann Neurol 2010;68:18-27): 747 patients initiating levodopa randomized to levodopa/carbidopa (LC) vs levodopa/carbidopa/entacapone (LCE) 4x/day at 3.5-h intervals for 134 weeks. LCE gave a *shorter* time to dyskinesia (HR 1.29; p=0.04) and higher dyskinesia frequency at week 134 (42% vs 32%; p=0.02), attributed to higher levodopa-equivalent exposure and failure to achieve truly continuous delivery. — [Stocchi 2010, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20582993/)

**Extended-release oral levodopa: Rytary (IPX066) and Crexont (IPX203)**
- ADVANCE-PD (Hauser et al., Lancet Neurol 2013;12:346-356): IPX066 (ER carbidopa-levodopa, later Rytary) reduced OFF time by an extra 1.17 h/day vs IR (95% CI -1.69 to -0.66; p<0.0001) at ~3.6 vs 5.0 doses/day; end-of-study OFF 23.82% vs 29.79% of waking day. — [Hauser 2013, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23485610)
- RISE-PD (Hauser, Espay, Ellenbogen et al., JAMA Neurol 2023;80:1062-1069): 20-week, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled phase 3 at 105 centers; 630 enrolled, 506 randomized (IPX203 n=256; IR CD/LD n=250). Primary endpoint met: good ON time +0.53 h/day vs IR (95% CI 0.09-0.97; P=.02) with IPX203 dosed a mean 3x/day vs 5x/day for IR; good ON time per dose +1.55 h (95% CI 1.37-1.73; P<.001). Most common AEs: nausea (4.3% vs 0.8%), anxiety (2.7% vs 0%). — [Hauser 2023, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37578800/)
- RISE-PD 9-month open-label extension (Espay et al., Mov Disord 2024;39:428-432): efficacy maintained, dosing frequency and total dose stable; 52.7% had ≥1 TEAE, mostly mild/moderate and within the first 90 days. — [Espay 2024, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38111267/)
- FDA approved IPX203 as Crexont (carbidopa/levodopa ER capsules, IR granules plus ER pellets) on August 7, 2024; the label permits conversion from Rytary; US launch September 2024. — [Amneal press release](https://investors.amneal.com/news/press-releases/press-release-details/2024/Amneal-Receives-U.S.-FDA-Approval-for-IPX203-for-Treatment-of-Parkinsons-Disease-to-Be-Launched-as-CREXONT-Carbidopa-and-Levodopa-Extended-Release-Capsules/default.aspx); [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-carbidopa-levodopa-extended-release-capsules-formulation-parkinson-disease-treatment); in the double-blind phase 29.7% of IPX203 vs 18.8% of IR patients rated themselves "much/very much improved," and OFF time difference was -0.48 h/day (P=.03) — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-carbidopa-levodopa-extended-release-capsules-formulation-parkinson-disease-treatment)

**Inhaled levodopa (CVT-301 / Inbrija)**
- SPAN-PD (LeWitt et al., Lancet Neurol 2019;18:145-154): 351 patients randomized to CVT-301 60 mg, 84 mg or placebo, self-administered as needed up to 5x/day for OFF periods. Primary endpoint: UPDRS-III change predose to 30 min postdose at week 12 in-clinic OFF: -9.83 (84 mg) vs -5.91 (placebo); difference -3.92 (95% CI -6.84 to -1.00; p=0.0088). Serious AEs in 3% overall. — [LeWitt 2019, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30663606/)
- FDA approved Inbrija on December 21, 2018 for intermittent treatment of OFF episodes in patients taking carbidopa/levodopa; Arcus dry-powder technology (particles 1-5 µm). — [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/inhaled-levodopa-time-approved-fda); [Drugs.com approval history](https://www.drugs.com/history/inbrija.html); [technology evaluation, Expert Opin Drug Deliv 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17425247.2021.1960820)

**Guideline position on early PD formulations (see KQ6 for detail)**
- AAN 2021: "Long-acting forms of levodopa and levodopa with entacapone do not appear to differ in efficacy from immediate-release levodopa for motor symptoms in early disease." — [Pringsheim 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34782410/)
- MDS EBM 2025 motor fluctuations update rated "levodopa extended release" as *efficacious* for motor fluctuations. — [de Bie 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)

### Inferences
- The combination of LEAP (no progression difference at 80 weeks or 5 years; no excess dyskinesia with earlier start), Cilia 2014 (complications track disease duration and mg/kg dose), and PD MED (small persistent patient-rated advantage for levodopa-first; see KQ6) explains why both AAN 2021 and NICE 2017 moved to levodopa-first for symptomatic patients.
- The ~40% at 4-6 years figure (Ahlskog) is incident, not necessarily "troublesome," dyskinesia; the higher 80% figures refer to advanced disease, so headline rates should be quoted with their time horizon.
- STRIDE-PD's negative result supports the view that pulsatile delivery must be converted to genuinely continuous delivery (infusions, KQ3) to alter dyskinesia risk, not simply prolonged by enzyme inhibition.
- Crexont's marginal ON-time gain (+0.53 h/day) was achieved at ~2 fewer doses/day; the clinically meaningful signal is convenience and per-dose duration rather than a large efficacy jump over IR.

### Gaps
- Carbidopa vs benserazide: I found no comparative trial or review in this session specific to the two decarboxylase inhibitors; details are not sourced here.
- Full text of the 1967 and 1969 Cotzias papers were not accessible; only bibliographic data were verified.
- Exact wording of the Crexont label and any post-marketing data beyond the 9-month extension were not retrieved (Drugs.com returned 403).

## Key Question 2: Dopamine agonists, MAO-B inhibitors (rasagiline/ADAGIO, safinamide), COMT inhibitors (opicapone), amantadine ER (Gocovri), istradefylline, apomorphine (sublingual Kynmobi, infusion), and impulse-control disorders

### Takeaway
Adjunct agents each add roughly 0.4-1.0 h/day of good ON time (opicapone 50 mg -60.8 min OFF vs placebo; safinamide +0.96 h ON; istradefylline -0.38 to -0.45 h OFF; amantadine ER -7.9 UDysRS points and -0.9 h OFF; apomorphine infusion -1.89 h OFF in TOLEDO). ADAGIO's claim of rasagiline disease modification was positive for 1 mg but not 2 mg and is not accepted by guidelines; dopamine agonists carry a 2-4-fold ICD risk (13.6% point prevalence in DOMINION; 46% 5-year cumulative incidence in DIG-PD). Sublingual apomorphine (Kynmobi) was approved in 2020 but withdrawn from the US market for limited use; the first US apomorphine infusion device (SPN-830/Onapgo) was approved in 2025.

### Cited Findings

**Dopamine agonists and impulse-control disorders**
- DOMINION (Weintraub et al., Arch Neurol 2010;67:589-595): cross-sectional, 3,090 treated PD patients at 46 North American centers. ICD in 13.6% (gambling 5.0%, compulsive sexual behavior 3.5%, compulsive buying 5.7%, binge eating 4.3%); 3.9% had ≥2 ICDs. ICDs in 17.1% on a dopamine agonist vs 6.9% not (OR 2.72; 95% CI 2.08-3.54; P<.001); pramipexole vs ropinirole similar (17.7% vs 15.5%). — [Weintraub 2010, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20457959/); [JAMA Neurology full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/800232)
- DIG-PD longitudinal (Corvol et al., Neurology 2018;91:e189-e201): 411 patients, mean follow-up 3.3 years; 5-year cumulative ICD incidence 46.1% (DA ever-users 51.5%; never-users 12.4%); prevalence rose from 19.7% to 32.8%; prevalence ratio with DA ever-use 4.23 (95% CI 1.78-10.09); dose- and duration-effect relationships; ICDs resolved progressively after DA discontinuation; no strong association with levodopa. — [Corvol 2018, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29925549)
- AAN 2021 guideline: "There is a higher risk of impulse control disorders associated with the use of dopamine agonists than levodopa," and no evidence that any agonist formulation is superior. — [Pringsheim 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34782410/)
- PD MED (Gray et al., Lancet 2014;384:1196-1205): 28% of 632 patients allocated dopamine agonists and 23% of 460 allocated MAO-B inhibitors discontinued for side effects vs 2% of 528 on levodopa (p<0.0001). — [PD MED 2014, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24928805)
- NICE NG71 1.3.7/1.3.11: do not offer ergot-derived agonists first line; choose non-ergot agonists in most cases; 1.3.8 requires oral and written information about ICD risk "with all dopaminergic therapy (and the increased risk with dopamine agonists)" at treatment start. — [NICE NG71 Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/Recommendations)

**MAO-B inhibitors**
- ADAGIO (Olanow et al., N Engl J Med 2009;361:1268-1278): 1,176 untreated PD patients, delayed-start design; rasagiline 1 mg/day met all three hierarchical endpoints (slower UPDRS worsening weeks 12-36: 0.09 vs 0.14 points/week, P=0.01; less worsening baseline-to-week-72: 2.82 vs 4.52 points, P=0.02; noninferior slopes weeks 48-72), but the 2 mg/day dose did not, leaving the disease-modification claim unconfirmed. — [Olanow 2009, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19776408/)
- MDS EBM 2018 (Fox et al.): "There are no clinically useful interventions to prevent/delay disease progression"; selegiline and rasagiline are clinically useful as monotherapy; rasagiline, safinamide and zonisamide clinically useful for motor fluctuations. — [Fox 2018, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570866/)
- NICE NG71 1.6.4: "Do not use MAO-B inhibitors as neuroprotective therapies ... except in the context of clinical trials." — [NICE NG71](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/Recommendations)
- SETTLE (Schapira et al., JAMA Neurol 2017;74:216-224): 549 patients with >1.5 h/day OFF randomized to safinamide 50→100 mg or placebo for 24 weeks; ON time without troublesome dyskinesia +1.42 vs +0.57 h (LS mean difference 0.96 h; 95% CI 0.56-1.37; P<.001); dyskinesia most frequent AE (14.6% vs 5.5%). — [Schapira 2017, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27942720/)
- Safinamide (MAO-B inhibition plus glutamate-release modulation via Na+/Ca2+ channels) was FDA-approved as adjunct to carbidopa/levodopa in 2017; approved in Europe 2015. — [Aradi & Hauser, Neurotherapeutics 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13311-020-00889-4); [Regensburger 2023, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36964457)

**COMT inhibitors**
- BIPARK-1 (Ferreira et al., Lancet Neurol 2016;15:154-165): 600 randomized across 106 centers to opicapone 5/25/50 mg once daily, placebo, or entacapone 200 mg with each levodopa dose for 14-15 weeks. OFF-time change: placebo -56.0 min; entacapone -96.3 min; opicapone 50 mg -116.8 min. Opicapone 50 mg superior to placebo (-60.8 min; 95% CI -97.2 to -24.4; p=0.0015) and non-inferior to entacapone (-26.2 min; p=0.0051). Dyskinesia most common AE (18 patients on 50 mg vs 5 placebo). — [Ferreira 2016, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26725544/)
- Opicapone (Ongentys) FDA approval April 2020 for OFF episodes; in BIPARK-1 open-label extension, patients switching from entacapone to opicapone gained 39.3 min less OFF time. — [Aradi & Hauser 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13311-020-00889-4); [Expert Opin Pharmacother 2019 review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/14656566.2019.1681971)
- Opicapone and entacapone are rated *efficacious* and *likely efficacious*, respectively, for motor fluctuations in MDS EBM 2025. — [de Bie 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)

**Amantadine extended release for dyskinesia**
- EASE LID (Pahwa et al., JAMA Neurol 2017;74:941-949): 126 randomized (121 mITT) to ADS-5102 274 mg at bedtime vs placebo for up to 25 weeks. UDysRS change at week 12: -15.9 vs -8.0 (difference -7.9; 95% CI -12.5 to -3.3; P<.001); OFF time -0.6 vs +0.3 h (difference -0.9 h; P=.02). AEs: visual hallucinations 23.8% vs 1.7%, peripheral edema 23.8% vs 0, dizziness 22.2% vs 0. — [Pahwa 2017, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28604926/)
- EASE LID 3 confirmed and pooled analyses showed Gocovri increased ON time without dyskinesia; EASE LID 2 was a 2-year open-label extension. — [Pooled analysis, PMC8032973](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8032973/); [EASE LID 2 (ResearchGate record)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338582415_EASE_LID_2_A_2-Year_Open-Label_Trial_of_Gocovri_Amantadine_Extended_Release_for_Dyskinesia_in_Parkinson's_Disease); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02136914](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02136914)
- MDS EBM 2018: for dyskinesia, "amantadine, clozapine, and bilateral STN DBS and GPi DBS are clinically useful." MDS 2025: amantadine ER *likely efficacious* for motor fluctuations. — [Fox 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570866/); [de Bie 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)

**Istradefylline (adenosine A2A antagonist)**
- Approved in Japan 2013 and in the US 2019 (Nourianz) as adjunct to levodopa/carbidopa for OFF episodes. — [Aradi & Hauser 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13311-020-00889-4); [FDA review documents NDA 022075](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2019/022075Orig1s000RiskR.pdf)
- Pooled analysis of 8 phase 2b/3 trials (Hauser et al., J Parkinsons Dis 2021;11:1663-1675): 2,719 patients; OFF h/day reduction vs placebo -0.38 h (20 mg) and -0.45 h (40 mg; p<0.0001); four-study pool -0.75/-0.82 h; dyskinesia most frequent AE (9.6% placebo vs 16.1%/17.7%); completion 89.2%. — [Hauser 2021, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34486986)
- Post hoc pooled analysis (Hattori et al., J Mov Disord 2025;18:262-267): benefit independent of baseline dyskinesia (56% had it); treatment-emergent dyskinesia 17%, higher in women (21%), BMI <18.5 (22%), and those on COMT-I + DA (22%) or MAO-B-I (25%). — [Hattori 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40276907)
- MDS EBM 2025 rates istradefylline *likely efficacious* for motor fluctuations. — [de Bie 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)

**Apomorphine**
- Sublingual film CTH-300 (Olanow et al., Lancet Neurol 2020;19:135-144): phase 3, 109 randomized; MDS-UPDRS III difference vs placebo -7.6 points at 30 min (95% CI -11.5 to -3.7; p=0.0002); oropharyngeal AEs in 31% vs 7%, causing 17% discontinuation; one fatal cardiac arrest in a patient with cardiac risk factors. — [Olanow 2020, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31818699)
- Kynmobi FDA label (approved 2020, NDA 210875). — [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/210875lbl.pdf)
- Sunovion voluntarily withdrew Kynmobi from the US (and Canadian) market "due to limited utilization"; all strengths 10-30 mg discontinued. — [Parkinson's News Today](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/kynmobi-discontinued-us-canada-limited-use-sunovion/); [Drugs.com generic availability](https://www.drugs.com/availability/generic-kynmobi.html)
- TOLEDO (Katzenschlager et al., Lancet Neurol 2018;17:749-759): first double-blind RCT of apomorphine infusion; 107 randomized (106 FAS) at 23 European hospitals; 3-8 mg/h for 16 h/day for 12 weeks; mean final dose 4.68 mg/h; OFF time -2.47 vs -0.58 h/day (difference -1.89 h; 95% CI -3.16 to -0.62; p=0.0025); six withdrawals for treatment-related AEs. — [Katzenschlager 2018, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30055903/)
- Auffret (Parkinsonism Relat Disord 2025) notes the "first U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval of a subcutaneous apomorphine infusion device for advanced Parkinson's disease (SPN-830)" and that TOLEDO removed the historic lack-of-class-1-evidence barrier. — [Auffret 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40393897)
- MDS EBM 2025 rates continuous subcutaneous apomorphine *likely efficacious* for motor fluctuations. — [de Bie 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)

**Class-level summary**
- MDS EBM 2025 (102 RCTs, GRADE-based): *efficacious* for motor fluctuations — levodopa ER, pramipexole IR and ER, ropinirole IR, rotigotine, opicapone, safinamide, bilateral STN DBS; *likely efficacious* — intestinal levodopa infusion, subcutaneous levodopa, subcutaneous apomorphine, ropinirole PR and patch, entacapone, rasagiline, istradefylline, amantadine ER, zonisamide, bilateral GPi DBS, pallidotomy. — [de Bie 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)

### Inferences
- The incremental ON-time gain of each oral adjunct is modest (roughly 0.5-1 h/day) and dyskinesia is the shared dose-limiting AE across COMT inhibitors, safinamide and istradefylline, so clinicians typically trade OFF reduction against increased dyskinesia or add amantadine ER.
- Kynmobi's commercial withdrawal despite a positive phase 3 illustrates that tolerability (oropharyngeal irritation) and reimbursement can decide a product's fate independent of efficacy.
- Apomorphine infusion's US availability (SPN-830) arrives just as subcutaneous levodopa infusion (Vyalev) competes for the same "pre-DBS" niche; head-to-head data are absent.

### Gaps
- Exact FDA approval date and brand details for SPN-830 (Onapgo) could not be confirmed from a primary source in this session (search budget exhausted; Bing/Yahoo/DDG fetches failed); only the Auffret 2025 review's statement that it is the first FDA-approved apomorphine infusion device was verified.
- Exact date of the Kynmobi discontinuation announcement was not retrievable (Parkinson's News Today page returned 403; only the search snippet was available).
- No pivotal trial for istradefylline is cited individually; the 8-trial pooled analysis is used instead. Istradefylline's 2008 FDA non-approval history was not verified in this session.
- Rotigotine, ropinirole and pramipexole pivotal trials were not individually retrieved; their ratings come from the MDS 2025 review.

## Key Question 3: Continuous infusions — foslevodopa/foscarbidopa (Vyalev), ND0612 (BouNDless), levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel (Duopa/Duodopa), levodopa-entacapone-carbidopa intestinal gel (Lecigon)

### Takeaway
Continuous 24-h subcutaneous levodopa prodrug infusion (Vyalev) was FDA-approved on Oct 17, 2024 on the strength of a 12-week double-blind trial showing +1.75 h good ON and -1.79 h OFF versus oral levodopa, with infusion-site reactions driving 22% discontinuation; ND0612 produced a similar +1.72 h good ON gain in BouNDless but received two FDA complete response letters (June 2024, October 2025). Intrajejunal LCIG remains the best-established infusion (OFF -1.91 h vs oral in the 2014 double-blind trial), and LECIG (Lecigon) uses entacapone to cut levodopa dose and pump size, with 12-month ELEGANCE data showing -3.47 h OFF.

### Cited Findings

**Foslevodopa/foscarbidopa (ABBV-951, Vyalev)**
- Pivotal trial (Soileau et al., Lancet Neurol 2022;21:1099-1109): 12-week, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled, 65 US/Australian centers; 141 randomized (infusion n=74; oral IR levodopa/carbidopa n=67). ON time without troublesome dyskinesia +2.72 vs +0.97 h (difference 1.75 h; 95% CI 0.46-3.05; p=0.0083); OFF time -2.75 vs -0.96 h (difference -1.79 h; 95% CI -3.03 to -0.54; p=0.0054). AEs 85% vs 63%; serious AEs 8% vs 6%; infusion-site erythema 27%, pain 26%, cellulitis 19%, edema 12%; discontinuation for AEs 22% vs 1%. — [Soileau 2022, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36402160/)
- 52-week open-label phase 3 (Aldred et al., Neurol Ther 2023;12:1937-1958): 244 enrolled, 107 discontinued, 137 completed; at week 52 normalized ON without troublesome dyskinesia +3.8 h and OFF -3.5 h; morning akinesia fell from 77.7% to 27.8%; serious AEs in 25.8%; infusion-site events most common. — [Aldred 2023, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37632656)
- FDA approval of Vyalev (foscarbidopa/foslevodopa) for motor fluctuations in advanced PD announced October 17, 2024 — first 24-hour continuous subcutaneous levodopa-based infusion; most frequent AEs infusion-site reactions, hallucinations, dyskinesia; Medicare coverage anticipated 2H 2025. — [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-approves-foscarbidopa-foslevodopa-for-motor-fluctuations-in-adults-with-parkinson-disease); [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/neurology/fda-approves-vyalev-for-advanced-parkinson-disease); [APDA](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/vyalev-a-newly-approved-medication-for-parkinsons-disease-motor-fluctuations/)

**ND0612 (liquid levodopa/carbidopa subcutaneous infusion)**
- BouNDless (Espay et al., Lancet Neurol 2024;23:465-476): phase 3, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled at 117 sites in 16 countries; after open-label optimization of both regimens, 259 randomized (ND0612 n=128; oral n=131) to 12 weeks; 94% completed. Good ON +1.72 h vs oral (95% CI 1.08-2.36; p<0.0001; -0.48 vs -2.20 h change from post-run-in baseline); OFF -1.40 h; MDS-UPDRS II -3.05; PGI-C OR 5.31; CGI-I OR 7.23. Infusion-site reactions in 83% during open-label ND0612 and 57% vs 43% during double-blind; serious treatment-related AEs in 4 (cellulitis x2, abscess/ulcer, neuropathy); one unrelated death. — [Espay 2024, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499015/)
- FDA issued a Complete Response Letter on June 11, 2024 to Mitsubishi Tanabe/NeuroDerm. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/parkinson-therapy-nd0612-receives-complete-response-letter)
- The company resubmitted in May 2025 after gathering more safety data on one drug component plus product quality, device and manufacturing-site inspection details; FDA accepted the resubmission; a second CRL was issued in October 2025 (Tanabe announcement dated October 23, 2025) indicating further questions remained. — [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/second-fda-response-letter-issued-nd0612-therapy); [NeuroDerm](https://neuroderm.com/our-company/news-and-events/us-fda-accepts-resubmission-for-nd0612-an-investigational-treatment-for-motor-fluctuations-in-parkinson-s-disease/); [Tanabe Pharma, 2025-10-23](https://www.tanabe-pharma.com/en/news/news20251023103060.html)

**Levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel (LCIG; Duodopa/Duopa)**
- Olanow et al. (Lancet Neurol 2014;13:141-149): 12-week, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, double-titration trial at 26 centers (Germany, New Zealand, USA); all had PEG-J placement, then LCIG + oral placebo (n=37) or oral IR + placebo gel (n=34). OFF time -4.04 vs -2.14 h (difference -1.91 h; 95% CI -3.05 to -0.76; p=0.0015); ON without troublesome dyskinesia +4.11 vs +2.24 h (difference 1.86 h; p=0.0059). AEs 95% and 100%, serious 14% and 21%, mainly PEG-J related; "first demonstration of the benefit of continuous levodopa delivery in a double-blind controlled study." — [Olanow 2014, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24361112/)
- MDS EBM 2018 rated levodopa intestinal infusion clinically useful for motor fluctuations; MDS 2025 rates it *likely efficacious*. — [Fox 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570866/); [de Bie 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)

**Levodopa-entacapone-carbidopa intestinal gel (LECIG; Lecigon)**
- LECIG was introduced in 2018, first in Sweden; entacapone increases levodopa bioavailability so lower levodopa doses/volumes are needed, allowing a smaller cartridge and pump; compatible with an existing PEG-J from LCIG; motor efficacy similar to LCIG. — [Nyholm & Jost, Ther Adv Neurol Disord 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35785401)
- ELEGANCE interim analysis (Weiss et al., Mov Disord Clin Pract 2025;12:1075-1085; NCT05043103; 13 European countries, ~300 planned, 24-month follow-up): 167 patients from 37 centers; OFF time reduced by 3.47 ± 3.56 h at 3-6 months (baseline 5.15 h; P<0.0001); MDS-UPDRS IV -4.24, Part II -3.63; PDSS-2 -7.38; PDQ-8 -13.3; 1.8% discontinued; AEs mostly procedure/device-related. — [Weiss 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40129359); [PMC12371452](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12371452/); [Wiley DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mdc3.70046)
- Spanish real-world cohort (Santos-García et al., Eur J Neurol 2025): 73 patients, 35.6% switched directly from LCIG; OFF time 5.2 → 1.9 h/day (p<0.0001); 46.6% had ≥1 LECIG/device AE; 6.8% discontinued. — [Santos-García 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39466665)

**Class positioning**
- The MDS 2025 EBM review lists "continuous intestinal levodopa infusion, continuous subcutaneous levodopa, continuous subcutaneous apomorphine" all as *likely efficacious* (not *efficacious*) for motor fluctuations, reflecting single pivotal RCTs with limited blinding durability. — [de Bie 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)
- Fabbri, Barbosa and Rascol (Neurol Ther 2023) frame the choice among DBS, intestinal/subcutaneous levodopa and apomorphine as a shared decision based on contraindications, target symptom, preference, caregiver availability and health-system factors. — [Fabbri 2023, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36633762)

### Inferences
- The two subcutaneous levodopa programs delivered near-identical ON-time gains (+1.75 h and +1.72 h) with the same Achilles' heel (infusion-site reactions in >50%), yet only Vyalev was approved; ND0612's CRLs appear to concern component safety, quality, device and manufacturing rather than efficacy.
- Skin tolerability, not motor efficacy, is the main differentiator against surgical options (LCIG's AEs are PEG-J-related; subcutaneous infusions trade a tube for nodules/cellulitis).
- LECIG's smaller pump and entacapone-driven dose reduction address usability complaints about LCIG; there is no randomized LCIG-vs-LECIG trial, so equivalence rests on pharmacokinetics and observational data.

### Gaps
- Duopa's US approval date (2015) and the Lecigon approval chronology beyond "introduced in Sweden in 2018" were not confirmed from a primary regulatory source in this session.
- Vyalev's label details (cannula-change interval, titration) could not be verified (Drugs.com 403); the APDA page lacked them.
- Head-to-head randomized comparisons among LCIG, foslevodopa, apomorphine infusion and DBS were not found.
- Specific content of the FDA's second ND0612 CRL (October 2025) is not public in the sources reached.

## Key Question 4: Deep brain stimulation — landmark trials, STN vs GPi, long-term outcomes, adaptive/closed-loop DBS (Medtronic BrainSense, ADAPT-PD), directional leads, asleep DBS

### Takeaway
Five RCTs against best medical therapy (Deuschl 2006, Weaver 2009, Williams 2010, Okun 2012, Schuepbach 2013/EARLYSTIM) established DBS as superior to medication for advanced and early-complication PD (e.g., +4.6 h/day good ON in the VA trial; PDQ-39 +8.0 points at 2 years in EARLYSTIM); STN and GPi are broadly equivalent for motor outcome over 24 months (Follett 2010) and 10 years (Ostrem 2025), with STN allowing greater medication reduction. The FDA approved the first commercial adaptive DBS (Medtronic BrainSense, Percept) on Feb 24, 2025 based on ADAPT-PD (JAMA Neurol 2025), in which 91%/79% of participants met the ON-time performance goal and energy use fell 15%; directional leads widen the therapeutic window by 41% (PROGRESS), and the GALAXY RCT found asleep DBS non-inferior to awake.

### Cited Findings

**Landmark RCTs versus best medical therapy**
- Deuschl et al. (N Engl J Med 2006;355:896-908): 156 patients <75 years with severe motor complications, pairwise randomized to bilateral STN DBS or medication; at 6 months DBS improved PDQ-39 (50/78 pairs; mean 9.5 points; P=0.02) and UPDRS-III (55/78 pairs; mean 19.6 points; P<0.001); serious AEs 13% vs 4% including a fatal intracerebral hemorrhage. — [Deuschl 2006, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16943402/)
- VA CSP #468 (Weaver et al., JAMA 2009;301:63-73): 255 patients (25% ≥70 years) at 7 VA and 6 university hospitals; DBS (STN n=60, GPi n=61) vs best medical therapy (n=134); at 6 months DBS gained 4.6 h/day ON without troubling dyskinesia vs 0 h (difference 4.5 h; 95% CI 3.7-5.4; P<.001); clinically meaningful motor improvement (≥5 UPDRS-III points) in 71% vs 32%. — [Weaver 2009, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19126811/)
- PD SURG (Williams et al., Lancet Neurol 2010;9:581-591): 366 UK patients randomized to immediate surgery plus BMT or BMT alone; at 1 year PDQ-39 improved 5.0 vs 0.3 points (difference -4.7; 95% CI -7.6 to -1.8; p=0.001); 19% had serious surgery-related AEs; one procedure-related death. — [Williams 2010, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20434403/)
- Okun et al. (Lancet Neurol 2012;11:140-149): first controlled trial of a constant-current STN device; 136 implanted, randomized 3:1 to immediate vs 3-month-delayed activation; good-quality ON time +4.27 vs +1.77 h at 3 months (difference 2.51 h; 95% CI 0.87-4.16; p=0.003). — [Okun 2012, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22239915/)
- EARLYSTIM (Schuepbach et al., N Engl J Med 2013;368:610-622): 251 patients with early motor complications (mean age 52; disease duration 7.5 years) randomized to STN DBS plus medication vs medication; 2-year PDQ-39 improved 7.8 points vs worsened 0.2 (difference 8.0; P=0.002); superior for UPDRS-III, ADL, motor complications, and time with good mobility without dyskinesia; serious AEs 54.8% vs 44.1%; device/surgery-related serious AEs 17.7%. — [Schuepbach 2013, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23406026/)
- EARLYSTIM behavioural secondary analysis (Lhommée et al., Lancet Neurol 2018;17:223-231): better overall behavioural outcomes with STN DBS; antidepressants stopped in 12 vs 4; neuroleptics started in 1 vs 9; two suicides in the DBS arm and one in the medical arm over 2 years. — [Lhommée 2018, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29452685)
- Deuschl and Agid (Lancet Neurol 2013;12:1025-1034) argued EARLYSTIM supports earlier use "for carefully selected patients" and that by reducing fluctuations DBS "changes the disease course," while noting no evidence that levodopa-resistant symptoms are delayed. — [Deuschl & Agid 2013, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24050735)

**STN versus GPi**
- Follett et al. (N Engl J Med 2010;362:2077-2091): 299 randomized (GPi 152; STN 147); 24-month blinded off-medication UPDRS-III change did not differ (P=0.50); STN allowed lower dopaminergic doses (P=0.02) but visuomotor processing speed declined more (P=0.03) and depression worsened after STN while improving after GPi (P=0.02); serious AEs 51% vs 56%. — [Follett 2010, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20519680/)
- NSTAPS (Odekerken et al., Lancet Neurol 2013;12:37-44): 128 randomized; no difference in primary outcomes (weighted ALDS; composite cognitive/mood/behavioral AEs 58% vs 56%), but STN gave larger off-drug UPDRS-III improvement (20.3 vs 11.4; p=0.03) and greater LEDD reduction (546 vs 208 mg; p=0.01); authors suggested STN "could be the preferred target." 3-year follow-up found no cognitive/psychiatric differences. — [Odekerken 2013, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23168021); [Boel 2016, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27688200)
- VA/NINDS CSP #468F 10-year substudy (Ostrem, Luo, Weaver et al., Front Neurol 2025;16:1728999): 156 enrolled; 68 GPi/49 STN at 7 years and 49 GPi/28 STN at 10 years; no difference in time trend between targets. Off-med/on-stim UPDRS-III improvement from baseline: GPi 39.9% (2 y), 16.4% (7 y), 22.3% (10 y; P=0.10); STN 34.9%, 16.9%, 32.8% (P<0.001). Tremor improved most, then rigidity; bradykinesia and axial gains attenuated; UPDRS IV and diaries showed durable benefit; PDQ-39 no longer improved at 7-10 years; cognitive impairment increased in both targets. — [Ostrem 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41626017/); [PMC12856926](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12856926/)
- Meta-analysis (Lachenmayer et al., NPJ Parkinsons Dis 2021;7:77): 39 STN studies (2,035 subjects) and 5 GPi studies (292): UPDRS-III improved 50.5% with STN and 29.8% with GPi; STN reduced dyskinesia 64%, OFF time 69.1%, LEDD 50%; infection 5.1%, intracranial hemorrhage 3.1%; preoperative levodopa response predicted outcome. — [Lachenmayer 2021, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34489472); [Nature article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-021-00223-5)

**Long-term outcomes**
- Krack et al. (N Engl J Med 2003;349:1925-1934): first 49 consecutive bilateral STN patients; at 5 years off-medication motor scores improved 54% and ADL 49% vs baseline; speech did not improve; on-medication akinesia, speech, postural stability and freezing worsened from year 1 to 5; dopaminergic dose and dyskinesia reduced; dementia in 3 patients. — [Krack 2003, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14614167/)
- INTREPID 5-year (Starr et al., JAMA Neurol 2025;82:1181-1190): 313 enrolled, 191 implanted with Vercise STN system, 137 (72%) completed; off-med UPDRS-III 42.8 → 21.1 at 1 year (51%) and 27.6 at 5 years (36%); UPDRS-II 41% → 22%; dyskinesia -75% → -70%; LEDD -28% stable; infection most common serious AE (9); 10 unrelated deaths. — [Starr 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40952750/)
- 15-year STN-DBS cohorts (Zampogna et al., NPJ Parkinsons Dis 2022;8:121 and 2025;11:284): of 302 patients, 102 and 57 available at 10 and 15 years; axial scores stable at 1 year but worsened at 10 and 15 years with rising frequent-faller prevalence; resting tremor remained responsive long term while action tremor worsened. — [Zampogna 2022, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36153351/); [Zampogna 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41038844/)
- Mahlknecht, Foltynie, Limousin and Poewe (Mov Disord 2022;37:1581-1592): controlled long-term observational signals that STN DBS may delay psychosis, falls and institutionalization and slightly prolong survival vs matched medically managed patients, without evidence of effects on underlying progression. — [Mahlknecht 2022, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35560443)

**Adaptive / closed-loop DBS**
- ADAPT-PD pivotal trial (Bronte-Stewart, Beudel, Ostrem et al., JAMA Neurol 2025;82:1171-1180; NCT04547712): international, open-label, prospective; 68 participants stable on cDBS with Percept PC (STN or GPi); those tolerating both single-threshold (ST) and dual-threshold (DT) aDBS modes were randomized/blinded to 30 days each (crossover), then optional 10-month long-term follow-up. Primary endpoint (≥50% meeting ON-time-without-troublesome-dyskinesia performance goal vs cDBS) met: DT-aDBS 91%, ST-aDBS 79% (no difference between modes, P=.51); TEED -15% with ST-aDBS (nominal P=.01); all but one stimulation-related AE resolved during setup; no serious device AEs; exploratory improvement in good ON time with DT-aDBS. — [Bronte-Stewart 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40982287/); [PMC12455485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12455485/)
- ADAPT-PD sensing methodology (NPJ Parkinsons Dis 2024): LFP signal detection in 84.8% of medicated and 92% of unmedicated patients; 84% met inclusion criteria for aDBS programming. — [Nature NPJ PD 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00772-5); [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-medtronic-adaptive-deep-brain-stimulation-parkinson-disease)
- FDA approved Medtronic BrainSense Adaptive DBS and BrainSense Electrode Identifier on February 24, 2025 (Percept RC/PC), the first commercially available adaptive DBS, following CE mark about two months earlier. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-medtronic-adaptive-deep-brain-stimulation-parkinson-disease); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250303/Medtronic-receives-FDA-approval-for-BrainSense-Adaptive-DBS-technology.aspx); [APDA](https://www.apdaparkinson.org/article/fda-approves-new-adaptive-dbs-system/); [TIME Best Inventions 2025 release](https://news.medtronic.com/2025-10-09-Medtronic-BrainSense-TM-Adaptive-Deep-Brain-Stimulation-named-a-2025-TIME-Best-Inventions)
- Early real-world experience, ADAPT-START (Cascino et al., NPJ Parkinsons Dis 2026;12:85): of 20 consecutive chronic-cDBS Percept patients offered DT-aDBS (Jan-Apr 2025), 9 were eligible (exclusions: artifacts, no distinct beta peak, incompatible settings); by July 2025 five on chronic aDBS, one reverted by preference, three optimizing; unblinded MDS-UPDRS III ~35% greater improvement than cDBS, gait most consistently improved (~40% FOG-Q reduction); authors caution that technical and programming constraints limit adoption. — [Cascino 2026, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41741462); [Nature article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01269-z)
- Low-frequency (60 Hz) aDBS pilot for freezing (Negi et al., medRxiv 2025): 8 STN patients with Summit RC+S; 60 Hz cDBS tolerated acutely by 6/8 and shortened beta bursts; gait improved with 60 Hz aDBS/cDBS in those with baseline FOG, but overall motor signs including tremor improved only with 140 Hz cDBS. — [Negi 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41480041/)

**Directional leads**
- PROGRESS (Schnitzler et al., Neuromodulation 2022;25:817-828): 234 STN patients; crossover omnidirectional (3 months) then directional (3 months); therapeutic window wider with directional stimulation in 183/202 (90.6%), mean +41% (2.98 vs 2.11 mA); UPDRS-III on-med improved 42.4% and 43.3% at 3 and 6 months. — [Schnitzler 2022, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34047410/)
- A randomized long-term trial of directional DBS on motor and non-motor symptoms was posted as a medRxiv preprint (2023). — [medRxiv 2023](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.10.30.23297793.full.pdf)

**Asleep versus awake surgery**
- GALAXY (Holewijn et al., JAMA Neurol 2021;78:1212-1219): single-center RCT, 110 randomized (awake n=56; asleep under general anesthesia n=54), both with frame-based microelectrode recording; composite cognitive/mood/behavioral AEs 29% vs 22% (OR 0.7; 95% CI 0.3-1.7); off-med MDS-UPDRS III improvement -27.3 vs -25.3 points (difference -2.0; 95% CI -8.1 to 4.2); asleep surgery less burdensome and 26 minutes shorter. — [Holewijn 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34491267/)

**Guideline ratings**
- MDS EBM 2018: bilateral STN and GPi DBS clinically useful for motor fluctuations and for dyskinesia. MDS 2025: bilateral STN DBS *efficacious*; bilateral GPi DBS *likely efficacious* for motor fluctuations. — [Fox 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570866/); [de Bie 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)
- Monje et al. (Curr Neurol Neurosci Rep 2026;26:11) summarize that imaging-guided programming may shorten programming time with equivalent outcomes, adaptive DBS may improve symptoms and QoL with lower energy use, and remote programming expands access. — [Monje 2026, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41706242)

### Inferences
- The robust and durable DBS benefits are on levodopa-responsive signs (tremor, rigidity, fluctuations, dyskinesia); axial/gait, speech and cognition follow the natural history, which is why 10-15-year cohorts show PDQ-39 returning to baseline despite persistent UPDRS-IV gains.
- The ADAPT-PD design (non-inferiority-style performance goal in patients already stable on cDBS, open-label long-term phase) establishes safety and tolerability more than superiority; the energy saving and exploratory ON-time gain are the main efficacy signals, and ADAPT-START shows only ~45% of chronic cDBS patients are currently eligible.
- GPi is favored where medication reduction is less important and mood/cognition are concerns; STN where dose reduction matters. The 10-year VA data show neither target "wins" on motor trajectory.

### Gaps
- EARLYSTIM long-term (5-year or beyond) randomized outcomes were not found in this session; only the 2-year primary paper and behavioural secondary analysis are cited.
- The ADAPT-PD final 10-month long-term dataset beyond the JAMA Neurology report, and any randomized aDBS-vs-cDBS superiority trial, were not located (ADAPT-PD completion was projected for October 2025).
- Reimbursement/uptake statistics for adaptive DBS and directional leads are not covered.
- The Boston Scientific and Abbott adaptive-sensing programs were not researched.

## Key Question 5: MR-guided focused ultrasound — Exablate Neuro approvals (tremor 2016, tremor-dominant PD 2018, unilateral pallidotomy 2021, staged bilateral pallidothalamic tractotomy 2025), the Krishna 2023 NEJM trial, and comparison with DBS

### Takeaway
Unilateral MRgFUS pallidotomy produced a 69% vs 32% responder rate against sham at 3 months in the pivotal NEJM 2023 trial (with dysarthria, gait disturbance and taste/visual effects as lesion-related AEs), and on July 9, 2025 the FDA extended Exablate Neuro to staged bilateral pallidothalamic tractotomy based on a 54-patient phase 3 (40 bilateral) showing 33.9% off-medication MDS-UPDRS III improvement after the second side. FUS is incisionless, single-session and non-adjustable; retrospective comparisons show equivalent contralateral tremor control to DBS but no axial/contralateral benefit without bilateral treatment.

### Cited Findings
- Pivotal RCT (Krishna, Fishman, Eisenberg et al., N Engl J Med 2023;388:683-693; NCT03319485; funded by Insightec): 94 patients with dyskinesia or motor fluctuations randomized 3:1 to unilateral GPi ablation (n=69) or sham (n=25); response (≥3-point decrease in treated-side off-med MDS-UPDRS III or in on-med UDysRS at 3 months) in 45/65 (69%) vs 7/22 (32%) (difference 37 points; 95% CI 15-60; P=0.003); of responders 19 met motor-only, 8 dyskinesia-only, 18 both; 30/39 responders assessed at 12 months maintained response; pallidotomy-related AEs: dysarthria, gait disturbance, loss of taste, visual disturbance, facial weakness. — [Krishna 2023, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36812432/); [NEJM PDF](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/nejmoa2202721); authors' reply to correspondence N Engl J Med 2023;388:1823-1824 — [Fishman 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163638/)
- Exablate Neuro regulatory chronology: essential tremor (unilateral) 2016; tremor-dominant PD 2018; unilateral pallidotomy for PD dyskinesia/motor symptoms 2021; contralateral staged ET 2022; staged bilateral pallidothalamic tractotomy for advanced PD approved July 9, 2025 (limited launch at select centers in 2025). — [NeurologyLive, July 2025](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/exablate-neuro-gains-fda-approval-staged-bilateral-focused-ultrasound-advanced-pd); [NeurologyLive 2021 approval](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/exablate-neuro-device-approved-treatment-advanced-parkinson-disease/1000); [FDA patient guide P150038/S037](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf15/P150038S037C.pdf); [post-approval unilateral pallidotomy registry NCT05539196](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05539196)
- Bilateral staged PTT phase 3 (NCT04728295; 9 centers in US, Europe, Asia; presented as late-breaking abstract LBA-21 by Rodríguez-Oroz at the MDS International Congress, Honolulu, October 5-9, 2025): 54 unilateral, 40 proceeded to staged bilateral ≥6 months later; primary endpoint off-med upper/lower-extremity MDS-UPDRS III at 3 months post-bilateral. After unilateral treatment: 50% improvement in MDS-UPDRS III OFF and 56.3% in Part IV; after bilateral: 33.9% improvement in Part III OFF (total 51.3 → 33.4; P<.0001), sustained to 12 months. AEs: post-unilateral 30 events in 21 patients (87% mild, 0% severe; 60% resolved by 6 months); post-bilateral 43 events in 22 patients (70% mild, 2% severe; 40% resolved by 12 months); most common imbalance, dysarthria, fatigue. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/bilateral-mr-guided-focused-ultrasound-shows-benefit-managing-parkinson-motor-complications-phase-3-trial); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04728295](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04728295)
- Historical basis of PTT: Magara et al. (J Ther Ultrasound 2014) first MRgFUS pallidothalamic tractotomy series; repeated sonications needed for white-matter lesioning; mean UPDRS reduction 60.9% in the nine fully lesioned patients. — [Magara 2014, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25512869)
- MDS EBM 2025 rates "pallidotomy" *likely efficacious* for motor fluctuations (lesioning class; FUS-specific trials were included only as available). — [de Bie 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/)
- Monje et al. (2026): approved MRgHIFU targets now include VIM, GPi and the pallidothalamic tract, with STN under investigation, "each target with distinct benefits and adverse event profiles." — [Monje 2026, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41706242)

**Comparison with DBS**
- Single-surgeon retrospective comparison in tremor (Baumann et al., Mov Disord 2025;40:834-843): equivalent tremor reduction in the more affected hand (DBS 62.4% vs MRgFUS 69.4%; P=0.958) but contralateral and axial tremor improved only with bilateral DBS; DBS had more procedure/hardware AEs (17% vs 2%; P=0.023) but a nonsignificantly lower rate of persistent treatment-related AEs affecting ADLs (7% vs 13%); SAEs 23.3% vs 19.2%. — [Baumann 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40013514)
- Bilateral FUS thalamotomy vs bilateral DBS for ET (Sarica et al., Mov Disord 2025): comparable efficacy; AE-free rates 41% DBS vs 32% MRgFUS, all grade 1-2. — [Sarica 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40318052)
- Network meta-analysis of DBS vs FUS for parkinsonian tremor (2021) found comparable tremor suppression. — [PubMed 33461975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33461975/)
- Systematic comparison of STN-DBS, GPi-DBS and STN-FUS (2025): all improved motor performance and QoL; STN-FUS "potentially serving as an alternative to STN_DBS with comparable efficacy." — [PMC12074956](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12074956/)
- Reviews emphasize FUS is incisionless, single-session, requires no hardware/battery, but is ablative and non-adjustable, versus DBS's reversibility and programmability. — [Ko et al., Parkinsonism Relat Disord 2023](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37164870); [AHRQ comparative effectiveness references](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619801/); [Focused Ultrasound Foundation](https://www.fusfoundation.org/diseases-and-conditions/parkinsons-disease/)

### Inferences
- The 2025 bilateral approval is the first regulatory acceptance of bilateral ablative surgery for PD since bilateral radiofrequency pallidotomy was abandoned for speech/cognitive risk; staging (≥6 months) and PTT targeting appear to be the risk-mitigation strategy, but 30% of post-bilateral AEs were moderate and 60% unresolved at 12 months.
- FUS is best positioned for patients who are poor DBS candidates (age, comorbidity, anticoagulation, preference against implants) or have asymmetric tremor/dyskinesia; adjustable, bilateral, and progressive-disease needs favor DBS.

### Gaps
- No randomized head-to-head trial of FUS vs DBS in PD was found.
- Peer-reviewed publication of the bilateral PTT phase 3 was not located; data come from a congress late-breaker as reported by NeurologyLive.
- Long-term (>12-month) durability of pallidal FUS beyond the 39-patient 12-month subset in NEJM 2023 was not retrieved.

## Key Question 6: Treatment guidelines — MDS EBM reviews (Fox 2018; de Bie 2025 update), AAN 2021 early-PD dopaminergic guideline, NICE NG71

### Takeaway
The three major guidelines converge: levodopa is the preferred initial therapy for symptomatic early PD (AAN 2021 Level B; NICE 2017 "offer levodopa" when symptoms affect quality of life), with agonists and MAO-B inhibitors as alternatives when symptoms are mild, explicit ICD counselling, and no agent endorsed as neuroprotective; the MDS EBM reviews (2018, with the 2025 motor-fluctuations update using Cochrane risk-of-bias and GRADE) grade specific interventions as efficacious/likely efficacious.

### Cited Findings
- MDS EBM 2018 (Fox, Katzenschlager, Lim et al., Mov Disord 2018;33:1248-1266): literature to Dec 31, 2016, 143 new studies; no clinically useful disease-modifying interventions; early monotherapy: non-ergot agonists, levodopa, selegiline, rasagiline clinically useful; adjunct early/stable: non-ergot agonists, rasagiline, zonisamide; gait: rivastigmine possibly useful, physiotherapy clinically useful; motor fluctuations: most non-ergot agonists, pergolide, levodopa ER, intestinal levodopa, entacapone, opicapone, rasagiline, zonisamide, safinamide, bilateral STN and GPi DBS clinically useful; dyskinesia: amantadine, clozapine, STN/GPi DBS clinically useful. — [Fox 2018, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570866/)
- MDS EBM 2025 motor fluctuations update (de Bie, Katzenschlager, Swinnen et al., Mov Disord 2025;40:776-794; search Jan 1, 2023; 102 RCTs ≥3 months): new methodology with Cochrane Risk of Bias tool and modified GRADE; gradings listed in KQ2. — [de Bie 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055961/); [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.30162)
- Companion MDS review of treatment options for motor fluctuations (Höglinger et al., Mov Disord 2025). — [Wiley mds.70107](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70107); [PMC12710155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12710155/)
- AAN 2021 practice guideline (Pringsheim, Day, Smith et al., Neurology 2021;97:942-957; published Nov 15-16, 2021): clinicians "should recommend levodopa as the initial preferential dopaminergic therapy" (Level B); levodopa gives superior motor benefit vs agonists but more dyskinesia within 5 years; no agonist formulation superior; long-acting levodopa/levodopa-entacapone not superior to IR in early disease; higher ICD risk with agonists; discontinuation for AEs more likely with agonists and MAO-B inhibitors. — [Pringsheim 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34782410/); [Neurology full text](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000012868); [AAN press release](https://www.aan.com/PressRoom/Home/PressRelease/4936)
- PD MED (Gray et al., Lancet 2014;384:1196-1205; 1,620 patients, 7-year observation): "Very small but persistent benefits ... for patient-rated mobility scores when treatment is initiated with levodopa compared with levodopa-sparing therapy"; EQ-5D utility 0.03 better with levodopa; dementia HR 0.81, institutionalization 0.86, death 0.85 (all NS); MAO-B inhibitors at least as effective as agonists as levodopa-sparing therapy. — [PD MED 2014, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24928805)
- NICE NG71 "Parkinson's disease in adults" published 19 July 2017 (replacing CG35 of 2006), amended Dec 2020 (modafinil MHRA advice), last reviewed 19 December 2024 (links to technology appraisals for drooling and advanced PD added, no change in practice). Recommendations: 1.3.5 "Offer levodopa to people in the early stages ... whose motor symptoms impact on their quality of life"; 1.3.6 "Consider a choice of dopamine agonists, levodopa or MAO-B inhibitors" when symptoms do not impact quality of life; 1.3.7 no ergot agonists first line; 1.3.8 give oral and written information on ICD risk and record the discussion; 1.3.10 offer agonists, MAO-B or COMT inhibitors as adjuncts for dyskinesia/fluctuations despite optimal levodopa; 1.3.2-1.3.4 never withdraw drugs abruptly or use "drug holidays" (NMS risk); 1.6.1-1.6.4 do not use vitamin E, co-enzyme Q10, dopamine agonists or MAO-B inhibitors as neuroprotective therapies outside trials. — [NICE NG71 overview](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71); [NG71 Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/Recommendations); BMJ summary: Rogers G et al., "Parkinson's disease: summary of updated NICE guidance," BMJ 2017;358:j1951 — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28751362)
- German DGN S2k guideline (Höglinger, Trenkwalder et al., Neurol Res Pract 2024;6:30) covers initial pharmacotherapy, pump therapies and DBS, "state of the art at the beginning of 2024." — [Höglinger 2024, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38845028)

### Inferences
- Guideline convergence on levodopa-first rests on LEAP, PD MED, CALM-PD's efficacy gap and the ICD data; the residual role for agonists/MAO-B inhibitors is in mild, non-disabling early disease or younger patients where dyskinesia risk is weighed more heavily.
- The 2025 MDS shift to GRADE lowered several infusion therapies from "clinically useful" to "likely efficacious," reflecting single pivotal trials rather than loss of efficacy.

### Gaps
- NICE NG71 DBS and LCIG recommendations (section 1.3 advanced disease) were not extracted; only the overview and first-line/adjunct text were retrieved.
- The MDS EBM review of non-motor and early-PD treatments has not been updated beyond 2018/2019 in the sources reached; no 2025-2026 MDS early-PD monotherapy update was located.
- Whether the AAN has issued any update since 2021 was not verified.

## Key Question 7: Gait, freezing and falls — cholinesterase inhibitors, spinal cord stimulation, cueing, and the limits of pharmacotherapy

### Takeaway
Axial symptoms respond poorly to dopaminergic drugs and DBS and progress despite them (freezing/postural instability worsened years 1-5 after STN DBS in Krack 2003; axial scores and falls rose at 10-15 years); the only positive RCT of a drug for falls is ReSPonD (rivastigmine improved step-time variability, phase 2, n=130), rated "possibly useful" by MDS; cueing training (RESCUE) gives small, short-lived gains; and spinal cord stimulation remains investigational, with a 2026 double-blind feasibility RCT (n=12) failing its 6-month gait endpoint while showing 12-month lower-body improvement.

### Cited Findings
- Nutt, Bloem, Giladi et al. (Lancet Neurol 2011;10:734-744): FOG involves frontal cortex, basal ganglia and midbrain locomotor region; "Medications, deep brain stimulation, and rehabilitation techniques can alleviate symptoms of FoG in some patients, but these treatments lack efficacy in patients with advanced FoG." — [Nutt 2011, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21777828/)
- FOG affects 38-65% of people with PD. — [Salomon et al., Nat Commun 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38844449)
- Cognition is worse in freezers across all domains (145 papers, n=9,010). — [Monaghan 2023, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36738813)
- Gao et al. (Transl Neurodegener 2020): levodopa-resistant FOG has limited drug options; istradefylline and rasagiline "showed promise"; rTMS over SMA, dual-site DBS, SCS, VNS and wearable cueing devices are promising non-pharmacological approaches. — [Gao 2020, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32322387)

**Cholinesterase inhibitors**
- ReSPonD (Henderson, Lord, Brodie et al., Lancet Neurol 2016;15:249-258): single-center phase 2, 130 PD fallers without dementia randomized to rivastigmine (to 12 mg/day) or placebo for 32 weeks; step-time variability improved for normal walking (ratio of geometric means 0.72; 95% CI 0.58-0.88; p=0.002) and simple dual task (0.79; p=0.045), not complex dual task; nausea 31% vs 5%, vomiting 17% vs 5%; "might reduce the frequency of falls"; phase 3 needed. — [Henderson 2016, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26795874/); [protocol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3880104/)
- MDS EBM 2018: rivastigmine "possibly useful" for gait; physiotherapy clinically useful; movement-strategy training and formalized patterned exercises possibly useful. — [Fox 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570866/)

**Cueing and physiotherapy**
- RESCUE (Nieuwboer et al., J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry 2007;78:134-140): home cueing training, crossover RCT; posture-and-gait score +4.2% (p=0.005); freezing severity -5.5% in freezers (p=0.007); gait speed, step length, balance improved; effects "had reduced considerably at 6-week follow-up," arguing for permanent cueing devices. — [Nieuwboer 2007, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17229744)
- Systematic review/meta-analysis of cueing for FOG (Cosentino et al., Parkinsonism Relat Disord 2023; 33 studies): gait initiation benefits from visual and auditory cues; step/stride length improved by visual cues; turning time and step-time variability reduced by auditory and visual cues; "one cue does not fit all." — [Cosentino 2023, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37086934)
- Physiotherapy meta-analysis (Radder et al., J Parkinsons Dis 2020; 191 trials, 7,998 participants): conventional physiotherapy improved motor symptoms, gait and QoL; treadmill and resistance training improved gait; dual-task training did not significantly improve outcomes. — [Radder 2020, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32917125)
- Cochrane network meta-analysis of exercise (Ernst et al., 2023, CD013856): beneficial effects on motor sign severity and QoL for most exercise types with little difference between types. — [Ernst 2023, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36602886)

**Spinal cord stimulation**
- Terkelsen, Hvingelby, Johnsen et al. (Nat Commun 2026;17; NCT05110053): 14 patients assessed with [18F]-FDG and [18F]-FEOBV PET; 12 randomized to 6 months MicroBurst SCS or sham then 6-month open extension; 17 (12%) of screened eligible; recruitment feasible (1.2/month), SCS well tolerated; at 6 months MicroBurst did not improve gait vs sham (primary PIGD endpoint not met) though bradykinesia/rigidity improved; at 12 months Lower Body and Gait score improved -4.31 points (p=0.0012) with decreased thalamic metabolism and right anterior insula FEOBV uptake; larger trials warranted. — [Terkelsen 2026, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41611672/); [Nature article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68782-w)
- Ongoing randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled crossover trial of implanted SCS for refractory FOG (29 patients, 3 paradigms including sham over 3 weeks). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06798844](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06798844); related SCS trials [NCT04683861](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04683861), [NCT02388204](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02388204)
- Non-invasive transcutaneous magnetic SCS pilot reported 22% improvement in FOG and 17.4% in UPDRS-III. — [ScienceDirect 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967586820315538); [NCT04783493](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04783493)

**DBS and axial symptoms**
- Krack 2003: on-medication akinesia, speech, postural stability and freezing of gait worsened between years 1 and 5 after STN DBS (P<0.001). — [Krack 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14614167/)
- Zampogna 2022: axial scores worsened at 10 and 15 years post STN DBS; preoperative axial score, frontal dysfunction and age at onset predicted axial progression. — [Zampogna 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36153351/)
- Adaptive and low-frequency DBS show preliminary gait signals (ADAPT-START ~40% FOG-Q reduction, unblinded; 60 Hz aDBS pilot). — [Cascino 2026](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41741462); [Negi 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41480041/)
- Heim and Poewe (J Parkinsons Dis 2026;16:24-37) list gait/balance, posture, speech and swallowing among the persistent unmet needs of motor pharmacotherapy. — [Heim 2026, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40702823)

### Inferences
- The cholinergic hypothesis (pedunculopontine/basal forebrain degeneration) is the main pharmacological lead for falls, but ReSPonD remains an unreplicated single-center phase 2 after a decade; no agent is approved for FOG or falls.
- Because SCS has now failed a (small) blinded primary gait endpoint while showing delayed benefits, the field's next step is adequately powered sham-controlled trials with standardized paradigms rather than open-label series.

### Gaps
- No phase 3 trial of rivastigmine (or any cholinesterase inhibitor) for falls was located; status of a planned confirmatory study is unknown.
- Istradefylline's FOG signal is from secondary analyses summarized in reviews; no dedicated RCT was retrieved.
- Data on wearable/closed-loop cueing devices (e.g., laser shoes, vibrotactile) RCTs were not retrieved in this session.

## Key Question 8: Pipeline for symptomatic therapy — tavapadon (approved as Juvmo, Sept 2026), P2B001, mesdopetam, solriamfetol, buntanetap, others

### Takeaway
Tavapadon, the first selective D1/D5 partial agonist, completed three positive phase 3 trials (TEMPO-1/-2 monotherapy: ~-11.5 to -12.1 and -9.1 point MDS-UPDRS II+III differences; TEMPO-3 adjunct: +1.1 h good ON), was submitted to FDA on Sept 26, 2025, and was approved as Juvmo in late September 2026 with US availability expected October 2026. P2B001 (low-dose ER pramipexole + rasagiline) beat its components and matched titrated pramipexole-ER with less sleepiness in a 544-patient phase 3 (2024) but its regulatory status could not be confirmed; mesdopetam (D3 antagonist) missed its phase 2b primary endpoint on good ON time but improved UDysRS and is slated for phase 3; buntanetap and solriamfetol have no PD motor efficacy evidence retrievable in this session.

### Cited Findings

**Tavapadon (Juvmo)**
- Rationale (Bezard et al., Mov Disord 2024;39:476-487): D2/D3-mediated AEs (somnolence, ICDs, hallucinations, hypotension) motivated D1/D5-selective partial agonism; prior full D1 agonists failed on cardiovascular AEs and pharmacokinetics. — [Bezard 2024, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36999711)
- TEMPO-1 (Pahwa, Moro, Espay et al., JAMA Neurol 2026;83:452-460; NCT04201093): fixed-dose, early PD (mean duration 0.7 y), 529 randomized to 5 mg (n=177), 15 mg (n=177) or placebo (n=175); week-26 MDS-UPDRS II+III: -9.7 vs +1.8 (difference -11.5; 95% CI -13.8 to -9.2; P<.001; d=1.14) for 5 mg and -10.2 (difference -12.1; d=1.20) for 15 mg; AEs nausea 25.4%, headache 16.7%, dizziness 12.7%. — [Pahwa 2026, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41860533)
- TEMPO-2 (Fernandez, Bhatia, Cloud et al., Lancet Neurol 2026;25:721-730): flexible-dose 5-15 mg, 304 randomized (151/153), 75 sites, 13 countries, disease duration 0.86 y; week-26 MDS-UPDRS II+III LSM -10.3 vs -1.2 (difference -9.1; 95% CI -11.7 to -6.5; p<0.0001); discontinuation 38% vs 15% (AE-related 24% vs 4%); nausea 30% vs 3%, headache 17%, dizziness 16%; "low incidence of somnolence and impulse control disorders." — [Fernandez 2026 (TEMPO-2), Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42456682); [AbbVie topline Dec 9, 2024](https://news.abbvie.com/2024-12-09-AbbVie-Announces-Positive-Topline-Results-for-the-Phase-3-TEMPO-2-Trial-Evaluating-Tavapadon-as-a-Monotherapy-for-Parkinsons-Disease)
- TEMPO-3 (Fernandez, Isaacson, Hauser et al., JAMA Neurol 2026;83:442-451): adjunct to levodopa (≥400 mg) in fluctuators, 148 sites, 14 countries, 507 randomized (252/255), baseline OFF 5.5 h; good ON +1.70 vs +0.60 h (difference 1.10 h; 95% CI 0.60-1.70; P<.001); OFF -1.88 vs -0.93 h (difference -0.94 h); AEs 71.7% vs 55.1%, 93.2% nonserious; nausea 14.3%, dyskinesia 10.0%, dizziness 7.6%. — [Fernandez 2026 (TEMPO-3), PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41860544/)
- TEMPO-4 open-label extension interim (MDS 2025 late-breaker): sustained long-term safety; AbbVie reports at 85 weeks 93% did not increase levodopa dose and 94% of those without levodopa never initiated it. — [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/parkinson-agent-tavapadon-continued-efficacy-safety-phase-3-tempo-4-trial); [AbbVie approval release](https://news.abbvie.com/2026-09-28-U-S-FDA-Approves-AbbVies-JUVMO-TM-tavapadon-for-Parkinsons-Disease)
- NDA submitted to FDA September 26, 2025 based on TEMPO-1/-2/-3. — [AbbVie, Sept 26, 2025](https://news.abbvie.com/2025-09-26-AbbVie-Submits-New-Drug-Application-to-U-S-FDA-for-Tavapadon-for-the-Treatment-of-Parkinsons-Disease)
- FDA approval of Juvmo (tavapadon) for "treatment of Parkinson's disease in adults," once daily with or without levodopa; 5/10/15 mg tablets plus 0.25/1 mg titration pack; label AEs nausea, headache, taste change, fatigue, vomiting, dry mouth, anxiety; US availability expected October 2026. AbbVie's release is dated September 28, 2026 — [AbbVie press release](https://news.abbvie.com/2026-09-28-U-S-FDA-Approves-AbbVies-JUVMO-TM-tavapadon-for-Parkinsons-Disease); the Michael J. Fox Foundation gives the approval date as September 25, 2026 and describes lower rates of dyskinesia, orthostatic hypotension and hallucinations than older agonists — [MJFF](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/fda-approves-tavapadon-parkinsons-motor-symptoms); [MJFF drug page](https://www.michaeljfox.org/news/juvmo-tavapadon); additional coverage [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/tavapadon-for-parkinson-disease-approved-by-fda), [Endpoints](https://endpoints.news/fda-approves-abbvie-next-gen-dopamine-therapy-for-parkinsons/), [VJNeurology](https://www.vjneurology.com/fda-approves-tavapadon-the-first-and-only-selective-d1-d5-receptor-agonist-for-parkinsons-disease/)

**P2B001 (ER pramipexole 0.6 mg + ER rasagiline 0.75 mg)**
- Phase 3 (Olanow, Hauser, Burdick et al., Mov Disord 2024;39:350-359; NCT03329508): 544 untreated PD patients randomized 2:2:2:1 to P2B001, pramipexole-ER 0.6 mg, rasagiline-ER 0.75 mg, or titrated pramipexole-ER (1.5-4.5 mg; mean 3.2 mg) for 12 weeks; UPDRS II+III differences -2.66 vs pramipexole component (P=0.0018) and -3.30 vs rasagiline component (P<0.0001); comparable to titrated pramipexole-ER with less Epworth worsening (-2.66; P<0.0001) and fewer somnolence, orthostatic hypotension and neuropsychiatric AEs. — [Olanow 2024, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37886872/); [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/p2b001-meets-phase-3-primary-secondary-end-points-early-parkinson-disease); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/991228)
- Pharma Two B stated in 2022 that it planned to file with FDA. — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/health-and-wellness/article-689352); [Parkinson's News Today](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/p2b001-shows-good-efficacy-safety-early-parkinsons-phase-3-trial/)

**Mesdopetam (IRL790; D3 antagonist for dyskinesia)**
- Phase 2b (Antonini, O'Suilleabhain, Stocchi et al., Mov Disord Clin Pract 2025;12:796-806): 12-week RCT of 2.5/5/7.5 mg BID vs placebo in patients with ≥2 h troublesome dyskinesia; primary endpoint (good ON time) not met; modified UDysRS significantly improved at 2.5 and 7.5 mg; dose-dependent OFF reduction greatest at 7.5 mg; AE profile similar to placebo; 7.5 mg BID preferred for phase 3. — [Antonini 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40008823/); [Wiley](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mdc3.70004)
- Ipsen acquired global rights and leads phase 3; a phase 3 LID trial has been announced as planned. — [Parkinson's News Today (Ipsen)](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/ipsen-acquires-global-rights-mesdopetam-parkinsons-treatment/); [Parkinson's News Today (phase 3 planned)](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/phase-3-trial-mesdopeta-parkinsons-dyskinesia-planned/)
- LID pipeline review (Al-Kassmy et al., Neurodegener Dis Manag 2025;15:235-244): promising phase 2 results for befiradol (5-HT1A), mesdopetam and CPL500036 (PDE inhibitor); foliglurax (mGlu4 NAM) and JM-010 (buspirone + zolmitriptan) failed; robot-assisted rTMS of pre-SMA promising. — [Al-Kassmy 2025, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40616455)

**Other / context**
- Buntanetap (posiphen; (+)-phenserine) — the only PD-relevant primary literature retrieved concerned pharmacokinetics of a new crystalline form (Morin et al., Biomolecules 2025) and a gastroretentive formulation (Verma et al., AAPS PharmSciTech 2024); these describe it as improving cognition in AD and PD patients but report no motor efficacy data. — [Morin 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41008606/); [Verma 2024, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39192157/)
- Solriamfetol — PubMed/Europe PMC searches for "solriamfetol AND Parkinson" returned only hypersomnolence guidelines (AASM 2021), i.e., no PD motor trial. — [Maski 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34743789/)
- Pipeline overview (McFarthing et al., J Parkinsons Dis 2023;13:427-439): 139 active trials as of Jan 31, 2023, 76 (55%) symptomatic and 63 (45%) disease-modifying; 20 (14%) in phase 3; "slow progress and lack of agents transitioning from Phase 2 to Phase 3 is concerning." — [McFarthing 2023, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37302040)
- Unmet needs framing (Heim & Poewe, J Parkinsons Dis 2026): poorly responsive tremor, motor complications, gait/balance/posture/speech/swallowing; future directions include novel targets, new delivery modes, combinations and wearable-guided dosing. — [Heim 2026, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40702823)

### Inferences
- Tavapadon's effect sizes as monotherapy (≈-9 to -12 MDS-UPDRS II+III points; d≈1.1-1.2) are large relative to historical agonist trials, but 24% AE-driven discontinuation in TEMPO-2 and 30% nausea indicate titration burden; its clinical niche will hinge on confirmed low ICD/somnolence rates in long-term use.
- P2B001's strategy (low-dose fixed combination to avoid titration-related AEs) targets the same tolerability problem as tavapadon from the opposite direction (D2/D3 at lower dose).
- Mesdopetam's dissociation (UDysRS improved, good ON time unchanged) suggests diary-based "good ON" may be insensitive to dyskinesia severity, which matters for phase 3 endpoint selection.

# Subtypes

verified: 2026-10-19

Parkinson's subtypes are five overlapping axes, not one taxonomy. A person sits somewhere on all five. This page gives, for each axis, what the categories are, how they are confirmed, and what each changes about treatment today. Claims marked `unverified` could not be traced to a primary source inside this repo and should be confirmed before being relied on.

## Axis 1: Genetic

Confirmed by a gene panel. See `get-typed.md` for routes. About 13 percent of people tested through PD GENEration have a reportable variant.

**LRRK2** (mostly G2019S). Autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance: roughly 25 to 42 percent by age 80 in non-Ashkenazi carriers, about 26 percent in Ashkenazi Jewish carriers, higher in Arab-Berber populations. Later onset, slower progression, more tremor, less cognitive decline. Around a third are negative on the CSF synuclein assay. Treatment: standard symptomatic therapy. The LRRK2 kinase inhibitor BIIB122 failed its phase 2b LUMA trial in idiopathic PD in May 2026 despite over 90 percent peripheral kinase inhibition, and Biogen dropped the LRRK2 antisense oligonucleotide in February 2025. The only remaining clinical test of the LRRK2 hypothesis is BEACON, a phase 2a in carriers, active but not recruiting, data expected first half of 2027. Sources: corpus files 02 and 06; [LUMA update](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/21/3299820/0/en/Biogen-and-Denali-Therapeutics-Provide-Update-on-Phase-2b-LUMA-Study-of-BIIB122-DNL151-in-Early-Stage-Parkinson-s-Disease.html).

**GBA1.** The most common genetic risk factor, up to about 5 percent of PD overall and 15 to 20 percent in Ashkenazi Jews; an African-ancestry intronic variant (rs3115534) is common in some populations. Risk factor rather than deterministic. Earlier onset, faster progression, markedly higher rates of cognitive impairment, hallucinations, depression, REM sleep behavior disorder and hyposmia. Treatment implications are the most concrete of any subtype: monitor cognition early; avoid anticholinergics and other cognition-impairing drugs where possible; discuss cognitive risk before deep brain stimulation (`unverified`: evidence of worse post-operative cognitive outcomes in GBA1 carriers, reported in Annals of Neurology 2022). The GBA1 pathway has failed with substrate reduction (venglustat, 2023), a small-molecule activator (BIA 28-6156, June 2026) and partially with ambroxol in PD dementia (2025). ASPro-PD, a UK phase 3 of ambroxol, is the last large randomized test of the chaperone approach and is recruiting; GREAT in the Netherlands is also recruiting. Gene therapy PR001/LY3884961 (PROPEL) is active but not recruiting, with completion estimated 2031. Sources: corpus files 02 and 06.

**PRKN, PINK1, PARK7 (DJ-1).** Autosomal recessive, often onset before 40. Slow progression, dystonia at onset, strong sustained levodopa response, early dyskinesias, cognition usually preserved. Parkin cases frequently lack Lewy bodies and test synuclein-negative. Treatment: excellent long-term candidates for deep brain stimulation (`unverified` as a formal guideline statement; it is the consensus in the clinical literature). Mitochondrial-pathway trials target this biology.

**SNCA** multiplications and point mutations. Rare, fully penetrant, aggressive, early dementia. Relevant mainly for prognosis and family planning.

**VPS35, RAB32 and others.** Rare dominant forms; RAB32 Ser71Arg (2024) acts through LRRK2 kinase activation.

## Axis 2: Alpha-synuclein status

The axis the 2024 biological frameworks are built on. Confirmed by CSF seed amplification assay (SYNTap) or skin biopsy (Syn-One); blood assays remain research-grade at 80 to 85 percent sensitivity. Dopaminergic imaging (DaTscan) then stages neurodegeneration.

**Synuclein-positive (S+).** The large majority of clinically diagnosed PD. Defines "neuronal alpha-synuclein disease" under the NSD-ISS framework (Simuni, Chahine, Marek; Lancet Neurology, February 2024) and the S+ category under SynNeurGe (Höglinger, Lang; same issue). Most people with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder or hyposmia plus dopaminergic deficit are already S+, which is why prevention trials target them. Treatment: this is the biological population for anti-synuclein antibodies. Prasinezumab is the only such agent with a replicated directional signal (PASADENA trend, PADOVA hazard ratio 0.84, levodopa subgroup 0.79) and is in phase 3 (PARAISO, 900 participants, readout 2029). Cinpanemab, minzasolmin and several others failed. No trial yet enrolls on S+ status alone; the FDA issued a Letter of Support in 2024 for using the assay as an enrichment biomarker.

**Synuclein-negative (S−) with clinical PD.** Estimated 10 to 15 percent of clinical PD. Disproportionately LRRK2 and Parkin carriers, plus people with normal smell, plus some misdiagnosed tauopathies (progressive supranuclear palsy, corticobasal degeneration). SynNeurGe keeps these people inside "Parkinson's disease"; NSD-ISS excludes them. Treatment: anti-synuclein drugs are unlikely to apply; the diagnosis deserves a second look; standard symptomatic therapy still works.

Caveats on the frameworks: neither assay separates PD from MSA or DLB at the individual level, results are qualitative, and NSD-ISS staging has been criticized because levodopa treatment moved nearly half of PPMI participants to an earlier stage (Espay, Movement Disorders, June 2025). Source: corpus file 03.

## Axis 3: Body-first versus brain-first

Borghammer's alpha-synuclein origin-site and connectome model: pathology starts either in the enteric and autonomic nervous system and climbs via the vagus to the brainstem (body-first), or in the olfactory bulb or amygdala and descends (brain-first). Supported by molecular imaging and clinical clustering studies; contradicted by some structural imaging and asymmetry studies. Confirmation is research-grade: polysomnography for REM sleep behavior disorder, cardiac sympathetic imaging, autonomic testing, symmetry on dopamine imaging, colon biopsy. The clinical proxy: whether dream-enactment sleep behavior preceded motor symptoms by years.

**Body-first.** REM sleep behavior disorder before motor onset, early constipation and orthostatic hypotension, symmetric motor onset, faster progression, substantially higher dementia risk. Treatment: aggressive management of orthostatic hypotension, earlier cognitive screening, fall prevention. No subtype-specific drug. Gut-targeted approaches are an active research direction; epidemiology on vagotomy and appendectomy is in corpus file 09.

**Brain-first.** Asymmetric onset, no sleep disorder at diagnosis, autonomic symptoms later, slower cognitive decline. Better prognosis.

A working hypothesis in the literature pairs GBA1 with body-first and LRRK2 with brain-first (`unverified` as a quantified claim). Source: corpus file 01; [Horsager and Borghammer 2024](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/brain-first-vs-body-first-parkinsons-disease-an-update-on-recent-/).

## Axis 4: Clinical motor subtype

Tremor-dominant, postural instability and gait difficulty (PIGD), or indeterminate, computed from MDS-UPDRS item ratios. No test needed. This axis has the most immediate treatment implications because it is about what symptomatic therapy works.

**Tremor-dominant.** Slower progression. Tremor is often the least levodopa-responsive motor symptom but responds well to deep brain stimulation and to MR-guided focused ultrasound (Exablate Neuro: tremor-dominant PD approved 2018, unilateral pallidotomy 2021, staged bilateral pallidothalamic tractotomy July 2025).

**Gait-dominant.** Faster progression, more falls, more cognitive decline. Freezing of gait responds poorly to stimulation and to dopaminergic drugs. Structured high-intensity exercise (SPARX and Park-in-Shape protocols) is the best-evidenced intervention; cholinesterase inhibitors have trial evidence for reducing falls (`unverified` against primary source here). Source: corpus files 04 and 08.

## Axis 5: Data-driven prognostic clusters

Cohort analyses repeatedly find three clusters: mild motor-predominant, intermediate, and diffuse malignant. Diffuse malignant means early cognitive impairment plus REM sleep behavior disorder plus orthostatic hypotension at baseline, and it tracks closely with body-first. Confirmed by baseline clinical assessment. Implications are prognosis and trial stratification; for a person, it sets how closely to monitor and how early to plan. (`unverified` inside this repo; the primary sources are Fereshtehnejad et al., JAMA Neurology 2015 and Brain 2017.)

Age at onset is a related axis: young-onset (before 50) has more dystonia, slower progression, more dyskinesia and a much higher genetic yield; late-onset has more cognitive and gait involvement.

## Prodromal

People with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder convert to a synucleinopathy at about 6.3 percent per year and 73.5 percent by 12 years; most are already synuclein-positive. Hyposmia plus dopaminergic deficit conferred a 17-fold relative risk in PARS. The MDS prodromal criteria compute a probability from these markers. Prevention trials are beginning to recruit this group; PPMI enrolls them by smell test and sleep disorder. Source: corpus file 03.

## What subtype changes today, in one list

- Which disease-modifying trial a person can enter. Every running pivotal program is restricted by subtype or stage.
- Deep brain stimulation and focused ultrasound candidacy and expected benefit.
- Cognitive risk, which drugs to avoid, and how early to screen.
- Autonomic management and fall prevention priority.
- Whether a synuclein-negative result should prompt a diagnostic re-look.
- Whether relatives have a reason to consider testing.

Symptomatic treatment itself is still mostly subtype-agnostic. Levodopa works across all of these.
